//角落書屋/Slices/Travel & Directions
北	běi	north; northern; to flee — Two people standing back-to-back: the direction of cold and turning away that became the name of China's capital.
朝	cháo	dynasty, imperial court, to face toward; (zhāo) morning — The sun rising among grasses with the moon still in the sky. From that dawn scene grow two readings: zhāo, the morning and its youthful vigor, and cháo, the court that met at daybreak, the dynasty it served, and the preposition toward.
城	chéng	city, city wall, town — A rampart of earth raised around a settlement: the character where the wall and the city it protects are one and the same word.
出	chū	to exit, to emerge, to produce — 出 is two plants growing upward out of the earth: emergence itself written as a glyph, from physical exits to publication to standing out from the crowd.
穿	chuān	to pierce, to bore through, to wear, to put on — A tooth gnawing through the roof of a cave: one image for boring a hole and for the body slipping into clothing.
船	chuán	ship, boat, vessel — A pictophonetic built on 舟 (boat): the hull carries the meaning while the right side supplies the sound, covering every craft from sampan to ocean liner.
到	dào	to arrive, to reach; to — As a resultative complement suffix, 到 converts any action verb into a claim of completion: 听到 means not just hearing but hearing that arrived, perception proven by contact.
道	dào	way, path — The character that opens the Dàodéjīng and names Daoism: way, path, principle, and the measure word for rivers and light.
地	dì	earth, ground, land — Heaven above, earth below: the second power of the cosmos, and the root of every word about place, ground, and belonging.
东	dōng	east; eastern — The sun rising behind a tree, and the direction that anchored China's cosmological, political, and poetic imagination.
动	dòng	to move, to act, to stir — Force applied to a thing sets it in motion. From that one idea grow the words for sport and activity, for the stirred heart, for the drive that pushes a machine or a life forward, and for the verb itself.
飞	fēi	to fly, to dart; high, swift — A single bird caught mid-flap, one outstretched wing and the rising line of its body, frozen at the instant it leaves the ground.
广	guǎng	wide, broad, to spread — A radical and a word: the cliff-shelter that became the prefix for everything expansive, from Guangzhou to Guangdong to radio broadcasts.
海	hǎi	sea, ocean, a vast quantity — Water plus the phonetic 每: the sea, the ocean, and by extension any vastness. From 大海 and 海洋 to the coastal city 上海, the navy 海军, and the crowd that becomes a 人海.
河	hé	river, the Yellow River — 河 began as the personal name of one river, the Yellow River, before it widened to mean any river at all.
后	hòu	back, behind, after; empress — One simplified character collapsed two completely separate traditional forms: 後 (a trailing foot in motion) and 后 (a commanding mouth), binding the spatial sense of behind with the imperial sense of empress in a single glyph.
换	huàn	to change, to exchange, to swap, to trade — A hand verb: every kind of swap, from changing money to changing trains, runs through one moving palm.
回	huí	to return, to turn back, a time — Two squares nested like a coiling eddy. The character for coming back: returning home, turning around, replying, and counting the times a thing happens.
活	huó	to live, to be alive, lively, work — Water gives the verb its life: a stream over a tongue, the flow that means being alive, and from there everything that moves, breathes, and gets done.
接	jiē	to connect, to join, to receive, to meet — The hand that joins: 接 puts a wrist behind every link, every catch, and every arrival you go out to greet.
街	jiē	street, avenue, thoroughfare — The walk radical 行 splits open into a crossroads, and the jade-tablet phonetic 圭 sits inside, marking the place where roads cross and people pass.
近	jìn	near; close; recent — Walking toward something as close as a hatchet strike, and the character that covers both spatial proximity and the specific historical era Chinese scholars call the modern period.
进	jìn	to enter, to advance, to take in — A foot on the road and a bird that only ever walks forward. From that image grows everything that moves ahead and moves in: to enter, to advance, to progress, to carry out, and to take goods in.
开	kāi	to open, to start, to operate — Two hands lifting the bar across a closed gate. From that single image flow most of modern life: opening a door, starting a meeting, driving a car, writing a check, blooming.
口	kǒu	mouth, opening, entrance — A simple rectangle for an open mouth: one of the most productive radicals in the script, present in hundreds of characters for speaking, eating, and openings.
跨	kuà	to stride; to span; to bestride — The character of the long stride: a foot-radical verb that reaches from "step over a threshold" to "span an era" to the modern policy vocabulary of cross-border, cross-industry, cross-cultural everything.
来	lái	to come, to arrive, toward the speaker — Oracle bone wheat plant turned direction-word: 来 pulls everything toward the speaker, including time itself.
离	lí	to leave, to be apart, away from — Once the name of a netted mythical bird, 离 was borrowed so long ago for to part that almost no speaker recalls its feathers.
里	lǐ	inside, within; Chinese mile — From field stacked on earth, 里 named the bounded cultivated interior of a settlement and became the unit of distance that measured the realm's interior reach.
路	lù	road, path, route — The walked road, where 道 is the navigated way: 路 is the surface beneath the foot, the route the messenger takes, the journey of a life measured in distance.
南	nán	south; southern — The direction that Chinese buildings face and emperors receive guests: warmth, productivity, and the seat of imperial ritual power.
跑	pǎo	to run; to flee; to operate — The everyday verb of running: a foot-radical character whose semantic range stretches from "the legs in motion" to "running an errand" to "the engine is running" to a small but productive family of escape-and-flee compounds.
票	piào	bank note, ticket, vote, a slip of paper — Flames rising over an altar where spirit money burned: 票 carries the smoke of an offering into the modern banknote, the ticket stub, and the ballot.
前	qián	front, before, forward; former — Chinese places the past in front (前) and the future behind: the known faces you while the unknown approaches from behind, the inverse of the English spatial metaphor for time.
去	qù	to go, to leave, to depart, to remove — The character that marks every departure, from leaving the house to the euphemism for death. 去 is the verb of things moving away.
山	shān	mountain, hill, sacred peak — Three peaks rising from the earth: the oldest pictograph and the axis of Chinese sacred geography.
上	shàng	above, up, to go up, to mount — The most productive spatial primitive in Chinese. 上 starts as a notch above a line and ends up meaning everything from logging on to getting addicted.
送	sòng	to see off, to send, to give — A figure waving 关 at someone whose feet 辶 are already carrying them away: 送 is farewell built into the road itself.
通	tōng	to go through; to connect; general; fluent — The walk-movement radical drives a path through every obstacle, giving 通 a single character that covers passage, communication, expertise, and the ordinary general state of things.
外	wài	outside, outer, foreign, besides — A diviner reading the shells at the wrong hour, off the normal schedule. From that image grows everything beyond the boundary: the outside, the outer surface, the foreign land, and the relatives on the mother's side.
西	xī	west; western — A bird settling into its nest at sunset: the direction that gave Chinese the word for "things" when it paired with east.
下	xià	below, down, to descend, to issue — The mirror image of 上: a notch below the line that governs everything from going downstairs to issuing imperial decrees.
向	xiàng	direction, toward, to face, all along — A window opening in a wall, facing one way. From that image grows the whole field of orientation: the direction a thing points, the preposition toward a target, the inclination of a heart, and the sense of all along, up to now.
行	xíng	to walk, to act, a row, a trade — One of the great polyphonic characters: xíng "to walk; to act; conduct" and háng "row; profession; firm." A pictograph of a four-way crossroads, the place where walking and the world are organized.
右	yòu	right; to assist; to favor — A right hand raised to a mouth: the hand that feeds and speaks, whose authority in Chinese extended to favoring, protecting, and placing the highest things to the right of the seat.
远	yuǎn	far, distant, remote — Long robes trailing as you walk away: distance encoded in motion, and the ambivalence of far-off places in Chinese thought.
越	yu è	to cross over, to surpass — The character of crossing-and-exceeding: a foot-radical verb that travels from "stride over a barrier" to "the more, the more" to a Chinese state name and a national poetic gesture.
站	zhàn	to stand, to halt, a station, a stop — A person planted upright on the ground, holding their place: from the body that will not move grows the station, the stop, and the website you log on to.
走	zǒu	to walk, to go, to leave — A figure in motion with feet below: the basic verb of movement that Chinese uses where English uses dozens of different words.
足	zú	foot; sufficient; enough — The foot that became the word for sufficiency, and the radical anchoring every character involving legs, running, and roads.
左	zuǒ	left; unorthodox; to assist — A left hand holding a carpenter's square: the assisting hand at the ruler, and by extension the word for unorthodox, heterodox, and everything that deviates from the orthodox path.
茶道	chá dào	the way of tea — From wild forest leaf to civilizational ritual: how tea became China's quintessential art of presence.
道德	dào dé	morality, ethics, virtue — The compound at the center of Chinese moral philosophy: the Way and its virtue, inseparable and mutually defining.
东西	dōng xi	things — East-west as the word for 'stuff': a window into directional cosmology.
方向	fāng xiàng	direction; orientation — 方向 is the compass-word that holds together physical heading, life direction, political orientation, and the deeply Chinese sense that getting your bearings: 辨方向, is the first move in any undertaking.
江山	jiāng shān	rivers and mountains, the realm — The classical Chinese word for the nation: not a territory but a landscape; not a state but a living geography that dynasties rose and fell to possess.
跨越	kuà yu è	to leap across, to span, to transcend — The compound that lets the Chinese language span gaps: physical, historical, technological, generational. The verb of choice when "越长城" is read figuratively.
然后	rán hòu	then; after that — The everyday sequence word for "then, after that," the 先…然后… frame for ordering steps, its overuse as a spoken filler, and how it differs from 接着, 后来, and 于是.
山水	shān shuǐ	mountains and water; the landscape — The paired word that names the Chinese landscape: not "scenery" in the Western sense, but a cosmological pairing of the still and the flowing that became the central subject of a thousand years of painting and poetry.
山水画	shān shuǐ huà	Chinese Landscape Painting — Mountains and waters as a cosmological pair -- the genre that made ink the equal of philosophy.
丝绸之路	sī chóu zhī lù	the Silk Road — The term Silk Road was coined by a German geographer in 1877: the Chinese themselves had no single name for the trade network that defined their contact with the world for a millennium.
天下	tiān xià	all under heaven — The pre-nation-state Chinese political imagination: the civilized world.
长城	Cháng chéng	the Great Wall — A series of fortification systems built across multiple dynasties, whose most visible sections are Ming-era brick: not the continuous ancient rampart of popular imagination.
知道	zhī dào	to know — The everyday verb for knowing a fact, and how it differs from 认识 (to be acquainted with) and 了解 (to understand deeply).
中国	Zhōng guó	the Middle Kingdom; China — Two characters older than the country they now name. 中 (center) and 国 (state) joined three thousand years ago to mean "the central states"; the modern country took the phrase for itself only in the 19th century.
安步当车	ān bù dàng chē	to walk calmly in place of riding: to be content with a simple, unhurried life — Lit: walk at ease, treat it as a carriage. Here 当 takes the dàng reading, "to treat as." From a Warring-States anecdote: to stroll on foot and reckon it as good as riding, content without luxury.
八仙过海，各显神通	bā xiān guò hǎi , gè xiǎn shén tōng	"the Eight Immortals cross the sea, each showing their powers" — everyone uses their own talents — The Eight Immortals, crossing the sea, each rely on a different magical skill rather than a single method. The saying describes a group facing a shared task in which every member contributes through their own distinct ability, and is used to praise resourceful, varied teamwork.
把握时机	bǎ wò shí jī	to seize the moment; to grasp the opportunity at the right time — 把握 bǎwò (grip; certainty; grasp) + 时机 shíjī (the right moment). A common exhortation in business, speech, and motivational contexts. Also used as a noun: 有把握 yǒu bǎwò = "to be confident, certain" (lit. to have a grip on it). 没有把握 = "not sure, not confident."
半部论语治天下	bàn bù Lún yǔ zhì tiān xià	half the Analects suffices to govern all under heaven — the centrality of the classics in governance — Attributed to the Song statesman 赵普 Zhào Pǔ, who supposedly told an emperor that he governed with only half his knowledge of the Analects and had the other half in reserve.
绑票勒索	bǎng piào lè suǒ	to kidnap and extort: to seize a hostage and demand ransom — 绑 bǎng = to tie up; to bind. 票 piào = here, a kidnapped person held for ransom (the slang 肉票 ròupiào, a human "ticket" to be cashed in). 勒索 lèsuǒ = to extort; to blackmail. A four-character description, not a classical chéngyǔ, but a fixed legal and news expression for kidnapping for ransom.
悲欢离合	bēi huān lí hé	sorrows and joys, partings and reunions — Literally "grief, joy, parting, union", the full arc of human fortune in four characters. 离 (parting) and 合 (reunion) form the pair at its heart, the comings and goings of a life. From a famous Song lyric by Su Shi on the moon and human separation.
北风怒号	běi fēng nù háo	"the north wind howls in fury" — biting cold; adversity — 北风 (north wind) + 怒 (furious) + 号 (to howl). A classical weather image used in poetry and prose to evoke bitter cold and desolation. Du Fu's famous poem 茅屋为秋风所破歌 uses the howling north wind as a metaphor for the hardships of his refugee years during the An Lushan Rebellion (755–763 CE).
本来面目	běn lái miàn mù	original face: a thing's true, unaltered nature — Lit: original-face-and-features. A Chan Buddhist phrase for one's true self before all conditioning, now used broadly for the real character of anything once the disguises are stripped away.
逼上梁山	bī shàng liáng shān	forced up Mount Liang — driven to a desperate course — From the heroes' repeated path of being pushed by injustice until they have no refuge but the outlaw stronghold of Liangshan. The chengyu now means being driven by circumstance to take a drastic step one would never have chosen freely, the very pattern by which the novel's decent men become rebels.
笔走龙蛇	bǐ zǒu lóng shé	the brush moves like dragons and snakes: bold, flowing, vigorous calligraphy — Lit: brush-runs-dragon-snake. The line of cursive writing twists and surges with the energy of a dragon and a snake in motion. High praise for a calligrapher whose hand is alive and free.
必由之路	bì yóu zhī lù	the road one must take; the only viable path forward — 必 (must) + 由 yóu (to pass through; to travel by way of) + 之路 (the road). The path through which one must pass, with no alternative route. Used when describing the single viable way forward in a difficult situation: 改革是必由之路 (Reform is the only path forward).
鞭长莫及	biān cháng mò jí	the whip is long but cannot reach — distance defeats even great strength or authority — Lit: whip-long-cannot-reach. From the Zuǒzhuàn (左传), a Spring and Autumn period chronicle. A ruler may have power, but he cannot govern what is too distant from him.
辨方正位	biàn fāng zhèng wèi	"to discern direction and rectify position" — to orient oneself correctly before acting — From the Zhouli (周礼). The phrase belongs to the rituals of city-founding and palace-construction: before laying the foundations, the official must determine the cardinal directions and align the building correctly. By extension, in any major undertaking, one must first establish one's bearings.
表里如一	biǎo lǐ rú yī	outside and inside are as one: consistent in word and deed; the same within as without — 表 biǎo = the outside, the surface. 里 lǐ = the inside, the interior. 如一 rú yī = as if one; identical. A person whose outward conduct matches their inner thoughts, who is honest and undivided, with no gap between the surface they show and the substance underneath. A strong compliment to integrity.
兵临城下	bīng lín chéng xià	troops at the foot of the wall: the enemy at the gates — 兵 bīng = soldiers, troops; 临 lín = to arrive at, to overlook; 城下 = below the city wall. The enemy army has reached the base of the wall, the last moment before a siege becomes an assault.
兵者，诡道也。	bīng zhě , guǐ dào yě .	Warfare is the way of deception. — A blunt rejection of chivalric ideals: success in conflict depends on shaping the enemy's perceptions, concealing one's own strength and intentions, and striking where one is not expected.
不耻下问	bù chǐ xià wèn	"not ashamed to ask those below" — intellectual humility; willingness to learn from anyone regardless of status — From the 论语 (Analects) , Confucius praising the minister Kong Wenzi: 敏而好学，不耻下问，是以谓之'文'也 (Quick-minded and fond of learning, not ashamed to ask those of lower rank , this is why he was given the posthumous title 'Wen').
不到长城非好汉	bú dào cháng chéng fēi hǎo hàn	"he who has not reached the Great Wall is not a true hero" — From Mao Zedong's 1935 poem 清平乐·六盘山. Not technically a 成语 (it is seven characters), but functions as a fixed quotation. Often paired in modern usage with 越长城: to "cross the wall" is to prove oneself. The verb implied is 越 (to cross over) or 到 (to reach).
不胫而走	bù jìng ér zǒu	"to travel without legs" — (of news) to spread rapidly on its own — A classical chengyu (using 走 in its classical "to run" sense, before 跑 took over). 胫 jìng = the lower leg; the image is of news that has no legs but somehow runs everywhere by itself. Used today for rumors, viral information, and news that spreads without effort.
不翼而飞	bù yì ér fēi	to fly off without wings: to vanish suddenly — Used of objects that disappear inexplicably, as if something with no wings had taken to the air. Also of news that spreads everywhere overnight.
沧海桑田	cāng hǎi sāng tiān	the blue sea becomes mulberry fields — dramatic change over time; the world transformed beyond recognition — From a Daoist tale recorded in 神仙传 (Biographies of Immortals, attributed to Ge Hong, 4th century CE): the immortal Magu tells of having seen the eastern sea turn to mulberry fields three times. 沧海 cānghǎi = the dark blue sea (沧 = cold, dark; vast).
朝气蓬勃	zhāo qì péng bó	full of youthful vigor: brimming with life and energy — Lit: morning-vigor, luxuriant-thriving. 朝气 is the spirit of a new day, 蓬勃 the look of growth in full flush. Said of people, teams, or whole societies that are young and full of drive.
朝三暮四	zhāo sān mù sì	three in the morning, four at dusk: fickle, forever changing one's mind — From a fable in which a keeper offers his monkeys three nuts in the morning and four at night; they protest, so he swaps it to four and three, and they are content though the total is the same. Now it names someone fickle, who keeps switching with no real difference.
成千上万	chéng qiān shàng wàn	thousands upon thousands: a vast, countless number — Lit: amounting-to-thousands-up-to-ten-thousands. A vivid way to say a great many, used of crowds, stars, raindrops, anything too numerous to count. Here 成 carries the sense of reaching a quantity.
承前启后	chéng qián qǐ hòu	carry on the past and open the way for the future — To inherit what came before and pass it on, serving as the bridge between earlier and later generations, a 代 to 代 handing-on. Said of a person, work, or era that links what preceded it to what follows, the connective role between ages.
出类拔萃	chū lèi bá cuì	to stand out from the class and rise above the finest — exceptional; head and shoulders above — From Mencius (孟子): 出于其类，拔乎其萃. 类 lèi = category; type; peers. 拔 bá = to pull up; to surpass. 萃 cuì = the finest, the gathered best. To emerge beyond one's category and surpass even the finest specimens within it. Classical Confucian praise for moral and intellectual excellence.
出奇制胜	chū qí zhì shèng	to achieve victory through surprise — to win by doing the unexpected — 出奇 = to deploy the unusual, to emerge with something unexpected. 制胜 = to achieve victory; to control the outcome.
出人意料	chū rén yì liào	"beyond people's expectation" — unexpected; surprising; contrary to what was anticipated — Lit: exceed-people-intention-anticipate. One of the most common ways to say "surprisingly" or "unexpectedly" in formal Chinese. 这个结果出人意料，没有人预测到。"This result was unexpected , no one had predicted it." Also appears in the variant 出乎意料 chū hū yìliào with identical meaning.
穿针引线	chuān zhēn yǐn xiàn	thread the needle and lead the thread: to act as go-between — Literally the seamstress's first move. Figuratively, to broker a connection between two parties, matchmaking a deal, a marriage, a meeting.
船坚炮利	chuán jiān pào lì	sturdy ships and sharp guns: overwhelming naval and military might — A late-Qing phrase for the foreign gunboats whose strength forced the treaty ports open. Now shorthand for raw technological force.
闯南走北	chuǎng nán zǒu běi	"boldly moving through south and north" — widely traveled; experienced in the world — 闯 (to push through boldly) + 南 (south) + 走 (to travel) + 北 (north). A person who has made their way through the whole country, acquiring experience and resilience. The verb 闯 suggests the travel was not comfortable or planned but forced through obstacles.
春暖花开	chūn nuǎn huā kāi	spring is warm and flowers bloom: the good season has come — The stock image of spring at its best. Used literally for the season and figuratively for the right moment, a turn for the better after hardship.
大好河山	dà hǎo hé shān	the great and beautiful rivers and mountains; the magnificent homeland — The near-synonym of 大好江山, using 河山 héshān (Yellow River and mountains) instead of 江山 (Yangtze and mountains). 河 hé specifically refers to the Yellow River , 黄河 Huáng Hé , the cradle of northern Chinese civilization.
大江东去	dà jiāng dōng qù	the great river flows east — time and history flow on; the brilliant figures of the past are gone — From Su Shi's 苏轼 famous ci poem "Niannujiao · Red Cliff Nostalgia" (念奴娇·赤壁怀古): 大江东去，浪淘尽，千古风流人物. "The great river flows east; its waves wash away the brilliant figures of a thousand ages." Invoked for historical grandeur and the irreversible passage of time.
大街小巷	dà jiē xiǎo xiàng	big streets and small lanes: everywhere in town — The standard way to say all over the city, every street and back alley. Neutral register, extremely common in news and speech: 大街小巷都挂满了灯笼, lanterns hung all over the streets.
大开眼界	dà kāi yǎn jiè	to greatly open one's horizons — to have one's eyes opened — Lit: greatly-open-eye-boundary. The standard phrase for the experience of suddenly seeing something new and broadening one's outlook. Said after a trip, a museum visit, a striking piece of work.
大运河	dà yùn hé	the Grand Canal — The Sui's enduring monument: 运 yùn (to transport) + 河 hé (river), a man-made transport river. Built at terrible human cost under Emperor Yang, it linked the rice-rich south to the political north and remained the backbone of Chinese internal trade for over a millennium.
当机立断	dāng jī lì duàn	to decide decisively at the right moment; not to hesitate when the time comes — Lit: facing-the-opportunity-immediately-decide. The complement of 把握时机 , not just grasping the moment but acting on it decisively. 断 here means to cut through, to decide without wavering. The virtuous counterpart to 犹豫不决 (hesitating and unable to decide).
荡气回肠	dàng qì huí cháng	stirring the breath and coiling the gut: deeply moving, soul-stirring — Lit: surging-breath-coiling-innards. Said of music, writing, or a story so moving that it seems to turn one's very insides. 回 keeps its coiling sense here.
道不同不相为谋	dào bù tóng bù xiāng wéi móu	"those who follow different ways cannot work together" — fundamental value divergence ends cooperation — From the Analects: when the fundamental principles (道) that govern a person's life diverge, strategic cooperation becomes impossible. Used when parting ways with a colleague, organization, or ally over principle , not personal dislike but incompatible directions.
道可道，非常道。	dào kě dào , fēi cháng dào .	The Way that can be spoken is not the constant Way. — The opening line of chapter 1, and the most famous sentence in the entire text. It establishes at once that the 道 lies beyond the reach of fixed language: any 道 that can be fully captured in words is already a limited, conditioned thing, not the constant, nameless source.
道听途说	dào tīng tú shuō	"heard on the road, told on the path" — hearsay; rumor; unverified gossip — 途 tú = road; path (in transit). Things overheard on the road, passed on without verification. The road is a place of unfiltered information, strangers, and half-truths. 不要相信道听途说。(Don't believe hearsay.) Used to dismiss rumors or unverified claims.
得道多助	dé dào duō zhù	"the righteous have many helpers" — moral legitimacy generates real-world support — From Mencius (孟子). Full phrase: 得道多助，失道寡助 (Those who have the Way have many helpers; those without the Way have few). Moral legitimacy is not merely symbolic , it creates tangible coalitions. Those who govern well attract support; those who govern badly find themselves isolated.
低声下气	dī shēng xià qì	"lower the voice, lower the breath" — to speak with extreme humility; to be submissive in order to placate someone — 低声 (lower the voice) + 下气 (bring down one's breath/spirit). The physical image is precise: a person who speaks quietly and exhales slowly is physiologically in a submissive, de-escalated state.
滴水穿石	dī shuǐ chuān shí	dripping water bores through stone: persistence wins — The image of a single drip, repeated for years, drilling a hole in rock. The standard idiom for patient, steady effort overcoming a hard obstacle.
顶天立地	dǐng tiān lì dì	head touching heaven, feet on earth — a person of complete integrity and heroic stature — 顶 dǐng = to stand at the top of; to press against from below. 立 lì = to stand; to be upright. The image is of a person so morally complete that they fill the entire vertical axis of the universe , feet firmly planted on earth, head pressing against heaven.
东奔西跑	dōng bēn xī pǎo	"running east and dashing west" — to rush around in every direction — A four-character formula combining 奔 and 跑 across the directional pair 东 dōng / 西 xī. Used for someone constantly on the move, often in service of obligations or in pursuit of a livelihood.
东奔西走	dōng bēn xī zǒu	"rushing east, running west" — frantically busy everywhere; constantly in motion — Lit: east-rush-west-walk. Describes someone perpetually in motion, always somewhere else, never settled. Often implies exhausting, stressful busyness with little to show for it. 他为了这件事东奔西走了好几年。"He spent years running all over the place trying to make this happen."
东拼西凑	dōng pīn xī còu	"pieced together from east and west" — cobbled together from miscellaneous sources — 东拼 (stitched from the east) + 西凑 (gathered from the west). Something assembled by scraping together bits and pieces from wherever they could be found. Used for a speech that draws from random sources, a meal made from leftovers, or a budget patched together with emergency measures.
东山再起	dōng shān zài qǐ	"rise again from the Eastern Mountain" — to make a comeback after defeat — From the biography of Xiè Ān (謝安, 320–385 CE), a Jin dynasty statesman who retired to 东山 (Eastern Mountain, in present-day Zhejiang) and refused office for years. When the court fell into crisis, he was recalled and restored Jin to stability.
东施效颦	Dōng Shī xiào pín	to imitate blindly without understanding, and make things worse — A homely woman copies Xi Shi's famous frown and clears the street: Zhuangzi's parable about form without essence.
东张西望	dōng zhāng xī wàng	"looking east and west" — glancing around nervously or curiously; gawking — Lit: east-look-west-gaze. Used for someone whose eyes dart around, whether out of nervousness, suspicion, or simple distraction. The four-character structure vividly enacts the scanning motion itself. 他在街上东张西望，好像在找人。"He was looking all around the street as if searching for someone."
动手动脚	dòng shǒu dòng jiǎo	to move hands and feet: to get physical, to grope or rough up — Lit: move-hand-move-foot. To put one's hands on someone, either to start a fight or to touch improperly. The doubled 动 spreads the action across hands and feet alike.
读万卷书，行万里路	dú wàn juàn shū , xíng wàn lǐ lù	read ten thousand scrolls of books, walk ten thousand li of road — learn from text and from travel both — A Ming-dynasty saying attributed to the painter and theorist Dong Qichang. The pairing of 读 and 行 as the two halves of an educated life: book-knowledge and lived experience as equally necessary, neither sufficient alone.
对症下药	duì zhèng xià yào	prescribe according to the symptoms; tailor the remedy to the problem — 对 (match) + 症 (symptoms) + 下药 (administer medicine). From the case lore of the Han physician Hua Tuo, who gave two patients with identical headaches different prescriptions because the causes differed.
翻江倒海	fān jiāng dǎo hǎi	to overturn rivers and upend seas: overwhelming, world-shaking force — Lit: overturn-river topple-sea. Said of a power great enough to churn the waters of the world, and by extension of turbulent emotion or upheaval: 心里翻江倒海 "the heart in utter turmoil."
翻山越岭	fān shān yu è lǐng	"to cross mountain ridge after mountain ridge" — to traverse difficult terrain, by extension, to overcome great obstacles — A four-character compound combining 翻越 (to climb over) split across 山 (mountain) and 岭 (ridge). Used both literally (an arduous mountain journey) and figuratively (overcoming a series of difficulties to reach a goal). Frequent in war-history and biographical writing. 他翻山越岭，终于来到了家乡。
放下屠刀，立地成佛	fàng xià tú dāo , lì dì chéng fó	lay down the butcher's knife and become a Buddha on the spot — radical redemption is always possible — The full form of 立地成佛. Rooted in the Chan teaching that Buddha nature is present in all beings, even the most hardened. Used both seriously (in discussions of rehabilitation, forgiveness, religious conversion) and ironically (with skepticism about a villain's sudden change of heart). 只要放下屠刀，立地成佛.
放眼天下	fàng yǎn tiān xià	cast one's eyes across all-under-heaven — to think broadly; to have a global perspective — 放眼 fàng yǎn (to let one's gaze range freely; to look far) + 天下. To look beyond one's immediate circumstances, neighborhood, or nation and think on a civilizational or global scale. The opposite of 鼠目寸光 (shǔ mù cùn guāng , mouse-eyes seeing only an inch; short-sightedness). 要放眼天下，不能只看眼前。
飞黄腾达	fēi huáng téng dá	Feihuang gallops upward: to rise rapidly in rank — 飞黄 (Fēihuáng) is a legendary divine steed; the phrase pictures a career rising as if riding it skyward. The standard idiom for a meteoric rise in office or fortune.
否极泰来	pǐ jí tài lái	after extreme obstruction (yin) comes peace (yang) — when things hit rock bottom, improvement follows — From the Yijing hexagrams 否 pǐ (stagnation; obstruction , a yin hexagram) and 泰 tài (peace; harmony , a yang hexagram). The cosmological law: forces cycle, yin cannot dominate forever, the turn of yang is inevitable. A comforting phrase in hard times: 否极泰来，好日子还在后面。
改朝换代	gǎi cháo huàn dài	to change dynasty and era: a change of regime — Lit: change-dynasty, swap-era. 朝 and 代 are the dynasty and its age. The phrase names the fall of one ruling house and the rise of another, and by extension any sweeping change of who holds power.
改头换面	gǎi tóu huàn miàn	change the head, swap the face: a superficial makeover — Originally Buddhist, on the soul taking a new body. Now usually pejorative: the form is redone but the substance is unchanged, like a rebranded scam.
改弦易辙	gǎi xián yì zhé	"change the bowstring, switch the wheel-rut" — to change direction; to take a new approach — 改 (change) + 弦 (xián, bowstring) + 易 (change) + 辙 (zhé, wheel-rut). The two halves are parallel images of changing tools and changing track. Used for any decisive shift in policy, method, or life-direction.
高山流水	gāo shān liú shuǐ	high mountains, flowing water — finding someone who truly hears you — The piece Boya played when Ziqi first identified the mountains and rivers in his music. As a chengyu, 高山流水 has two uses: the meeting of a 知音 (someone who understands you perfectly), and more broadly, music or artistry of the very highest order.
高瞻远瞩	gāo zhān yuǎn zhǔ	"look high and see far" — farsighted vision; strategic perspective — 高 (high) + 瞻 (to look up, to look ahead) + 远 (far) + 瞩 (to gaze at closely). Looking from an elevated position and therefore seeing farther: the physical metaphor for strategic or visionary thinking. Used to praise leaders, planners, and thinkers who see beyond immediate circumstances.
古往今来	gǔ wǎng jīn lái	from ancient times to the present — Literally "the ancient goes, the present comes," spanning all of history up to now. Used to introduce a truth that has held across the ages: 古往今来，人人都怕死 ("from ancient times to now, everyone fears death"). Here 今 is the classical single character for "the present, now."
过河拆桥	guò hé chāi qiáo	cross the river, then tear down the bridge — A vivid picture of ingratitude: discarding the very means or person that got you across once you are safely over. A sharp accusation in modern speech.
海不扬波	hǎi bù yáng bō	the sea does not surge — peaceful passage; a stable, unchallenged realm — Lit: sea-not-raise-waves. A classical phrase for the ideal of peaceful sea passage , exactly what Mazu provides. Used in Mazu temple inscriptions and ritual prayers.
海枯石烂	hǎi kū shí làn	till the seas run dry and the rocks crumble: forever, unchanging — Lit: sea-dry stone-rot. A vow of eternal constancy, used in oaths of love and loyalty: even when the impossible happens and the ocean dries up, the feeling will not change.
海阔天空	hǎi kuò tiān kōng	the sea is wide and the sky is open: boundless; (of talk) freewheeling and unconstrained — Lit: sea-wide sky-empty. It paints limitless space, hence both a feeling of total freedom and a way to describe rambling, anything-goes conversation: 海阔天空地聊 "to chat about everything under the sun."
海量	hǎi liàng	sea-sized capacity: a vast tolerance or a huge amount — 海 hǎi (sea) + 量 liàng (capacity). A capacity as large as the sea. Traditionally a generous compliment for someone of great tolerance, whether a magnanimous temperament (大人有海量, your honor is broad-minded) or a formidable drinking capacity at the table.
好汉不吃眼前亏	hǎo hàn bù chī yǎn qián kuī	a wise fellow avoids a loss he can plainly see coming — Lit: a good-fellow does not eat the loss before his eyes. Folk wisdom that real courage includes the sense to step back from a fight you cannot win, rather than charging in for pride.
喝西北风	hē xī běi fēng	to drink the northwest wind: have nothing to eat, go hungry — A vivid colloquial idiom: with no food, all you can swallow is the cold wind. Said wryly of poverty or a failed livelihood, "我们都去喝西北风了".
何去何从	hé qù hé cóng	which way to turn; what course to take — Literally "where to go, what to follow", the weighing of a hard choice between paths. A more literary cousin of 怎么办, asking not just "what to do" but "which direction to commit to". Here 何 is the classical "how / what" that 怎么 replaced in everyday speech.
河东狮吼	hé dōng shī hǒu	the lioness of Hedong roars: a fierce, jealous wife — From a Song poem by Su Shi teasing his friend Chen Jichang (Chen Zao), whose wife's temper made him quail. 河东 (Hedong) names her clan's home east of the Yellow River. Still used, half teasing, for a domineering spouse.
河清海晏	hé qīng hǎi yàn	the river runs clear and the sea is calm: peace under good rule — Since the Yellow River is famously muddy, its running clear is a near-impossible omen of a sage age. Paired with a calm sea, it stands for an era of order and prosperity. Formal, literary register.
后顾之忧	hòu gù zhī yōu	"the worry of looking back" — concern for what you leave behind; anxiety about what is happening at the rear — 后 (behind; the rear) + 顾 (to look back; to attend to) + 之 (classical possessive particle) + 忧 (worry; anxiety).
后来居上	hòu lái jū shàng	"the latecomer occupies the top" — the newcomer surpasses those who came first — 后来 (later arrival) + 居上 (occupies the upper position). From the Shǐjì (史记), the court historian Sima Qian. A newly promoted official sitting above older ministers in court: the latecomer had outranked his seniors.
后生可畏	hòu shēng kě wèi	"the young are to be feared" — the next generation will surpass those before them — From the Analects of Confucius (论语·子罕): 后生可畏，焉知来者之不如今也？(The young are to be feared , how do we know the ones still to come will not surpass those of today?) 后生 = those born later, the younger generation. 可畏 = worthy of awe, of apprehension.
湖光山色	hú guāng shān sè	lake light and mountain hues: beautiful lake-and-mountain scenery — A set phrase praising scenic landscape, the stock compliment paid to West Lake and its kin.
花街柳巷	huā jiē liǔ xiàng	flower streets and willow lanes: the pleasure quarters — A classical euphemism for the red-light district, where 花 (flower) and 柳 (willow) stand for courtesans. Literary and old-fashioned; appears in Ming and Qing fiction.
花开见佛	huā kāi jiàn fó	when the flower blooms, the Buddha is seen — sudden illumination after patient waiting — Originally a Pure Land image (the lotus flower opening in the Pure Land to reveal the practitioner reborn there, seeing Amitābha), adopted by Chan as a metaphor for sudden enlightenment: the moment when all the conditions are right and understanding blooms.
话不投机	huà bù tóu jī	the talk does not meet: the conversation does not click — Lit: words-not-matching-the-moment. Said when two people simply cannot find common ground in conversation. The fuller saying 话不投机半句多 adds that "when talk does not click, even half a sentence is too much."
话里有话	huà lǐ yǒu huà	there are words within the words: a remark carries a hidden meaning — Lit: inside-the-words-there-are-words. Said when someone speaks with an implied second meaning, hinting at more than the surface remark admits.
回头是岸	huí tóu shì àn	turn your head and there is the shore: it is never too late to repent — Lit: turn-the-head-is-shore. From the Buddhist 苦海无边，回头是岸 ("the sea of suffering is boundless; turn back and the shore is there"). To stop a wrong course and come back is itself salvation.
回心转意	huí xīn zhuǎn yì	to turn the heart and shift the will: to change one's mind, to relent — Lit: turn-heart-shift-intent. Said of someone who softens, reconsiders, or comes back to a relationship or decision they had set aside.
活蹦乱跳	huó bèng luàn tiào	alive and bouncing about: brimming with health and energy — Lit: alive-leaping-wildly-jumping. The image of a fish on the deck or a healthy child who cannot keep still. Used for someone bursting with vitality, often after recovering from illness.
活到老，学到老	huó dào lǎo , xu é dào lǎo	live until old, learn until old — lifelong learning — The most common proverb for lifelong learning: you learn for as long as you live. An encouragement to keep studying at any age, and a gentle reminder that no one has ever learned enough to stop. Equivalent in spirit to the English "you're never too old to learn."
活灵活现	huó líng huó xiàn	vivid and lifelike: so vivid it seems to come alive before you — Lit: alive-spirit-alive-appearing, with 活 doubled for emphasis. Describes a story, performance, or portrait so vivid and animated that the thing depicted seems present in the room. High praise for vividness.
火上加油	huǒ shàng jiā yóu	to add oil to the fire — to make a bad situation worse — Lit: fire-above-add-oil. The Chinese equivalent of "adding fuel to the fire." 你别火上加油了 "Don't make things worse." The equivalent English idiom "add fuel to the fire" has exactly the same logic.
鸡蛋里挑骨头	jī dàn lǐ tiāo gǔ tou	to pick bones out of an egg: to nitpick, to find fault where there is none — A vivid colloquial saying: an egg has no bones, so hunting for one means inventing flaws. Used of a critic or boss who complains about everything.
兼爱天下	jiān ài tiān xià	love all under Heaven impartially — the Mohist manifesto in four characters — 兼 jiān (jointly; all together) + 爱 ài (love) + 天下 tiānxià (all under Heaven; the world). The compressed statement of Mozi's central ethical position: love extended equally to every person without gradation by family, state, or social proximity.
江山如画	jiāng shān rú huà	rivers and mountains like a painting; the land is breathtakingly beautiful — From Su Shi's 念奴娇·赤壁怀古 (Remembering Chibi, to the tune of Nian Nu Jiao), written in 1082 at the site of the famous Three Kingdoms battle. 江山如画，一时多少豪杰 (the rivers and mountains like a painting , how many heroes in that one moment).
江山社稷	jiāng shān shè jì	the rivers, mountains, and altars of soil and grain: the whole realm, the state — Literally "rivers and mountains, the altars of soil and grain." 江山 the land and 社稷 the altars together name the entire country a ruler holds in trust. A grand, classical phrase for the state as a whole, the territory and the legitimacy that come with it.
江山易改，本性难移	jiāng shān yì gǎi , běn xìng nán yí	easy to change the landscape, hard to change one's nature; a leopard can't change its spots — The full proverb. 易 yì (easy) + 改 gǎi (to change; to alter) / 难 nán (difficult) + 移 yí (to shift; to move; to transplant). The contrast is between the external world , rivers and mountains, the landscape of history , and the internal: 本性, innate character, the temperament you were born with.
江山易改本性难移	jiāng shān yì gǎi běn xìng nán yí	territory is easy to change; fundamental nature is hard to move — a leopard cannot change its spots — Classic proverb about human nature used in resignation or rueful acknowledgment. 他就是这样的人，江山易改本性难移。(He's just that kind of person; a leopard can't change its spots.) Often invoked by someone who has tried to change another person and given up.
交头接耳	jiāo tóu jiē ěr	heads together, ears joined: to whisper privately — Two people lean in until one mouth nearly touches the other's ear. The image of leaning close to murmur in secret, often disapproving when done during a meeting or class.
脚踏实地	jiǎo tà shí dì	feet planted on solid ground: down-to-earth, steady and practical — Lit: feet-tread-solid-ground. High praise for someone who works steadily and realistically rather than chasing empty schemes. The 实 here is the solid earth underfoot.
接二连三	jiē èr lián sān	one after another: coming in quick succession — Literally "joining the second, linking the third," counting events as they pile up. Describes things happening in rapid, repeated succession, most often a run of incidents or problems: 接二连三地出事 (one mishap after another).
接连不断	jiē lián bù duàn	linked and unbroken: continuous, without interruption — 接连 doubles the sense of joining; 不断 says the chain never breaks. Describes rain, applause, or news that keeps coming.
街谈巷议	jiē tán xiàng yì	street talk and lane discussion: common gossip, popular chatter — The talk of the town, idle public discussion of an affair. Carries a faintly dismissive tone, the chatter of ordinary streets as opposed to considered opinion.
街头巷尾	jiē tóu xiàng wěi	street corners and lane ends: all about town, on everyone's lips — Names the public places where people gather and gossip. Often used of news or rumor that has spread everywhere: 这件事在街头巷尾传开了, the story spread to every corner of town.
节节上升	jié jié shàng shēng	"rising section by section" — steady, continuous increase — 节 jié = section; segment; node. Each node advances the rise. Used for prices, temperatures, performance metrics, or any quantity that climbs steadily over time. Common in economic and news reporting.
节外生枝	jié wài shēng zhī	a branch sprouts from the joint: new complications arise unexpectedly — Lit: joint-outside-grow-branch. A side branch springs from the node of a stalk where none was wanted. Said when an already settled matter throws up fresh, unwelcome problems.
借风使船	jiè fēng shǐ chuán	borrow the wind to sail the boat: turn outside conditions to your own ends — A close cousin of 顺水推船, stressing the borrowed force: you let someone else's momentum carry your own plan forward.
锦上添花	jǐn shàng tiān huā	"adding flowers on top of brocade" — making something excellent even better — Brocade (锦 jǐn) is already the finest silk, already decorated with woven patterns. Adding flowers on top of it is a gift that was not needed but is delightful. Contrasted with 雪中送炭 (sending coal in the snow), helping someone in genuine need. 锦上添花 is the luxury gesture; 雪中送炭 is the necessary one.
近朱者赤，近墨者黑	jìn zhū zhě chì , jìn mò zhě hēi	those near red become red; those near ink become black — environment shapes character — Two parallel 者 constructions: 近朱者 = the one(s) near vermilion / 近墨者 = the one(s) near ink. The argument is that proximity to good or bad influences changes a person's nature, just as a white object held near ink absorbs its color.
进退两难	jìn tuì liǎng nán	hard to advance and hard to retreat: caught in a dilemma — Lit: advance-retreat-both-hard. To be stuck where neither going forward nor falling back will do, the classic no-win bind. The 进 / 退 pairing makes the trap vivid.
惊天动地	jīng tiān dòng dì	"startling heaven, shaking earth" — earth-shaking; of tremendous power or significance — 惊 jīng (to startle; to alarm) + 动 dòng (to move; to shake). When something is so significant that heaven is startled and earth trembles, the hyperbole names genuine magnitude. Used for historic events, monumental achievements, and powerful works. 他们完成了一项惊天动地的壮举。
九霄云外	jiǔ xiāo yún wài	"beyond the ninth heaven and the clouds" — utterly out of mind; flung far away — 九霄 (the ninefold heaven, the top of the sky) plus 云外 (beyond the clouds). To cast something to the ninth heaven and past the clouds is to banish it completely from thought: 把承诺忘到九霄云外 (to forget a promise entirely).
旧去新来	jiù qù xīn lái	"the old departs, the new arrives" — natural renewal and change — 旧 jiù (old; worn; the previous) + 去 qù (departs) + 新 xīn (new) + 来 lái (arrives). The cycle of replacement: what has aged goes away, what is fresh comes in. Used for seasons (winter's cold 去, spring 来), generations, technologies, fashions.
开启民智	kāi qǐ mín zhì	to enlighten the people; to awaken popular wisdom and intelligence — 开启 kāiqǐ (to open; to initiate; to unlock) + 民 mín (the people; the populace) + 智 zhì (wisdom; intelligence; discernment).
开天辟地	kāi tiān pì dì	"to split open heaven and cleave earth" — to create the world; a groundbreaking, unprecedented achievement — From the Pangu myth (盘古 Pángǔ). In the beginning, all was 混沌 hùndùn: undifferentiated chaos. Pangu broke it open. The light, clear elements rose and became Heaven; the heavy, turbid elements sank and became Earth. Pangu stood between them for 18,000 years until the separation was permanent.
开元盛世	Kāi yuán Shèng shì	the Glorious Era of Kaiyuan — the High Tang at its zenith — The reign-era name 开元 (713–741 CE) under Emperor Xuanzong gave its name to the period of Tang's greatest cultural and economic flourishing. Population reached approximately 50 million; the examination system produced its greatest talents; Chang'an was at its most cosmopolitan.
开源节流	kāi yuán jié liú	open up the source and restrain the flow: increase income and curb spending — Lit: open-source-restrain-flow. The classic principle of sound finance: widen what comes in while narrowing what goes out. Applied to household budgets, companies, and the state alike.
空前绝后	kōng qián ju é hòu	"empty before, cut off behind" — unprecedented and unrepeated; without equal in all of history — 空 kōng (empty; void) + 前 (before; the past) + 绝 jué (cut off; without) + 后 (after; the future). Nothing like it has existed in the past (the front is empty of precedent) and nothing like it will exist in the future (the back is cut off from repetition).
口是心非	kǒu shì xīn fēi	the mouth says yes but the heart says no — saying one thing while thinking another — Lit: mouth-yes-heart-no. The classic expression for hypocrisy or false agreement: when outward words and inner feelings are opposites. Used for people who agree to your face while disagreeing inside, or who say what they think you want to hear rather than what they actually believe.
苦尽甘来	kǔ jìn gān lái	bitterness ends, sweetness comes: hardship gives way to better days — 苦 (bitter) and 甘 (sweet) frame a full turn of fortune. The standard consolation offered to anyone enduring a long hard stretch; the reward is built into the saying.
苦口婆心	kǔ kǒu pó xīn	bitter mouth, grandmotherly heart: to urge earnestly and patiently — Describes someone who repeats hard advice tirelessly out of genuine care, the way an old woman nags her grandchild for their own good.
跨海越洋	kuà hǎi yu è yáng	"to span seas and cross oceans" — to travel vast distances — A modern four-character compound that pairs 跨 and 越 in parallel slots: exactly the kind of construction Chinese loves. Used for international trade, transcontinental travel, or any long voyage. Not classical, but stylistically formal enough to appear in policy speeches and ceremonial writing.
跨马提刀	kuà mǎ tí dāo	"to mount one's horse and lift one's blade" — to ride out armed and ready — A four-character battle-formula common in classical novels and modern wuxia. The image is a general or knight-errant hoisting himself astride his horse with weapon drawn: the moment of departure for combat.
跨年度	kuà nián dù	"crossing fiscal years" — spanning more than one annual cycle — Bureaucratic and accounting vocabulary, not classical, but ubiquitous in modern policy and business documents. 跨年度项目 (a project spanning fiscal years), 跨年度预算 (multi-year budget). Useful as a tag of how 跨 has migrated from the body's stride into the calendar's accounting.
来得及	lái de jí	to have enough time; to make it in time — A potential complement construction: 来 (come/arrive) + 得 + 及 (reach in time). The negative 来不及 is one of the highest-frequency colloquial expressions. 来不及了！ "There's no time!" Both forms exemplify the potential complement use of 得/不.
来历不明	lái lì bù míng	of unknown origin: provenance unclear, background not established — 来历 láilì = origin, background, where something or someone came from (历 here is the path-walked-through that produced the present state); 不明 bù míng = not clear. Said of a person whose background cannot be verified, or an object whose provenance is suspect. It carries a note of suspicion.
来去自如	lái qù zì rú	"coming and going as one pleases" — complete freedom of movement; unhindered — 来去 (coming and going, the full freedom of movement) + 自如 (naturally; freely; as one wishes). The ideal of total freedom: moving in any direction without restriction or obstacle.
来日方长	lái rì fāng cháng	the days to come are still long — there is still much time ahead — 来日 = the coming days, the future (days that come toward us). 方长 = just beginning to be long. A comfort phrase: things not accomplished now can still be done; relationships not deepened yet have time. 我们来日方长，不必着急 "We still have plenty of time ahead , no need to rush."
来者不拒	lái zhě bù jù	turn no one away; welcome all comers; refuse nothing that comes — 来者 = those who come + 不拒 = do not refuse. Originally a statement of generous hospitality or all-accepting policy. Used in commercial contexts (a shop that accepts all customers), in descriptions of a person with an open-door policy, and ironically for someone who takes on too much work.
离经叛道	lí jīng pàn dào	to depart from the classics and rebel against the Way: heterodox, unorthodox — From the Confucian frame: to stray from the canonical texts (经) and turn against orthodox doctrine (道). Modern usage is broad, describing any rebel who breaks with convention, sometimes admiringly.
离题万里	lí tí wàn lǐ	to stray ten thousand li from the topic: to wander hopelessly off subject — Lit: leave-topic-ten-thousand-li. The 万里 (ten thousand li) is hyperbole for an enormous distance, so the phrase describes talk or writing that has drifted so far it has lost the thread entirely.
离乡背井	lí xiāng bèi jǐng	to leave one's native land — Literally "to leave the village and turn one's back on the well", to go far from home, often driven by hardship. The 井 (well) stands for the home community that gathered around shared water. Here 离 is the verb of parting at its most poignant, leaving the place one belongs to.
礼尚往来	lǐ shàng wǎng lái	rites value reciprocity — one courtesy deserves another; give and take — From the 礼记 Lǐjì (Record of Rites): 礼尚往来，往而不来，非礼也；来而不往，亦非礼也. "礼 values mutual exchange; to offer without receiving back is not 礼; to receive without offering back is also not 礼." The principle of reciprocity as the governing logic of social life.
礼贤下士	lǐ xián xià shì	"to honor the worthy and condescend to the lower-ranked" — a leader's courteous treatment of those beneath them — Originally describing rulers and high officials who treated talented commoners and lower-ranked scholars with genuine respect , a quality praised throughout the dynastic histories. 礼 is verbal here ("to treat with 礼"); 贤 xián = the worthy; 下士 = lower-ranked scholars.
里应外合	lǐ yīng wài hé	"inside responds, outside coordinates" — inside-outside cooperation; a coordinated attack from within and without — 里 (inside) + 应 (yīng, to respond, to correspond) + 外 (outside) + 合 (to coordinate, to join). A military and strategic idiom: the force inside a city or position responds to the force attacking from outside, each coordinating with the other.
良药苦口	liáng yào kǔ kǒu	good medicine is bitter to the mouth: honest advice is hard to hear — From the Kǒngzǐ Jiāyǔ. Often completed by 忠言逆耳 (loyal words grate on the ear). Used to soften unwelcome but useful criticism.
邻里之间	lín lǐ zhī jiān	"between neighbors and the neighborhood" — community bonds; relations among people living nearby — 邻 (lín, neighbor) + 里 (the neighborhood unit) + 之间 (between). Uses 里 in its oldest sense , the bounded residential community of the Zhou system.
龙飞凤舞	lóng fēi fèng wǔ	dragon flying, phoenix dancing — describes bold, unrestrained calligraphy — Lit: dragon-fly phoenix-dance. Originally a description of grand, sweeping calligraphy where the strokes move with the energy of mythic creatures. Now used both admiringly (fluid, powerful brushwork) and ironically (illegible scrawl that only the writer understands).
路不拾遗	lù bù shí yí	"on the road no one picks up what others have dropped": the mark of a well-governed society — From the Hánfēizǐ (Legalist text, 3rd century BCE), describing the ideal state under good rule. Paired with 夜不闭户 yè bù bì hù ("at night doors are not bolted").
落后就要挨打	luò hòu jiù yào ái dǎ	if you fall behind, you will be beaten — the fundamental lesson of the century of humiliation — The defining phrase of modern Chinese nationalist historiography. Attributed to various leaders; popularized by Deng Xiaoping. The phrase frames national development not as aspiration but as existential necessity: weakness invites predation, therefore modernization is survival.
落井下石	luò jǐng xià shí	to throw stones at someone who has fallen in a well — to kick someone when they're down — Lit: fall-well-down-stone. The act of throwing rocks on top of someone already trapped in a well. Vivid idiom for betrayal or cruelty toward someone already in misfortune.
妈祖显灵	mā zǔ xiǎn líng	Mazu's divine power manifests — a miracle attributed to her — 显灵 xiǎn líng (lit. "spirit manifests") is the standard phrase for a deity's miraculous intervention. 妈祖显灵 is used when a ship survives a storm, a prayer is answered, or an illness is healed , any event attributed to Mazu's protective intervention.
马到成功	mǎ dào chéng gōng	immediate success upon arrival — "when the cavalry arrives, victory follows" — Lit: horse-arrive-success. Originally a wish for military campaigns, deeply resonant with the Mongol cavalry tradition that shook the known world. Used today as a congratulatory phrase for new endeavors. 祝你马到成功！ "Wishing you swift success!"
满街都是圣人	mǎn jiē dōu shì shèng rén	saints fill the streets — everyone already has the innate moral knowledge to be a sage — Said to have been uttered by Wang Yangming after a student asked skeptically whether ordinary people could really possess 良知. His response: walk out onto any busy street and observe; anyone who acts from their innate moral sense in that moment is a sage.
孟姜女哭长城	Mèng Jiāng nü kū Cháng chéng	"Meng Jiangnu weeping at the Great Wall" — grief strong enough to move stone — One of China's four great folk legends (四大民间故事 sì dà mínjiān gùshì). Meng Jiangnu's husband Fan Xiliang was conscripted to build the Qin wall and died of exhaustion.
面面俱到	miàn miàn jù dào	every aspect covered; thorough in all directions — 面 miàn (face; surface; aspect) + 面 + 俱 jù (all; together) + 到 (arrived at). Arrival has reached every face, every surface, every angle of the matter. Used to praise comprehensive coverage , a plan that misses nothing, a speech that addresses every concern, an analysis that leaves no gap.
妙手回春	miào shǒu huí chūn	miraculous hands bring back the spring; masterly healing — 妙手 (wondrous hands) + 回春 (return spring, restore life's season). The stock phrase of gratitude to a doctor, embroidered on the silk banners (锦旗 jǐnqí) that patients present to hospital departments, a living custom: the walls of a respected clinic hang red with them.
名落孙山	míng luò Sūn Shān	one's name fell behind Sun Shan's — to fail an examination — Sun Shan, a Song-dynasty wit, passed the provincial exam in last place. Asked by a neighbor whether the neighbor's son had passed, he answered in verse: "The last name on the list was Sun Shan; your worthy son came after Sun Shan." The gentlest failure notice in the language, still the standard lite…
摸着石头过河	mō zhe shí tou guò hé	crossing the river by feeling for stones — Deng's gradualist pragmatism — The most important political metaphor of the reform era. 摸 (to grope, to feel one's way) + 着 (progressive aspect) + 石头 (stones) + 过河 (cross the river).
南腔北调	nán qiāng běi diào	a jumble of accents: speech mixing many regional tongues — Lit: southern resonance and northern tones. Said of speech that mixes accents from all over, or of a gathering where many dialects are heard at once. Here 调 takes the diào reading, "accent, manner of speech."
南辕北辙	nán yuán běi zhé	"going south in the shafts, tracks heading north" — completely self-contradictory — From the Zhànguó Cè: a man in the state of Wèi claimed he was going to the state of Chǔ (which lies south) but pointed his carriage north. When told he was heading the wrong way, he replied that his horse was fast, his driver skilled, and he had plenty of provisions, all the wrong justifications.
内外有别	nèi wài yǒu bié	inside and outside are distinct: insiders and outsiders are treated differently — Lit: inside-outside-have-distinction. The principle that there is a proper line between those within a family, group, or nation and those without, and that the two are not handled the same way.
内忧外困	nèi yōu wài kùn	troubles within, pressures without: beset on every side — A state or organization squeezed simultaneously by internal worry and external pressure. Common in political and business commentary; a near twin of 内忧外患.
泥菩萨过河	ní pú sà guò hé	a clay Bodhisattva crossing a river — unable to save even oneself — Lit: clay-bodhisattva-cross-river. 菩萨 púsà (Bodhisattva) is made of clay , crossing water would destroy it. Used to say someone is in no position to help others when they can barely save themselves. 泥菩萨过河，自身难保。
跑了和尚跑不了庙	pǎo le hé shàng pǎo bu liǎo miào	"the monk may run, but the temple cannot run" — even if the person flees, the institution stays put — A folk saying, not a classical chengyu, but functionally fixed. The proverb captures a specific reasoning about accountability: a debtor or wrongdoer may try to flee, but the property, family, or institution they are tied to will remain, and can be held responsible.
跑龙套	pǎo lóng tào	"to run the dragon costume" — to play a minor role; to be a bit-part player — From traditional Chinese opera. 龙套 lóngtào is the costume worn by the chorus of attendants (soldiers, servants, courtiers) who fill out a scene without lines. To 跑龙套 is to be one of these background figures: present, busy, but never central.
朋友遍天下	péng you biàn tiān xià	to have friends all over the world — A warm saying praising someone with a wide circle of friends everywhere. 遍 biàn means "all over," 天下 "under heaven, the whole world." Often paired with the encouraging proverb 在家靠父母，出门靠朋友 ("at home you rely on parents; away from home you rely on friends"), which captures the practical importance of…
平易近人	píng yì jìn rén	"level and easy, close to people" — approachable; unpretentious; accessible — 平易 (flat and easy , without obstacles or pretension) + 近 (close to; approachable) + 人 (people). A person who is 平易近人 does not stand on ceremony, does not use their position as a barrier, and can be approached by ordinary people without formality or anxiety.
普度众生	pǔ dù zhòng shēng	to universally deliver all sentient beings from suffering — A Buddhist phrase (from Guanyin's vow) applied also to Mazu, who is often assimilated with the Goddess of Mercy in folk practice. 普度 pǔdù = universally deliver; 众生 zhòngshēng = all living beings.
七上八下	qī shàng bā xià	"seven up, eight down" — nervous and unsettled; anxious about the outcome — Seven and eight are the neighbors of ten who cannot agree on direction. Seven goes up, eight goes down: the image is of something that cannot find rest, oscillating without resolution. The idiom describes the feeling of anxiety before a result, the inner turbulence of waiting without knowing.
气吞山河	qì tūn shān hé	spirit that could swallow mountains and rivers — magnificent, all-encompassing force — Lit: qi-swallow-mountain-river. A classical idiom for heroic grandeur: the qi of a great army, a historical figure, or a piece of calligraphy or poetry so powerful it seems to contain all of nature within it. Often used in literary and historical commentary.
气壮山河	qì zhuàng shān hé	magnificent spirit that fills the mountains and rivers — Lit: qi-strong-mountain-river. Similar to 气吞山河 but with a sense of steadiness rather than swallowing, the qi standing as firm and vast as mountains and rivers. Used for patriotic spirit, heroic resolve, or great art. A phrase from Wen Tianxiang's 正气歌 tradition.
恰到好处	qià dào hǎo chù	just right; arriving at exactly the right point — 恰 qià (just; exactly) + 到 dào (arrive at) + 好 hǎo (good; right) + 处 chù (place; point). Arriving at precisely the right place: the perfectly calibrated amount, the perfectly timed remark, the execution that is neither too much nor too little.
千里之行，始于足下	qiān lǐ zhī xíng , shǐ yú zú xià	"a thousand-li journey begins beneath the feet" — every great endeavor starts with a single step — From the Daodejing, chapter 64. 千里 (a thousand li) + 之 (connecting particle) + 行 (journey, travel) + 始 (to begin) + 于 (at, from) + 足下 (beneath the feet).
千里之行，始于足下。	qiān lǐ zhī xíng , shǐ yú zú xià .	A journey of a thousand li begins beneath one's feet. — From chapter 64, now a chengyu used worldwide. The surrounding passage teaches that great things grow from small beginnings and that the wise act early, while problems are still tiny and easy to handle: the tree that fills an armspan grew from a sprout, the nine-storey tower rose from a heap of eart…
前车之鉴	qián chē zhī jiàn	"the lesson of the overturned cart ahead" — learn from the mistakes of those who came before — 前 (in front) + 车 (cart) + 之 (possessive) + 鉴 (mirror; lesson; to take as a warning). A cart that has already overturned lies visible on the road ahead of you , it is the past made legible, a warning you can see precisely because the past stands in front.
前仆后继	qián pū hòu jì	"the front one falls, the next one follows" — wave after wave of sacrifice; undeterred succession — 前 (front; those ahead) + 仆 pū (to fall forward, to fall face-down) + 后 (back; those behind) + 继 jì (to continue; to succeed). Those in the front ranks fall; those behind step up to continue.
前因后果	qián yīn hòu guǒ	the full cause and effect — Literally "the prior cause and the later result", the whole chain from why to what-happened. To know the 前因后果 is to understand a matter completely, from the reason a 为什么 seeks to the outcome it explains. 因 (cause) and 果 (effect, "fruit") are the two poles of explanation.
轻举妄动	qīng jǔ wàng dòng	to act rashly and recklessly: to move without due thought — Lit: light-lifting-reckless-moving. A warning against hasty, ill-considered action, often 不要轻举妄动 "do not act rashly," said when caution and patience are called for.
倾国倾城	qīng guó qīng chéng	to topple states and cities: a woman of overwhelming beauty — 倾 qīng = to overturn, to topple; 国 guó = state; 城 chéng = city. The image is hyperbolic: a beauty so dazzling that rulers neglect their duties and whole cities and states fall because of her.
穷途末路	qióng tú mò lù	"the road's end, the last stretch": at the end of one's resources; cornered, no way forward — 穷 qióng (exhausted) + 途 tú (way) + 末 mò (final) + 路 lù (road). Used for someone whose options have run out: a bankrupt business, a defeated army, a person at the end of their rope. Slightly literary in register.
去而复返	qù ér fù fǎn	"to go and then come back again" — to depart and return unexpectedly — 去 qù (to leave) + 而 ér (but; and then) + 复 fù (again; to return) + 返 fǎn (to turn back). Someone who had departed comes back, often unexpectedly or after a significant interval.
去留无意	qù liú wú yì	indifferent to going or staying — free of attachment to outcome — From the famous couplet: 宠辱不惊，去留无意 , "Undisturbed by honor or disgrace; indifferent to going or staying." A Daoist attitude of equanimity and non-attachment. 去 (go/leave) contrasts with 留 (stay/remain). The phrase captures the ideal of acting without being determined by results.
去伪存真	qù wěi cún zhēn	"remove the false, preserve the true" — to sift out falsehood and keep what is genuine — 去 wěi = send away the false. 存 cún = preserve; 真 zhēn = the true. The epistemological program of discernment: not just believing what you are told but actively removing the counterfeit to preserve what is real.
人山人海	rén shān rén hǎi	a mountain of people, a sea of people — an enormous crowd — Lit: person-mountain-person-sea. The go-to phrase for describing massive crowds: national holidays, concerts, train stations during Spring Festival rush. Pairing 山 (mountain) with 海 (sea), the two largest natural forms, captures the sense of scale without irony.
人往高处走，水往低处流	rén wǎng gāo chù zǒu , shuǐ wǎng dī chù liú	"people move toward high places; water flows to low places" — the natural human drive for self-improvement; ambition as gravity — A folk proverb rather than a classical four-character chengyu, but one of the most widely cited sayings about ambition and social mobility in Mandarin. Water follows physical gravity downward; people follow social gravity upward.
人心向背	rén xīn xiàng bèi	whether hearts face toward or turn away: popular support or opposition — Lit: people's-hearts, facing-or-backing. 向 is to face toward (support), 背 to turn one's back (oppose). The phrase names the balance of public sentiment that decides whether a cause or a ruler will stand.
仁义道德	rén yì dào dé	humaneness, righteousness, the Way, and virtue — the full Confucian moral vocabulary — The four great concepts of Confucian ethics strung together: 仁 rén (humaneness), 义 yì (righteousness), 道 dào (the Way , the correct path of conduct), 德 dé (virtue , moral power accumulated through right conduct). Together they describe the ideal moral life.
仁者乐山，智者乐水	rén zhě yào shān , zhì zhě yào shuǐ	"the benevolent love mountains, the wise love water" — The companion form of the Analects couplet, often quoted with the order reversed. 仁 (benevolence) and 智 (wisdom) are matched to mountain (immobile, supporting) and water (mobile, responsive) respectively.
任重道远	rèn zhòng dào yuǎn	"the burden is heavy, the road is far" — great responsibility with a long journey ahead — From the Analects of Confucius (论语): 仁以为己任，不亦重乎？死而后已，不亦远乎？(Is taking benevolence as one's charge not heavy? Does the journey end only at death , is that not far?) Used to describe serious long-term undertakings: social reform, academic work, national development. Conveys gravitas without complaint.
融会贯通	róng huì guàn tōng	"melt together, thread through": to master a subject by integrating all its parts — 融 róng (to melt; to merge) + 会 huì (to come together) + 贯 guàn (to thread through; to run a string through) + 通 (to penetrate fully).
三人行，必有我师	sān rén xíng , bì yǒu wǒ shī	where three walk together, one of them is sure to be my teacher — From the Analects (论语). Confucius's most-quoted statement on learning: in any company of three people, there will be something to learn , from the better among them by emulation, from the worse by avoidance.
三阳开泰	sān yáng kāi tài	three yang lines open into prosperity: an auspicious fresh start, a New Year blessing — From the hexagram 泰 (peace) in the Book of Changes, whose lower three lines are 阳, marking yang rising as winter turns to spring. A traditional New Year greeting wishing renewal and good fortune for the year ahead.
山高水长	shān gāo shuǐ cháng	"mountains high, water long" — virtue or reputation that endures across time — From a Northern Song memorial by Fan Zhongyan (范仲淹) describing the legacy of a virtuous official: 云山苍苍，江水泱泱；先生之风，山高水长 ("the cloudy mountains stretch dark; the rivers run wide; the master's spirit , high as the mountains, long as the rivers").
山清水秀	shān qīng shuǐ xiù	Mountains Clear, Waters Lovely — Clear mountains, lovely waters. The standard Chinese phrase for a picturesque landscape: four characters that compress the whole aesthetic vocabulary of natural beauty.
山穷水尽	shān qióng shuǐ jìn	"the mountains are exhausted, the waters are at their end" — at the end of one's resources — From Lu You (陆游, Song dynasty), 游山西村: 山穷水尽疑无路，柳暗花明又一村 ("when the mountains are spent and the waters are done and there seems to be no road , there, beyond the dim willows and bright flowers, is another village").
山重水复	shān chóng shuǐ fù	mountains piled on mountains, waters winding through waters — a seemingly hopeless impasse — From a poem by Lu You 陆游. Full line: 山重水复疑无路，柳暗花明又一村, "Mountain on mountain, water on water, seemingly no path; then through dark willows and bright flowers, a village appears." The proverb for finding hope when all seems lost.
上善若水	shàng shàn ruò shuǐ	the highest good is like water — effortless giving that sustains without striving — Daodejing, Chapter 8. Functions as a classical four-character literary phrase rather than an everyday idiom. Cited in calligraphy, philosophy, and any context invoking the Daoist ideal of natural, uncontending benefit.
上善若水。	shàng shàn ruò shuǐ .	The highest good is like water. — From chapter 8. Water is the master image of the Daodejing: it benefits all things without contending, it seeks the low places that everyone else disdains, and in this very humility it comes closest to the 道.
上下其手	shàng xià qí shǒu	"hands moving up and down" — to manipulate facts; to connive — From the Zuǒzhuàn (左传), Spring and Autumn period. A general signaled with his hand upward or downward to indicate whether a captured soldier was high-ranking (ransom-worthy) or low-ranking (irrelevant) , the signal determined the truth, not the facts.
上行下效	shàng xíng xià xiào	"what the top does, the bottom imitates" — those in power set the moral tone — A foundational principle of Confucian political thought: the ruler's virtue or vice flows downward through society. If the one above is corrupt, those below will follow. If the one above is upright, the people will follow that too. 上 and 下 carry the full social hierarchy in two strokes.
声东击西	shēng dōng jī xī	"feign east, strike west" — strategic deception; misdirection before attacking — Lit: declare-east-strike-west. A classic military stratagem from the Sun Tzu tradition , make noise in the east to draw attention, then attack from the west. Used broadly for any deceptive misdirection tactic in business, politics, or argument. 他用声东击西的策略迷惑了对手。
时来运转	shí lái yùn zhuǎn	when the time comes, fortune turns — one's luck changes for the better — Lit: time-comes fortune-turns. Fortune is not fixed; it turns like a wheel. Used when someone's luck dramatically improves after a period of misfortune. The Daoist wheel of fortune encoded in a four-character phrase.
世外桃源	shì wài táo yuán	a haven of peace: an idyllic refuge cut off from the troubled world — Lit: a peach-blossom spring beyond the world. From Tao Yuanming's fable of a fisherman who stumbles on a hidden valley untouched by war and time. Now any tranquil place sheltered from the strife and bustle of ordinary life.
事到如今	shì dào rú jīn	now that things have come to this — Literally "matters have arrived at the present," a set opener used when a situation has reached its current, often difficult, point and one must face it: 事到如今，只能这样了 ("now that it's come to this, this is all we can do"). Built on 如今 (nowadays, the present).
手到擒来	shǒu dào qín lái	the hand arrives and the capture is made — easily accomplished — Lit: hand-arrives-capture-comes. Used to describe a task accomplished with ease and confidence , the expert reaches out and the thing is done. An expression of mastery making difficulty look effortless. 对他来说，这件事手到擒来 "For him, this was a cinch."
手足无措	shǒu zú wú cuò	"hands and feet without placement" — at a complete loss, not knowing what to do — 手 (hands) + 足 (feet) + 无措 (nowhere to place them). So flustered that your hands and feet don't know where to go. From the Analects (Book XIII): Confucius warns that without ritual propriety, the people will be 手足无措.
书同文，车同轨	shū tóng wén , chē tóng guǐ	unified script, standardized axle widths — the rhetorical formula of systemic national unity — Still quoted in policy arguments about national standards: language standardization, infrastructure compatibility, technical regulation. The pairing condenses the Qin unification logic: physical connectivity (roads) plus cultural connectivity (writing) equals empire.
水到渠成	shuǐ dào qú chéng	when water flows, a channel forms — Literally "when the water arrives, the channel is made", success comes naturally once conditions ripen, with no forcing. It is the gentle counterpart to 终于's hard-won arrival: the long-awaited outcome that, in the end, simply falls into place.
水滴石穿	shuǐ dī shí chuān	water drips and the stone is bored through: small force, long time — The same image reordered, traced to the Han text Hàn Shū. Stresses the result, the worn-through stone, as proof that small efforts accumulate.
水泄不通	shuǐ xiè bù tōng	"not even water can leak through": packed solid; completely blocked or surrounded — 水 shuǐ (water) + 泄 xiè (to leak; to drain out) + 不 bù (not) + 通 (to pass through). So densely packed that not even a drop of water could trickle out.
水涨船高	shuǐ zhǎng chuán gāo	when the water rises the boat rises too: gains that lift everything along with the tide — Originally a Chán Buddhist phrase. Used when one rising element pulls others up with it, whether prices, status, or a whole industry.
顺水推船	shùn shuǐ tuī chuán	push the boat along with the current: go with the flow and make the easy move — When circumstances already lean your way, you give a light push and take the credit. Often used for a decision that costs nothing because the moment favors it.
说曹操，曹操到	shuō Cáo Cāo , Cáo Cāo dào	speak of Cao Cao and Cao Cao arrives — speak of the devil — The exact Chinese equivalent of "speak of the devil." Said the instant someone shows up just as you were talking about them. It is the most common everyday phrase in which Cao Cao's name survives, owed to his reputation, in the stories, for moving with sudden speed.
说曹操，曹操到。	shuō cáo cāo , cáo cāo dào .	Speak of Cao Cao and Cao Cao arrives. — The Chinese equivalent of "speak of the devil," drawn from the novel's portrait of Cao Cao as swift and unexpectedly present. Said when a person appears just as they are being talked about. One of the clearest examples of how thoroughly the Romance's characters entered everyday speech.
说到做到	shuō dào zuò dào	to do what one says — to be as good as one's word — 说 shuō (to say) + 到 dào (reach the point of) + 做 + 到. Literally "say it to the point, do it to the point." A high compliment: this is a person whose words and deeds match, who does not promise idly. The structure leans on 做到 (to achieve, to follow through).
说来话长	shuō lái huà cháng	to start telling it makes a long story: it is a long story — Lit: to-speak-of-it-the-words-grow-long. The standard preface to a complicated explanation, the Chinese equivalent of "well, it's a long story."
死去活来	sǐ qù huó lái	to die away and come back: to the point of fainting and reviving — Lit: die-go-live-come, pairing 死 and 活. Describes extremity of feeling or pain so severe one seems to faint and revive: to cry 哭得死去活来 (to weep oneself into a faint), to be beaten half to death.
四海为家	sì hǎi wéi jiā	to make one's home in all four seas — at home anywhere; cosmopolitan; rootless wandering — 四海 = the four seas = everywhere under heaven. 为 = to treat as; to make into. The whole globe is one's household. Used for travelers, exiles, and those rootlessly at ease anywhere. Both admiring (cosmopolitan freedom) and elegiac (loss of fixed roots) , depending on context.
四面八方	sì miàn bā fāng	"four faces, eight directions" — from every direction; from everywhere — The most common chengyu using 方. 四面 (the four faces of a square; the four sides) + 八方 (the eight directions). Used for crowds, news, demands, attacks , anything converging from every direction. 来自四面八方的客人 (guests from all over).
四通八达	sì tōng bā dá	"reaching in four and through eight" — accessible in every direction; well-connected — 四通 (passing through in the four directions) plus 八达 (extending out the eight ways). Used of roads, railways, and transport networks that radiate to everywhere, and by extension of any hub that connects in all directions. A 交通四通八达 city is one you can reach and leave by any route.
送佛送到西	sòng fó sòng dào xī	if you escort the Buddha, escort him all the way west: finish what you start, see help through to the end — A colloquial saying drawing on the westward journey of Buddhist pilgrimage. Urges you not to abandon a kindness or a task halfway, but to carry it to completion.
送旧迎新	sòng jiù yíng xīn	to send off the old and welcome the new: ring out the old year, ring in the new — A New Year and year-end phrase, balancing 送 (sending off) the old against 迎 (welcoming) the new. Common on greeting cards and in festival speeches.
送往迎来	sòng wǎng yíng lái	to send off those leaving and welcome those arriving: the endless round of social hospitality — Traces to the Lǐjì (Book of Rites). Describes the constant ceremony of receiving guests and seeing them off, often with a faint note of how exhausting such social duty becomes.
所向披靡	suǒ xiàng pī mǐ	all before one is swept aside: to carry all before one, irresistible — Lit: wherever-faced, grass-flattens. 所向 is "wherever one turns," 披靡 the way grass bends flat before the wind. Said of an army or a force so strong that everything in its path gives way.
他山之石	tā shān zhī shí	stones from other hills — outside perspectives can polish your own jade — From the Book of Songs (诗经): "stones from other hills can be used to grind jade." External viewpoints, foreign models, and outside criticism all carry value that self-regard misses. 他山 (other hills) + 之 (possessive particle) + 石 (stones).
桃李满天下	táo lǐ mǎn tiān xià	peaches and plums fill the world: a teacher's students are everywhere — Lit: peach-plum-fill-the-world. 桃李 (peach and plum trees) stand for a teacher's 弟子, the disciples cultivated over a lifetime. To say someone's 桃李满天下 is high praise for a great educator.
替天行道	tì tiān xíng dào	to carry out the Way on Heaven's behalf — The slogan on the great banner of the Liangshan outlaws, claiming that in punishing the corrupt and protecting the weak they act as the agents of Heaven's justice where the earthly state has failed.
天后圣母	Tiān hòu Shèng mǔ	Heavenly Empress, Holy Mother — Mazu's full honorific — The full formal title combines 天后 (Heavenly Empress, the highest imperial designation) with 圣母 Shèngmǔ (Holy Mother, a term shared with the Blessed Virgin in Catholic contexts, creating interesting resonance in Fujian where Portuguese missionaries arrived in the 16th century).
天南地北	tiān nán dì běi	heaven south, earth north — from all corners of the world; talking about everything under the sun — 南 nán = south; 北 běi = north. The phrase works in two ways. Used of people: 天南地北的朋友 (friends from all corners of the world , scattered to the four directions, heaven-south and earth-north).
天下大势分久必合	tiān xià dà shì , fēn jiǔ bì hé	the great tendency of all-under-heaven: long divided, must unite; long united, must divide — The opening principle of the Romance of the Three Kingdoms (三国演义). A cyclical theory of political history: unity and division alternate as inevitably as the seasons. Endlessly cited in Chinese political discourse to frame any current period as part of a longer historical rhythm.
天下太平	tiān xià tài píng	"all under heaven is at peace" — universal peace and order — 天下 tiānxià (all under heaven , the entire world as governed territory) + 太平 (great peace). The ideal end-state of Confucian governance and the dream of every dynasty: a world so well-ordered that there is nothing left to struggle against.
天下为公	tiān xià wéi gōng	the world belongs to all — the realm is the public good, not private property — Sun Yat-sen's personal motto, drawn from the ancient Book of Rites. Inscribed on buildings and monuments across Taiwan and mainland China. The phrase directly opposes the Confucian concept of dynasties as family property (家天下 jiā tiānxià, "the realm as one family's possession").
天下乌鸦一般黑	tiān xià wū yā yī bān hēi	all crows under heaven are equally black — it's the same everywhere; they're all the same — Usually cynical: no matter where you go, officials/bosses/people of that type are all equally corrupt/problematic/the same. 当官的天下乌鸦一般黑 (Officials everywhere are equally corrupt , all crows under heaven are black.) A statement of resigned cynicism about systemic problems.
天下无不散之筵席。	tiān xià wú bù sàn zhī yán xí .	No feast under heaven goes on forever; every banquet must end. — A proverb woven through the novel and epitomising its theme of impermanence: every gathering, however joyful, must break up, just as the splendour of the Jia family must pass. Used widely in Chinese to console at partings and to acknowledge that good times, like all things, come to an end.
天下无难事只怕有心人	tiān xià wú nán shì , zhǐ pà yǒu xīn rén	under heaven there are no difficult things — only fear the determined person — Determination overcomes all obstacles , there is nothing under heaven so hard that a truly committed person cannot accomplish it. A companion to 功夫不负有心人. 有心人 yǒuxīn rén = a person of resolve and intention. Used as encouragement in the face of daunting tasks: 天下无难事，只怕有心人，你一定能做到。
天下兴亡，匹夫有责	tiān xià xīng wáng , pǐ fū yǒu zé	the rise and fall of the realm is the responsibility of every common person — Attributed to the late Ming / early Qing Confucian scholar 顾炎武 Gù Yánwǔ (1613–1682), though the current form was popularized by Liang Qichao in the late Qing. A founding text of Chinese civic patriotism.
天涯海角	tiān yá hǎi jiǎo	the ends of the earth — sky-edge, sea-corner — Lit: sky-edge sea-corner. The farthest, most remote reaches of the world. High frequency in romantic poetry and pop songs about separation and longing.
通情达理	tōng qíng dá lǐ	"through to feeling, reaching to reason": sensible, reasonable, understanding — 通 (to be through to) + 情 qíng (human feeling; the way people are) + 达 dá (to reach) + 理 lǐ (reason; principle). A person who is 通情达理 understands both how people feel and what reason requires, and weighs the two fairly.
头头是道	tóu tóu shì dào	clear and logical at every point: well-reasoned and articulate — Lit: every-head-is-the-way. Said of speech or analysis that is orderly and convincing from start to finish, with each point landing soundly. 说得头头是道 "to argue every point convincingly."
突飞猛进	tū fēi měng jìn	to advance by leaps and bounds: to progress at great speed — Lit: sudden-fly-fierce-advance. Said of rapid, dramatic progress in study, technology, or an economy. 科技突飞猛进 "technology advances by leaps and bounds."
脱胎换骨	tuō tāi huàn gǔ	shed the womb, swap the bones: a total transformation — From Daoist alchemy, on the adept reborn into an immortal frame. Modern usage praises a genuine, root-and-branch change for the better.
脱颖而出	tuō yǐng ér chū	the awl-tip comes through the bag — talent that emerges despite obstacles — From the Shǐjì (史记, Records of the Grand Historian). 颖 yǐng = the pointed tip of an awl. 脱 tuō = to escape; to break free. An awl placed in a bag will always eventually poke through: genuine talent, given any opportunity, will emerge and make itself visible.
外儒内法	wài Rú nèi Fǎ	Confucian on the outside, Legalist on the inside — the structural formula of Chinese imperial governance — A modern scholarly formulation summarizing two thousand years of Chinese governance. 外 wài = outer, exterior; 内 nèi = inner, interior. The outer face of government (edicts, titles, rituals, examination texts) was thoroughly Confucian.
万里长城	wàn lǐ Cháng chéng	the ten-thousand-li Great Wall — symbol of colossal collective effort — 万里 wànlǐ (ten thousand li) is figurative hyperbole. The wall's actual total length across all dynasties is approximately 21,196 km. Used metaphorically for any enormous undertaking requiring sustained collective effort.
万里长征	wàn lǐ cháng zhēng	"the ten-thousand-li long march" — the 1934–1935 retreat of the Red Army; any prolonged and arduous undertaking — 万里 (ten thousand li , vast) + 长征 (long expedition, long march). The Chinese Communist Party's Long March (1934–1935) covered approximately 12,000 kilometers as the Red Army retreated from Nationalist forces. 万里 again functions as rhetorical superlative rather than measurement.
万事俱备，只欠东风	wàn shì jù bèi , zhǐ qiàn dōng fēng	all ten thousand things are ready; only the east wind is lacking — From the Romance of the Three Kingdoms (三国演义). Before the Battle of Red Cliffs (208 CE), Zhuge Liang tells Zhou Yu that the plan is complete in every respect , except that the wind is blowing in the wrong direction to set the enemy fleet alight.
万事俱备，只欠东风。	wàn shì jù bèi , zhǐ qiàn dōng fēng .	Everything is ready; we lack only the east wind. — From the eve of Red Cliffs, when the allies' fire-attack plan was fully prepared but needed a wind from the east to carry the flames into Cao Cao's fleet. The line is now a common chengyu for a situation in which all preparations are complete and only one final, decisive condition is missing.
往来不绝	wǎng lái bù ju é	a constant stream of coming and going — uninterrupted traffic — 往 wǎng (to go toward) + 来 (to come) + 不绝 (without cessation). Describes a place or relationship with continuous back-and-forth movement: traffic, commerce, correspondence, social connection. 街上人来人往，好不热闹 "People coming and going on the street , what a lively scene!"
往事如烟	wǎng shì rú yān	the past is like drifting smoke — Literally "bygone matters are like smoke", the past fading and dispersing beyond grasp. Here 往 takes its time sense, 往事 ("past events"), and the phrase carries a gentle melancholy about how what is gone slips away. The mirror of 往 reaching backward rather than ahead.
望眼欲穿	wàng yǎn yù chuān	gazing till the eyes would bore through: aching with anticipation — Eyes straining at the road so hard they might pierce the distance. Describes longing for someone's return or news that will not come.
为学日益，为道日损。	wéi xu é rì yì , wéi dào rì sǔn .	In pursuit of learning, every day something is gained. In pursuit of the Dao, every day something is dropped. — Chapter 48. The contrast with Confucius's emphasis on continuous learning and accumulation of knowledge is probably deliberate. The Daodejing does not reject learning but insists that the Dao requires a different movement: subtraction rather than addition, unlearning rather than accumulation.
文以载道	wén yǐ zài dào	writing carries the Way — literature serves moral truth — The classical Chinese theory of literature: writing is not merely aesthetic entertainment but a vehicle for moral truth (道 Dào). Proposed by Han Yu (韩愈) in the Tang Dynasty and central to the Chinese literary tradition.
稳如泰山	wěn rú Tài Shān	as stable as Mount Tai — utterly immovable, rock solid — Mount Tai (泰山, Shandong), the most sacred of the Five Peaks and site of imperial Heaven-sacrifice. Used to praise a person's composure, a government's stability, or a company's financial health.
无出其右	wú chū qí yòu	"nothing comes to the right of it" — unrivaled; the best of its kind — 无 (nothing; none) + 出 (to go beyond; to surpass) + 其 (its; this) + 右 (right side). In classical Chinese ranking, the person or thing of highest rank occupied the rightmost position in certain formal arrangements. "Nothing surpasses it to the right" means nothing ranks higher.
无处不到	wú chù bù dào	all-pervading; present everywhere; reaching every corner — 无 wú (without; no) + 处 chù (place) + 不 bù (not) + 到 (arrive). There is no place that is not arrived at. The double negative , no place that has not been reached , produces the emphatic positive: total pervasiveness.
五湖四海	wǔ hú sì hǎi	the five lakes and four seas: from every corner of the land — A sweep of the whole country. Used warmly for people gathered from everywhere: 我们来自五湖四海。
物换星移	wù huàn xīng yí	things change, stars shift — time passes and the world turns over — From Wang Bo's Tang-dynasty Pavilion of Prince Teng poem: 物换星移几度秋, "things change, stars shift, how many autumns now?" The stars' slow precession as the clock of dynasties. Used for nostalgia at scale: returning to a transformed hometown, rereading an old diary.
雾里看花	wù lǐ kàn huā	"looking at flowers through mist" — seeing something obscurely, without clarity — 雾 (mist, fog) + 里 (inside, within) + 看花 (looking at flowers). The object of perception is real, but the medium prevents clear sight. Used for situations where the truth is obscured , murky political signals, ambiguous intentions, confusing circumstances.
夕阳西下	xī yáng xī xià	"the evening sun descends in the west" — dusk; things in decline — 夕阳 (setting sun) + 西 (west) + 下 (to descend). A classic image from Tang dynasty poetry: the sun dropping below the western horizon. Used literally for beautiful sunsets and metaphorically for the decline of an era, a career, or a life.
西方极乐	xī fāng jí lè	the Western Pure Land — and by extension, any paradise or blissful realm — The formal name 西方极乐世界 Xīfāng Jílè Shìjiè is abbreviated to 西方极乐 in common usage. In pure religious context: Amitābha's Buddha-field. In broader cultural use: any ideal realm of beauty, abundance, and peace.
喜出望外	xǐ chū wàng wài	joy beyond what one hoped — overjoyed by surprise — 喜 (delight) arriving from outside (外) the range of expectation (望). The precise word for good news you did not dare hope for: the scholarship, the recovered wallet, the friend at the airport gate. Stronger and more specific than 很高兴.
先来后到	xiān lái hòu dào	first come, first served — Literally "the early-comers and the later-arrivers", the order of precedence set by who came first. It rests on the same 先 ("first") and 后 ("after") that frame 然后, here applied to fairness in turn-taking and priority. The natural order of a queue.
先礼后兵	xiān lǐ hòu bīng	"first courtesy, then arms" — try diplomacy before resorting to force — From the Romance of the Three Kingdoms (三国演义). The classical strategic principle: open with 礼 (courteous communication), and only if that fails escalate to 兵 (military force or coercive action).
先小人后君子	xiān xiǎo rén hòu jūn zǐ	petty men first, gentlemen after — settle terms up front — The contract-signing proverb: state the money, the split, and the deadlines with un-gentlemanly explicitness now, so that everyone can afford to be a gentleman later. Spoken at the start of partnerships between friends precisely because friendship is what it protects.
弦外之音	xián wài zhī yīn	the note beyond the strings: an unspoken meaning, the overtone behind the words — Lit: outside-the-string's sound. The sense a speaker implies without saying it outright, like the overtones a plucked string sets ringing. To catch the 弦外之音 is to hear what someone really means.
香火	xiāng huo	incense smoke; the vitality of a temple's active veneration — Literally "incense fire" or "incense smoke," but 香火 carries meanings beyond the physical act of burning. A temple with 香火鼎盛 xiānghuo dǐngshèng (incense burning vigorously) is a temple with a healthy, active congregation, good reputation, and real spiritual efficacy.
小孩没娘，说来话长	xiǎo hái méi niáng , shuō lái huà cháng	the child has no mother: it's a long story — A 歇后语 (xiēhòuyǔ) two-part saying. The literal image, a motherless child, sets up the punchline 说来话长, "it's a long story." Spoken as a preface when the backstory is tangled and the speaker hardly knows where to begin.
笑里藏刀	xiào lǐ cáng dāo	a dagger hidden in a smile: friendly on the surface, treacherous beneath — Lit: laugh-inside hide-knife. Traced to the Tang minister Lǐ Yìfǔ, whose mild smile masked a ruthless heart. Names a person whose warmth is a weapon, the dark twin of 微笑.
心快口直	xīn kuài kǒu zhí	quick heart, straight mouth — frank and direct by nature — Lit: heart-fast-mouth-straight. A description of someone whose inner state and outer speech are perfectly aligned: no strategic filtering, no diplomatic softening. Used as a compliment among people who value directness, and occasionally as an excuse after saying something pointed.
心外无理，心外无物	xīn wài wú lǐ , xīn wài wú wù	outside the mind there is no principle; outside the mind there are no things — Two formulations of Wang Yangming's central thesis in their most direct form. 心外无理 xīn wài wú lǐ directly contradicts Zhu Xi: principle is not outside the mind awaiting investigation.
欣欣向荣	xīn xīn xiàng róng	flourishing and thriving: full of vigor and growth — Lit: joyful-joyful, facing-flourishing. 欣欣 is the look of glad, vigorous growth; 向荣 is turning toward flourishing. Said of plants in full bloom, and by extension of a thriving enterprise or society.
信口开河	xìn kǒu kāi hé	to open the mouth like a river — to speak carelessly; to say whatever comes to mind without regard for truth — 信 xìn here shifts sense: 信 kǒu = to trust one's mouth, to follow wherever the mouth goes (the archaic sense of 信 as "to follow freely"). 开河 kāi hé = to open a river, to let it flow unobstructed.
信手拈来	xìn shǒu niān lái	pick up casually with a free hand — to write or create with effortless skill — Lit: trust-hand-pick up-come. Used of writers, calligraphers, and scholars who produce refined work without apparent effort. The idiom implies deep mastery: the hand moves freely because the mind no longer needs to deliberate. Often said of someone who writes beautifully on demand.
行尸走肉	xíng shī zǒu ròu	a walking corpse, a moving piece of flesh — alive in body but dead in spirit — Lit: walking-corpse, walking-flesh. Said of someone who has lost the inner life that makes a person alive in any meaningful sense , going through motions without thought, feeling, or purpose. Sharp, almost cruel; reserved for those judged to have given up entirely.
行行出状元	háng háng chū zhuàng yuán	every trade produces its champion — every line of work has its top practitioner — Lit: every-row produces a 状元 (the top scholar in the imperial exam). The folk-wisdom rebuttal to anyone who says one trade is beneath another: every trade has its master, every craft has its top practitioner, and excellence is available wherever you are.
行云流水	xíng yún liú shuǐ	like drifting clouds and flowing water — natural, effortless, unforced — Lit: walking-clouds, flowing-water. The classical praise for prose, calligraphy, conversation, or any skilled action that has reached the point of unforced motion. The sister-image to 自然 (naturalness): the result is fluid because nothing is being held against the grain.
行走天下	xíng zǒu tiān xià	walk throughout the world — to travel the world boldly — The romantic image of the 江湖 adventurer or itinerant scholar: someone who has traveled all under Heaven, accumulating experience and connections. The phrase captures the Chinese ideal of the person who has moved through the world and is at home everywhere.
形影不离	xíng yǐng bù lí	form and shadow never part: inseparable — Lit: form and shadow do not separate. A shadow follows its form everywhere; two people who are 形影不离 are constant companions, always together. Warm and affectionate, used for close friends, devoted couples, or a child who will not leave a parent's side.
削尖脑袋往里钻	xu ē jiān nǎo dai wǎng lǐ zuān	to sharpen one's head and bore in: to push to the front of the queue — A colloquial saying, not a classical 成语, paired here with 加塞儿 (jiāsāir, to cut the line). Mentioned to contrast the orderly 排队 ideal with the elbows-out reality of a crowded queue.
学富五车	xu é fù wǔ chē	"learning fills five carts" — immensely learned; erudite — 学 (learning) is 富 (rich enough) to fill 五车 (five cartloads of books). The image comes from an age when books were bamboo strips, so that a serious library was measured by the cartload. Originally said of the philosopher Hui Shi, whose writings were said to fill five carts.
雪上加霜	xu ě shàng jiā shuāng	frost on top of snow — to make a bad situation even worse — 雪 xuě (snow) + 上 shàng (on top of) + 加 jiā (to add) + 霜 shuāng (frost). Frost piled on snow already covering the ground: one hardship stacked on another. The everyday equivalent of "to rub salt in the wound" or "when it rains, it pours." 他刚失业，妻子又生病了，真是雪上加霜。
雪中送炭	xu ě zhōng sòng tàn	to send charcoal in the snow: timely help when it is needed most — The image is bringing fuel to someone freezing in a blizzard. Praises aid that arrives at the moment of real need, the warm counterpart of 锦上添花 (adding flowers to brocade), which only embellishes what is already fine. Still in everyday use.
循序渐进	xún xù jiàn jìn	to advance step by step in order — Literally "following the sequence, advancing gradually", to progress in proper order without rushing. It is the principle behind any 先…然后…最后 plan: each step in its turn, the next only after the last. A watchword in learning and training.
言行一致	yán xíng yī zhì	words and actions align — to practice what you preach — 言 yán (words) + 行 xíng (actions) + 一致 yīzhì (consistent; in agreement). The Confucian virtue of correspondence between speech and conduct. Used as a positive description of trustworthy, reliable people, and as a standard against which behaviour is measured.
养家活口	yǎng jiā huó kǒu	to support the family and feed its mouths: to provide for one's household — Lit: support-family-keep-alive-mouths. Here 活 is causative, to keep alive by feeding. The duty of the breadwinner to put food on the table and sustain everyone under the roof.
药到病除	yào dào bìng chú	the medicine arrives, the illness is gone: an instant, effective cure — A praise phrase for a skilled doctor or a decisive remedy. Extended to any solution that fixes a problem cleanly and at once.
一步跨过	yī bù kuà guò	"to cross over in a single step" — to clear an obstacle in one move — Not a classical 成语 but a fixed conversational formula. Often used in development and policy rhetoric: 一步跨过工业化 "to leap past industrialization in a single step." Carries a faint flavor of skipping stages: sometimes admiring (decisive progress), sometimes ironic (rushing through what should have been…
一步一个脚印	yī bù yī gè jiǎo yìn	"one step, one footprint" — steady, methodical progress — Lit: each step leaves exactly one footprint; nothing rushed, nothing skipped. A modern proverb for diligent, unhurried work that builds on itself. Often used in career advice, study guidance, and praise for craftsmen.
一动不动	yí dòng bú dòng	not moving a muscle: perfectly still, motionless — Lit: one-move-not-move. The everyday phrase for total stillness, a person frozen in place, an animal holding its breath. The doubled 动 frames the stillness as the complete absence of motion.
一口气	yī kǒu qì	in one breath; without stopping; in one go — Lit: one-mouth-breath. Describes doing something continuously, without pause, in one uninterrupted exhalation of effort. 他一口气跑了十公里 "He ran ten kilometers without stopping." 一口气读完这本书 "Read the whole book in one sitting." The most common and versatile 气 idiom in everyday speech.
一路顺风	yí lù shùn fēng	"the whole road with following winds": bon voyage, may your trip go smoothly — A maritime image (favorable wind for the sails) generalized to all travel. Ubiquitous farewell phrase, slightly less formal than 一路平安.
一票否决	yí piào fǒu ju é	a single vote vetoes it: one negative vote overrides the rest; absolute veto power — 一票 yí piào = a single vote. 否决 fǒujué = to vote down; to veto. A decision rule in which one opposing vote is enough to block a proposal outright, the power a permanent member holds on the UN Security Council.
一往无前	yì wǎng wú qián	to press forward without turning back — Literally "going straight on, nothing ahead can stop it", an image of fearless forward drive. The 往 here is pure forward motion, the direction-of-travel sense, intensified into dauntless momentum. Said of armies, reformers, and anyone who advances undeterred.
一意孤行	yī yì gū xíng	to walk one's own way alone — to push through with one fixed idea, ignoring all advice — Lit: one-intention-alone-walk. Said of someone who refuses to listen, refuses to compromise, refuses to take counsel , and walks straight into the consequences. The 孤 "alone" is what makes it pejorative: not principled persistence (which would be praiseworthy) but stubborn isolation.
异口同声	yì kǒu tóng shēng	different mouths, the same sound — unanimous — Everyone says the same thing at once: the committee that answers in chorus, the witnesses whose accounts match. Neutral to positive in tone; suspicious unanimity would be phrased differently. The mirror image of 众说纷纭, where every mouth says something else.
异想天开	yì xiǎng tiān kāi	wildly fanciful ideas; to have one's head in the clouds — 异 yì (strange; different; fantastical) + 想 xiǎng (thought; imagine) + 天 tiān (sky; heaven) + 开 kāi (to open). Fantastical thoughts that open the sky itself: thinking so far outside reality that heaven itself cracks open.
应接不暇	yìng jiē bù xiá	too busy to cope: more coming in than one can attend to — Lit: responding and receiving without a spare moment. 应接 is "to attend to"; 不暇 is "no leisure." Said when guests, sights, or tasks arrive faster than one can handle them. The yìng reading, "to meet."
有朝一日	yǒu zhāo yī rì	should there come a day: one day, someday — Lit: there-is morning one-day. 朝 here is zhāo, a morning standing for some future day. A set way of saying "one day," usually pointing to a hope or ambition: 有朝一日我会成功 "one day I will succeed."
有口难言	yǒu kǒu nán yán	having a mouth but unable to speak — in a position where one cannot explain oneself — The frustration of having the means to speak but being unable to, because of social constraints, grief, or incriminating circumstances. One of the most vivid expressions for the experience of being misunderstood with no way to clear one's name.
有口无心	yǒu kǒu wú xīn	mouth but no heart — speaking without malice; thoughtless but innocent — Lit: have-mouth-lack-heart. Describes someone who says careless or blunt things but means no harm , the words come from the mouth, not the heart. Used to excuse or explain unintentionally offensive speech. 他说话直，有口无心，别放在心上。"He speaks bluntly , no malice intended."
有来有往	yǒu lái yǒu wǎng	"there is coming and there is going" — mutual exchange; reciprocity — 往 wǎng = to go; toward. 来 and 往 together make up the full back-and-forth of social exchange: gifts, visits, words, favors. A relationship characterized by 有来有往 is balanced and healthy , neither party gives without receiving. Often used to encourage reciprocity in social or diplomatic contexts.
有缘千里来相会	yǒu yuán qiān lǐ lái xiāng huì	"those with fate meet across a thousand miles" — destiny brings people together regardless of distance — The full saying: 有缘千里来相会，无缘对面不相识。"Those with karmic connection will meet across a thousand miles; those without it won't recognize each other face to face." One of the most beloved Chinese sayings about fate and connection.
右文之治	yòu wén zhī zhì	"governance that favors the literary" — an administration that prioritizes civil culture over military force — 右文 (to favor the literary arts: to place the civil over the martial) + 之 (possessive particle) + 治 (governance; rule).
愚公移山	yú gōng yí shān	The Foolish Old Man Moves the Mountains — Two mountains blocked his door. He and his sons began to move them, one basket at a time. A mocker laughed. He said: when I die my sons will continue; when they die, their sons.
雨后春笋	yǔ hòu chūn sǔn	spring bamboo shoots after rain: springing up everywhere at once — The image of bamboo shoots erupting after spring rain. Modern usage describes new businesses, buildings, or trends multiplying rapidly.
远亲不如近邻	yuǎn qīn bù rú jìn lín	"a distant relative is no match for a close neighbor" — proximity creates community — 远亲 (distant kin) + 不如 (not as good as) + 近邻 (near neighbor). A proverb rather than a classical chengyu with a single attributed source, but documented in Song dynasty collections and widely cited since.
远走高飞	yuǎn zǒu gāo fēi	walk far and fly high — to go far away; to escape and seek freedom — Lit: far-walk-high-fly. The image of a bird launching into open sky. Used when someone leaves a constraining situation (a bad job, a difficult relationship, a suffocating hometown) to seek a better life elsewhere. Carries a tone of liberation and sometimes wistfulness.
越来越好	yu è lái yu è hǎo	"better and better" — a wish or observation of continuous improvement — Not classical 成语 but a near-fixed conversational formula and a cultural mantra of optimism. The opening line of a famous song by Sun Yue (孙悦) used at the 2000 Lunar New Year gala, 越来越好 ("Better and Better"), fixed it in popular memory.
越陷越深	yu è xiàn yu è shēn	"the more one sinks, the deeper one falls" — to become increasingly trapped — A 越…越… frame applied to 陷 (xiàn, to sink, to become trapped). Used for any situation where each step makes escape harder: debt, addiction, scandal, a bad relationship.
越俎代庖	yu è zǔ dài páo	"to overstep the sacrificial vessel and take the cook's place" — to do someone else's job, to overstep one's role — From the Zhuangzi (庄子). 俎 zǔ is the sacrificial chopping block , the priest's tool , and 庖 páo is the kitchen's domain. The original image is of a priest abandoning the altar to do the cook's work.
晕头转向	yūn tóu zhuàn xiàng	"dizzy head, spinning direction" — disoriented; thrown off; bewildered — 晕 (yūn, dizzy) + 头 (tóu, head) + 转 (zhuàn, to spin) + 向 (xiàng, direction). The character 向 alone , not 方向 , is doing the directional work in the chengyu. Used when someone is overwhelmed, confused, or has lost track of what is going on.
瞻前顾后	zhān qián gù hòu	"look ahead and glance behind" — to be overly cautious; to hesitate by considering every angle — 瞻 zhān (to look ahead; to gaze forward) + 前 qián (front) + 顾 gù (to look back; to attend to) + 后 hòu (back). Looking anxiously in both temporal directions , toward the past and the future , before acting. Used critically of someone who deliberates so carefully they become paralyzed.
站不住脚	zhàn bù zhù jiǎo	cannot stand on its feet: untenable — Literally "the feet cannot hold their stand." Used of an argument, excuse, or theory that collapses under scrutiny. Colloquial and very frequent.
站稳脚跟	zhàn wěn jiǎo gēn	to stand firm; to gain a foothold — Literally "to stand steady on one's heels." A common set phrase, not a classical four-character idiom: to establish oneself securely in a place, job, or market before making a move. Everyday modern usage.
站在风口浪尖	zhàn zài fēng kǒu làng jiān	to stand where the wind and waves meet: at the eye of the storm — To be exposed at the most dangerous, contested forefront of events, where pressure and scrutiny are fiercest. Modern journalistic register, common in commentary.
蒸蒸日上	zhēng zhēng rì shàng	"rising like steam, day by day" — prospering and flourishing continuously — 蒸 zhēng = steam; to rise in vapor. The image of steam rising without ceasing, day after day. Used of businesses, careers, nations, or any enterprise that is consistently growing and thriving. A very positive set phrase, commonly used in well-wishes and assessments of success.
正中下怀	zhèng zhòng xià huái	hitting exactly what I hoped for — exactly what I wanted — Lit: exactly-hit-one's-heart/intention. Note: 中 here is zhòng (tone 4, to hit). The expression describes something that lands exactly where you hoped (a proposal, a result, a turn of events) as if it aimed directly at your wishes. 这个方案真是正中下怀 "This plan is exactly what I had in mind."
郑和下西洋	Zhèng Hé xià Xī yáng	Zheng He's expeditions to the Western Oceans — shorthand for Chinese maritime greatness — Used as a historical reference point in modern discussions of Chinese global engagement, maritime strategy, and the "peaceful rise" narrative. The voyages are cited as evidence that Chinese power projection has historically been tributary rather than colonial.
支离破碎	zhī lí pò suì	limbs apart and shattered to bits: fragmented, in pieces — Here 离 means scattered or come apart. Describes something broken into incoherent fragments, a wrecked argument, a shattered country, a disjointed text.
知足常乐	zhī zú cháng lè	knowing sufficiency brings lasting happiness — contentment is the source of joy — From Laozi (老子·道德经, Chapter 33): 知足者富 (zhī zú zhě fù) , "Those who know sufficiency are wealthy." The fuller proverb 知足常乐 is not directly from the Daodejing text but is a Taoist-derived commonplace built on that chapter's logic.
执法如山	zhí fǎ rú shān	to enforce the law like a mountain: to uphold it firmly and without bending — Lit: enforce-law-like-mountain. Praise for an official or judge who applies the law unshakably, immovable as a mountain, favoring no one and yielding to no pressure.
纸上谈兵	zhǐ shàng tán bīng	strategize on paper — armchair theorizing without practical ability — From the Battle of Changping (260 BCE): Zhao Kuo (赵括) could discuss military theory fluently but had no battlefield experience. His appointment as Zhao's commander, reversing the caution of the veteran Lian Po, led to the encirclement and massacre of 400,000 Zhao troops.
志同道合	zhì tóng dào hé	sharing the same goals and ideals — kindred spirits — Describes friends or partners who share the same ambitions (志 zhì) and follow the same path (道 dào). The classical ideal of friendship: not mere companionship but a meeting of values and direction. Used for close friends, allies, and collaborators who are truly aligned in what they want and believe.
置身事外	zhì shēn shì wài	to place oneself outside the matter: to stay aloof, keep clear of it — Lit: place-body-affair-outside. To keep oneself out of an affair, neither helping nor interfering. Often mildly critical, of someone who stands apart when they ought to be involved.
众口铄金	zhòng kǒu shuò jīn	many mouths melt gold — From the Guoyu and other classical texts: collective speech is so powerful it can melt metal. A more extreme formulation of the same principle as 三人成虎: the force of accumulated voices is overwhelming, even against something as solid as reputation or fact.
众志成城	zhòng zhì chéng chéng	"when wills unite, a city wall is formed" — collective will is an impregnable defense — From the Guoyu (国语 Guóyǔ), a pre-Qin historical text. 众 (the many; the people) + 志 (will; resolve) + 成 (form; become) + 城 (city wall). When a people are of one mind, they become as strong as a city's walls. Used in political speeches, team motivation, and times of national crisis. 面对困难，我们众志成城，一定能克服。
重于泰山	zhòng yú tài shān	weightier than Mount Tai: of the gravest possible significance — From Sima Qian: a death may be 重于泰山 (weightier than Mt Tai) or 轻于鸿毛 (lighter than a goose feather), depending on what it is given for. The zhòng reading, weight as worth.
字里行间	zì lǐ háng jiān	within the words and between the lines: the meaning an author implies but does not state — Lit: inside-the-characters, between-the-lines. The unspoken sense that lives in the spaces of a text. 字里行间流露出真情 "true feeling shows between the lines."
走马观花	zǒu mǎ guān huā	"look at flowers from a galloping horse" — a superficial, rushing view — 走马 (galloping horse) + 观花 (observe flowers). The mounted rider moves too fast to see the flowers properly , their gaze skims the surface without stopping to appreciate what is there. A criticism of rushed, inattentive engagement with a subject.
左顾右盼	zuǒ gù yòu pàn	"look left, glance right" — looking around hesitantly or alertly; distracted attention — 左 (left) + 顾 (to look back; to turn and look) + 右 (right) + 盼 (to look; to gaze with expectation). The eyes move ceaselessly left and right , taking in everything around, unable or unwilling to settle on a single point.
左思右想	zuǒ sī yòu xiǎng	thinking left and right — to turn something over and over in the mind; to deliberate from all angles — 左 zuǒ (left) + 思 sī (to think) + 右 yòu (right) + 想 xiǎng (to think). The spatial metaphor makes tangible what overthinking feels like: the mind moving left, then right, finding no ground to rest on.
左右逢源	zuǒ yòu féng yuán	"meet a spring on the left and on the right" — resourceful; successful in every direction — 左右 (left and right) + 逢 (to encounter; to meet with) + 源 (a spring, source of water). Wherever you turn, you find a source of nourishment or support. Originally from Mencius (孟子·离娄下): a scholar who has deeply internalized principle will find it arising naturally from every direction.
左右为难	zuǒ yòu wéi nán	"left and right both difficult" — caught in a dilemma; no good option in either direction — 左右 (left and right) + 为 (to be; causing) + 难 (difficult). Whichever direction you move , left or right , you face difficulty. The idiom captures the specific kind of dilemma where the problem is bilateral: pleasing one side offends the other, taking one path closes the other.
来	lái / qù	去 · directional complements — 上来, 下去, 出去, 进来: the motion complement system mapped.
离	lí	away from; distance — The coverb for distance and separation: the A 离 B + distance pattern for "how far", the verb sense "to leave / part from", and how 离 differs from 从 and 到 in marking space and time.
起来	qǐ lái	directional complement: upward, inward, beginning — The most versatile directional complement in Mandarin: three distinct grammatical lives.
往	wǎng	toward; to head for — The directional coverb for "toward", the 往 + direction + verb pattern, the verb sense "to go / in the past", and how 往 differs from 向 and 朝 for facing and motion.
一下	yí xià	the verb-softener — The verbal-quantity word that makes an action brief and casual, how verb + 一下 softens a request, and how it differs from verb reduplication and from the time-word 一会儿.
越来越	yu è lái yu è	more and more; increasingly — The degree escalation pattern: expressing a quality that grows continuously over time.
在	zài	location, existence, and the progressive — One of the busiest words in Mandarin: the verb "to be at," the location coverb "at/in," and the progressive marker for ongoing action.
"骸骨筑入墙内"	hái gǔ zhù rù qiáng nèi	"bodies buried inside the wall" — Folk tradition since at least Tang times has held that the bones of dead labourers were mixed into the rammed earth. Archaeology has confirmed nearby labourer cemeteries with graves of those who died building the wall, but no systematic evidence that bodies were used as construction material.
"可以从太空看见"	kě yǐ cóng tài kōng kàn jiàn	"visible from space" — The wall cannot be seen from space with the naked eye. It is long but only four to eight metres wide, narrower than most highways. The claim was a 1932 invention from Ripley's Believe It or Not! — published before any human had reached orbit.
"秦始皇修了长城"	qín shǐ huáng xiū le cháng chéng	"Qin Shihuang built the Great Wall" — He connected and extended pre-existing Warring States walls. He did not build from nothing, and what he did build is mostly gone. The visual wall most people picture is Ming, fifteen centuries later.
"一道连续的墙"	yī dào lián xù de qiáng	"a continuous wall" — The Great Wall is not, and never was, a single continuous line. Even the Ming wall has parallel branches, doubled-back sections, gaps where rivers and mountains made building unnecessary, and stretches where it thins to a chain of isolated beacon towers.
八大菜系	bā dà cài xì	the Eight Great Cuisines — every regional banquet runs on baijiu
把包放下	bǎ bāo fàng xià	put the bag down — 放 fàng (to place; put) + 下 xià (down; into a lower position). A simple directional 把 sentence , the object (bag) is moved to a lower location. 下 here signals downward movement or lowering into position. The 把 foregrounds the deliberate, targeted nature of the placing.
把窗户打开	bǎ chuāng hu dǎ kāi	to open the window (strike it into an open state) — 打 dǎ (to hit; to strike , used as a general action verb with many objects) + 开 kāi (open; apart; into an open state). The combination 打开 means "open" , the object is moved into an open or separated state. 开 as a resultative complement implies separation, opening, or dispersal.
把门关上	bǎ mén guān shàng	to close the door (shut it into the closed position) — 关 guān (to close) + 上 shàng (up; into a closed/attached state). Here 上 is a resultative complement indicating that the door has been moved into the closed, shut position.
把书拿出来	bǎ shū ná chū lái	take the book out (bring it out into the open) — 拿 ná (to take; to hold in the hand) + 出来 chūlái (out-coming; out toward the speaker). The book is extracted from some container or location and brought into the present space. 出来 implies emergence from a hidden or enclosed place toward an open, accessible position.
把椅子搬进来	bǎ yǐ zi bān jìn lái	bring the chair inside (move it in-coming direction) — 搬 bān (to move heavy things; to transport) + 进来 jìnlái (in-coming; into this space). The directional complement 进来 specifies both the direction (in) and the orientation toward the speaker (来). The 把 construction signals the chair is a definite, specific object being moved as a deliberate act.
霸道	bà dào	hegemony; domineering; the way of force — 霸 bà (hegemon; overlord, one who rules by force rather than virtue) + 道 dào (way; path). Classically: governing through coercion rather than moral example, the antithesis of 王道. Colloquially in modern Mandarin: overbearing, domineering (of a person's behavior).
白话文	bái huà wén	vernacular written Chinese — 白 bái (plain; white; clear; spoken) + 话 huà (speech; talk) + 文 wén (written text). Writing that matches how people actually speak. 白话 literally = "plain speech," the ordinary spoken language as opposed to the elevated classical register.
白切鸡	bái qiē jī	White-Cut Chicken
败北	bài běi	to be defeated; to suffer a loss — 败 (bài, to be defeated, to lose) + 北 (běi, to flee , the original meaning). The classical military compound where 北 carries its original "flight in defeat" meaning. Used in formal and literary contexts for losing a battle, an election, or a competition.
搬	bān	move (objects)
帮我翻译一下	bāng wǒ fān yì yí xià	please translate this for me — For your interpreter, or for the English-speaking salesperson when the engineer goes too fast. Using an interpreter for one hard passage and Chinese for the rest is common and costs no face.
帮我一下	bāng wǒ yí xià	give me a hand; help me out — A model of 一下 as a politeness softener. The bare 帮我 ("help me") is blunt; adding 一下 frames it as a small, quick favor and sounds far friendlier. Note that 一下 can follow the object here (帮我一下) because 帮 takes a person directly. The same softening works across requests: 看一下, 等一下, 问一下.
报道	bào dào	news report; to report (news) — 报 bào (to announce; to report; newspaper) + 道 dào (way; path). To map and announce the course of events: journalism as the act of charting what has happened. Used both as a noun (a report, a piece of coverage) and as a verb (to report on something).
北斗七星	běi dǒu qī xīng	the seven stars of the Big Dipper — 北斗 (the Northern Dipper) + 七星 (seven stars). The seven bright stars of the Big Dipper, central to Chinese astronomy, navigation, and Daoist ritual.
北方	běi fāng	the North; northern
北港朝天宫	Běi gǎng Cháo tiān Gōng	Beigang Chaotian Temple — Taiwan's great Mazu shrine — The Chaotian Temple (朝天宫) in Beigang, Yunlin County, Taiwan, is one of the oldest and most revered Mazu temples on the island, traditionally dating its founding to 1694 CE. With Dajia's Zhenlangong, it is one of Taiwan's two dominant Mazu pilgrimage centers.
北京	Běi jīng	Beijing (capital of China) — 北 (north) + 京 (capital). The Northern Capital , counterpart to 南京 Nánjīng (Southern Capital). Beijing has served as the capital of the Yuan, Ming, and Qing dynasties, and of the People's Republic. The name dates to 1403 when the Yongle Emperor moved the Ming court north from Nanjing.
北京话	Běi jīng huà	Beijing dialect — Beijing dialect: the basis of standard Mandarin (普通话 pǔtōnghuà). Beijing speech is characterized by heavy retroflex consonants and the 儿化 (érhuà) suffix that makes 这里 → 这儿, 哪里 → 哪儿. A strong Beijing accent is audible and distinct.
北洋军	Běi yáng Jūn	Beiyang Army (Yuan's power base)
背	bèi	back; to carry on the back
背后	bèi hòu	behind someone's back; in secret — 背 bèi (back of the body; to turn one's back on) + 后 (behind). The space behind a person's back , the zone they cannot see. In figurative use, 背后 means to act covertly, without someone's knowledge or consent. 在背后说人坏话 (to speak ill of someone behind their back) is a strong social criticism.
被动	bèi dòng	passive
奔跑	bēn pǎo	to dash; to run hard; to gallop — 奔 (bēn, to rush; to gallop) + 跑. A more vivid and literary form than plain 跑. 奔 originally described the gallop of a horse; 奔跑 carries that sense of full-speed exertion.
本来	běn lái	originally; at first; (it goes without saying that) — 本 běn (root; origin) + 来 lái (come). What was the case from the start, before things changed: 我本来想去 "I originally meant to go (but didn't)." It often carries a quiet sense that the original state was the natural or proper one.
庇佑	bì yòu	divine protection and blessing
边关	biāng uān	border pass; frontier checkpoint — 边 (biān, border; edge) + 关 (guān, pass; strategic gate). The fortified checkpoints through the wall where military control, taxation, and passage were managed.
边远	biān yuǎn	remote and peripheral (border regions)
不道德	bù dào dé	immoral; unethical; morally wrong — 不 bù (negation) + 道德. The direct negation , applied to actions, behavior, people, and policies alike. Notably versatile in everyday condemnation: this is the word a person reaches for in an argument when something feels wrong without being illegal.
不来了	bù lái le	not coming anymore; decided not to come — 不…了 is the negation of sentence-final 了 , it signals a reversal of an expected or prior state. The 了 here paradoxically signals a new situation: the new situation is "not doing X anymore." Extremely common for announcing changed plans, habits that have been given up, or states that have ended.
不行	bù xíng	not OK; won't do; no good; (terminally) declining — 不 bù + 行 xíng. The everyday refusal: "no, that won't work." 行 on its own as an interjection means "OK, fine, that'll do" , and 不行 is its negation. Also extends to "not capable" (我数学不行 "I'm bad at math") and, in context of illness or business, "in serious decline" (他不行了 "he's failing / fading").
不知道	bù zhī dào	don't know; I'm not sure; I have no idea — 不 bù (negation) + 知道. One of the most common phrases in conversational Mandarin: the honest, unadorned acknowledgment of not having found the path. As a standalone response, 不知道！is perfectly natural and socially acceptable (not rude) in most contexts.
不足	bù zú	insufficient; not enough; shortcoming — 不 (not) + 足 (sufficient). The negation of sufficiency. 时间不足 (insufficient time); 不足为奇 (not enough to be surprising , meaning "nothing to be surprised about"); 美中不足 (a flaw in an otherwise perfect situation , literally "not enough in the beautiful center").
步行街	bù xíng jiē	pedestrian street
菜系	cài xì	cuisine school; culinary lineage — Cuisine system , the term for a recognized regional cooking tradition. The 八大菜系 (eight great cuisine systems) are the canonical eight, though culinary scholars recognize anywhere from four to dozens of regional traditions.
叉烧	chā shāo	Char Siu — Cantonese BBQ Pork

茶马古道	Chá Mǎ Gǔ Dào	Ancient Tea-Horse Road — the trade route that carried tea to Tibet

钞票	chāo piào	banknote: paper money; cash — 钞 chāo (paper money) + 票 piào (slip; note). A banknote, paper currency, cash in hand. This compound shows 票 at its purest: a printed slip of paper that stands for money. Closely related is 支票 zhīpiào (a cheque), where 支 zhī (to pay out) marks the slip as an instrument of payment.
超越	chāo yu è	to surpass; to exceed; to transcend — 超 (chāo, to overtake; to exceed) + 越. The figurative summit of the family: surpassing a competitor, exceeding a limit, transcending a category. Used in business, sport, philosophy, and self-development contexts.
朝代	cháo dài	dynasty: a reigning house and its era — 朝 cháo (court, reign) + 代 dài (generation, age). The standard word for a dynasty, the span during which one ruling house held the throne. Chinese history is told as a sequence of 朝代, each with its own name, capital, and character.
朝气	zhāo qì	youthful spirit: vigor, the lively air of a new day — 朝 zhāo (morning) + 气 qì (air, energy). The energy of morning seen in people, a fresh, lively spirit. Said of the young or of any group full of drive: 充满朝气 "brimming with vigor." Its opposite is 暮气 mùqì, the listlessness of dusk.
朝前	cháo qián	forward; toward the front; facing ahead — 朝 cháo (toward) + 前 qián (front). Directed at the space ahead: 朝前走 "walk forward," 朝前看 "look ahead." A close everyday counterpart to 向前.
朝圣	cháo shèng	pilgrimage
朝廷	cháo tíng	the imperial court: the ruler and the central government — 朝 cháo (court) + 廷 tíng (court hall). The ruler together with the ministers and offices around the throne, the seat of central power. To act 代表朝廷 is to act in the name of the throne itself.
朝夕	zhāo xī	morning and evening: every hour, day after day — 朝 zhāo (morning) + 夕 xī (evening). The two ends of the day standing for all of it: from dawn to dusk, every hour. 朝夕相处 "to be together morning and evening," to live in close daily company; 只争朝夕 "to seize every moment."
朝向	cháo xiàng	to face toward; the aspect or facing of (a building) — 朝 cháo (to face) + 向 xiàng (toward). To face a given way, or the facing itself. Common in describing buildings: 这套房子朝向南 "this flat faces south," a prized aspect for sunlight in Chinese homes.
朝阳	zhāo yáng	the morning sun: the rising sun, dawn light — 朝 zhāo (morning) + 阳 yáng (sun). The sun as it rises, and the fresh light it brings. Used figuratively for anything new and full of promise: 朝阳产业 "a sunrise industry," a sector on the rise.
朝着	cháo zhe	toward; in the direction of (with sustained motion) — 朝 cháo (toward) + 着 zhe (the durative marker). The preposition with a sense of ongoing facing or movement toward something: 朝着大海 "facing the sea," 朝着目标努力 "to work toward a goal." Common in figurative aims as well as physical ones.

车间	chē jiān	workshop, shop floor — Named by process: 冲压车间 stamping, 焊接车间 welding, 注塑车间 injection molding, 喷涂车间 painting, 组装车间 assembly, 包装车间 packing. 无尘车间 (wúchén chējiān) is a clean room.
车票	chē piào	travel ticket: a ticket for a bus, train, or coach — 车 chē (vehicle; car; train) + 票 piào (slip; ticket). A ticket for ground transport: a bus ticket, a train ticket, a coach ticket. The slip that proves you have paid your fare and may board. By the same pattern, 机票 jīpiào is a plane ticket and 船票 chuánpiào is a boat ticket.
车同轨	chē tóng guǐ	carts made to the same track — Qin standardized the gauge of cart axles so that all wheeled vehicles fit the same ruts. The empire-wide road system, including the 直道 (zhídào, "straight roads") radiating from the capital Xianyang, depended on this.
车站	chē zhàn	station; bus or train stop — 车 (chē, vehicle) + 站 (stop): the place where buses or trains halt and passengers board. The base word for any ground-transit station; specify with 火车站 huǒchēzhàn (train station) or 汽车站 qìchēzhàn (bus station).
成都	Chéng dū	Chengdu city
城堡	chéng bǎo	castle; fortress — 城 chéng (wall; fortified place) + 堡 bǎo (fort; stronghold). A castle or fortress, the walled stronghold of a lord. 城堡 is the standard translation for the European castle, and the word most readers meet first in fairy tales and fantasy.
城门	chéng mén	city gate — 城 chéng (city; wall) + 门 mén (gate; door). The controlled opening in the wall, the single point where the inside and outside of a city legally met. City gates were closed at night, manned by guards, and named individually; a walled capital might have a dozen, each a landmark in its own right.
城墙	chéng qiáng	city wall; rampart — 城 chéng (city; wall) + 墙 qiáng (wall). The original sense of 城 made explicit. Where 城 alone once meant the wall, modern Mandarin pairs it with 墙 to specify the physical structure as opposed to the city it encloses.
城区	chéng qū	urban district
城市	chéng shì	city — 城 chéng (walled settlement) + 市 shì (market). The most ordinary modern word for "city," and a fossil of how cities formed: a wall (城) for defense, a market (市) for exchange. The two together make the thing.
城镇	chéng zhèn	towns and cities; urban areas — 城 chéng (city) + 镇 zhèn (town). A collective term spanning the whole range of urban settlement, from large cities down to market towns, used especially in contrast to the countryside (农村 nóngcūn).
充足	chōng zú	ample; abundant; fully sufficient — 充 (chōng, to fill; to be full) + 足 (sufficient). Filled to sufficiency , not merely enough but abundantly enough. 阳光充足 (ample sunlight); 准备充足 (fully prepared); 充足的理由 (compelling reasons). Stronger than 足够 zúgòu (merely enough).
绸	chóu	silk fabric
出版	chū bǎn	to publish; to put out an edition — 出 chū (emerge; emit) + 版 bǎn (board; printing plate; edition). To emit a printing plate , to send a text out into the world as a published work. The metaphor is the woodblock or copper plate of traditional printing, the fixed form from which copies emerge.
出产	chū chǎn	to produce; to yield; output — 出 chū (emerge; put out) + 产 chǎn (produce; give birth to; output). To yield products from a source , a region produces goods, a field yields a crop, a factory puts out units. Used for natural resources, agricultural yield, and manufactured goods. As a noun: the output, the produce.
出发	chū fā	to set out; to depart; to start from (a position or principle) — 出 chū (exit; emerge from) + 发 fā (to send out; to dispatch; to start moving). The moment of departure , leaving a point of origin for a destination. Physical: a train, a traveler, a team setting out.
出口	chū kǒu	to export; an exit; to speak — 出 chū (exit; emerge) + 口 kǒu (mouth; opening; port). Three interlocking meanings from the same compound. As trade: to export , goods going out through the port. As architecture: the exit of a building or tunnel. As speech: 出口成章 (words emerge perfectly formed , to speak with eloquence).
出口退税	chū kǒu tuì shuì	export VAT rebate — The refund of input VAT on exported goods, claimed by whoever exports them. A factory with its own export licence (自营出口 zìyíng chūkǒu) claims it directly; one without exports through a trading company (外贸公司 wàimào gōngsī), which adds a layer of margin.
出来	chū lái	to come out (toward speaker)
出路	chū lù	a way out; prospects, future direction — 出 chū (to go out, to exit) + 路 lù (road). Literally "the road out." Used for both physical exit (the way out of a building) and, more often, figurative prospects: career options, an escape from a bad situation, the future of an industry. 没有出路 méiyǒu chūlù = no way forward, no prospects.
出名	chū míng	to become famous; to make a name for oneself — 出 chū (emerge) + 名 míng (name; fame; reputation). To emerge into collective naming , to become the person or thing that others talk about and recognize by name. The 出 marks the transition: previously unknown, now known. 出名 carries no inherent judgment of whether the fame is deserved or welcome.
出去	chū qù	to go out; to head outside — 出 chū (to exit; to emerge) + 去 qù (away from here). To exit and move away from the speaker's reference point. Contrast: 出来 chūlái (to come out , exiting toward where the speaker is). 出去 is used by someone inside sending someone outside, or by someone outside talking about leaving an enclosed space.
出色	chū sè	outstanding; excellent; remarkable — 出 chū (emerge; stand out) + 色 sè (color; appearance; quality). "Emerging in color" , a vivid presence that distinguishes itself against its background. The color metaphor is apt: something outstanding has a quality that registers immediately, the way a saturated color stands out on a pale surface.
出生	chū shēng	to be born; birth — 出 chū (emerge) + 生 shēng (life; to be born; raw). Emergence into life: the original threshold crossing. 出生 is used for persons: 他在北京出生 (he was born in Beijing). The date is 出生日期 chūshēng rìqī (date of birth).
出现	chū xiàn	to appear; to emerge; to come into view — 出 chū (emerge) + 现 xiàn (appear; manifest; current). To emerge into visibility , applicable to everything from a rainbow appearing after rain to a new problem appearing in a system. One of the most common verbs in news language and everyday description.
出众	chū zhòng	to stand out from the crowd; exceptional — 出 chū (emerge) + 众 zhòng (crowd; the many; multitude). To emerge from the mass of people , to be the one figure that rises above the surrounding level. Often used for talent, appearance, or personal quality that is genuinely rare rather than merely good.
川菜	chuān cài	Sichuan cuisine — 川 chuān (Sichuan) + 菜 cài (cuisine). The cooking of Sichuan, famous for the numbing-spicy 麻辣 málà of Sichuan peppercorn and chili. 麻婆豆腐 and 回锅肉 are classic 川菜 dishes.
穿过	chuāng uò	to pass through; to cross — 穿 (to pass through) + 过 (guò, across, a directional complement). The verb of moving through and out the other side, a crowd, a forest, a tunnel. 过 marks the full traverse, in one side and out the far side.
穿上	chuān shàng	to put on (and have it on) — 穿 (to thread on) + 上 (shàng, up/onto, here a result complement). The complement 上 marks the garment as now in place. 穿上 stresses the completed act of getting it on, against bare 穿 which can describe the habit of wearing.
穿梭	chuān suō	to shuttle back and forth
穿透	chuān tòu	to penetrate; to pierce through — 穿 (to bore through) + 透 (tòu, thoroughly/through). Two synonyms stacked for force, used for light, sound, water, or radiation breaking all the way through a barrier. The technical word in physics and medicine for penetration.
穿衣	chuān yī	to put on clothes; to dress — 穿 (to thread through) + 衣 (yī, clothing). A verb-object pair, the plainest way to say "get dressed." 衣 is the general word for what covers the upper body, so 穿衣 frames dressing as passing the body into a garment.
穿越	chuān yu è	to pass through; to cut across; (popular fiction) to time-travel — Cutting through a region. Also the standard verb for popular time-travel fiction (穿越剧, 穿越小说) where a modern character is thrown into a past dynasty.
穿着	chuān zhe	wearing; dressed in (current state) — 穿 (to put on clothing) + 着 = the dressed state is in force. This is among the most natural uses of durative 着, because clothing occupies a position continuously. The same pattern applies to 戴着 (wearing a hat/glasses), 拿着 (holding), 带着 (carrying/bringing).
船舶	chuán bó	shipping, vessels (formal)
船票	chuán piào	boat ticket, ferry ticket — 船 (boat) + 票 (ticket): the fare for a passage by water, parallel to 车票 (bus/train ticket) and 机票 (plane ticket).
船员	chuán yuán	crew member, sailor
船长	chuán zhǎng	ship's captain — 船 (boat) + 长 (zhǎng, chief): the head of the ship. The same 长 head-of suffix gives 校长 (principal) and 队长 (team leader).
船只	chuán zhī	vessels, shipping (collective) — 船 (boat) + 只, here a classifier turned collective suffix. The pair names boats as a category rather than one hull, the way 车辆 names vehicles in bulk. Standard in port notices, news, and maritime law.

"Coming from the past all the way here," from some starting point up to the present moment. Almost always paired with negation: 从来不 (never; has never) / 从来没有 (never; has never happened).
从前	cóng qián	once upon a time; formerly; in the old days — 从 cóng (from; following) + 前 qián (front; before). "From the front / from the time that was in front": an even more remote and literary sense of "formerly" than 以前.

打开	dǎ kāi	to open up (a closed object)

到达	dào dá	to arrive; to reach (a destination) — 到 dào (arrive) + 达 dá (reach; attain). Both components mean roughly the same thing, making 到达 more emphatic and formal than 到 alone. 到底	dào dǐ	after all; in the end; (interrogative intensifier) — 到 dào (arrive at) + 底 dǐ (bottom; base). Arriving at the bottom: getting to the root of the matter, reaching the real answer. 到来	dào lái	arrival; advent; to arrive (literary) — 到 dào (arrive) + 来 lái (come). 道路	dào lù	road; path; way — 道 dào (way; path) + 路 lù (road; route). The most standard, neutral word for a road or path. Almost always takes an adjectival or clausal complement.
感动	gǎn dòng	to be moved; to touch someone's heart — 感 gǎn + 动 dòng (to move). The heart set in motion by something beautiful, kind, or touching. One of the most emotionally central verbs in Chinese, high frequency in literature, film, and daily speech.

This is one of the most common patterns in spoken Mandarin for expressing impressions and evaluations.

Originally to link two waterways by a channel; now overwhelmingly used for communication between people: to open a channel of understanding so meaning can flow back and forth.

街角	jiē jiǎo	street corner — 街 jiē (street) + 角 jiǎo (corner). The physical corner where two streets meet. In narrative, the street corner is a classic site of waiting, meeting, and chance encounter.

街头	jiē tóu	street corner; on the streets — 街 (street) + 头 (head, end). The mouth of a street or the streetside in general. It frames public, open-air activity: 街头艺人 (jiētóu yìrén) a street performer, 街头篮球 (jiētóu lánqiú) streetball.

解开	jiě kāi	to untie; to undo; to unfasten — 解 jiě + 开 kāi (to open). The most literal 解-word: to untie a knot, undo a button, unfasten a rope. 借口	jiè kǒu	excuse, pretext — 借 (to borrow) + 口 (mouth). A "borrowed mouth," words you reach for that are not the true reason. As a noun it is the excuse itself; as a verb, "to use as a pretext."
近代	jìn dài	early modern era (1840–1949)
近来	jìn lái	recently; lately; in recent times — 近 jìn (near; close; recent) + 来 lái (coming; approaching the present). "What has come near," the recent past approaching the present. A slightly more formal or literary synonym of 最近 zuìjìn. Common in formal speech, writing, and news contexts.
近年来	jìn nián lái	in recent years; over recent years — 近 (recent) + 年 (year) + 来 (coming from , a directional particle marking elapsed duration). Time that has been coming toward the present moment across recent years. A standard phrase in news, academic writing, and formal speech: 近年来，中国的城市化速度加快 (in recent years, China's urbanization has accelerated).
近期	jìn qī	in the near future; recently; in the near term — 近 (near) + 期 qī (a period of time; a term; a scheduled point). A time period that is near , used for both the recent past and the near future depending on context. 近期内 (within the near term); 近期计划 (near-term plans). The period implied is typically weeks to a few months, not years.
近视	jìn shì	nearsighted; myopic — 近 (near) + 视 shì (to see; vision). Seeing only what is near , the clinical and colloquial term for myopia. 近视眼镜 (myopia glasses , corrective lenses); 高度近视 (high myopia, severe nearsightedness).
进步	jìn bù	progress; to improve; progressive — 进 jìn (to advance) + 步 bù (a step). To move forward step by step, hence to improve. Works as a verb (你进步了 "you've improved") and as a noun (很大的进步 "great progress").
进城	jìn chéng	to go into town; to go to the city — 进 jìn (to enter) + 城 chéng (city; town). A verb-object phrase: to go into the city. The expression preserves the old sense of the city as a bounded place one literally enters, crossing from outside to inside.
进口	jìn kǒu	to import; imports; entrance (of a place) — 进 jìn (to take in) + 口 kǒu (mouth; opening). To bring goods in across a border, the opposite of 出口 chūkǒu (to export). The same word also names a physical "entrance," the opening you go in through.
进来	jìn lái	to come in (toward the speaker) — 进 jìn (to enter) + 来 lái (to come). To enter moving toward where the speaker is. 请进来 "please come in" is what you say from inside a room to someone at the door.
进去	jìn qù	to go in (away from the speaker) — 进 jìn (to enter) + 去 qù (to go). To enter moving away from where the speaker stands. From outside a building you tell someone 你先进去 "you go in first."
进入	jìn rù	to enter; to go into (a place or stage) — 进 jìn (to enter) + 入 rù (to go in). A more formal "to enter," used of physical spaces and of abstract stages alike: 进入房间 "enter the room," 进入新阶段 "enter a new phase."
进香	jìn xiāng	to make a pilgrimage offering incense
进行	jìn xíng	to carry out; to be in progress; to conduct — 进 jìn (to advance; to enter) + 行 xíng. The all-purpose formal verb for "to carry out" , used before two-syllable activity nouns: 进行讨论 "carry out a discussion," 进行调查 "conduct an investigation," 进行比赛 "hold a competition." Functions like a grammaticalized "do" for activities that are too dignified for…
进展	jìn zhǎn	to make progress; headway; development — 进 jìn (to advance) + 展 zhǎn (to unfold). The headway a task, plan, or situation makes over time: 工作有了进展 "the work has made progress." More about an undertaking than a person.

The verb preserves the original image of legs spread on either side. The poetic phrase 跨上战马 kuà shàng zhànmǎ "to mount the war-horse" recurs throughout classical narrative and modern wuxia.
跨文化	kuà wén huà	cross-cultural; intercultural — 跨 kuà (to straddle, to cross over) + 文化 (culture). Cross-cultural: spanning or bridging different cultural contexts. 跨文化交际 kuà wénhuà jiāojì (intercultural communication) is an academic field. 跨文化婚姻 (cross-cultural marriage) appears in demographic and social discussions.
跨学科	kuà xu é kē	interdisciplinary — 跨 + 学科 (xuékē, academic discipline). The standard rendering of "interdisciplinary" in Chinese academic writing. 跨学科研究 (kuà xuékē yánjiū) "interdisciplinary research" is now a frequent funding category.
跨越式	kuà yu è shì	leap-forward style; "leap-across" type (modifier) — The modifier form, suffixing 式 (shì, type/style) to make an adjective. Most common in 跨越式发展 (kuàyuè shì fāzhǎn, "leap-forward development"), the standard term in Chinese policy and journalism for skipping intermediate stages of economic or technological growth.

快活	kuài huó	carefree; cheerful; lively and at ease — 快 kuài (unobstructed) + 活 huó (alive; living; moving freely). The image is life flowing without constraint. Carries a slightly old-fashioned or literary flavor in modern Mandarin, but remains warm and vivid in fiction and colloquial northern speech. Describes people who seem naturally unburdened.
来不及	lái bu jí	there's not enough time; too late to do something — 来 lái (to come; to reach) + 不 bù (not) + 及 jí (to reach; to catch up to). The time has not come far enough to reach this action, so there is not enough time. Opposite: 来得及 lái de jí (there is still time; still possible).
来了	lái le	(he/she/it) has come; has arrived; here it is — 来 lái (to come) + 了 le (completion/change-of-state particle). The arrival has been completed: the thing or person is now here. One of the most basic and high-frequency phrases in Mandarin.

来世	lái shì	the next life; the afterlife; the life to come — 来 lái (coming) + 世 shì (life). The life that follows this one in the Buddhist cycle of rebirth: 来世再相见 "to meet again in the next life." Paired with 前世 (a former life) and 今世 (this life) to span the chain of rebirths.
来源	lái yuán	source; origin; where something comes from — 来 lái (to come) + 源 yuán (source; origin; spring, where water comes from). "The place from which it comes." Used for the source of funding (资金来源), information (信息来源), authority (权力来源), and anything whose origin needs to be named.
来自	lái zì	to come from; to originate from; hailing from — 来 lái (to come; to arrive) + 自 zì (from; self; origin). The standard formal expression for geographic or conceptual origin. Used for people (来自北京), ideas (这个想法来自实验), data (数据来自2023年), and institutions (来自联合国的报告).
乐山乐水	yào shān yào shuǐ	to take joy in mountains and water; to be a person of refined nature — A condensed echo of the Analects line. Note the alternative reading: in this fixed phrase 乐 is sometimes read yào in the classical pronunciation (the same character as 乐 lè / yuè, with multiple historical readings , yào here marks affective preference).
离 + 时间点 + 还有	lí + ti me + hái yǒu	until (a point in time) — 离 measures distance in time as well as space. Naming a future point, then 还有 ("there still is") plus a span, counts down to it: 离新年还有两个星期 ("there are still two weeks until New Year"), 离上课还有五分钟 ("five minutes until class"). It is the natural way to say "how long until" something.
离别	lí bié	to part, to take leave (of people) — 离 (to part) plus 别 (bié, to part, to bid farewell). A literary word for the parting of people, weighted with feeling. It carries the ache of long goodbyes.

离开	lí kāi	to leave, to depart from — 离 (to part) plus 开 (kāi, to open, to move away). The most common verb for leaving a place or a person. It takes a direct object: you leave something.
离去	lí qù	to leave; to depart (with finality) — 离 lí (to separate from; to leave; distance) + 去 qù (to go away). To separate and go , departure with a sense of severance. More emphatic and often more final than 走 alone. Used for leaving a job, a relationship, a city, or a life. 她悄悄地离去了 (She quietly departed) , the 去 carries the irreversibility.
离职	lí zhí	to resign, to leave a post — 离 (to leave) plus 职 (zhí, post, office). The formal verb for quitting a job or stepping down from a position.
礼物	lǐ wù	gift; present — 礼 lǐ (ritual offering) + 物 wù (thing; object; matter). Lit. "a ritual-offering made material." A gift in Chinese culture is always, at some level, a ritual object that carries social meaning exceeding its material value.
里程	lǐ chéng	mileage, distance covered
里面	lǐ miàn	inside; interior; within — 里 (inside) + 面 (face, surface, side). The most direct spatial interior term in Mandarin. Used for concrete physical interiors (inside a box, inside a building) and abstract interiors (inside a system, inside a relationship).

The person chosen is the one who would be most expected to know or be able to do the thing , making their failure maximal evidence for the claim.

In her spirit-state, she crossed the sea, seized her father's boat with one hand and her brother's boat with the other, guiding them through a terrible storm.
零下	líng xià	below zero; sub-zero (temperature) — 零 líng (zero) + 下 xià (below). Below zero degrees, the standard way to state freezing temperatures: 零下十度 (minus ten degrees). Its counterpart 零上 (above zero) is less often said, since positive temperatures are usually given as a bare number.
另外	lìng wài	"Besides" or "in addition — "Besides" or "in addition." Marks a related but separate point. Useful when you want to add information that is not strictly part of the same argument: 另外, 我想问你一件事 ("also, I want to ask you something").
流行	liú xíng	popular; trending; to spread (of fashion, illness, songs) — 流 liú (to flow) + 行 xíng. Flowing-and-traveling. The verb for something that spreads through a population , fashion, slang, popular songs, viruses. 流行音乐 = pop music; 流行病 = an epidemic; 这种说法很流行 = "this expression is very widespread."
六道	liù dào	the six realms of rebirth — 六 (six) + 道 (path; way; realm). The six realms through which beings cycle in Buddhist cosmology: gods, asuras, humans, animals, hungry ghosts, and hell beings. The human realm (人道 rén dào or 人间道 rénjiān dào) occupies the middle tier. Beings move between realms through karma generated in each life.
六道轮回	liù dào lún huí	the six-realm cycle of rebirth; the full Buddhist cosmological cycle — The full phrase naming the complete cycle: 六道 (six realms) + 轮回 (the turning cycle). Used when the complete cosmological picture is being invoked rather than just the concept of rebirth in general.
龙场悟道	Lóng chǎng wù dào	the Longchang Enlightenment — The awakening Wang Yangming experienced in 1508 while exiled to Longchang in Guizhou province. In the middle of the night, he grasped that the principles he had been seeking in external objects were already present in the mind itself , the insight that became the foundation of 心学.
龙脉	lóng mài	dragon vein; underground energy channel — 龙 lóng + 脉 mài (vein; pulse; channel). In feng shui 风水 theory, mountains are the visible backs of buried dragons; the ridgelines and peaks are the spine of a living creature moving through the earth. The 龙脉 are the underground channels through which this vital energy flows.
楼上	lóu shàng	upstairs; the floor above — 楼 lóu (building; storied structure; floor) + 上 shàng (above; up). The floor above; upstairs. 楼下 is the natural pair. These two form one of the most common directional pairs in everyday speech, used for apartments, offices, restaurants, and any multi-story space.

旅行	lǚ xíng	to travel; travel, a trip — 旅 lǚ (to journey; a traveler) + 行 xíng. The standard word for travel as recreation or as an extended trip. Distinguished from 出差 chūchāi (business travel) and 旅游 lǚyóu (tourism, sightseeing). 一次愉快的旅行 "a pleasant trip."

麻辣	má là	numbing-spicy — Numbing-spicy , the characteristic flavor combination of Sichuan cuisine, combining the numbing sensation (麻 má) of Sichuan pepper (花椒 huājiāo) with the heat (辣 là) of chili pepper. The geographic signature of the Sichuan basin's isolated, humid interior.

马上	mǎ shàng	immediately; right away; at once — 马 mǎ (horse) + 上 shàng (on top of; mounted on). Literally "on horseback," from the military image of an order being carried out while still mounted, without dismounting. One of the most common adverbs in spoken Mandarin.

满足	mǎn zú	satisfied; to satisfy; contentment — 满 (mǎn, full; filled to capacity) + 足 (sufficient). Completely sufficient: fulfillment, contentment, the state of having enough and knowing it. Both a verb (满足需求, to satisfy a need) and an adjective (感到很满足, to feel deeply satisfied).

慢走	màn zǒu	walk slowly — polite farewell to a departing guest — 慢 màn (slow) + 走 zǒu (to walk; to leave). Literally "walk slowly" , said by a host as a guest departs, equivalent to "take care" or "safe travels." The Chinese farewell phrase, so common it has become almost automatic. Originally a TCM and qigong term for the field of qi that radiates from a practitioner. Absorbed into popular culture as a way of describing the palpable aura of a charismatic person. 气场强 = powerful aura; 气场弱 = weak presence.
千里	qiān lǐ	a thousand li; a great distance — 千 qiān + 里 lǐ (the Chinese mile). Literally a thousand li, but in practice any great distance. 千里迢迢 qiānlǐ tiáotiáo describes a long, arduous journey from far away.
千里迢迢	qiān lǐ tiáo tiáo	from a thousand li away; across a great distance — 千里 (a thousand li) + 迢迢 (tiáotiáo, remote, far off , a reduplication conveying long distance and weariness). The compound evokes a journey undertaken across great distance with effort and determination.
千里缘	qiān lǐ yuán	a connection across a thousand miles; long-distance destined bond — 千里 qiānlǐ = a thousand li (the traditional Chinese unit of distance; figuratively: any great distance). The phrase evokes connections that transcend physical separation , the meeting of people from far apart who nonetheless find each other.
千里之行	qiān lǐ zhī xíng	a journey of a thousand li
前辈	qián bèi	predecessor; senior; elder in one's field — 前 qián (before; preceding) + 辈 bèi (generation; people of the same era). Those of earlier generations who walked the path before you in your field, institution, or family. A term of respect: addressing someone as 前辈 acknowledges their experience and positions you as the newer arrival.
前朝	qián cháo	former dynasty; previous ruling house — 前 qián (former; preceding) + 朝 cháo (dynasty; court; morning). The dynasty that came before the current one , a term of historical reference used constantly in Chinese historical writing, since each dynasty defined itself partly in relation to what it replaced.
前方	qián fāng	ahead; the forward direction; the front (military) — 前 qián (front) + 方 fāng (direction; area; square). A more formal and often grander term than 前面 , used in military contexts, official broadcasts, and literary or elevated speech. 前方记者 (correspondent at the front) is a journalism term.
前后	qián hòu	front-back; before and after
前进	qián jìn	to advance; to go forward; to march ahead — 前 qián (front; forward) + 进 jìn (to advance). To move ahead in space or in cause: 向前进 "advance forward," 勇往直前 the spirit of pressing on. Its opposite is 后退 hòutuì, to fall back.
前面	qián miàn	in front; ahead; the front side — 前 qián (front) + 面 miàn (face; surface; side). The most common everyday expression for spatial "in front." Works for physical space (前面有个红绿灯 , there is a traffic light ahead) and for sequential position (前面的章节 , the preceding chapter).
前排	qián pái	front row — 前 qián (front) + 排 pái (row; line; rank). The front row of a theater, cinema, classroom, or any seated arrangement. Also used figuratively for first-rank prominence: 前排位置 (front-row position) implies visibility and status in any field.
前任	qián rèn	former occupant of a role; the "ex" (ex-partner, ex-boss) — 前 qián (former; preceding) + 任 rèn (appointment; term of office; to hold a post). The person who formerly held a given role or position.
前世	qián shì	a former life
前世今生	qián shì jīn shēng	past life and present life; across lifetimes — 前世 qiánshì (previous lifetime; past existence) + 今生 jīnshēng (this present life). A phrase that spans the axis of rebirth, linking the karmic conditions of a previous life to the circumstances of the current one.
前天	qián tiān	the day before yesterday — 前 qián (front; before) + 天 tiān (day; sky). The day that stands two steps "in front of" today.

亲近	qīn jìn	close and dear; intimate; to feel close to — 亲 qīn (family; intimate; close , the same phonetic component that appears in 新 as structural filler, but here fully semantic: kin, dear ones) + 近 (near). Closeness that is also warmth , proximity that has become affection.

清淡	qīng dàn	Clear and light , the flavor aesthetic of coastal and delta cuisines, particularly Cantonese. Emphasizes the natural flavors of fresh, high-quality ingredients with minimal masking by heavy seasoning. The direct expression of a geography where fresh seafood and produce are excellent and abundant.

This is Chinese's primary relative clause mechanism , there are no separate relative pronouns. The entire clause precedes 的 which directly precedes the noun being modified.
去除	qù chú	to remove; to eliminate; to get rid of — 去 qù (to send away; to remove) + 除 chú (to eliminate; to remove; to divide out). To cause something to go away , to strip out an unwanted element. Used in formal and technical contexts: 去除污染 (remove contamination), 去除杂质 (remove impurities), 去除顾虑 (dispel concerns).
去掉	qù diào	to get rid of; to remove; to drop (something) — 去 qù (to cause to go) + 掉 diào (resultative complement: dropped away; gone). A colloquial and direct expression for removal. 掉 as a complement emphasizes that the thing has been fully removed and is gone. More informal than 去除; appropriate for everyday speech. 把它去掉！= Get rid of it!
去年	qùn ián	last year — 去 qù (to have gone; to have departed) + 年 nián (year). The year that has departed , last year. A vivid temporal use: the year that went away. Contrast: 来年 láinián (the year that is coming , next year; somewhat literary) and 今年 jīnnián (this year).
去声	qù shēng	the departing tone; tone 4
去世	qù shì	to pass away; to die (respectful) — 去 qù (to depart; to leave) + 世 shì (the world; this age; this life). To depart from the world , the standard respectful expression for the death of an adult. Not used for children or animals, and not used for enemies or condemned criminals (for those, more neutral or harsher terms apply).
确认一下	qu èr èn yí xià	let me confirm — 确认 (quèrèn) is the verb of the whole relationship: samples are confirmed, drawings are confirmed, orders are confirmed. 我确认一下 before restating a point; 您确认一下 when handing the other side something to check.
绕境	rào jìng	divine procession through the territory
人道	rén dào	the human realm; the human path of rebirth — The human realm is considered the most favorable position for Buddhist practice precisely because of its mixture of suffering and capacity. Humans experience enough pain and impermanence to feel the urgency of liberation, while retaining enough cognitive clarity and agency to pursue the path.
人海	rén hǎi	a sea of people; a vast crowd — 人 rén (person) + 海 hǎi (sea). A crowd so large it reads as an ocean of heads. Famous in the phrase 茫茫人海 "the boundless sea of humanity," the throng in which two people might never meet.
人口	rén kǒu	population — lit. "people-mouths" — 人 rén (person) + 口 kǒu (mouth). Population , the number of mouths to feed, the count of human presences. 中国人口约14亿 "China's population is approximately 1.4 billion." 人口众多 rénkǒu zhòngduō = "large population" (a standard description of China).
认真起来	rèn zhēn qǐ lái	to get serious about it; to start taking it seriously
入	rù	to enter; to go in
赛跑	sài pǎo	to race; foot race — 赛 (sài, to compete) + 跑. The verb-noun for a competitive run. 百米赛跑 (bǎi mǐ sàipǎo, hundred-meter dash) and 长跑赛 (chángpǎo sài, long-distance race) are the standard sports terms.
三才	sān cái	the Three Powers
三星堆	Sān xīng duī	Sanxingdui bronze site — Sanxingdui , "Three Star Mounds," the Bronze Age archaeological site near Guanghan. The bronze masks, standing figure, and sacred tree uncovered there belong to a culture with no connection to the contemporaneous Shang dynasty. 堆 means "mound" or "pile."
僧璨 · 道信 · 弘忍	Sēng càn Dào xìn Hóng rěn	Third, fourth, and fifth patriarchs — 僧璨 Sengcan (d. 606 CE), the third patriarch, is remembered for the 信心铭 Xìnxīn Míng (Verses on the Faith-Mind), a verse text expressing the Chan teaching that discriminating mind (the tendency to prefer and reject) is the root of suffering.
山峰	shān fēng	mountain peak; summit — 山 shān + 峰 fēng (peak , itself contains 山). The redundancy is expressive emphasis. Used literally (climbing a peak) and figuratively (事业的山峰 "the peak of one's career").
山海关	Shān hǎi guān	Shanhai Pass — eastern terminus of the Ming wall
山河	shān hé	mountains and rivers; the land
山脚	shān jiǎo	the foot of a mountain — 山 shān (mountain) + 脚. The base of a mountain , where the slope meets the plain, where villages and trailheads sit. Standard geographical vocabulary.
山路	shān lù	mountain path
山脉	shān mài	mountain range — 山 shān + 脉 mài (vein; pulse; connected system, the word for pulse in traditional Chinese medicine). A mountain range as a vein of the earth, all peaks connected underground. Same logic as 水脉 (water vein) and 血脉 (bloodline). 秦岭山脉 the Qinling Mountains.
山神	shān shén	mountain deity
山水风光	shān shuǐ fēng guāng	landscape scenery; mountain-and-water vistas — 山水 + 风光 (fēngguāng, scenery; views). The standard tourism and travel-writing phrase. 桂林山水甲天下 (Guìlín shānshuǐ jiǎ tiānxià, "the landscape of Guilin is first under heaven") is one of the most-quoted lines in Chinese tourism , a fixed praise-formula, originally a Song-dynasty poem.
山水诗	shān shuǐ shī	landscape poetry; the mountain-and-water poem genre — 山水 + 诗 (shī, poem). The poetic genre that parallels the painting genre. Founded in the Six Dynasties period by Xie Lingyun (谢灵运, 385–433) and brought to its high point in the Tang by Wang Wei (王维) and Meng Haoran (孟浩然).
山水之间	shān shuǐ zhī jiān	amid mountains and waters; in the landscape — A literary phrase widely used in modern essays, song lyrics, and travel writing. 之间 zhī jiān = between; amid. The phrase carries the Daoist resonance of being in the landscape rather than looking at it. Common in tea-house names, hotel names, and book titles aimed at evoking refinement.
商场是个江湖	shāng chǎng shì gè jiāng hú	the marketplace is a rivers-and-lakes world — X是个江湖 as a productive formula — The formula X是个江湖 applies 江湖 logic , its hidden codes, dangers, unwritten rules, and webs of obligation , to any competitive domain.
商队	shāng duì	merchant caravan
商量一下	shāng liang yí xià	to talk it over — The deferral. Buys time without saying no, and is the expected answer to any significant new proposal. Name when you will reply so it does not read as a brush-off.
商业街	shāng yè jiē	shopping street
上班	shàng bān	to go to work; to be at work; on duty — 上 shàng (to mount; to attend) + 班 bān (shift; work roster; class). To show up for and engage in one's work shift. The most common word for "going to work" in daily speech. Contrast 下班 (to finish work; to get off work): the 上/下 pair governs the entire working day.
上船	shàng chuán	to board a boat — 上 (to go up onto) + 船 (boat): you step up onto a deck, so boarding is 上, and disembarking is 下船 (xià chuán). The same up/down logic governs 上车 / 下车 for vehicles.
上当	shàng dàng	to be fooled; to be taken in; to fall for a trick — 上 shàng (to go up to) + 当 dàng (the pawnshop). From the image of being shortchanged at the pawnbroker's counter, it now means to be cheated or deceived. 上当受骗 shàngdàng shòupiàn is the common four-character pairing.
上帝	shàng dì	God; the Lord on high
上坟	shàng fén	to visit and offer at the grave
上海	Shàng hǎi	Shanghai (city); literally upon the sea — 上 shàng (upon; on) + 海 hǎi (sea). China's largest city and busiest port, at the mouth of the Yangtze. The name reads "on the sea," fitting for a town that grew from fishing village to global harbor.
上海话	Shàng hǎi huà	Shanghainese — Shanghainese: the Wu Chinese topolect spoken in Shanghai, mutually unintelligible with Mandarin. A marker of local identity increasingly in tension with the spread of Putonghua as the dominant urban language for the younger generation.
上火	shàng huǒ	to get internal heat; to be inflamed; to get irritable — 上 shàng (to rise; to ascend) + 火 huǒ (fire). A TCM concept: excess heat (fire) in the body rising upward, causing sore throat, mouth ulcers, red eyes, or irritability. Used medically and colloquially. 吃辣的容易上火 "Eating spicy food easily causes 上火."
上进	shàng jìn	motivated; ambitious; striving to improve — 上 shàng (upward; advancing) + 进 jìn (to advance; to enter). Moving upward and forward. 上进心 shàngjìnxīn (the drive to improve; ambition) is one of the most positively valenced personal traits in Chinese educational culture: a quality parents want to see in children and teachers in students.
上来	shàng lái	come up; manage to (ability)
上流社会	shàng liú shè huì	high society; the upper class; the social elite — 上流 (shàngliú, upper stream; the upper reaches of a river) + 社会. High society, the social elite. The metaphor is of the upper current of a river: those who move at the top of the social flow. Used in both admiring and ironic registers.
上面	shàng miàn	above; on top; the upper surface — 上 shàng (above; up) + 面 miàn (face; side; surface). The upper side of something; the surface on top; the direction above. In spatial description, 上面 is the default way to say "on top of" or "above." In discourse, 上面 can also mean "what was mentioned above" or "the higher authorities."
上去	shàng qù	go up; from outside appearance
上山下乡	shàng shān xià xiāng	up to mountains, down to countryside (policy)
上天	shàng tiān	heaven above; God/fate; to ascend into the sky — 上 shàng (above; to ascend) + 天 tiān (heaven). As a noun: the heavens conceived as a moral agent, equivalent to "God" or "fate" in many colloquial contexts. As a verb: to ascend to the sky (a rocket launching; a person dying).
上网	shàng wǎng	to go online; to use the internet — 上 shàng (to mount; to get onto) + 网 wǎng (net; network; the internet). To mount the internet. The spatial metaphor is the same as getting on a vehicle or into an institution: you step up onto a platform. Formed in the early 1990s as Chinese internet usage began, and immediately adopted the 上 frame.
上下	shàng xià	up and down; approximately; around (a number) — 上 shàng (above) + 下 xià (below). The full vertical range , top to bottom , which collapses into an estimate of the middle. When placed after a number, it means "approximately" or "give or take." 三十上下 = around thirty.
上学	shàng xu é	to go to school; to attend school — 上 shàng (to attend; to mount into) + 学 xué (learning; school). To attend school, covering both the daily action (going to school today) and the general state (being a school student). Contrast: 上大学 (to attend university), 上幼儿园 (to go to preschool).
上瘾	shàng yǐn	to become addicted; to get hooked — 上 shàng (to mount into; to be caught by) + 瘾 yǐn (addiction; craving, a disease radical character). To have mounted into the craving, to be in its grip. Works for any powerful habit or compulsion: 游戏上瘾 (game addiction), 咖啡上瘾 (caffeine dependency), 手机上瘾 (phone addiction).
上座	shàng zuò	The seat of honor , the seat facing the door, assigned to the most honored guest. The term literally means "upper seat." Understanding the seating logic around 上座 is a practical necessity at any formal Chinese meal. Equivalent: 主位 zhǔwèi.
烧鸭	shāo yā	Roast Duck
深远	shēn yuǎn	profound and far-reaching (of influence or impact) — 深 shēn (deep) + 远 yuǎn (far). The compound stacks vertical depth with horizontal reach. Reserved for influence that is both substantial and long-lasting: 深远影响 shēnyuǎn yǐngxiǎng (profound, far-reaching influence) is a fixed collocation that appears in historical analysis and political commentary.
什么东西	shén me dōng xi	what (thing); what on earth; exclamation of disgust — 什么东西 has two registers. As a genuine question, it means "what is this thing?" As an exclamation, it expresses shock, disgust, or contempt , "what the heck is this?" or simply "ugh!" The exclamatory reading is a complete utterance on its own: 什么东西！registers revulsion at something offensive, absurd, o…
神诞	shén dàn	deity's birthday

生活	shēng huó	life; to live; daily living — 生 shēng (to be born, to grow) + 活 huó (to live). The pairing of two life-words covers both the verb (to live, to make a life) and the noun (life as it is lived day to day). This is the everyday word for "life," distinct from 命 mìng, the life that can be lost.
生机	shēng jī	vitality, life-force
声调	shēng diào	tone (in a tonal language) — 声 shēng (sound, voice) + 调 diào (pitch pattern). The linguistic tone: the rise and fall of pitch that distinguishes meaning in Mandarin. 第一声 (first tone), 四个声调 (the four tones). Every syllable on this very page carries a 声调.
失去	shī qù	to lose; to be deprived of — 失 shī (to lose; to miss; to fail) + 去 qù (to go away). Something that was yours has gone away , loss. 失去 covers losing people (失去亲人 , lose a loved one), opportunities (失去机会), abilities (失去记忆 , lose one's memory), and qualities (失去耐心 , lose patience). 十字路口	shí zì lù kǒu	crossroads; intersection (lit. "ten-character road opening") — 十字 shízì (cross shape) + 路 lù (road) + 口 kǒu (opening; mouth). The point where two roads cross to form the shape of 十: a four-way intersection. Widely used both literally (traffic intersections) and metaphorically (a crossroads in life, a moment of decision between multiple paths).
时机	shí jī	the right moment; timing; an opportune occasion — 时 shí (time) + 机 jī (machine; pivot; crucial moment; opportunity). 时机 is the Confucian classical meaning of 时 made into a compound: the moment that is ripe, when conditions align. 实际上	shí jì shàng	in actual fact; in reality

市场经济	shì chǎng jīng jì	market economy — what China was moving toward — 市场 (market, literally marketplace) + 经济 (economy). The conceptual opposite of 计划经济 jìhuà jīngjì (planned economy, the command economy the PRC had used since 1949).

事上磨炼	shì shàng mó liàn	tempering oneself through practical affairs — Wang Yangming's insistence that moral cultivation is not a retreat into contemplation but a process that occurs within engagement with actual affairs. 事实上	shì shí shàng	as a matter of fact

收千里眼顺风耳	shōu Qiān lǐ yǎn Shùn fēng ěr	subjugating the two divine generals — Two demonic generals, 千里眼 Qiānlǐyǎn (Eyes That See a Thousand Li, a green-faced demon) and 顺风耳 Shùnfēng'ěr (Ears That Hear Downwind, a red-faced demon), terrorized the coastal people. The harder and longer task: not the dramatic moment of conquest but the slow, unglamorous work of governing, maintaining order, and preventing the realm from unraveling.

针线	zhēn xiàn	needle and thread
真理	zhēn lǐ	truth (philosophical, capital-T) — 真 zhēn + 理 lǐ (principle; pattern; reason). The truth in the philosophical, abstract sense , the deep principle that governs, the ultimate reality. The word used in religious, scientific, and political discourse for capital-T Truth.
镇	zhèn	town
正体字	zhèng tǐ zì	Traditional characters — Traditional characters: the term used in Taiwan for the traditional character system that Taiwan retained when the PRC introduced simplified characters in the 1950s. Taiwan uses 正體字 (traditional/correct form characters); PRC uses 简体字 (simplified characters).
之后	zhī hòu	after; following
之前	zhī qián	before
知道了	zhī dào le	got it; understood; okay — The everyday acknowledgement that you have received and registered information or an instruction. Extremely common as a one-word reply. Beware the tone: a flat or clipped 知道了 can read as impatient, like "yeah, I know already." 好的 (okay) or 明白了 (understood) are gentler alternatives.
执行	zhí xíng	to execute; to enforce; to carry out (an order, a plan, a sentence) — 执 zhí (to grasp; to hold) + 行 xíng. To grasp-and-act. The formal verb for executing , orders, contracts, court rulings, software programs. 执行命令 "carry out an order"; 执行任务 "carry out a mission"; 执行总裁 (CEO) , literally "executing chief manager."
直接	zhí jiē	direct, directly — 直 (zhí, straight) + 接 (join). The two ends meet with nothing in between, a straight join. As an adverb it means doing a thing with no go-between, no detour.
职业	zhí yè	occupation; profession; vocational — 职 zhí (post; duty) + 业 yè (work). The line of work a person practices for a living: 职业选择 "choice of profession." As a modifier it means professional or vocational: 职业运动员 "professional athlete," 职业学校 "vocational school."
职业道德	zhí yè dào dé	professional ethics; occupational morality; the moral standards specific to a role — 职业 zhíyè (occupation; profession; vocation) + 道德. The morality that attaches specifically to a role , what it means to be a good doctor, journalist, teacher, or official, as distinct from merely following the general rules of society.
指明方向	zhǐ míng fāng xiàng	to point out the direction; to provide guidance — 指 (zhǐ, to point) + 明 (míng, clear) + 方向. The fixed phrase for guidance from a teacher, leader, or authority. Common in educational and political registers , a teacher 为学生指明方向, a leader 为国家指明方向. Carries a slightly elevated, often exhortatory tone.
指南针	zhǐn án zhēn	compass (the navigational instrument) — 指 (to point) + 南 (south) + 针 (needle). A south-pointing needle: the Chinese compass was designed to point south rather than north, in keeping with the cosmological primacy of the south.
至	zhì	to arrive (classical)
志向	zhì xiàng	aspiration; ambition; the goal one is set on — 志 zhì (will; resolve) + 向 xiàng (direction). The direction of one's will, what one aspires to: 远大的志向 "lofty aspirations." The facing of ambition, where a person has set their life.
智者乐水，仁者乐山	zhì zhě lè shuǐ , rén zhě lè shān	the wise take joy in water; the benevolent take joy in mountains — Lúnyǔ 6.23. The locus classicus of the 山水 dyad. 乐 is read lè (to take joy in) here , the older causative/affective reading. The couplet pairs water with 智 (wisdom , adaptive, flowing, responsive) and mountains with 仁 (benevolence , enduring, supporting, still).

中原	Zhōng yuán	Central Plains; the heartland — The Central Plain , the heartland of the Yellow River basin, corresponding broadly to modern Henan province and adjacent areas. To "take the Central Plain" (逐鹿中原 zhú lù Zhōngyuán, "chase the deer across the Central Plain") is a classical idiom for competing for control of China.
终点站	zhōng diǎn zhàn	terminus; last stop — 终点 (zhōngdiǎn, end point) + 站 (stop): the final stop on a route, where the line ends and everyone gets off. Its opposite is 起点站 qǐdiǎnzhàn, the first stop.
舟	zhōu	boat (literary)
主动	zhǔ dòng	to take the initiative; proactive; of one's own accord — 主 zhǔ (master; to take charge) + 动 dòng (to act). To move first and on one's own, rather than waiting to be pushed. Its opposite is 被动 bèidòng (passive). 主动帮忙 "to help without being asked."
转换	zhuǎn huàn	to switch, convert
转经筒	zhuǎn jīng tǒng	Prayer wheel — Prayer wheel: a cylindrical object containing mantras, rotated clockwise by practitioners. Each rotation is considered equivalent to reciting the mantra contained within.
转口港	zhuǎn kǒu gǎng	Entrepôt , a port where goods are imported, stored, and re-exported without significant processing. Hong Kong's entire colonial economic logic was entrepôt trade, and it retains this function today as a major transshipment hub alongside its role as a financial center.
桌上有书	zhuō shàng yǒu shū	The existential pattern: place + 有 + thing — The canonical existential 有 structure. Place word or place phrase comes first, followed by 有, followed by the indefinite thing being asserted to exist. The place functions as the topic , the frame within which existence is being asserted.
自动	zì dòng	automatic; automatically; of one's own accord — 自 zì (self) + 动 dòng (to move). Moving by itself, without anyone working it: 自动门 "automatic door," 自动售货机 "vending machine." Also of people acting unprompted: 自动报名 "to sign up on one's own."
自行车被偷了	zì xíng chē bèi tōu le	the bicycle was stolen — The most prototypical 被 sentence. Theft is unwanted, the subject (bicycle) is the victim, the agent (the thief) may or may not be named. The 了 marks the completion of the unfortunate act.
走遍天下	zǒu biàn tiān xià	to travel the whole world; to have been everywhere — 走遍 zǒu biàn (to walk/travel all over; to cover thoroughly , 遍 is a comprehensive coverage complement) + 天下 (all under heaven; the whole world). The ambition of the traveler: to have seen everything, to have left no corner of the world unvisited.
走出	zǒu chū	to walk out of; to emerge from — 走 zǒu (walk; leave) + 出 (exit complement). The most neutral directional compound: walking across a threshold from inside to outside. Used for physical exits and metaphorical ones , 走出困境 (walk out of a difficult situation), 走出阴影 (emerge from the shadow of something).
走道	zǒu dào	corridor; aisle; walkway — 走 zǒu (walk; go) + 道 (way; path). The path you walk: a corridor between rooms, an aisle on a plane or train, a narrow walkway between structures. Practical and architectural: the space designated for movement through an enclosed or constrained area.
走关系	zǒu guān xi	to use personal connections to get something done — 走 zǒu = to go through; to travel via. 走关系 means to go through the channel of personal connections to accomplish something that might otherwise be difficult or slow.
走后门	zǒu hòu mén	Literally "going through the back door" — Literally "going through the back door": using gifts and personal relationships to obtain favors, preferential treatment, or access that would not be available through formal channels. The phrase acknowledges the reality of this practice without fully endorsing it.
走江湖	zǒu jiāng hú	to travel the roads making a living; to hustle; to work itinerantly — Traditional use: traveling performers, fortune-tellers, medicine sellers, acrobats , people who made their living moving from town to town, outside the settled economy.
走廊	zǒu láng	corridor; hallway
走路	zǒu lù	to walk; walking — 走 zǒu (to walk/go) + 路 lù (road; path). To walk , the action of foot-travel. 走路去 = to walk there (contrast: 开车去 to drive, 坐地铁去 to take the subway). Also used metaphorically: 走上了一条不归路 "walked a road of no return" · 走对了路 "took the right path (in life)." 请走路 = "please use the footpath" (on signs).
走向	zǒu xiàng	to move toward; trend; the run or layout of something — 走 zǒu (to go) + 向 xiàng (toward). As a verb, to head toward an outcome: 走向成功 "to head toward success." As a noun, the trend or physical run of a thing, as in the 走向 of a mountain range or a market.
走运	zǒu yùn	to be lucky
走资派	zǒu zī pài	capitalist roader (target of persecution)
租界	zū jiè	Concession , the zones of extraterritorial foreign governance established in Shanghai and other treaty ports. The foreign concessions in Shanghai operated from 1843 to 1943, when wartime politics prompted their formal retrocession to China.
足迹	zú jī	footprint; trace; tracks — 足 (foot) + 迹 (trace, mark). The imprint a foot leaves , both physical footprints and the metaphorical traces left by a life or a civilization. 留下足迹 (to leave one's footprints); 遍布全国的足迹 (footprints spread across the whole country).
足球	zú qiú	football; soccer — 足 (foot) + 球 (ball). The foot-ball: soccer in China and Taiwan. The Chinese Football Association (中国足球协会) governs 足球 nationally.
钻	zuān	to bore, to drill
最后	zuì hòu	finally; in the end; last — 最 zuì (most; superlative) + 后 (behind; last). The furthest back position in a sequence: the final item, the last moment, the ultimate outcome. Functions as both an adverb before a verb (最后他同意了 , in the end he agreed) and as an adjective modifying a noun (最后一页 , the last page).
左边	zuǒ biān	the left side; on the left — 左 (left) + 边 (side; edge). The most neutral, everyday word for "the left side." Used in directions, descriptions of position, and navigation. 在左边 (on the left), 左边的门 (the door on the left). Paired with 右边 yòubiān (the right side) for balanced descriptions of both sides of something.
左传	Zuǒ Zhuàn	the Zuo Commentary — the primary narrative source for the Spring and Autumn period — Traditionally attributed to Zuo Qiuming (左丘明 Zuǒ Qiūmíng) and covering 722–468 BCE, the 左传 is the great narrative expansion of the Chūnqiū chronicle.
左道旁门	zuǒ dào páng mén	heterodox sects and methods; unorthodox ways — 左道 (the left way, the deviant path) + 旁门 (a side gate, a door off to the side). 道 is the orthodox central path; 左道 is the path that veers left of it. 旁门 extends the spatial metaphor , not the main gate, but a side entrance used by those who cannot or will not use the front.
左手	zuǒ shǒu	the left hand — 左 (left) + 手 (hand). Straightforward compound for the left hand. 左撇子 zuǒpiězi is the colloquial word for a left-handed person (撇 piě is the name of the left-falling stroke in calligraphy; 子 nominalizes).
左右	zuǒ yòu	both sides; approximately; to control
佐	zuǒ	to assist; an aide; subordinate official — The cognate character: 人 (person radical) + 左 (left, the assisting hand). 辅佐 fǔzuǒ means "to assist a ruler or superior" , the two characters are near-synonymous, both meaning to support from a flanking position.
