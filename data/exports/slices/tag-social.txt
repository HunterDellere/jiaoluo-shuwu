//角落書屋/Slices/Social
队	duì	team, group, band, military unit; measure word for groups — A crowd of people 人 packed against an earthen mound 阝, the image that gives 队 its sense of a formed-up team or column.
哥	gē	elder brother, a familiar term for an older young man — Two 可 stacked into one glyph; the doubled phonetic came to carry the warmest word in the family, elder brother.
关	guān	to close, to concern, the frontier pass — The bolt that closes a gate, the mountain pass that controls a frontier, and the relation that ties two people together. One character holds all three.
好	hǎo	good; to like — Good, well, to like, and the tone split that doubles its expressive range.
和	hé	harmony, and, with; peace — 禾 (grain) beside 口 (mouth): 和 is both Mandarin's everyday "and" and the Confucian ideal of harmony without uniformity.
价	jià	price, value, worth — A person standing beside 介 jiè: the worth a thing takes on only once two people sit down to trade for it.
姐	jiě	elder sister, miss, young lady — A woman radical fused to the sound-lender 且, 姐 names the elder sister and stretches to cover every miss and young lady in modern address.
借	jiè	to borrow, to lend, to make use of — One glyph carries both halves of a loan: 借 means borrow and lend at once, the direction settled only by context.
脸	liǎn	face, cheeks, social face, reputation — The flesh-radical face that doubles as a ledger of dignity: in Chinese you can lose it, save it, or tear it apart.
民	mín	the people, a citizen, folk — The common people as a collective subject: the populace, the citizen, the folk and the nation, and the civilian set against the military. From 人民 the people to 民族 ethnic nation to 民主 democracy.
名	míng	name, fame, reputation — 名 is 夕 dusk plus 口 mouth: darkness falls, you cannot see who approaches, so you call out a name. Identity asserted through speech.
你	nǐ	you — The everyday second-person pronoun whose formal twin 您 embeds 心 (heart) directly into the character: address with warmth.
朋	péng	friend, companion; of the same group — Two strings of cowrie shells hung side by side, an old unit of value, borrowed early for companions of equal standing who keep close company.
社	shè	society, an association, the god of the soil — The altar radical beside the earth: a shrine to the god of the soil and the community that gathered around it. From that image grow society itself, the many kinds of association and agency, and the ancient soil-and-grain spirits that once stood for the state.
送	sòng	to see off, to send, to give — A figure waving 关 at someone whose feet 辶 are already carrying them away: 送 is farewell built into the road itself.
他	tā	he, him; another — 他 was gender-neutral for a millennium until 她 was coined in 1917 to solve a translation problem that Classical Chinese had never needed to solve.
她	tā	she, her — 她 is the youngest character in everyday Chinese, invented in 1917 to translate "she," and the cause of one of the loudest grammatical fights in modern Chinese letters.
我	wǒ	I, me, myself — The character for 'I' began as a toothed bronze halberd: the assertive grip of a weapon became the grammatical mark of a self.
做	zuò	to do, to make, to act as — 做 is the everyday verb for doing and making, a late character built to carry the work that 作 had grown too literary to do, and the two still split the labor between them.
风俗	fēng sú	customs, traditions, folk practices — The wind of culture and the ways of the people: how 风俗 differs from 习惯 and why the distinction matters.
关系	guān xi	relationship, connections — The untranslatable social fabric: relational leverage, it matters.
客气	kè qi	politeness, ceremony — The polite manners owed to guests: and the gentle dance of refusal, insistence, and ritual modesty that runs through every Chinese social interaction.
礼	lǐ	ritual, propriety, gift, courtesy — The cosmic blueprint Confucius spent his life trying to restore: and why manners are a civilizational technology.
礼貌	lǐ mào	politeness, courteous manner — The everyday word for politeness: what 礼 (ritual propriety) becomes when it is shrunk down to the scale of ordinary social life. Polite forms, polite faces, and the precise way modern Chinese distinguishes 礼貌 from 客气, 礼节, and the older 礼 itself.
面子	miàn zi	face, honor — Giving, losing, and saving face: the social grammar of dignity.
朋友	péng you	friend — The everyday word for friend, and the rich web of relationship terms (好朋友, 男朋友, 交朋友) built around it.
谦虚	qiān xū	humility, modesty — 谦虚 is both a Confucian virtue and a social practice: in Chinese interaction, it functions as a required performative register, not just an internal attitude.
社会	shè huì	society — The word for society that China borrowed from Japan, which had borrowed the characters from China: a compound forged at the altar of the earth.
小人	xiǎo rén	the petty person — Confucius took a word that meant "commoner" and turned it into the permanent opposite of the gentleman: the small-hearted schemer of every office.
宾朋满座	bīn péng mǎn zuò	guests and friends fill the seats: a house full of company — Lit: guests-friends fill the seats. 宾朋 pairs guest with friend; the image is a hall packed with visitors. Close kin to 高朋满座, used of a lively, well-attended occasion where every seat is taken.
彬彬有礼	bīn bīn yǒu lǐ	refined and courteous in bearing; gracefully well-mannered — From the Lúnyǔ (论语·雍也): 文质彬彬，然后君子 ("when refinement and substance are in balance , only then is one a 君子"). 彬彬 bīn bīn names the quality of refined balance , neither rough nor showy.
不耻下问	bù chǐ xià wèn	"not ashamed to ask those below" — intellectual humility; willingness to learn from anyone regardless of status — From the 论语 (Analects) , Confucius praising the minister Kong Wenzi: 敏而好学，不耻下问，是以谓之'文'也 (Quick-minded and fond of learning, not ashamed to ask those of lower rank , this is why he was given the posthumous title 'Wen').
不要脸	bú yào liǎn	to want no face: shameless, brazen — Not a classical four-character chengyu but a fixed colloquial phrase: to want no face is to discard all sense of shame. A sharp insult, stronger than it looks in English.
沉鱼落雁	chén yú luò yàn	"sinking fish and falling geese" — describing a woman so beautiful that fish dive and birds fall when she passes — From the Zhuangzi, originally a wry critique of relativism in beauty (the fish do not actually find her beautiful , they hide because they are afraid of humans).
成群结队	chéng qún jié duì	to form crowds and bands: in droves, in flocks — The most common 队 idiom in daily speech. Pictures people or animals gathering thick enough to clump into groups and lines. Used for crowds streaming somewhere: 游客成群结队地涌入古镇.
愁眉苦脸	chóu méi kǔ liǎn	knitted brows and a bitter face: a picture of gloom and worry — 愁 (worry) eyebrows over a 苦 (bitter) face. The whole head sags with anxiety. A vivid, everyday way to say someone looks miserable.
大哥别说二哥	dà gē bié shuō èr gē	the eldest shouldn't mock the second: pot calling the kettle black — A proverb on shared flaws: 大哥 and 二哥 are equally at fault, so neither has standing to criticize the other. The Mandarin take on glass houses.
大姐大	dà jiě dà	top sister: a commanding, dominant woman — A colloquial trigram, not a classical idiom, for the female leader of a group or a forceful, authoritative woman. Carries a half-admiring, half-wary tone.
丢人现眼	diū rén xiàn yǎn	"lose people, show eyes" — to make a spectacle of oneself; disgraceful public behavior — Lit: lose-person-expose-eyes. A stronger and more colorful version of 丢脸 , specifically about embarrassing public display. 现眼 (to expose one's eyes to public ridicule) adds the dimension of being stared at and judged.
非我族类	fēi wǒ zú lèi	those not of our kin; those outside our group
敢做敢当	gǎn zuò gǎn dāng	to dare to act and dare to answer for it — to own one's actions — 敢 gǎn (to dare) + 做 + 敢 + 当 dāng (to bear, to take responsibility). The mark of a person of integrity: bold enough to act, and equally willing to shoulder the consequences. The phrase praises moral courage paired with accountability, the refusal to act and then duck the blame.
高朋满座	gāo péng mǎn zuò	distinguished friends fill the seats: a gathering of honored guests — Lit: high-friends fill the seats. 高朋 are eminent guests, the 高 marking rank or worth. The phrase from Wang Bo's preface to the Tengwang Pavilion praises a host whose hall is full of distinguished company, a warm word for a well-attended feast.
哥俩好	gē liǎ hǎo	the two of us are tight: a toast and a drinking-game call — A finger-guessing drinking call and a stock phrase for two close male friends. 俩 (liǎ) is "the two of us," so the whole thing means "we two brothers are solid."
顾此失彼	gù cǐ shī bǐ	attend to this and lose that — unable to manage everything at once — 顾 gù (to attend to; to look after) + 此 cǐ (this) + 失 shī (to lose) + 彼 bǐ (that; the other). Fix your attention on one thing and something else slips away. Describes resource constraints, divided attention, and the predicament of managing competing priorities.
关系密切	guān xi mì qiē	"relationship close and tight" — closely connected; intimately related — A descriptive fixed phrase rather than a classical 成语, but widely used as a set expression in formal Chinese writing and speech. 这两家公司的关系密切，共享很多资源。"These two companies are closely connected and share many resources." The closeness can be personal, institutional, or strategic.
国泰民安	guó tài mín ān	the country is prosperous and the people are at peace — The classical blessing for a nation: 泰 tài (great peace) + 民安 (the people are secure). Used in toasts, New Year wishes, and political rhetoric. The Confucian ideal of good governance.
好景不长	hǎo jǐng bù cháng	good times don't last — a beautiful scene cannot endure — Lit: good-scenery-not-long. An expression of Buddhist impermanence and Chinese realism about fortune. Used when pleasant circumstances are ending or were brief.
好事多磨	hǎo shì duō mó	good things take time — good outcomes require much grinding and effort — Lit: good-things-much-grinding. Used to console someone experiencing delays or obstacles on the path to something valuable. The consolation: friction is the price of worth.
好自为之	hǎo zì wéi zhī	take good care of yourself; do well for yourself — A formal farewell or parting injunction: "do well in your own conduct." Can be warm (a mentor to a student) or pointed (a warning to behave). Context determines whether it is affectionate or admonishing.
和光同尘	hé guāng tóng chén	to soften one's light and share the dust: to blend in and live among ordinary people — From the Daoist classics: to dim one's brilliance and mix with the common crowd rather than stand apart. A vision of how the wise move within society, joining its dust instead of towering over it. The ideal of fitting unobtrusively into the social whole.
和气生财	hé qì shēng cái	harmony brings wealth — a gentle manner attracts prosperity — 和气 héqi (harmony of manner; friendliness) + 生 shēng (to generate) + 财 cái (wealth). The principle that amicable dealings create the trust and goodwill that sustain business. Posted in shops across the Chinese-speaking world.
狐朋狗友	hú péng gǒu yǒu	fox-friends and dog-companions: a bad crowd — Lit: fox-friends, dog-friends. 狐 fox and 狗 dog stand for the sly and the low. The phrase scorns worthless drinking companions and hangers-on, the kind of friends who lead you nowhere good. A parent's word of warning.
患难之交	huàn nàn zhī jiāo	a friend made in times of hardship — a friend in adversity — A friendship forged through shared difficulty, prized in Chinese culture as the truest kind. 患难 huànnàn means hardship and danger; 交 jiāo here is the noun "friendship, bond." The related proverb is 患难见真情 ("adversity reveals true feeling"): you learn who your real friends are when times are hard.
价值连城	jià zhí lián chéng	worth a string of cities: of immense value — From the Warring States tale of the 和氏璧 jade, which King Zhao of Qin offered fifteen cities to obtain. To be worth 连城 is to command a value beyond ordinary reckoning.
江山社稷	jiāng shān shè jì	the rivers, mountains, and altars of soil and grain: the whole realm, the state — Literally "rivers and mountains, the altars of soil and grain." 江山 the land and 社稷 the altars together name the entire country a ruler holds in trust. A grand, classical phrase for the state as a whole, the territory and the legitimacy that come with it.
教我如何不想她	jiào wǒ rú hé bù xiǎng tā	"How could I not think of her" — Liu Bannong's 1920 poem, set to music by Zhao Yuanren — Not classical, but the most famous early appearance of 她 in print. The poem is addressed to "her", variously read as a lover, the homeland (Liu was abroad in Britain when he wrote it), or both. The song became one of the defining works of early modern Chinese music.
姐妹情深	jiě mèi qíng shēn	deep sisterly affection — A common modern four-character set phrase for the strong bond between sisters or close female friends. Frequent in titles, toasts, and warm descriptions of female friendship.
借刀杀人	jiè dāo shā rén	borrow a knife to kill a man: destroy a rival by another's hand — The third of the Thirty-Six Stratagems (三十六计). You stay clean while a third party does the deed; still vivid in business and political commentary today.
借古讽今	jiè gǔ fěng jīn	use the past to satirize the present — A classic mode of Chinese writing: cloak criticism of present rulers in a story about an ancient dynasty. Long the safest way to speak truth to power.
借花献佛	jiè huā xiàn fó	to offer flowers borrowed from another — presenting something that isn't yours — Used with self-deprecating humor when giving a gift that wasn't originally yours , passing along a recommendation, re-gifting, or presenting another person's work as an offering. The image: lacking flowers of your own to offer at the Buddhist altar, you borrow someone else's.
借题发挥	jiè tí fā huī	seize on a topic to make a wider point: use a pretext to vent — Literally "borrow the subject to elaborate." Common for someone who turns a small matter into a platform for a bigger grievance.
巾帼不让须眉	jīn guó bù ràng xū méi	"the woman's headdress yields nothing to the man's beard" — a woman who is the equal of any man — 巾帼 jīnguó , a woman's hair ornament, by metonymy "woman." 须眉 xūméi: beard and brows, by metonymy "man." Used in praise of female generals (Mulan, Hua Mulan; Liang Hongyu), athletes, and accomplished women.
礼尚往来	lǐ shàng wǎng lái	rites value reciprocity — one courtesy deserves another; give and take — From the 礼记 Lǐjì (Record of Rites): 礼尚往来，往而不来，非礼也；来而不往，亦非礼也. "礼 values mutual exchange; to offer without receiving back is not 礼; to receive without offering back is also not 礼." The principle of reciprocity as the governing logic of social life.
礼贤下士	lǐ xián xià shì	"to honor the worthy and condescend to the lower-ranked" — a leader's courteous treatment of those beneath them — Originally describing rulers and high officials who treated talented commoners and lower-ranked scholars with genuine respect , a quality praised throughout the dynastic histories. 礼 is verbal here ("to treat with 礼"); 贤 xián = the worthy; 下士 = lower-ranked scholars.
利害关系	lì hài guān xi	"interest-harm relationship" — a relationship involving mutual stakes and risks — Lit: benefit-harm-relationship. Describes a situation where parties are bound by shared interests and shared risks , where what benefits or harms one affects the others. 他们之间有复杂的利害关系，不能轻易分开。"There are complex stakes between them , they can't be easily separated."
满招损，谦受益	mǎn zhāo sǔn , qiān shòu yì	"fullness invites loss; humility attracts benefit" — the cosmological argument for modesty — From the 尚书 (Shangshu, Book of Documents), one of the Five Classics , among the oldest extant Chinese prose.
面不改色	miàn bù gǎi sè	"face doesn't change color" — unruffled; keeping composure under pressure; poker-faced — Lit: face-not-change-color. The mark of composure under pressure , your face doesn't betray fear, surprise, or discomfort. 他面不改色地接受了批评，继续进行演讲。"He received the criticism without changing expression and continued his speech." Often admired as a sign of courage or self-discipline.
面面俱到	miàn miàn jù dào	every aspect covered; thorough in all directions — 面 miàn (face; surface; aspect) + 面 + 俱 jù (all; together) + 到 (arrived at). Arrival has reached every face, every surface, every angle of the matter. Used to praise comprehensive coverage , a plan that misses nothing, a speech that addresses every concern, an analysis that leaves no gap.
民不聊生	mín bù liáo shēng	the people can scarcely survive: a populace plunged into misery — Literally "the people have no means to make a living." A grim phrase for times of misrule, war, or disaster when ordinary people cannot even keep themselves alive. 聊 liáo here is the classical sense "to rely on, to depend on for," so 不聊生 means having nothing to depend on for life.
民以食为天	mín yǐ shí wéi tiān	to the people, food is heaven: nothing matters more than feeding the people — Literally "the people take food as heaven." An ancient maxim of statecraft: the first and highest concern of any ruler is that the people have enough to eat. Still quoted constantly in everyday life, often half-jokingly, to defend the seriousness of a good meal.
名不虚传	míng bù xū chuán	the reputation is not falsely spread: to live up to one's name; to be as good as people say — Lit: the name is not emptily passed around. Said of a person or thing whose fame turns out to be well deserved, the praise that circulated was no exaggeration. Here 传 keeps the chuán reading, the spreading of a reputation.
名副其实	míng fù qí shí	the name matches the reality — Literally "the name fits its reality," said of someone or something that truly lives up to its reputation or title. 名 (name) and 实 (reality) match. The opposite is 名不副实 ("the name does not match the reality"), all show, no substance. Note the shared 其实 morphemes 其 and 实.
名正言顺	míng zhèng yán shùn	the name is right and the speech follows smoothly — acting with proper authority and justification — Lit: name-correct speech-flows. Directly from the Analects 13.3 argument: when the name is rectified, speech follows naturally, and what follows from speech can be accomplished. Used today when someone has the proper credentials or title to do something , "now that you have the title, you can act."
排兵布队	pái bīng bù duì	to arrange soldiers and deploy units: to marshal forces — A military set phrase, sibling to 排兵布阵. Describes a commander laying out troops in formation; now applied to organizing a team or lineup before a contest or launch.
朋比为奸	péng bǐ wéi jiān	to band together for evil: to collude in wrongdoing — Lit: friends-allying to-do evil. 朋比 is to band together as a clique, 为奸 to commit villainy. A sharply negative idiom for cronies who join forces for corrupt or treacherous ends, the dark side of company kept on a level footing.
朋友遍天下	péng you biàn tiān xià	to have friends all over the world — A warm saying praising someone with a wide circle of friends everywhere. 遍 biàn means "all over," 天下 "under heaven, the whole world." Often paired with the encouraging proverb 在家靠父母，出门靠朋友 ("at home you rely on parents; away from home you rely on friends"), which captures the practical importance of…
劈头盖脸	pī tóu gài liǎn	splitting the head and covering the face: full in the face, without holding back — Describes a torrent that lands straight on you, a downpour of rain or a barrage of scolding, leaving no chance to dodge.
谦谦君子	qiān qiān jūn zǐ	"the humbly modest exemplary person" — the junzi whose modesty is doubled and genuine — From the 易经 (Yijing), first line of hexagram 15 谦 Qiān: 谦谦君子，用涉大川，吉 (The humbly modest exemplary person , it is advantageous to cross the great river. Good fortune). The doubling of 谦谦 marks the quality as thorough and genuine rather than surface-level.
亲贤臣，远小人	qīn xián chén , yuǎn xiǎo rén	keep worthy ministers close and petty men far — From Zhuge Liang's Chushibiao (出师表), the memorial he submitted before the northern campaigns, diagnosing why the Former Han rose and the Later Han fell.
琴瑟和鸣	qín sè hé míng	qin and se playing in harmony — a happy and loving marriage — 琴 qín (seven-string zither) and 瑟 sè (larger twenty-five string zither) are the classical paired instruments. When they sound together in harmony, they are the image of a couple in accord. A literary idiom for marital happiness, used in wedding toasts and poetry.
人面兽心	rén miàn shòu xīn	a human face with the heart of a beast: a cruel person behind a civil mask — Not built on 社 itself, but a classic measure of how a person fits or fails the bonds of human society: someone who looks like one of us yet behaves with brute cruelty. A reminder that belonging to a 社会 is judged by conduct, not appearance.
舍我其谁	shě wǒ qí shuí	if not I, then who? — the burden falls to me
身不由己	shēn bù yóu jǐ	the body is not controlled by oneself — driven by circumstances beyond one's will — 身 shēn (body; self) + 不 bù (not) + 由 yóu (controlled by; following from) + 己 jǐ (oneself). One's own body belongs to external forces , obligation, circumstance, social pressure, or fate. The counterpart to agency: when 他 (the other, external) overrides 己 (the self).
说到做到	shuō dào zuò dào	to do what one says — to be as good as one's word — 说 shuō (to say) + 到 dào (reach the point of) + 做 + 到. Literally "say it to the point, do it to the point." A high compliment: this is a person whose words and deeds match, who does not promise idly. The structure leans on 做到 (to achieve, to follow through).
死要面子活受罪	sǐ yào miàn zi huó shòu zuì	"dying for face, living in torment" — to suffer rather than ask for help because of pride — A seven-character folk saying rather than a classical 成语, but extremely widely used. Describes the pattern of letting one's face-consciousness cause unnecessary suffering , not asking for help, not admitting failure, not seeking medical attention, because doing so would cost face.
送佛送到西	sòng fó sòng dào xī	if you escort the Buddha, escort him all the way west: finish what you start, see help through to the end — A colloquial saying drawing on the westward journey of Buddhist pilgrimage. Urges you not to abandon a kindness or a task halfway, but to carry it to completion.
送旧迎新	sòng jiù yíng xīn	to send off the old and welcome the new: ring out the old year, ring in the new — A New Year and year-end phrase, balancing 送 (sending off) the old against 迎 (welcoming) the new. Common on greeting cards and in festival speeches.
送往迎来	sòng wǎng yíng lái	to send off those leaving and welcome those arriving: the endless round of social hospitality — Traces to the Lǐjì (Book of Rites). Describes the constant ceremony of receiving guests and seeing them off, often with a faint note of how exhausting such social duty becomes.
所谓伊人	suǒ wèi yī rén	"the one of whom I speak" — the beloved, the one I yearn for — From the Shijing (诗经) poem 蒹葭 ("Reeds"). One of the most famous lines of classical poetry, and the locus classicus for 伊 as feminine third person. The poem itself is gender-ambiguous in the classical text but read across two millennia as referring to a beloved woman across the river.
他山之石	tā shān zhī shí	stones from other hills — outside perspectives can polish your own jade — From the Book of Songs (诗经): "stones from other hills can be used to grind jade." External viewpoints, foreign models, and outside criticism all carry value that self-regard misses. 他山 (other hills) + 之 (possessive particle) + 石 (stones).
讨价还价	tǎo jià huán jià	ask a price, return a price: to haggle, bargain — Acts out the back-and-forth of the marketplace, one side naming a figure and the other countering. Extended to any drawn-out negotiation over terms.
铁哥们儿	tiě gē men r	iron buddies: rock-solid male friends — 铁 (iron) intensifies 哥们儿. The metaphor is metal: a friendship that won't bend or rust. Reserved for the few friends you would drop everything for.
忘我工作	wàng wǒ gōng zuò	to work with selfless devotion; absorbed work
我行我素	wǒ xíng wǒ sù	to go one's own way; to act as one always has
无关紧要	wú guān jǐn yào	"unrelated to the important" — trivial; beside the point; not worth worrying about — Lit: no-connection-tense-important. Used to dismiss irrelevant details or minor concerns. 这些细节无关紧要，我们不要在这里浪费时间。"These details are beside the point , let's not waste time on them." Often used as a diplomatic way to redirect a conversation.
无价之宝	wú jià zhī bǎo	a treasure beyond price: a priceless thing — 无价 (without price) marks what no sum could buy. Used for a rare heirloom, a one-of-a-kind artwork, or, warmly, for a person or a friendship.
无名英雄	wú míng yīng xióng	unsung hero — a hero without a name — Lit: without-name hero. Someone who performs heroic deeds without receiving fame or recognition. The tension between 名 (name, recognition) and 实 (substance, actual contribution) compressed into a phrase.
物美价廉	wù měi jià lián	the goods fine and the price cheap: excellent value for money — The four-character verdict every shopper hopes to pronounce: good quality and a low price at once. Standard praise in advertising and casual recommendation alike.
息息相关	xī xī xiāng guān	"every breath mutually connected" — closely and inextricably interconnected — Lit: breath-breath-mutually-connected. 息 = breath. The image: two systems breathing in such synchrony that they cannot be separated. Used for things that are organically and necessarily connected. 这两个问题息息相关，解决一个就能推动另一个。"These two issues are inextricably linked , solving one will advance the other."
嬉皮笑脸	xī pí xiào liǎn	grinning cheekily: to behave with a flippant, unserious grin — A face stuck in a smirk, used to describe someone who refuses to take a moment seriously. Often a parent's rebuke to a child who jokes through a scolding.
先礼后兵	xiān lǐ hòu bīng	"first courtesy, then arms" — try diplomacy before resorting to force — From the Romance of the Three Kingdoms (三国演义). The classical strategic principle: open with 礼 (courteous communication), and only if that fails escalate to 兵 (military force or coercive action).
先小人后君子	xiān xiǎo rén hòu jūn zǐ	petty men first, gentlemen after — settle terms up front — The contract-signing proverb: state the money, the split, and the deadlines with un-gentlemanly explicitness now, so that everyone can afford to be a gentleman later. Spoken at the start of partnerships between friends precisely because friendship is what it protects.

和谐社会	hé xié shè huì	harmonious society — 和谐 (héxié, harmonious; in harmony) + 社会. The governing ideal articulated by Hu Jintao in 2004.
红脸	hóng liǎn	to blush; to flush red (with anger or embarrassment) — 红 (red) + 脸 (face). The cheeks going red, whether from shyness or from a flare of temper. 脸红 (liǎnhóng) reverses the order for the same blush; 红脸 also names the stern, blunt role someone plays in a good-cop bad-cop pairing.
花轿	huā jiào	The decorated red sedan chair in which the bride was traditionally carried to the wedding. The chair was ornately painted and curtained, surrounded by musicians and attendants. The tradition has been revived in some contemporary weddings as a heritage element.
欢送	huān sòng	to give a warm send-off — 欢 (joyful) plus 送 (see off). To send someone off with celebration: a farewell party for a retiring colleague or a graduating class. The opposite number of 欢迎 (huānyíng), to welcome.
回礼	huí lǐ	To return a gift , to reciprocate with a gift of one's own — To return a gift , to reciprocate with a gift of one's own. 回礼 is expected and its absence is noticed. The return gift should be roughly equivalent in value to the original but need not be identical in kind. Returning a gift of equal or slightly higher value is considered courteous.
婚礼	hūn lǐ	wedding ceremony — 婚 hūn (marriage) + 礼 lǐ (rite; ceremony). Wedding as ritual. The 礼 suffix creates ceremony words for life's great transitions: 婚礼 wedding · 葬礼 zànglǐ funeral · 成人礼 chéngrénlǐ coming-of-age ceremony · 开幕礼 kāimùlǐ opening ceremony.
火锅	huǒ guō	hotpot; steamboat — 火 huǒ + 锅 guō (pot; wok). A simmering communal pot at the table , the quintessential Chinese social meal. 吃火锅 = to eat hotpot, one of the most common social dining activities. Regional varieties: 四川火锅 Sichuan hotpot (numbing-spicy 麻辣), 北京涮羊肉 Beijing lamb hotpot (clear broth).
伙伴	huǒ bàn	partner; companion
吉利	jí lì	auspicious; lucky; propitious — Auspicious, lucky, propitious , the standard term for something considered to bring good fortune. A number, date, phrase, or object that is 吉利 is sought after and displayed. Its opposite: 不吉利 (bù jílì), inauspicious.
集体	jí tǐ	collective; group; the community — 集 jí (to gather) + 体 tǐ (body). A gathered body of people acting as one: 集体活动 "a group activity," 集体主义 "collectivism." Set against the individual, 个人 gèrén.
寄	jì	to mail, post
夹菜	jiā cài	To pick food for someone else , placing food from a shared dish into another person's bowl as a gesture of care and hospitality. The action is performed with chopsticks and directed toward honored guests, elders, or someone whose preferences you are attending to.
家庭	jiā tíng	Family; household — Family; household. The nuclear unit of parents and children who share a home. 家庭作业 (jiātíng zuòyè, homework , lit. "family work") and 家庭医生 (jiātíng yīshēng, family doctor) show the compound in everyday use. The term carries a warmth and domesticity that the more formal 家族 lacks.
家族	jiā zú	Clan; extended family network; lineage group sharing a surname. 家族企业 (jiāzú qǐyè) is a family business , often meaning a business controlled across generations by a single clan. The word implies shared ancestry, shared surname, and historically shared clan property and ancestral hall (祠堂 cítáng).
假借	jiǎ jiè	to make use of, borrow (literary)
价格	jià gé	price — 价 (price) + 格 (a fixed standard, a slot). The settled, stated price of a good, the figure printed on the tag or quoted by the seller. The everyday word for what something costs.
价钱	jià qián	price (colloquial)
价值	jià zhí	value, worth — 价 (price) + 值 (to be worth). The abstract worth of a thing, an act, or an idea, far beyond any market figure. Carries the moral and intellectual sense, as in 价值观 (jiàzhíguān), a set of values.
嫁妆	jià zhuang	Dowry , the goods the bride's family sends with her to the new home. Traditionally included furniture, bedding, household items, and valuable objects. The dowry and betrothal gifts are understood as a two-way transfer that balances the exchange between families.
见外	jiàn wài	to treat as an outsider
讲礼貌	jiǎng lǐ mào	to mind one's manners; to be attentive to politeness — 讲 jiǎng (to attend to; to value; to make a point of) + 礼貌. The active form: not just having manners but consciously practicing them. Common in parental instruction and educational contexts. 讲礼貌 is what 有礼貌 looks like as a discipline rather than a state.
降价	jiàng jià	to cut the price, mark down — 降 (to lower) + 价 (price). To bring a price down, to discount. Its opposite is 涨价 (zhǎngjià), to raise the price. Both are verb-object compounds, so an amount can split them: 降了价.
交朋友	jiāo péng you	to make friends — A verb-object phrase: 交 jiāo (to associate with, to form bonds) + 朋友. To make a single friend is 交个朋友 (with 个 splitting the phrase). The same 交 appears in 交流 (to exchange, communicate) and 社交 (social interaction), all built on the sense of mutual exchange.
骄傲	jiāo ào	arrogant; proud (positive or negative depending on context) — 骄 jiāo (arrogant; a rearing horse , the original pictograph showed an unruly horse rearing up on its hind legs) + 傲 ào (proud; disdainful; lofty). The primary antonym of 谦虚. In moral discourse, always negative , 骄傲是谦虚的对立面 (arrogance is the opposite of humility).
窖	jiào	The fermentation pit — The fermentation pit: the earthen or stone pit in which the fermented grain mash (酒醅 jiǔpēi) undergoes anaerobic fermentation. Old pits with established microbial ecosystems are considered the most valuable assets of a distillery.
姐夫	jiě fu	elder sister's husband; brother-in-law — 姐 (elder sister) plus 夫 (husband) names the man married to one's older sister. Chinese kinship marks the relation precisely, distinct from 妹夫 (mèifu), a younger sister's husband.
姐姐	jiě jie	elder sister — The doubled character is the everyday word for one's older sister. The second 姐 takes a neutral tone, giving the warm, soft cadence Chinese kinship terms favor.
姐妹	jiě mèi	sisters; female friends — 姐 (elder sister) plus 妹 (younger sister) yokes the two into a single word for "sisters" as a pair or group, and by extension a tight circle of close female friends.
界面	jiè miàn	interface; the surface between two systems — 界 (boundary; boundary marker) + 面 (surface; face). The surface at the boundary between two things. In technology and computing: the user interface (用户界面 yònghù jièmiàn), screen interface. In science: the interface between two phases or systems.
借给	jiè gěi	to lend (to someone) — 借 (lend) + 给 (give to). Pinning 给 after 借 fixes the direction outward: the subject is the one handing the object over.
借鉴	jiè jiàn	to learn from, draw on as a model — 借 (to borrow) + 鉴 (mirror, to reflect on). To borrow another's experience as a mirror for your own, taking what is useful from a model or precedent.
借口	jiè kǒu	excuse, pretext — 借 (to borrow) + 口 (mouth). A "borrowed mouth," words you reach for that are not the true reason. As a noun it is the excuse itself; as a verb, "to use as a pretext."
借钱	jiè qián	to borrow / lend money — 借 (borrow/lend) + 钱 (money). A verb-object pair whose direction depends on the prepositions around it: 向他借钱 is borrowing from him, 借钱给他 is lending to him.
借条	jiè tiáo	IOU, written loan note
借用	jiè yòng	to borrow for use
借助	jiè zhù	to draw on, with the help of — 借 (to make use of) + 助 (aid). To accomplish something by leaning on an external tool, force, or person. Formal register, common in writing.
经济	jīng jì	economy; economic; economical — 经 jīng (manage; order) + 济 jì (aid; relieve). A shortening of 经世济民, "to order the age and aid the people." Now the standard word for the economy, and as an adjective "economical, thrifty": 经济实惠 "good value for money."
敬酒	jìng jiǔ	To toast , to offer a drink as a gesture of respect — To toast , to offer a drink as a gesture of respect. 敬 means "to respect" or "to offer respectfully"; 酒 means "alcoholic drink." The act of toasting at a Chinese meal is a structured social protocol, not a casual gesture.
酒量	jiǔ liàng	drinking capacity: how much alcohol one can hold — 酒 jiǔ (alcohol) + 量 liàng (capacity). A person's tolerance for drink, the amount they can take before they are affected. A culturally loaded word at the banquet table, where 酒量好 (a good capacity) is treated as a social asset: 他酒量很大 (he can really hold his drink).
酒桌	jiǔ zhuō	The drinking table , refers not just to the physical table but to the entire social ritual of the banquet. 酒桌文化 (jiǔzhuō wénhuà) is "drinking table culture," often discussed critically for its excesses.
居民	jū mín	resident; inhabitant (of a place) — 居 jū (to dwell, to reside) + 民. A person defined by where they live rather than by their nationality: the residents of a building, a neighbourhood, a city, a region. The everyday administrative word, as in 居民区 jūmínqū (a residential district) and 城市居民 (urban residents).
军队	jūn duì	army, the armed forces — 军 (military) + 队 (formed unit). The standing army as an institution, the original martial home of 队 made explicit.
开关	kāi guān	a switch
空姐	kōng jiě	flight attendant (female) — 空 (sky, air) plus 姐 is the colloquial word for a female flight attendant, literally a "sky sister." It is a clipped, affectionate everyday term beside the formal 空中小姐 (kōngzhōng xiǎojiě).
快递	kuài dì	express delivery; courier
筷子	kuài zi	chopsticks
老板	lǎo bǎn	boss; business owner — 老 lǎo + 板 bǎn (plank; board, extended to the person in charge). The everyday term for boss, owner, or the person in charge of a business. In restaurants, calling out 老板！is the standard way to get the owner's attention.
老大哥	lǎo dà gē	big brother (figure or nation)
老朋友	lǎo péng you	old friend; longtime friend — A friend of long standing. 老 lǎo (old) marks the length of the friendship, not the person's age. A warm word, often used on reunion: 老朋友了 ("we go way back"). The opposite, a new friend, is 新朋友 xīn péngyou.
乐队	yu è duì	band, orchestra
礼节	lǐ jié	formal etiquette; protocol
礼貌用语	lǐ mào yòng yǔ	polite expressions; courtesy phrases — 礼貌 + 用语 yòngyǔ (set expressions; phrases used in a particular register). Names the inventory of fixed polite phrases: 您好 nín hǎo, 谢谢 xièxiè, 不客气 bú kèqi, 对不起 duìbuqǐ, 请 qǐng, 麻烦您 máfan nín, 辛苦了 xīnkǔ le. Mastering 礼貌用语 is the visible threshold of being 有礼貌.
礼物	lǐ wù	gift; present — 礼 lǐ (ritual offering) + 物 wù (thing; object; matter). Lit. "a ritual-offering made material." A gift in Chinese culture is always, at some level, a ritual object that carries social meaning exceeding its material value.
礼仪	lǐ yí	etiquette; protocol; ceremony — 礼 lǐ + 仪 yí (appearance; manner; instrument of measurement). The formal standards of conduct for official occasions. 礼仪小姐 = ceremonial hostess. 外交礼仪 diplomatic protocol. More formal and institutional than 礼貌.
里子	lǐ zi	The lining; the private reality beneath the public presentation. The counterpart to 面子. 里子不足，面子撑着 ("the lining is insufficient, face holds it up") describes a situation where the private reality does not support the public claim.
联系	lián xì	to contact; to get in touch; to link; connection — 联 lián (to join; to unite) + 系 xì (tie). Both the act of making contact and the link itself. As a verb: 联系我 "get in touch with me." As a noun: 保持联系 "keep in contact."
廉价	lián jià	cheap, low-priced
脸蛋	liǎn dàn	cheeks; (affectionate) little face — 脸 (face) + 蛋 (egg, oval). The egg-smooth oval of the cheeks, a warm word usually aimed at children or a pretty face. Often softened further to 脸蛋儿 (liǎndànr) with the northern -r ending.
脸红	liǎn hóng	to blush
脸面	liǎn miàn	face (combining both concepts); dignity; decency — A compound that fuses both face concepts. 脸面 covers the full spectrum , both the social prestige dimension of 面子 and the moral character dimension of 脸. Often used in contexts where both are at stake: 要顾脸面 (to consider one's dignity) means both social standing and personal integrity.
脸盆	liǎn pén	washbasin — 脸 (face) + 盆 (basin). The basin you fill to wash your face, a fixture of older Chinese households before plumbing reached every sink. A small window onto domestic life packed into two characters.
脸皮	liǎn pí	facial skin; (figurative) sense of shame; nerve — 脸 (face) + 皮 (skin). The skin of the face, and by extension one's capacity for shame. 脸皮厚 (liǎnpí hòu), thick-skinned, means shameless or unembarrassable; 脸皮薄 (liǎnpí báo), thin-skinned, means easily mortified.
脸色	liǎn sè	complexion; facial expression; mood read off the face — 脸 (face) + 色 (color, look). Literally the color of the face, used both for physical complexion and for the mood it betrays. To 看脸色 (kàn liǎnsè) is to read someone's expression and act accordingly, a core social skill.
良朋	liáng péng	a good friend: a worthy companion — 良 liáng (good, fine) + 朋 péng (friend). A good and worthy friend, the term carrying the older weight of 朋. Literary in tone; often paired as 良朋益友 "good friends who do you good." You would write it, not say it over coffee.
刘半农	Liú Bàn nóng	Liu Bannong, the inventor of 她 (1891–1934)
六六大顺	liù liù dà shùn	"six-six, all goes smoothly" — a wish for things to go well — 六六 (six-six) + 大顺 (great smoothness). A set blessing built on the homophony of 六 liù with 流 liú (to flow) and the auspicious 顺 shùn (smooth, unobstructed). Said at weddings, openings, and new beginnings to wish that everything proceed without obstacle.
旅行社	lǚ xíng shè	travel agency — 旅行 lǚxíng (to travel) + 社 shè (agency). The business that arranges trips, tours, and tickets: 跟旅行社报名 "to sign up through a travel agency," 旅行社的导游 "the agency's tour guide." A textbook example of the agency sense of 社 attached to a whole two-syllable field word.
麻辣锅底	má là guō dǐ	mala broth base; numbing-hot pot
满月	mǎn yu è	the one-month celebration for a newborn — 满 mǎn (full; complete) + 月 yuè (month). When a baby has survived its first full month , historically the most dangerous period , the family celebrates with red eggs (红鸡蛋 hóng jīdàn), gifting of gold jewelry, and a feast. The custom signals that the child is likely to thrive.
毛肚	máo dǔ	tripe; honeycomb tripe
茅台	máo tái	Moutai — Moutai: the prestige baijiu produced in Maotai Town, Guizhou province. Made from sorghum using the sauce-aroma (酱香 jiàngxiāng) method, it is the most famous Chinese spirit and functions as a currency of respect in formal Chinese business culture.
没关系	méi guān xi	it doesn't matter; never mind; that's okay — One of the most indispensable phrases in Mandarin. The standard response to 对不起 (sorry). Literally "no relationship" , meaning "it has no effect on us; it carries no weight." Learn this before your first week of speaking Chinese.
没礼貌	méi lǐ mào	rude; ill-mannered; lacking in manners — 没 méi (lacks; does not have) + 礼貌. The standard rebuke. Heavier than English "rude" because it implies a failure of formation, not just a momentary lapse.
没面子	méi miàn zi	to lose face; to be embarrassed; without prestige — To be without face , whether because one has lost it through embarrassing behavior or because one's status is insufficient to command it. 没面子 is the social embarrassment register, distinct from the moral shame of 丢脸.
美好	měi hǎo	beautiful; wonderful
美女	měi nǚ	miss (lit. beauty); address for a young woman
妹妹	mèi mei	younger sister
面对	miàn duì	to face; to confront; to deal with — 面 (face) + 对 (to face; to be opposite to). Lit. "face-to-face with." To confront a reality, problem, or situation squarely rather than avoiding it. Widely used in motivational, psychological, and journalistic writing.
面条	miàn tiáo	noodles — The same 面 character. In northern China, 面 = wheat-flour dough → noodles. The flat, surface-like sheet of worked dough becomes strips (条 tiáo = strip). A vivid reminder that 面 means "surface/face" in the most general sense , the flat plane.
面子工程	miàn zi gōng chéng	Face engineering — Face engineering: infrastructure or projects built primarily for prestige rather than practical need. Empty airports in small cities, oversized government buildings, unnecessary highways: these are classic 面子工程.
民歌	míng ē	folk song — 民 + 歌 gē (song). A song that arises and circulates among the people rather than being composed for the concert hall: the traditional songs of a region or an ethnic group, often anonymous and passed down orally.
民间	mín jiān	among the people; folk; non-governmental — 民 + 间 jiān (among, within). The realm of ordinary life as opposed to the official, the courtly, or the state: that which exists among the people.
民生	mín shēng	the people's livelihood
民众	mín zhòng	the masses; the general public — 民 + 众 zhòng (multitude, crowd). The ordinary public viewed as a large body, common in news writing and formal description: how the public reacts, what the public expects. More descriptive and less ideologically charged than 人民, and broader and more neutral than the warmer, address-style 大家.
民主	mín zhǔ	democracy; democratic — 民 mín (the people) + 主 zhǔ (master). The people as master, the modern term for democracy. Also used adjectivally: 民主国家 "a democratic country," 民主选举 "a democratic election."
民族	mín zú	ethnic group; nationality; a people — 民 + 族 zú (clan, lineage, group). A people considered as a cultural and ethnic stock: an ethnic group, a nationality, a nation in the cultural sense. China officially recognises 56 民族, the 汉族 Hànzú (Han) and 55 少数民族 shǎoshù mínzú (ethnic minorities).
名家	Míng jiā	School of Names
名片	míng piàn	business card — 名 míng (name) + 片 piàn (flat slice; card). A "name slice" , the physical object that carries your name into another person's hand. Business card exchange in China carries ritual weight: present and receive with both hands, read the card before putting it away, never write on it.
名人	míng rén	famous person; celebrity; notable — 名 míng (name; fame) + 人 rén (person). A person whose name is widely known. Covers historical greats, contemporary celebrities, and anyone of notable standing. Context determines whether the register is elevated (historical figures) or casual (entertainers).
名声	míng shēng	reputation; standing (can be good or bad) — 名 míng (name) + 声 shēng (sound; voice; what is heard). What others say and hear about you , your name as it sounds in the world. 名誉	míng yù	reputation
名字	míng zi	name (personal name, general term) — 名 míng (name) + 字 zì (character; in classical usage, the courtesy name given at adulthood). In modern Mandarin, 名字 is the everyday word for a person's name , first name, full name, or nickname depending on context. Neutral register, universally usable.
冥币	míng bì	Ghost money — Ghost money: paper currency printed for burning at funerals and during ghost festivals. The term literally means "underworld currency." 男朋友	nán péng you	boyfriend: a male romantic partner — 男 nán (male) + 朋友 péngyou (friend). The standard word for a boyfriend; 女朋友 nǚ péngyou is the matching girlfriend. The 朋友 base shows how the modern romantic term grew out of the plain word for companion.

人情	rén qíng	human feeling; social obligation; the gift economy of favors — Human feeling, social obligation , the accumulated weight of favors, gifts, and consideration exchanged between people. To "owe someone rénqíng" is to be in their social debt. Gift-giving is one of the primary means of building and repaying 人情. The concept is inseparable from 关系.

荣誉	róng yù	honor; glory
入乡随俗	rù xiāng suí sú	when in a village, follow its customs — adapt to local ways — Lit: enter-village-follow-customs. The essential guide to cross-cultural conduct , observe and adopt local practices rather than imposing your own. Used practically and as a statement of cultural humility. 你到了那里，要入乡随俗 "When you get there, follow local customs."
上流社会	shàng liú shè huì	high society; the upper class; the social elite — 上流 (shàngliú, upper stream; the upper reaches of a river) + 社会. High society, the social elite. The metaphor is of the upper current of a river: those who move at the top of the social flow. Used in both admiring and ironic registers.
上座	shàng zuò	The seat of honor , the seat facing the door, assigned to the most honored guest. The term literally means "upper seat." Understanding the seating logic around 上座 is a practical necessity at any formal Chinese meal. Equivalent: 主位 zhǔwèi.
社会保障	shè huì bǎo zhàng	social security; social welfare system — 社会 + 保障 (bǎozhàng, guarantee; protection; safeguard). The welfare system: pensions, unemployment insurance, medical insurance, and other state-provided protections against life's major risks. The compound mirrors the English "social security" almost exactly in logic.
社会地位	shè huì dì wèi	social status; social standing; position in society — 社会 + 地位 (dìwèi, position; standing; status). One's rank or standing within the social order. Closely related to 面子 , social status is the structural foundation from which face is generated and maintained. Hierarchically sensitive: 地位 implies a vertical ordering.
社会风气	shè huì fēng qì	social atmosphere; moral climate; the prevailing spirit of society — 社会 + 风气 (fēngqì, prevailing atmosphere; social mores; lit. "wind and air"). The ambient moral climate of a society at a given moment: whether honesty, integrity, and mutual care are in the air, or whether cynicism, materialism, and corruption have taken hold.
社会关系	shè huì guān xi	social relationships; social network; connections — 社会 + 关系 (guānxi, relationship; connection). One's web of social connections. In Chinese social contexts, 社会关系 encompasses what is often simply called 关系 , the practical network of relationships that enables things to get done.
社会稳定	shè huì wěn dìng	social stability — 社会 + 稳定 (wěndìng, stable; stability). A central governing priority. 维护社会稳定 (to maintain social stability) appears throughout official documents, legal texts, and policy announcements.
社会信用体系	shè huì xìn yòng tǐ xì	social credit system — 社会 (society) + 信用 (xìnyòng, credit; trustworthiness) + 体系 (tǐxì, system; framework). A multifaceted platform for tracking trustworthiness and compliance across financial, legal, and behavioral domains.
社会学	shè huì xu é	sociology — 社会 + 学 (xué, the study of). The academic discipline. The word makes the Meiji translation story visible: 社会学 was coined as the direct Chinese equivalent of sociology (German Soziologie, French sociologie) through the same process that produced 社会. The discipline and its name arrived together.
社会责任	shè huì zér èn	social responsibility — 社会 + 责任 (zérèn, responsibility; obligation; duty). The obligation one has to the broader social world. Used both in the CSR (Corporate Social Responsibility) sense , 企业社会责任 (qǐyè shèhuì zérèn) , and in the broader sense of individual duty to society.
社会主义	shè huì zhǔ yì	socialism — 社会 + 主义 (zhǔyì, -ism; doctrine; principle). The dominant political compound built on 社会 in the PRC context. 中国特色社会主义 (Zhōngguó tèsè shèhuì zhǔyì, Socialism with Chinese Characteristics) is the formal name for the governing ideology.
社稷	shè jì	the altars of soil and grain; the state, the realm — 社 shè (the soil-god) + 稷 jì (the grain-god). Originally the two altars a ruler maintained, to the powers of the earth and of the harvest. Because land and food are the foundation of any polity, the word came to stand for the state itself: 江山社稷 "the rivers, mountains, and altars," the whole realm.
社交	shè jiāo	social interaction; socializing — 社 shè (social) + 交 jiāo (to associate, to exchange). The act of mixing with others, the give-and-take of social life: 社交活动 "social activities," 社交能力 "social skills," 社交媒体 "social media." It names the personal practice of meeting and getting along with people.
社区	shè qū	community; residential neighbourhood — 社 shè (community) + 区 qū (district, zone). A bounded local community: the people of a single neighbourhood, plus the services and administration that serve them. 社区服务 "community services," 社区医院 "a community clinic," 社区活动 "neighbourhood activities." Smaller and more concrete than 社会.
社团	shè tuán	club; society; an organized group — 社 shè (association) + 团 tuán (a group, a body). A voluntary organized group bound by a shared interest: 学生社团 "student clubs," 民间社团 "civic associations." The everyday word for the clubs and societies people choose to join, especially at school and university.
身份	shēn fèn	identity; status — 身 (self) + 份 (share, allotment): one's allotted position. 身份证 (shēnfènzhèng) is the national ID card, the document requested at every train station, hotel, and SIM-card counter, which makes this compound survival vocabulary rather than abstraction.
声誉	shēng yù	prestige; reputation; standing — 声 shēng (name; repute) + 誉 yù (praise; honor). A more formal, weightier word than 名声, leaning toward esteem and public standing: 国际声誉 "international prestige."
师傅	shī fu	Master , the reliable general-purpose address for service workers and skilled tradespeople. Also the formal term for a craft or trade teacher. The safest default when addressing an unfamiliar adult male in a service or public context.
市场	shì chǎng	Market, in both senses — Market, in both senses: the physical 菜市场 (vegetable market) and the abstract 市场经济 (market economy). 市场部 is the marketing department.
手信	shǒu xìn	A small gift brought back from travel — A small gift brought back from travel: a souvenir or local specialty given to family, friends, or colleagues on return from a trip. The term is particularly common in Cantonese-speaking areas and in Hong Kong. The expectation of bringing 手信 from any notable journey is near-universal.
帅哥	shuài gē	handsome guy; hey, handsome — 帅 (handsome) + 哥. A good-looking young man, and also a friendly way to flag down any young man whose name you don't know, the male counterpart of 美女 (měinǚ).
涮	shuàn	to swish (meat through hot liquid); to shabu
送别	sòng bié	to bid farewell, see off — 送 (see off) plus 别 (to part). A literary, emotionally weighted word for the moment of parting, the subject of countless Tang poems written at the roadside or the river crossing.
送达	sòng dá	to deliver, serve (formally) — 送 (send) plus 达 (reach, arrive). A formal verb stressing successful arrival, used for delivered mail and, in law, for serving legal documents.
送给	sòng gěi	to give to (someone) — 送 (give) plus 给 (to, for the benefit of). The recipient-marking pattern: 送给 + person + thing. The thing crosses over and stays with the receiver.
送货	sòng huò	to deliver goods — 送 (send) plus 货 (goods). Door-to-door delivery: the courier who 送货 to your home, the staple verb of online shopping and takeout.
送礼	sòng lǐ	to give a gift — 送 (give) plus 礼 (gift, ritual courtesy). A separable verb-object pair for the social act of presenting a present, with all the etiquette that surrounds it in Chinese life.
送行	sòng xíng	to see someone off (on a journey) — 送 (see off) plus 行 (to travel, set out). To go to the station, dock, or airport to bid a traveler farewell as they begin a journey.
随意	suí yì	As you like, freely, casually , the softer alternative to 干杯 that allows the recipient to drink as much or as little as they wish. Using 随意 rather than 干杯 is a show of consideration, often offered to guests who are known to drink little or who have already flagged a limitation.

他人	tā rén	others; other people (formal/literary) — 他 tā (he; other) + 人 rén. The formal and written equivalent of 别人. Appears in legal texts, philosophical writing, ethical discussions, and formal prose. In classical Chinese, 他 itself meant "other" before it was narrowed to the third-person pronoun, making 他人 doubly "other."

她字	tā zì	"the 她 character" — shorthand for the gendered-pronoun debate — A meta-term in linguistic and feminist writing. 她字之争 ("the dispute over 她") refers to the recurring debate over whether to use 她, abolish it, or replace it with a non-marked alternative.
祂	tā	He/She (capitalized, of a deity) — 礻 (the spirit/altar radical, a variant of 示) + 也. Coined later than 她, by Chinese Christian translators of the Bible, to render English "He" (capitalized of God) without committing to the human-male radical 亻. 祂 carries no gender; it carries divinity.
台阶	tái jiē	A step, a staircase , and in face contexts, a way out — A step, a staircase , and in face contexts, a way out. 给他一个台阶下 ("give him a step to descend") means to provide a face-saving exit from an awkward position. One of the most useful face-vocabulary phrases in practice.

土地	tǔ dì	land, soil; the local earth-god — 土 tǔ (earth) + 地 dì (ground). In everyday speech, land or soil: 土地肥沃 "fertile land." In folk religion it also names the 土地神 or 土地公, the humble local earth-god of a village or plot, the small-scale heir to the old 社 cult, honoured at modest roadside shrines.

做到	zuò dào	to accomplish; to achieve; to manage to do — 做 zuò (to do; to make) + 到 dào (arrive at the result). Doing that reaches its endpoint: the task is completed, the goal achieved. Carries a sense of managed difficulty , you worked until arrival.
做饭	zuò fàn	to cook (a meal)
做梦	zuò mèng	to dream; (figuratively) to fantasize
做人	zuò rén	to conduct oneself; to be the kind of person one should be — 做 zuò (to do; to act as; to make) + 人 rén. The verb that captures the Confucian view that being human is an achievement, not a given. 做人 is learned: how to navigate relationships, honor obligations, maintain dignity, and treat others with genuine consideration.
做生意	zuò shēng yi	to do business; to trade
做事	zuò shì	to handle matters; to work; to conduct oneself in action
做完	zuò wán	to finish doing
做作	zuò zuo	affected, pretentious
