报	bào	to report, to announce; newspaper; to repay — A hand cuffing a kneeling criminal once meant sentencing; today the same glyph carries newspapers, reports, and the moral arithmetic of repayment.
表	biǎo	surface, to express, a table, a watch — The garment radical worn on the outside: 表 runs from the outer surface of things to showing feeling, the chart on a page, and the watch on a wrist.
唱	chàng	to sing, to chant, to call out — A mouth beside the sun-doubled 昌: the radical names the act of singing while 昌 carries only the sound.
传	chuán	to pass on, transmit, spread; (zhuan) a biography, a commentary — The person radical carrying something onward: one figure handing a thing to the next. From that image grow the senses of 传, read chuan when it means to pass down, transmit, and spread, and read zhuan when it names a biography or a classical commentary.
词	cí	word, term, diction, the sung lyric — Speech beside a phonetic 司: the unit we call a word, the wording a speaker chooses, and the classical lyric form that grew up beside regulated verse to be sung to set tune-patterns.
错	cuò	error, mistake; wrong, incorrect — A metal character whose oldest sense was inlay and grinding; from crossed, interlocking lines it slid to things crossing wrongly, the modern sense of error.
调	diào	(diào) to transfer, tune, accent; (tiáo) to adjust, to blend, to mediate — A high-frequency polyphone with two readings. As diào it means to transfer, a melody or key, a tone, an accent; as tiáo it means to adjust, to blend, to mediate. The reading tells you whether 调 is moving things or harmonizing them.
懂	dǒng	to understand, to know, to grasp — A heart-radical verb where comprehension is felt, not just decoded: to truly get something is to take it in inwardly.
封	fēng	to seal, envelope, to confer (a fief) — A handful of earth heaped over a boundary mound, sealed by hand: 封 ties envelopes, granaries, and feudal grants to the same gesture of closing something off.
改	gǎi	to alter, to change, to correct, to reform — A hand raises a stick over a kneeling child: 改 is change enforced by correction, not drift, which is why it leans toward fixing what is wrong.
告	gào	to tell, to announce, to report — A mouth aimed upward: the character for delivering words to authority, and the root of advertising, accusations, and warnings.
歌	gē	song, lyrics; to sing, to chant — The radical 欠 (an open, exhaling mouth) drives the meaning of song, while 哥 supplies the gē sound.
广	guǎng	wide, broad, to spread — A radical and a word: the cliff-shelter that became the prefix for everything expansive, from Guangzhou to Guangdong to radio broadcasts.
话	huà	words, speech, talk, language — Speech beside a tongue: the everyday character for spoken words. From it grow talking and conversation, the telephone, a dialect or standard language, and the topics and sayings people pass around.
记	jì	to remember, to record, a mark — Speech set beside the self: the act of fixing something so it will not be lost. From that image grow three senses: to remember, to write down, and the mark left behind.
简	jiǎn	simple, brief, terse; a bamboo writing slip — Before paper, a letter was a strip of bamboo; from that narrow slip 简 grew into the words for simple, brief, and a short note.
解	jiě	to untie, to solve, to explain, to dissolve — The character for cutting apart and undoing: untying and liberating, solving and explaining, dissolving and relieving. A polyphone read jiě, jiè, and xiè, pictured as a hand prying an ox horn from the ox.
句	jù	sentence, phrase, a unit of speech — A wrapping line drawn over a mouth: speech bundled into a single unit. From that image grows the sentence, the line of verse, and the measure word that counts the remarks we say.
跨	kuà	to stride; to span; to bestride — The character of the long stride: a foot-radical verb that reaches from "step over a threshold" to "span an era" to the modern policy vocabulary of cross-border, cross-industry, cross-cultural everything.
练	liàn	to practice, to train, to refine, skilled — Before it meant drilling and rehearsal, 练 was a verb for boiling raw silk soft, the repetition of treatment that hardens into skill.
论	lùn	to discuss, a theory or treatise — Reasoned speech put in order: the verb for discussing, debating, and commenting, and the noun for a theory, a view, or a treatise. Also Lun in the title of the Analects.
声	shēng	sound, voice, tone, reputation — A chime-stone struck before a listening ear. From that image grow the everyday word for sound and voice, the tone of speech, and the wider sense of making something known: a declaration, a reputation, a name.
说	shuō	to speak, to say, to explain — To speak, say, explain, and persuade: the character at the center of all communication.
他	tā	he, him; another — 他 was gender-neutral for a millennium until 她 was coined in 1917 to solve a translation problem that Classical Chinese had never needed to solve.
它	tā	it; the non-human third person — 它 began as a picture of a snake and once meant other; in the twentieth century it was reassigned to the non-human third person, completing the homophonous 他/她/它 triad that exists only in writing.
她	tā	she, her — 她 is the youngest character in everyday Chinese, invented in 1917 to translate "she," and the cause of one of the loudest grammatical fights in modern Chinese letters.
题	tí	topic, question, problem, to inscribe — The head-radical character behind every exam question and discussion topic. From the leaf or head of a thing grows the question to solve, the theme to discuss, and the act of inscribing a poem.
通	tōng	to go through; to connect; general; fluent — The walk-movement radical drives a path through every obstacle, giving 通 a single character that covers passage, communication, expertise, and the ordinary general state of things.
问	wèn	to ask, to inquire, to ask after — A mouth at the gate, asking. From that image grow the everyday verb to ask, the noun for a question or problem, the warm act of asking after someone, and the stern act of holding to account.
现	xiàn	to appear, present, now — The luster of jade coming into view. From that glint of something becoming visible grows the whole vocabulary of appearing, of the present moment, and of what is ready and on hand.
笑	xiào	to laugh, to smile, to mock — Bamboo over a bending person: a form that has split scholars since the Song over whether laughter or rustling reeds came first.
新	xīn	new; fresh; recently; novel — Chopping fresh wood with an axe: the character whose compounds span New Year greetings, fresh produce, marriage, and the great question of what counts as modern.
信	xìn	trust, faith, credibility — A person standing by their word: one of the most visually transparent characters in the language, and the virtue that holds every contract, conversation, and relationship together.
帮助	bāng zhù	to help — The everyday word for help, both verb and noun, and how it differs from the lighter 帮 and the separable 帮忙 (to do a favor).
工作	gōng zuò	work, job — The everyday word for work, both the activity and the job itself, and how it differs from 上班 (to go to work), 职业 (occupation), and 事业 (career).
觉得	ju é de	to think, to feel — The everyday verb for giving an opinion or impression, and how it differs from the more formal 认为 and from 想 (to want / to think over).
跨越	kuà yu è	to leap across, to span, to transcend — The compound that lets the Chinese language span gaps: physical, historical, technological, generational. The verb of choice when "越长城" is read figuratively.
其实	qí shí	actually — The everyday adverb for "actually, in fact," used to correct an impression or reveal the real situation, and how it differs from 实际上, 事实上, and 本来.
然后	rán hòu	then; after that — The everyday sequence word for "then, after that," the 先…然后… frame for ordering steps, its overuse as a spoken filler, and how it differs from 接着, 后来, and 于是.
社会	shè huì	society — The word for society that China borrowed from Japan, which had borrowed the characters from China: a compound forged at the altar of the earth.
声音	shēng yīn	sound; voice — A chime-stone struck beside an ear: the word for sound welds raw noise to patterned tone, and Chinese theory kept the two distinct.
文明	wén míng	civilization; cultivated and refined — 文明 names both a state of cultivated life (literacy, ritual, the refined arts) and the global concept of civilization. The two meanings share a single Chinese word, and the doubling is the whole story.
问题	wèn tí	question, problem — One word for both "question" and "problem", and the everyday phrases (没问题, 有问题, 出问题) built from it.
希望	xī wàng	to hope — The everyday word for hope, both the verb and the noun, and how it differs from 想 (to want), 愿意 (to be willing), 盼望 (to long for), and 期待 (to look forward to).
现代	xiàn dài	modern times — The word for the modern era, built from 现 (present, manifest) and 代 (generation, age), how Chinese history slices time into 古代 / 近代 / 现代 / 当代, and how 现代 differs from 现在 now.
一定	yí dìng	definitely; certainly — The everyday word for "certainly, definitely," covering both confident prediction and firm resolve, the 一定要 (must) pattern, and how it differs from 肯定, 必须, and 一定会.
一起	yì qǐ	together — The everyday word for "together," the 跟…一起 frame for joint action, the "in one place" sense, the "altogether" use of 一起, and how it differs from 一块儿, 一同, and 一共.
知道	zhī dào	to know — The everyday verb for knowing a fact, and how it differs from 认识 (to be acquainted with) and 了解 (to understand deeply).
终于	zhōng yú	finally, at last — The adverb for "finally, at last," used when something long-awaited or hard-won comes to pass, where it sits in a sentence, and how it differs from 最后, 总算, and 到底.
爱莫能助	ài mò néng zhù	willing but unable to help — Literally "to love but be unable to aid," used when you sympathize and would gladly help, but cannot. A gracious, slightly rueful way to decline: 我也想帮你，可惜爱莫能助 ("I'd like to help, but sadly it's beyond me"). The 助 again is the aid of 帮助.
按部就班	àn bù jiù bān	to follow the proper sequence: to do things step by step in due order — Lit: according-to-section, settle-into-rank. Originally a writer's image of arranging material into its right divisions and lines; now it means to proceed methodically by the established order, neither rushing nor skipping steps. Here 部 keeps its old sense of an ordered division.
半懂不懂	bàn dǒng bù dǒng	half understanding, half not: imperfectly grasped — A colloquial set phrase built on the 半A不A frame (half-A, not-A). It marks shaky comprehension, the state of catching the gist of a lecture or a foreign language without truly mastering it.
半信半疑	bàn xìn bàn yí	half believing, half doubting — skeptical; not fully convinced — 半 bàn (half) + 信 xìn (to believe; to trust) + 半 bàn (half) + 疑 yí (to doubt; to suspect). The state of being precisely between trust and doubt: believing something enough to entertain it, doubting it enough not to act on it. 我对他说的话半信半疑 = I'm not sure whether to believe what he said.
报仇雪恨	bào chóu xu ě hèn	avenge a wrong and wipe out a grudge — 报仇 (take revenge) paired with 雪恨 (wash away the hatred). The standard four-character phrase for settling a blood debt, frequent in martial-arts fiction and historical drama.
悲欢离合	bēi huān lí hé	sorrows and joys, partings and reunions — Literally "grief, joy, parting, union", the full arc of human fortune in four characters. 离 (parting) and 合 (reunion) form the pair at its heart, the comings and goings of a life. From a famous Song lyric by Su Shi on the moon and human separation.
表里如一	biǎo lǐ rú yī	outside and inside are as one: consistent in word and deed; the same within as without — 表 biǎo = the outside, the surface. 里 lǐ = the inside, the interior. 如一 rú yī = as if one; identical. A person whose outward conduct matches their inner thoughts, who is honest and undivided, with no gap between the surface they show and the substance underneath. A strong compliment to integrity.
博闻强记	bó wén qiáng jì	widely read and retentive: of broad learning and a powerful memory — Lit: broad-hearing-strong-memory. Praise for a person who has read and heard much and remembers it all. Also written 博闻强识, with 识 read zhì in the sense of "to retain."
不耻下问	bù chǐ xià wèn	"not ashamed to ask those below" — intellectual humility; willingness to learn from anyone regardless of status — From the 论语 (Analects) , Confucius praising the minister Kong Wenzi: 敏而好学，不耻下问，是以谓之'文'也 (Quick-minded and fond of learning, not ashamed to ask those of lower rank , this is why he was given the posthumous title 'Wen').
不计其数	bú jì qí shù	countless; too many to count — Literally "not to reckon their number," so many that counting is hopeless. Used for vast, uncountable quantities, the far end of the scale that 多少 stretches toward. The answer to 多少 when the true reply is "beyond counting."
不解之缘	bù jiě zhī yuán	a bond that cannot be untied: an indissoluble tie — Lit: a connection 缘 that cannot 不 be untied 解. An unbreakable bond or lifelong affinity, often with a person, a place, or a pursuit. 他和音乐结下了不解之缘 = he formed a lifelong bond with music. Here 解 is "to untie," and the negation makes the tie permanent.
不可思议	bù kě sī yì	inconceivable; unimaginable — Literally "cannot be thought or discussed," beyond comprehension, originally a Buddhist term for the ineffable. Here 可 ("can / possible"), the first character of 可能, takes its sense of "able to be": what cannot possibly be grasped by thought.
不闻不问	bù wén bù wèn	neither hear nor ask: to pay no attention to a matter at all — Lit: not-hear-not-ask. To stay wholly indifferent, neither listening for news nor inquiring after it. Often a reproach for someone who ignores what should concern them.
不知所措	bù zhī suǒ cuò	at a loss what to do — Literally "not knowing what to set one's hands to", thrown into confusion and unable to act. It is the inner state behind a panicked 怎么办, the moment when you cannot even begin to choose a course of action.
唱独角戏	chàng dú jiǎo xì	to sing a one-man show: to carry the whole thing alone — From opera, where a 独角戏 is a one-actor play. Used when someone is left doing all the talking or all the work while no one else joins in.
朝令夕改	zhāo lìng xī gǎi	issue an order at dawn and change it by dusk — From debates on Han fiscal policy. A sharp criticism of fickle, unstable rule whose constant reversals leave people unable to plan.
沉鱼落雁	chén yú luò yàn	"sinking fish and falling geese" — describing a woman so beautiful that fish dive and birds fall when she passes — From the Zhuangzi, originally a wry critique of relativism in beauty (the fish do not actually find her beautiful , they hide because they are afraid of humans).
传宗接代	chuán zōng jiē dài	to carry on the family line: to continue the ancestral lineage through descendants — Lit: pass on the clan, link the generations. The traditional duty of producing heirs so that the family line does not end. 宗 is the ancestral lineage and 代 the generations; 传 and 接 are the passing-on and the picking-up that keep the chain unbroken.
词不达意	cí bù dá yì	the words fail to convey the meaning: to express oneself poorly — Lit: words-not-reach-meaning. Said when the language a speaker or writer chooses does not match what they meant, so the point comes across muddled or wrong. A common way to apologize for an unclear remark.
错综复杂	cuò zōng fù zá	crisscrossed and tangled: intricate and complicated — Lit: interlaced-gathered-doubled-mixed. Keeps the "crossing" sense of 错: relationships or situations so interwoven and layered that they are hard to untangle. 错综复杂的关系 "a web of tangled relationships."
答非所问	dá fēi suǒ wèn	the answer is not what was asked: to give a reply beside the question — Lit: answer-not-what-asked. A reply that fails to address the actual question, whether from evasion or misunderstanding. A near neighbor of 文不对题, but about spoken answers rather than written work.
大错特错	dà cuò tè cuò	greatly and especially wrong: utterly, completely mistaken — Lit: big-error-particularly-error. An emphatic doubling that drives home how badly someone has misjudged: 你这样想就大错特错了 "if you think that, you are gravely mistaken."
大失所望	dà shī suǒ wàng	to be greatly let down: bitterly disappointed in what one had hoped for — Literally "greatly to lose what one hoped for." Used when reality falls far short of expectation and the disappointment is severe, as when an eagerly awaited result turns out badly. The intensified form of plain 失望.
大有希望	dà yǒu xī wàng	very promising; full of hope — Literally "to greatly have hope," said of a person or prospect with bright chances: 这个项目大有希望 ("this project is very promising"). A warm, encouraging phrase built directly on 希望, the bright mirror of 大失所望.
多多益善	duō duō yì shàn	the more the better — Literally "more and more is the more beneficial," the more the merrier. From a remark attributed to the general Han Xin, who said that as a commander he could lead troops 多多益善 ("the more the better"). Built on the 多 ("many") of 多少.
恩将仇报	ēn jiāng chóu bào	to repay kindness with enmity: to bite the hand that feeds you — 恩 (kindness) is met with 仇 (enmity) as its 报 (return). A sharp condemnation of ingratitude, common in everyday speech to call out a betrayer.
二话不说	èr huà bù shuō	without a second word: at once, without hesitation or argument — Lit: a-second-word-not-said. Said of someone who acts immediately, raising no objection and wasting no breath: 他二话不说就答应了 "he agreed without a moment's hesitation."
废寝忘食	fèi qǐn wàng shí	to forget sleep and meals (in one's work) — Literally "to neglect sleep and forget to eat," so absorbed in work or study that one loses track of basic needs. Used to praise total dedication, though modern usage sometimes carries a gentle warning about overwork. The image of the scholar bent over the desk through the night.
风调雨顺	fēng tiáo yǔ shùn	favorable weather: good conditions for a harvest — Lit: the wind is well-tuned and the rain comes in due course. A set wish for a year of mild, timely weather and a good harvest, often paired with 国泰民安 (the country at peace and the people content). Here 调 takes the tiáo reading, "to harmonize," the weather brought into balance.
风雨同舟	fēng yǔ tóng zhōu	to weather a storm in the same boat — Literally "wind and rain, the same boat", to stand together through hardship, sharing the danger. From the Sunzi Bingfa, which notes that even old enemies in one storm-tossed boat will help each other. The deepest form of being 在一起, bound by shared trial.
封官许愿	fēng guān xǔ yuàn	to dole out titles and make promises: to buy support with offers of office
封妻荫子	fēng qī yìn zǐ	titles for the wife, privilege for the sons: rank and honor extended to one's family — The reward of high office under the empire: a man's success earned a title for his wife and protected status for his sons. Evokes the prestige of the old examination-and-rank system.
夫唱妇随	fū chàng fù suí	the husband sings and the wife follows: a harmonious married couple — Literally "the husband leads, the wife accompanies." A traditional image of marital harmony in which the couple act in accord, one taking the lead and the other in step.
改头换面	gǎi tóu huàn miàn	change the head, swap the face: a superficial makeover — Originally Buddhist, on the soul taking a new body. Now usually pejorative: the form is redone but the substance is unchanged, like a rebranded scam.
改邪归正	gǎi xié guī zhèng	to abandon the crooked and return to the straight: to mend one's ways — Lit: change-evil-return-upright. Said of someone who gives up wrongdoing and comes back to honest living. 邪 (crooked, evil) is the standing opposite of 正.
高谈阔论	gāo tán kuò lùn	to talk loftily and expansively: to hold forth grandly, often emptily — Literally "lofty talk, broad discoursing." Describes someone holding forth at length on grand themes, frequently with the implication that the talk is more impressive than substantial. Can be neutral (spirited, wide-ranging conversation) but often carries a faint edge of "all talk."
歌功颂德	gē gōng sòng dé	sing of deeds and praise virtue: to lavish flattering praise — Here 歌 works as a verb, to sing the praises of. Now usually critical, marking hollow or sycophantic eulogy of those in power.
革故鼎新	gé gù dǐng xīn	reform the old, establish the new — From the Book of Changes. The more active, transformative counterpart to 与时俱进; where 与时俱进 describes keeping pace, 革故鼎新 describes active renovation. The 鼎 (bronze tripod) is a symbol of legitimate authority; 鼎新 means to inaugurate a new order as one would inaugurate a new dynasty.
古往今来	gǔ wǎng jīn lái	from ancient times to the present — Literally "the ancient goes, the present comes," spanning all of history up to now. Used to introduce a truth that has held across the ages: 古往今来，人人都怕死 ("from ancient times to now, everyone fears death"). Here 今 is the classical single character for "the present, now."
古为今用	gǔ wéi jīn yòng	make the past serve the present — To draw on the heritage of antiquity for present-day purposes, using old wisdom, forms, or techniques in modern life. Often paired with 洋为中用 ("make the foreign serve China"), the two together forming a well-known formula for selectively adapting the old and the foreign to modern Chinese needs.
故步自封	gù bù zì fēng	to confine oneself to old ways: to be complacent and refuse to progress — Literally "wall yourself in with your old footsteps." 封 here is the self-imposed boundary. A common criticism of stagnant institutions or people who resist new ideas.
顾此失彼	gù cǐ shī bǐ	attend to this and lose that — unable to manage everything at once — 顾 gù (to attend to; to look after) + 此 cǐ (this) + 失 shī (to lose) + 彼 bǐ (that; the other). Fix your attention on one thing and something else slips away. Describes resource constraints, divided attention, and the predicament of managing competing priorities.
哈哈大笑	hā hā dà xiào	to roar with laughter: a full belly laugh — 哈哈 is the sound of laughter itself, 大笑 a big laugh. The everyday, vivid way to say someone burst out laughing. Common in narration and casual speech alike.
毫无问题	háo wú wèn tí	without the slightest problem; absolutely fine — A stronger, more emphatic version of 没问题, using 毫无 ("not the slightest"). It asserts that something is completely fine or certain, with no doubt or difficulty whatever. Common in confident assurances and formal contexts.
何去何从	hé qù hé cóng	which way to turn; what course to take — Literally "where to go, what to follow", the weighing of a hard choice between paths. A more literary cousin of 怎么办, asking not just "what to do" but "which direction to commit to". Here 何 is the classical "how / what" that 怎么 replaced in everyday speech.
话不投机	huà bù tóu jī	the talk does not meet: the conversation does not click — Lit: words-not-matching-the-moment. Said when two people simply cannot find common ground in conversation. The fuller saying 话不投机半句多 adds that "when talk does not click, even half a sentence is too much."
话里有话	huà lǐ yǒu huà	there are words within the words: a remark carries a hidden meaning — Lit: inside-the-words-there-are-words. Said when someone speaks with an implied second meaning, hinting at more than the surface remark admits.
焕然一新	huàn rán yī xīn	"completely transformed to something new" — completely renewed; a total makeover — 焕然 (huànrán, resplendently bright; the appearance of something that has been renovated or refreshed) + 一新 (completely new). The appearance of something that has undergone a total transformation: 装修完工后，房间焕然一新 (after the renovation was complete, the room was completely transformed).
绘声绘色	huì shēng huì sè	painting both sound and color: vivid and lifelike (of a description) — Lit: paint-sound-paint-color. Of telling or describing something so vividly that the listener seems to hear and see it. High praise for a gripping, animated account.
积毁销骨	jī huǐ xiāo gǔ	accumulated slander dissolves bones — From the Shiji (Records of the Grand Historian): repeated criticism, even if each instance is minor, accumulates into something that can destroy a person entirely. This is the specific personal consequence that Pang Cong feared, and that happened to him.
记忆犹新	jì yì yóu xīn	the memory is still fresh: as vivid as if it just happened — Lit: memory-still-new. Said of an event or scene one recalls so clearly that time seems not to have dulled it. 至今记忆犹新 "to this day it remains fresh in my mind."
坚定不移	jiān dìng bù yí	firm and unshakable — Literally "firm and settled, not shifting", steadfast in resolve, never wavering. The 定 ("settled, fixed") at its heart is the same 定 in 一定; here it describes a will that has fixed on its course. The deepest form of the resolve 一定要 voices.
简练揣摩	jiǎn liàn chuǎi mó	to study and refine through relentless, focused effort — From the Zhanguo Ce account of Su Qin reading until he was exhausted, jabbing his own thigh with an awl to stay awake. 练 carries the sense of working material over and over until it is honed.
简明扼要	jiǎn míng è yào	simple, clear, and gripping the essentials: succinct and pointed — 简 simple, 明 clear, 扼要 to grip the crux. Common in formal evaluation of summaries and briefings, marking a text that wastes no words.
见笑	jiàn xiào	to be laughed at: forgive my poor effort — Lit: be-seen laughed-at. A set humble phrase: 让您见笑了 ("I've let you laugh at me") deflects praise for one's own work or skill. Polite, slightly formal register.
将错就错	jiāng cuò jiù cuò	to take the mistake and run with it: to leave a wrong thing as it is — Lit: take-the-error-go-with-the-error. Rather than undo a mistake already made, one accepts it and proceeds on that footing, often because correcting it would cost more than living with it.
教我如何不想她	jiào wǒ rú hé bù xiǎng tā	"How could I not think of her" — Liu Bannong's 1920 poem, set to music by Zhao Yuanren — Not classical, but the most famous early appearance of 她 in print. The poem is addressed to "her", variously read as a lover, the homeland (Liu was abroad in Britain when he wrote it), or both. The song became one of the defining works of early modern Chinese music.
巾帼不让须眉	jīn guó bù ràng xū méi	"the woman's headdress yields nothing to the man's beard" — a woman who is the equal of any man — 巾帼 jīnguó , a woman's hair ornament, by metonymy "woman." 须眉 xūméi: beard and brows, by metonymy "man." Used in praise of female generals (Mulan, Hua Mulan; Liang Hongyu), athletes, and accomplished women.
兢兢业业	jīng jīng yè yè	diligent and careful in one's work — A phrase of high praise for someone who does their work with constant care and seriousness, never cutting corners. 兢兢 conveys cautious diligence; 业业 the steady attention to one's 业 (work, duties). Common in performance reviews and tributes, the model of the conscientious worker.
口是心非	kǒu shì xīn fēi	the mouth says yes but the heart says no — saying one thing while thinking another — Lit: mouth-yes-heart-no. The classic expression for hypocrisy or false agreement: when outward words and inner feelings are opposites. Used for people who agree to your face while disagreeing inside, or who say what they think you want to hear rather than what they actually believe.
跨海越洋	kuà hǎi yu è yáng	"to span seas and cross oceans" — to travel vast distances — A modern four-character compound that pairs 跨 and 越 in parallel slots: exactly the kind of construction Chinese loves. Used for international trade, transcontinental travel, or any long voyage. Not classical, but stylistically formal enough to appear in policy speeches and ceremonial writing.
跨马提刀	kuà mǎ tí dāo	"to mount one's horse and lift one's blade" — to ride out armed and ready — A four-character battle-formula common in classical novels and modern wuxia. The image is a general or knight-errant hoisting himself astride his horse with weapon drawn: the moment of departure for combat.
跨年度	kuà nián dù	"crossing fiscal years" — spanning more than one annual cycle — Bureaucratic and accounting vocabulary, not classical, but ubiquitous in modern policy and business documents. 跨年度项目 (a project spanning fiscal years), 跨年度预算 (multi-year budget). Useful as a tag of how 跨 has migrated from the body's stride into the calendar's accounting.
老调重弹	lǎo diào chóng tán	to rehash old ideas: to bring out a tired theme again — Lit: to pluck the old tune once more. A dismissive phrase for repeating a stale argument or worn-out idea as if it were new. Here 调 takes the diào reading, "a tune, a melody."
雷电交加	léi diàn jiāo jiā	thunder and lightning together — a violent storm; used of chaotic, intense situations — Lit: thunder-lightning-together-intensify. Describes a fierce storm where both thunder (雷 léi) and lightning (电 diàn) are striking simultaneously. Used metaphorically for any situation of simultaneous, intense upheaval: a chaotic meeting, an emotional confrontation, a turbulent period.
离题万里	lí tí wàn lǐ	to stray ten thousand li from the topic: to wander hopelessly off subject — Lit: leave-topic-ten-thousand-li. The 万里 (ten thousand li) is hyperbole for an enormous distance, so the phrase describes talk or writing that has drifted so far it has lost the thread entirely.
离乡背井	lí xiāng bèi jǐng	to leave one's native land — Literally "to leave the village and turn one's back on the well", to go far from home, often driven by hardship. The 井 (well) stands for the home community that gathered around shared water. Here 离 is the verb of parting at its most poignant, leaving the place one belongs to.
理所当然	lǐ suǒ dāng rán	as it should be by reason; a matter of course — Literally "what reason makes ought-to-be-so," something so right and natural that it needs no defense. 当然 ("of course") and 应当 ("ought to") share the 当 of fitting necessity. Sometimes used critically, of someone who takes a kindness as 理所当然 ("for granted").
力所能及	lì suǒ néng jí	within one's power; as far as one is able — Literally "what one's strength can reach," the things that lie within one's ability to do. The bright mirror of 无能为力: 我会尽力所能及 ("I'll do all I can"). Again built on 能, the capacity at the root of "possible."
练兵秣马	liàn bīng mò mǎ	drill the troops and feed the horses: ready oneself for the contest — 练 here is its oldest active sense, to drill. The image is an army putting in the reps before battle, so the phrase travels easily to any team or person training hard ahead of a test.
练达老成	liàn dá lǎo chéng	practiced and worldly, mature and steady — 练达 means seasoned in the ways of the world; pairs with 老成 (mature beyond one's years). A literary compliment for someone whose poise comes from experience, not age alone.
寥寥无几	liáo liáo wú jǐ	very few; scarcely any — Literally "sparse, sparse, not even a few," describing something so scarce it can almost be counted on one hand. Here 几 ("a few") takes its statement sense rather than its question sense. The mirror image of 多多益善.
懵懵懂懂	měng měng dǒng dǒng	muddled and only half-aware: in a fog — A vivid reduplication pairing 懵 (měng, dazed) with 懂 (dǒng, understand) to describe a hazy, half-comprehending state, like a child too young to grasp what is happening or a person fresh out of sleep.
名不虚传	míng bù xū chuán	the reputation is not falsely spread: to live up to one's name; to be as good as people say — Lit: the name is not emptily passed around. Said of a person or thing whose fame turns out to be well deserved, the praise that circulated was no exaggeration. Here 传 keeps the chuán reading, the spreading of a reputation.
名存实亡	míng cún shí wáng	existing in name but not in reality — Literally "the name survives, the reality is dead," describing something that keeps its title or form while its substance has vanished, an alliance, a rule, or an institution that is hollow inside. The 名 versus 实 contrast, the gap between what is called and what truly is, that 其实 itself points to.
名副其实	míng fù qí shí	the name matches the reality — Literally "the name fits its reality," said of someone or something that truly lives up to its reputation or title. 名 (name) and 实 (reality) match. The opposite is 名不副实 ("the name does not match the reality"), all show, no substance. Note the shared 其实 morphemes 其 and 实.
名正言顺	míng zhèng yán shùn	the name is right and the speech follows smoothly — acting with proper authority and justification — Lit: name-correct speech-flows. Directly from the Analects 13.3 argument: when the name is rectified, speech follows naturally, and what follows from speech can be accomplished. Used today when someone has the proper credentials or title to do something , "now that you have the title, you can act."
明知故问	míng zhī gù wèn	to ask knowing the answer — a rhetorical or teasing question — Lit: clearly-know-yet-ask. For when someone already knows the answer but asks anyway, either to tease, to confirm, or to draw out a confession. 你明知故问！"You already know the answer; why are you asking?"
摸着石头过河	mō zhe shí tou guò hé	crossing the river by feeling for stones — Deng's gradualist pragmatism — The most important political metaphor of the reform era. 摸 (to grope, to feel one's way) + 着 (progressive aspect) + 石头 (stones) + 过河 (cross the river).
墨守成规	mò shǒu chéng guī	to stick rigidly to established rules — The natural antonym of 与时俱进. 墨守 refers to Mozi's legendary skill at defensive warfare: holding a position without yielding. The phrase criticizes inflexibility and mechanical adherence to convention.
南腔北调	nán qiāng běi diào	a jumble of accents: speech mixing many regional tongues — Lit: southern resonance and northern tones. Said of speech that mixes accents from all over, or of a gathering where many dialects are heard at once. Here 调 takes the diào reading, "accent, manner of speech."
难解难分	nán jiě nán fēn	hard to untie, hard to part: locked together — Lit: hard 难 to untie 解, hard 难 to separate 分. Two sides so entangled they cannot be pulled apart: a deadlocked contest, a fierce struggle, or two people inseparably close. 双方打得难解难分 = the two sides fought to a standstill.
你觉得呢？	nǐ ju é de ne ?	What do you think? — An extremely common conversational turn, throwing the question back to the other person. The particle 呢 ne softens it and invites their view. One of the most natural ways to ask for an opinion in spoken Chinese, and a good phrase to keep a conversation going.
念念不忘	niàn niàn bù wàng	to keep thinking of without ever forgetting — to hold something constantly in mind — Lit: thought-after-thought, never forgetting. 念念 doubles the character to mean "moment after moment of mind." Used for an attachment, a wish, or a person one cannot let go of: 他对故乡念念不忘 (he never stops thinking of his hometown).
念念有词	niàn niàn yǒu cí	to mutter words under one breath: to chant or murmur incantations — Lit: muttering-and-having-words. Originally of a priest or sorcerer murmuring a spell, now of anyone muttering steadily to themselves, whether praying, reciting, or grumbling.
齐心协力	qí xīn xié lì	to make a concerted effort — Literally "aligned hearts, joined strength", everyone striving together as one. A close cousin of 同心协力, stressing that all hearts are lined up in the same direction. The collective togetherness that 一起 points toward when a group acts as a team.
前因后果	qián yīn hòu guǒ	the full cause and effect — Literally "the prior cause and the later result", the whole chain from why to what-happened. To know the 前因后果 is to understand a matter completely, from the reason a 为什么 seeks to the outcome it explains. 因 (cause) and 果 (effect, "fruit") are the two poles of explanation.
人言可畏	rén yán kě wèi	what people say is to be feared — A shorter, more colloquial formulation of the same truth: public opinion is a force that should not be underestimated. Less analytical than 三人成虎, more emotionally immediate. Often used in the context of reputation and social pressure.
任劳任怨	rèn láo rèn yuàn	to work hard without complaint — Literally "to take on the toil and take on the grumbling," to shoulder hard work and even unfair blame without complaint. A warm compliment for a dependable, uncomplaining worker, and a quietly admired virtue in the Chinese workplace.
日新月异	rì xīn yu è yì	changing by the day and the month — rapid, ceaseless progress — Literally "new each day, different each month," for change so fast it is visible from one stretch of time to the next. The standard phrase for the breakneck pace of modern development, especially technology and the transformation of cities, the felt texture of living in the 现代 age.
融会贯通	róng huì guàn tōng	"melt together, thread through": to master a subject by integrating all its parts — 融 róng (to melt; to merge) + 会 huì (to come together) + 贯 guàn (to thread through; to run a string through) + 通 (to penetrate fully).
三人成虎	sān rén chéng hǔ	three people make a tiger — A minister warns his king: if three people all claim there is a tiger in the marketplace, even the skeptical will believe it. The Zhanguo Ce parable of rumor that outlasts the warning against it.
三言两语	sān yán liǎng yǔ	"three words, two phrases" — in a few words; briefly — 三言 (three words) and 两语 (two phrases): just a handful of words, said briefly. Used to mean something can be (or cannot be) explained in a sentence or two: 三言两语说不清 (it can't be explained in a few words).
删繁就简	shān fán jiù jiǎn	cut the complex and move toward the simple: prune the excess — 删 to delete, 繁 the complicated, 就 to go toward, 简 the simple. An editor's motto, applied to prose, procedures, and design alike.
善始善终	shàn shǐ shàn zhōng	good beginning, good ending; to see something through properly from start to finish — Lit: good-start-good-end. The virtue of completing what one begins, and completing it well. 终 zhōng (end, conclusion) is the Classical Chinese counterpart to the grammatical 了 , both mark the reaching of an end. This idiom captures the moral weight of completion.
身不由己	shēn bù yóu jǐ	the body is not controlled by oneself — driven by circumstances beyond one's will — 身 shēn (body; self) + 不 bù (not) + 由 yóu (controlled by; following from) + 己 jǐ (oneself). One's own body belongs to external forces , obligation, circumstance, social pressure, or fate. The counterpart to agency: when 他 (the other, external) overrides 己 (the self).
声东击西	shēng dōng jī xī	"feign east, strike west" — strategic deception; misdirection before attacking — Lit: declare-east-strike-west. A classic military stratagem from the Sun Tzu tradition , make noise in the east to draw attention, then attack from the west. Used broadly for any deceptive misdirection tactic in business, politics, or argument. 他用声东击西的策略迷惑了对手。
实事求是	shí shì qiú shì	to seek truth from facts — address problems realistically — A classical phrase, later a famous modern slogan, meaning to approach a 问题 on the basis of facts rather than wishful thinking or ideology. 实事 (real matters) + 求是 (seek what is so). The standard expression for a pragmatic, evidence-based way of analysing and solving problems.
事出有因	shì chū yǒu yīn	everything happens for a reason — Literally "when a matter arises, there is a cause", nothing comes out of nowhere. It is the quiet conviction behind every 为什么: ask the question and there will be a 因 (cause) to find. The 因 here is the same one in 因为 ("because").
势在必行	shì zài bì xíng	bound to happen; imperative — Literally "the momentum lies in must-be-done", a course of action that circumstances make unavoidable. It is certainty of the necessary kind, the 必 ("must") that 一定 and 必须 both touch, applied to events that simply have to proceed.
守望相助	shǒu wàng xiāng zhù	to watch out for and help one another — From the Mencius: neighbors keeping watch and lending aid to each other, the ideal of a community that protects its own. 守望 (to keep watch) + 相助 (to help one another). Invoked for solidarity in hard times.
水到渠成	shuǐ dào qú chéng	when water flows, a channel forms — Literally "when the water arrives, the channel is made", success comes naturally once conditions ripen, with no forcing. It is the gentle counterpart to 终于's hard-won arrival: the long-awaited outcome that, in the end, simply falls into place.
水泄不通	shuǐ xiè bù tōng	"not even water can leak through": packed solid; completely blocked or surrounded — 水 shuǐ (water) + 泄 xiè (to leak; to drain out) + 不 bù (not) + 通 (to pass through). So densely packed that not even a drop of water could trickle out.
说来话长	shuō lái huà cháng	to start telling it makes a long story: it is a long story — Lit: to-speak-of-it-the-words-grow-long. The standard preface to a complicated explanation, the Chinese equivalent of "well, it's a long story."
说一不二	shuō yī bù èr	say one, not two — a person whose word is final; absolute authority — Lit: say-one-not-two. To speak once and have it stand; not to be contradicted. Describes a figure of absolute authority within their domain. Can be used admiringly (a decisive leader) or critically (an autocrat). 他在公司里说一不二。Tā zài gōngsī lǐ shuō yī bù èr. "In the company, what he says goes."
四面楚歌	sì miàn chǔ gē	"Chu songs on all four sides" — besieged on every side; utterly isolated — From the fall of Xiang Yu, the Hegemon-King of Chu, at the battle of Gaixia (202 BCE). Surrounded by Han forces, Xiang Yu heard his enemies singing the songs of his own homeland of Chu from every direction, and concluded that Chu had already fallen and his men had deserted to the enemy.
四通八达	sì tōng bā dá	"reaching in four and through eight" — accessible in every direction; well-connected — 四通 (passing through in the four directions) plus 八达 (extending out the eight ways). Used of roads, railways, and transport networks that radiate to everywhere, and by extension of any hub that connects in all directions. A 交通四通八达 city is one you can reach and leave by any route.
所谓伊人	suǒ wèi yī rén	"the one of whom I speak" — the beloved, the one I yearn for — From the Shijing (诗经) poem 蒹葭 ("Reeds"). One of the most famous lines of classical poetry, and the locus classicus for 伊 as feminine third person. The poem itself is gender-ambiguous in the classical text but read across two millennia as referring to a beloved woman across the river.
他山之石	tā shān zhī shí	stones from other hills — outside perspectives can polish your own jade — From the Book of Songs (诗经): "stones from other hills can be used to grind jade." External viewpoints, foreign models, and outside criticism all carry value that self-regard misses. 他山 (other hills) + 之 (possessive particle) + 石 (stones).
昙花一现	tán huā yí xiàn	to flower briefly and vanish — Literally "the night-blooming cereus shows itself once," for something splendid that lasts only a moment and is gone. The flower opens for a single short while in the night, then withers. A poignant image of fleeting brilliance, the shadow side of "a little while."
谈笑风生	tán xiào fēng shēng	to chat and laugh with verve: lively, easy conversation — Lit: talk-laugh wind-rises, as if a fresh breeze sprang up from the talk. Describes a person who holds a room with relaxed, witty conversation. A warm compliment to a host or a speaker.
韬光养晦	tāo guāng yǎng huì	hide your brightness and nurture your obscurity — keep a low international profile while building strength — 韬光 (conceal one's brilliance) + 养晦 (cultivate obscurity, from 晦 = darkness, the dark of the moon).
天经地义	tiān jīng dì yì	self-evident truth; axiomatic — too obvious to argue about — Lit: heaven's warp and earth's weft. 经 jīng = warp threads (the vertical structural threads in a loom); 义 yì = weft and righteous pattern. Together they constitute the fabric of moral reality , what is simply given. "孩子要孝顺父母，这是天经地义的事。
通情达理	tōng qíng dá lǐ	"through to feeling, reaching to reason": sensible, reasonable, understanding — 通 (to be through to) + 情 qíng (human feeling; the way people are) + 达 dá (to reach) + 理 lǐ (reason; principle). A person who is 通情达理 understands both how people feel and what reason requires, and weighs the two fairly.
通俗易懂	tōng sú yì dǒng	plain and easy to understand — High praise for writing or speech: 通俗 (accessible, popular) and 易懂 (easy to understand) together describe an explanation pitched so clearly that anyone can follow it. The standard compliment for good teaching.
同甘共苦	tóng gān gòng kǔ	to share joys and hardships — Literally "together the sweet, shared the bitter", to go through good times and bad side by side. It captures the deepest sense of 跟 as "with", a togetherness that holds through difficulty. Here 同 ("together") plays the accompanying role 跟 carries in speech.
同心协力	tóng xīn xié lì	to work together with one heart — Literally "same heart, joined strength", to pull together toward a shared goal in full unity. It is the spirit behind 一起努力 ("work hard together") raised to its ideal: not just acting jointly, but acting as one mind and one effort.
投桃报李	tóu táo bào lǐ	throw a peach, return a plum: to repay a kindness in kind; mutual gift-giving — This idiom shares the verb 投 tóu (to throw; to give) that drives 投票, though it does not contain 票 itself, so it sits here as a near neighbor on the 投 thread rather than a 票 idiom. 投桃报李 comes from the Shījīng (Book of Songs): you toss me a peach, I send back a plum.
推陈出新	tuī chén chū xīn	push out the old and bring forth the new — To discard the outdated and create something new, the constant renewal that drives a thing forward into its modern form. Used of art, technology, and institutions that modernize by building fresh work on inherited foundations rather than simply repeating the past.
往事如烟	wǎng shì rú yān	the past is like drifting smoke — Literally "bygone matters are like smoke", the past fading and dispersing beyond grasp. Here 往 takes its time sense, 往事 ("past events"), and the phrase carries a gentle melancholy about how what is gone slips away. The mirror of 往 reaching backward rather than ahead.
望梅止渴	wàng méi zhǐ kě	to quench thirst by gazing at plums — From a tale of Cao Cao, who told thirsty troops that plum groves lay ahead, so the thought of sour plums made their mouths water and eased their thirst. It means consoling oneself with an empty hope or imagined prospect. The 望 of gazing-toward is the same root that gives 希望 its reach.
为人师表	wéi rén shī biǎo	to be a teacher and model for others: to set an example worthy of imitation — 为人 wéi rén = to act as, to be for others. 师 shī = teacher. 表 biǎo = model; the standard others measure themselves against. To serve as a moral and intellectual example, especially said of teachers who must embody what they teach.
文不对题	wén bù duì tí	the writing does not match the topic: off the point, irrelevant — Lit: writing-not-match-topic. Said of an essay or answer that wanders away from what was asked, or of a reply that misses the actual question. The classic teacher's note on a stray composition.
问题的关键	wèn tí de guān jiàn	the crux of the problem; the key issue — A very common phrase pairing 问题 with 关键 (key, crux) to point at the heart of a matter: 问题的关键在于… ("the crux of the problem lies in…"). Used constantly in analysis and discussion to single out what really matters, the decisive point on which everything turns.
我觉得无所谓。	wǒ ju é de wú suǒ wèi .	I don't think it matters either way; I have no preference. — A useful everyday phrase pairing 觉得 with 无所谓 (it makes no difference, I don't mind). Used to signal an easygoing, no-strong-opinion stance when asked to choose. Shows how 觉得 frames even a non-opinion as a personal impression.
无可奈何	wú kě nài hé	nothing can be done about it — helpless; resigned; having no recourse — 无 (nothing) + 可 (can) + 奈何 (what can be done; how to deal with). The expression of pure helplessness: there is no available action. 无可奈何，只能接受。(There was nothing to be done; one could only accept it.) One of the most-used classical phrases in modern speech.
无能为力	wú néng wéi lì	powerless; unable to do anything about it — Lit: have-no-ability-to-act-with-force. 无能 = lacking ability; 为力 = to exert effort/force. The standard idiom for expressing helplessness in the face of a problem. 对于这件事，我也无能为力 "I am also powerless over this matter." Formal and literary; widely used.
先来后到	xiān lái hòu dào	first come, first served — Literally "the early-comers and the later-arrivers", the order of precedence set by who came first. It rests on the same 先 ("first") and 后 ("after") that frame 然后, here applied to fairness in turn-taking and priority. The natural order of a queue.
现身说法	xiàn shēn shuō fǎ	to appear in person and teach by example: to instruct from one's own experience — Lit: show-the-body-and-expound-the-teaching. Originally Buddhist, of a deity appearing in bodily form to preach. Now it means to teach others by drawing on what one has personally lived through.
小题大做	xiǎo tí dà zuò	to make a big production of a small topic — to overreact — 小 xiǎo (small) + 题 tí (topic, subject) + 大 dà (big) + 做 (to make, to treat). Originally from the examination tradition, where a trivial topic was expanded into a grand essay; now it means blowing a minor matter out of proportion.
笑里藏刀	xiào lǐ cáng dāo	a dagger hidden in a smile: friendly on the surface, treacherous beneath — Lit: laugh-inside hide-knife. Traced to the Tang minister Lǐ Yìfǔ, whose mild smile masked a ruthless heart. Names a person whose warmth is a weapon, the dark twin of 微笑.
心想事成	xīn xiǎng shì chéng	may what the heart wishes come true — 心 xīn (heart) + 想 xiǎng (think; wish) + 事 shì (matter; affair) + 成 chéng (to succeed; to be accomplished). A blessing structure: heart-wishes turn into accomplished realities. Among the most common felicitations in Chinese , standard at New Year (新年快乐，心想事成！), birthdays, and before examinations.
信口开河	xìn kǒu kāi hé	to open the mouth like a river — to speak carelessly; to say whatever comes to mind without regard for truth — 信 xìn here shifts sense: 信 kǒu = to trust one's mouth, to follow wherever the mouth goes (the archaic sense of 信 as "to follow freely"). 开河 kāi hé = to open a river, to let it flow unobstructed.
形影不离	xíng yǐng bù lí	form and shadow never part: inseparable — Lit: form and shadow do not separate. A shadow follows its form everywhere; two people who are 形影不离 are constant companions, always together. Warm and affectionate, used for close friends, devoted couples, or a child who will not leave a parent's side.
虚有其表	xū yǒu qí biǎo	to have only its surface: impressive on the outside but hollow within; all show and no substance — 虚 xū = empty; hollow; in vain. 有其表 yǒu qí biǎo = to have (only) its outer appearance. The phrase describes something or someone that looks fine on the surface but lacks real worth underneath. 这家公司虚有其表，其实早就负债累累。
循序渐进	xún xù jiàn jìn	to advance step by step in order — Literally "following the sequence, advancing gradually", to progress in proper order without rushing. It is the principle behind any 先…然后…最后 plan: each step in its turn, the next only after the last. A watchword in learning and training.
训练有素	xùn liàn yǒu sù	trained to a standard: well-drilled, disciplined — 素 here means a long-cultivated grounding. Praises a team, unit, or staff whose competence is visibly the product of systematic 训练 rather than luck.
鸦雀无声	yā qu è wú shēng	not a crow or sparrow to be heard — dead silence — The silence so complete even the loudest birds have stopped. The stock description of a classroom after a hard question, a courtroom before a verdict, a banquet after an unwise toast. Its cousin 悄无声息 describes stealth rather than hush.
言传身教	yán chuán shēn jiào	to teach by word and by example: to instruct both through speech and through one's own conduct — Lit: words pass it on, the body teaches it. Describes the ideal of a teacher or parent who not only explains in words (言传) but also models the lesson through their own actions (身教). The deeper teaching is the one carried by example.
言而有信	yán ér yǒu xìn	to speak and be trustworthy — to be as good as one's word — 言 yán (to speak; words) + 而 ér (and; then; connecting two qualities) + 有 yǒu (to have; to possess) + 信 xìn (trustworthiness).
言归正传	yán guī zhèng zhuàn	let the words return to the main story: to get back to the main topic — Lit: words return to the proper narrative. A storyteller's phrase, after a digression, for steering back to the central thread. Notably 传 takes the zhuàn reading here, since 正传 means the main body of the tale, the narrative proper, not a casual transmission.
言简意赅	yán jiǎn yì gāi	brief words, complete meaning — concise and comprehensive — 简 jiǎn = brief, few; 意 yì = meaning, intention; 赅 gāi = complete, thorough (a relatively rare character used almost exclusively in this idiom). The highest praise for a piece of writing or a speaker: nothing wasted, nothing missing.
掩耳盗铃	yǎn ěr dào líng	covering one's ears to steal the bell — self-deception — From the Lüshi Chunqiu: a thief found the great bell of the fallen Fan clan too heavy to carry, decided to break it up, and covered his own ears against the clang so no one would hear.
一步跨过	yī bù kuà guò	"to cross over in a single step" — to clear an obstacle in one move — Not a classical 成语 but a fixed conversational formula. Often used in development and policy rhetoric: 一步跨过工业化 "to leap past industrialization in a single step." Carries a faint flavor of skipping stages: sometimes admiring (decisive progress), sometimes ironic (rushing through what should have been…
一唱百和	yì chàng bǎi hè	one sings, a hundred answer: a single voice draws a crowd of agreement — One person 唱 starts the note and a hundred others 和 (hè) chime in. Used for an idea or call that instantly wins broad, eager support.
一唱一和	yì chàng yì hè	one sings, the other echoes: to chime in and back each other up — Originally one voice leads and another harmonizes (和 hè, to respond in tune). Now usually mildly negative: two people egging each other on or colluding to push the same line.
一蹴而就	yí cù ér jiù	to succeed in one step — Literally "one stamp of the foot and it is done," to accomplish something in a single easy stroke. Most often used in the negative, to warn that hard things cannot be done all at once: 学语言不能一蹴而就 ("you can't master a language in one go"). The single-action 一 again, the cousin of 一下子.
一概而论	yī gài ér lùn	to lump together and judge as one: to treat distinct cases as if identical — Literally "to discuss everything by one measure." Almost always negated: 不能一概而论 bùnéng yī gài ér lùn "you cannot lump them all together," a standard caution against treating different cases with a single blanket judgement.
一举两得	yī jǔ liǎng dé	one move, two gains — kill two birds with one stone — Lit: one-raise/move-two-gains. 举 here is a verbal measure word (one lift/movement). A perfect analogue of "kill two birds with one stone." 这样做一举两得 "Doing it this way achieves two goals at once." Among the most common four-character idioms in business and daily speech.
一目了然	yī mù liǎo rán	"one glance makes it clear" — obvious at a single look — 一 (one) + 目 (eye-glance) + 了然 (clearly understood). Describes things immediately comprehensible , a diagram that explains itself, an argument whose logic is visible. The phrase appears in Qing dynasty texts and entered general usage by the early Republic period.
一往无前	yì wǎng wú qián	to press forward without turning back — Literally "going straight on, nothing ahead can stop it", an image of fearless forward drive. The 往 here is pure forward motion, the direction-of-travel sense, intensified into dauntless momentum. Said of armies, reformers, and anyone who advances undeterred.
一知半解	yì zhī bàn jiě	to know a little, to half-understand: superficial knowledge — Lit: one part 一 known 知, half 半 understood 解. A shallow, fragmentary grasp of something, knowing a bit but not the whole. 对这门学问他只是一知半解 = he has only a superficial grasp of this subject. Usually mildly critical of half-baked understanding.
一字一句	yī zì yī jù	word by word, line by line: spoken or read with careful deliberation — Lit: one-character-one-sentence. Describes speaking, reading, or copying out so slowly and clearly that each character and each phrase lands on its own. 一字一句地说 "to say it word by word."
以怨报德	yǐ yuàn bào dé	to repay virtue with resentment — An inversion of the Confucian ideal: 德 (virtue, kindness) is answered with 怨 (resentment). The phrase appears in the Analects' discussion of how to respond to good and to harm.
义正词严	yì zhèng cí yán	righteous in cause and stern in speech: to speak with upright authority — Lit: principle-upright-words-stern. Said of someone whose argument rests on a just cause and whose wording is firm and severe, leaving no room for doubt that they hold the moral high ground.
亦步亦趋	yì bù yì qū	to follow every step slavishly — Literally "you walk, I walk; you hurry, I hurry", from the Zhuangzi, where a disciple copies his master's every move. It paints the extreme of 跟 as "follow": imitating so closely that one has no step of one's own. Often mildly critical of mere copying.
异口同声	yì kǒu tóng shēng	different mouths, the same sound — unanimous — Everyone says the same thing at once: the committee that answers in chorus, the witnesses whose accounts match. Neutral to positive in tone; suspicious unanimity would be phrased differently. The mirror image of 众说纷纭, where every mouth says something else.
因陋就简	yīn lòu jiù jiǎn	make do with the crude and the simple: get by with what little there is — 因 to rely on, 陋 crude, 就 to settle for, 简 the simple. Said of working within tight means, building or running something on a shoestring without waiting for better tools.
因时制宜	yīn shí zhì yí	act according to what the time requires — A closely related four-character phrase drawing explicitly on the 时中 tradition. Where 与时俱进 emphasizes forward movement alongside the era, 因时制宜 emphasizes responsive calibration: making decisions that fit the specific circumstances of the moment.
阴差阳错	yīn chā yáng cuò	yin off, yang astray — a chain of coincidences producing an unexpected result — 阴 yīn + 差 chā (off; awry) + 阳 yáng + 错 cuò (wrong). A tangle of small errors and coincidences, some hidden (阴), some accidental (阳), that combine to produce an outcome nobody intended, often a missed chance or a strange twist of fate. 因为阴差阳错，他们错过了那班火车。
引吭高歌	yǐn háng gāo gē	stretch the throat and sing loudly: to sing out at full voice — 吭 is the throat; the image is of someone tipping the head back to let the voice carry. Used warmly for hearty, unrestrained singing.
迎刃而解	yíng rèn ér jiě	to split apart at the meeting of the blade: solved with ease — Lit: meeting 迎 the blade 刃, it splits 解. Like bamboo that splits cleanly the moment the knife touches it: once the hardest part is cracked, everything else falls apart of its own accord. Used when solving one key difficulty makes all the rest resolve effortlessly.
有口难言	yǒu kǒu nán yán	having a mouth but unable to speak — in a position where one cannot explain oneself — The frustration of having the means to speak but being unable to, because of social constraints, grief, or incriminating circumstances. One of the most vivid expressions for the experience of being misunderstood with no way to clear one's name.
有始有终	yǒu shǐ yǒu zhōng	to see a thing through to the end — Literally "to have a beginning and have an end", to finish what one starts rather than abandon it midway. The 终 of completion again, valued as a virtue of persistence. The opposite is 有始无终, all start and no finish.
与时俱进	yǔ shí jù jìn	to advance together with the times — A classical phrase that became a political slogan in 2001, used to justify the Communist Party accommodating private enterprise, and criticized for justifying whatever the party currently does.
语重心长	yǔ zhòng xīn cháng	words are weighty, feeling is deep — earnest, heartfelt advice — Lit: words-weighty-heart-long. Used to describe sincere, deeply felt words from someone who genuinely cares: a mentor's advice, a parent's warning, a teacher's final lesson before a student departs. The heaviness (重) of the words comes from the depth (长) of the feeling behind them.
原封不动	yuán fēng bù dòng	the original seal unmoved: left exactly as it was, untouched — 原封 is the original seal on a package. To leave something 原封不动 is to return or keep it intact, never opened or altered. Everyday modern usage.
原形毕现	yuán xíng bì xiàn	the original form is fully revealed: a true nature laid completely bare — Lit: original-shape-completely-appears. Said when a disguise drops and someone's real, usually unflattering, character or identity becomes plain for all to see.
载歌载舞	zài gē zài wǔ	both singing and dancing: to celebrate with song and dance — 载…载… is a classical frame meaning doing two things at once. Standard description of festive crowds at a 春节 (chūnjié) gala or village celebration.
责无旁贷	zé wú páng dài	a duty one cannot shirk — Literally "the responsibility cannot be passed to another," a duty that falls squarely on you and that you ought to shoulder. Used when one accepts an obligation as rightfully one's own, the firm sense of "should" hardened into "must."
长篇大论	cháng piān dà lùn	a long-winded discourse: a lengthy, overdrawn speech or piece of writing — Literally "long chapters, big discoursing." A speech or text that goes on at excessive length. Usually mildly critical: the listener or reader feels the argument could have been made in a fraction of the words.
振振有词	zhèn zhèn yǒu cí	to argue with apparent conviction: to speak plausibly while in the wrong — Lit: assured-and-having-words. Describes someone who defends a weak or unjustified position with a confident flow of arguments, sounding righteous though the case does not hold. Mildly critical in tone.
争论不休	zhēng lùn bù xiū	to argue without end: an endless, unresolved dispute — Literally "dispute and discuss without ceasing." Describes a debate that drags on with no side yielding and no resolution in sight. 不休 bù xiū "without stopping" stresses the wearisome, going-nowhere quality of the argument.
知错就改	zhī cuò jiù gǎi	recognize a fault and at once correct it — Praise for the rare person who, the moment they see their error, fixes it without excuse. Echoes the Confucian ideal of 过则勿惮改, do not fear to mend a fault.
知己知彼	zhī jǐ zhī bǐ	know yourself and know your opponent — thorough strategic understanding — From Sun Tzu's Art of War (孙子兵法·谋攻篇): 知彼知己，百战不殆 (zhī bǐ zhī jǐ, bǎi zhàn bù dài) , "Know your opponent and know yourself, and in a hundred battles you will not be imperiled." The fuller phrase is often compressed to 知己知彼.
知之为知之，不知为不知。	zhī zhī wéi zhī zhī , bù zhī wéi bù zhī .	To know what you know and know what you don't know — that is real knowledge. — From the Analects of Confucius. The full saying ends 是知也 ("this is wisdom"). It teaches intellectual honesty: genuine knowledge includes a clear sense of the limits of one's knowledge.
众口铄金	zhòng kǒu shuò jīn	many mouths melt gold — From the Guoyu and other classical texts: collective speech is so powerful it can melt metal. A more extreme formulation of the same principle as 三人成虎: the force of accumulated voices is overwhelming, even against something as solid as reputation or fact.
众所周知	zhòng suǒ zhōu zhī	as everyone knows; it is common knowledge — A formal phrase, literally "what the crowd all knows," used to introduce a widely accepted fact. 众 (the masses) + 周知 (universally known). Common in writing and speeches as a way of appealing to shared knowledge, the Chinese equivalent of "as is well known."
助人为乐	zhù rén wéi lè	to take joy in helping others — Literally "to help others as one's joy," to find genuine pleasure in aiding people. A prized social virtue, often used to praise the selflessly helpful. Built on 助 (to aid), the same character at the heart of 帮助.
字斟句酌	zì zhēn jù zhuó	weighing every character, deliberating every sentence — writing with extreme care and precision — 斟酌 zhēnzhuó = to deliberate; to weigh carefully. Used for careful writing, diplomatic language, or painstaking editing. A compliment to someone who writes with rigor. 这份合同是字斟句酌写成的。(This contract was written with extreme care.)
自我感觉良好	zì wǒ gǎn ju é liáng hǎo	to feel good about oneself — often wryly, overly self-satisfied — A common modern expression using 感觉 gǎnjué (feeling), a close relative of 觉得. Literally "self-feeling is good," it describes someone with a high (often too high) opinion of themselves. Usually said with a touch of irony about another person's unearned confidence.
多少 / 几	duō shao / jǐ	how many, how much — The two question words for quantity: 几 for small countable numbers and 多少 for larger or unknown amounts, why 几 needs a measure word and 多少 may skip it, and how to ask prices and ages.
跟	gēn	with; and; to follow — The everyday coverb for "with" and conjunction for "and", how the 跟…一起 and 跟…说 patterns work, the original verb "to follow", and how 跟 differs from 和, 同, and 与.
可能	kěn éng	maybe, possible — The all-purpose word for possibility, working as adverb, adjective, and noun, and how it differs from 也许, 或许, 大概, and the modal 会.
离	lí	away from; distance — The coverb for distance and separation: the A 离 B + distance pattern for "how far", the verb sense "to leave / part from", and how 离 differs from 从 and 到 in marking space and time.
往	wǎng	toward; to head for — The directional coverb for "toward", the 往 + direction + verb pattern, the verb sense "to go / in the past", and how 往 differs from 向 and 朝 for facing and motion.
为什么	wèi shén me	why — The neutral question word for cause and reason, how to answer it with 因为 or 为了, and how a plain 为什么 differs from the surprised 怎么 and the purpose-asking 为了什么.
一下	yí xià	the verb-softener — The verbal-quantity word that makes an action brief and casual, how verb + 一下 softens a request, and how it differs from verb reduplication and from the time-word 一会儿.
应该	yīng gāi	the modal "should" — The modal verb for "should, ought to," covering both moral obligation and confident inference, and how it differs from 得, 要, and 必须.
怎么	zěn me	how, in what way; why — The all-purpose question word for manner and reason: how to do something, what is wrong, and the surprised "how come," plus how 怎么 differs from 为什么 and 怎么样.
安静	ān jìng	quiet
办	bàn	to handle; to do (a matter)
帮	bāng	to help
帮忙	bāng / / máng	to help (lend-a-busy-hand) — 帮 (assist) + 忙 (busyness): to take on someone's busyness. The polite request is 帮个忙 or, upgraded, 帮我一个大忙 (do me a big favor). Direct objects go on bare 帮.
帮我一下	bāng wǒ yí xià	give me a hand; help me out — A model of 一下 as a politeness softener. The bare 帮我 ("help me") is blunt; adding 一下 frames it as a small, quick favor and sounds far friendlier. Note that 一下 can follow the object here (帮我一下) because 帮 takes a person directly. The same softening works across requests: 看一下, 等一下, 问一下.
绑	bǎng	to tie; to bind
保持	bǎo chí	to keep, to maintain
报仇	bào chóu	to avenge, take revenge — 报 (to requite) plus 仇 (chóu, enmity): to pay back a wrong. The dark twin of 报答, turning the same logic of return toward vengeance.
报酬	bào chou	remuneration, pay
报答	bào dá	to repay (a kindness) — 报 (to requite) plus 答 (dá, to answer): to answer a kindness with thanks made real. Reserved for repaying gratitude, a warmer word than the neutral 回报.
报道	bào dào	news report; to report (news) — 报 bào (to announce; to report; newspaper) + 道 dào (way; path). To map and announce the course of events: journalism as the act of charting what has happened. Used both as a noun (a report, a piece of coverage) and as a verb (to report on something).
报告	bào gào	to report; a report — 报 (to announce) plus 告 (gào, to tell): to deliver an account formally, and the document that delivers it. Works as both the act of reporting and the written or spoken report itself.
报警	bào jǐng	to call the police; sound an alarm
报名	bào míng	to sign up, register — 报 (to report) plus 名 (míng, name): to report your name onto a list. The standard verb for signing up or enrolling for a class, a contest, or an exam.
报销	bào xiāo	to claim reimbursement
报纸	bào zhǐ	newspaper — 报 (the thing announced) plus 纸 (zhǐ, paper): the daily report rendered on paper. The everyday word for a printed newspaper, and the head noun behind titles like 《人民日报》.
本草	běn cǎo	Materia medica — Materia medica: the traditional term for the pharmacological literature and its subject matter.
本草纲目	běn cǎo gāng mù	the materia medica
本来	běn lái	originally; at first; (it goes without saying that) — 本 běn (root; origin) + 来 lái (come). What was the case from the start, before things changed: 我本来想去 "I originally meant to go (but didn't)." It often carries a quiet sense that the original state was the natural or proper one.
辩论	biàn lùn	to debate; to argue (a formal case) — 辩 biàn (to dispute, to argue points) + 论. To debate opposing positions, each side marshalling arguments to win. The standard word for a debate competition (辩论赛 biànlùnsài) and for adversarial argument generally.
标点	biāo diǎn	punctuation
标记	biāo jì	a marker; a label; to mark — 标 biāo (to label, to mark out) + 记 jì (a mark). A marker or tag that identifies a thing, and the act of placing it: 在地图上标记位置 "to mark a location on a map."
标题	biāo tí	headline; title; caption — 标 biāo (to mark; label) + 题 tí (heading). The title line printed at the top of an article, book, or section: 新闻标题 "a news headline," 大标题 "a big banner headline." Literally the marked heading.
表达	biǎo dá	to express: to convey; to articulate (thoughts and feelings) — 表 biǎo (to bring to the surface) + 达 dá (to reach; to arrive at; to get through). To convey thoughts or feelings so that they reach another person clearly. The 达 element adds the sense of successful transmission: not just showing something, but getting it across.
表格	biǎo gé	form: a table; a chart; a grid laid out for filling in — 表 biǎo (a chart; a surface where data is laid out) + 格 gé (a grid; a square; a cell). A printed or digital form, table, or grid: anything with rows and cells to be read or filled in.
表面	biǎo miàn	surface; outside; outward appearance — 表 biǎo (outer; to show) + 面 miàn (face). The outer surface of a thing, and by extension the surface meaning as opposed to the truth beneath: 表面上 biǎomiàn shàng "on the surface, ostensibly."
表皮	biǎo pí	outer skin: the epidermis; the outermost layer or rind — 表 biǎo (outer) + 皮 pí (skin; hide; rind). The outermost layer of a living thing: the epidermis of human skin, the rind of a fruit, the bark-like surface of a plant. A precise term used in biology, medicine, and everyday talk about peels and surfaces.
表示	biǎo shì	to express: to indicate; to show (a feeling, attitude, or position) — 表 biǎo (to bring to the surface) + 示 shì (to show; to indicate; to make manifest). To express or indicate an attitude, opinion, or feeling, often through a word, gesture, or symbolic act. 表示 frequently introduces what someone states or signals: 表示同意 (express agreement), 表示感谢 (express thanks).
表现	biǎo xiàn	to show; to display; (noun) performance, conduct, showing — 表 biǎo (outer; to express) + 现 xiàn (show). To make an inner quality visible on the outside. As a noun it is one's showing or conduct: 他今天表现很好 "he did very well today."
表演	biǎo yǎn	to perform: to act; a performance; a show — 表 biǎo (to show outwardly) + 演 yǎn (to act; to perform; to develop). To perform before an audience, and the performance itself: music, dance, drama, magic, acrobatics.
不错	bú cuò	pretty good, not bad; correct, right — 不 bù (not) + 错 cuò (wrong). Two readings from one shape: as praise, "quite good, not bad" (成绩不错 "good grades"); as confirmation, "right, correct" (一点不错 "exactly right"). Note the tone sandhi: 不 shifts to bú before the falling 错.
不懂	bù dǒng	to not understand
不可能	bù kěn éng	impossible; cannot be — The flat negation, "impossible, no way." 这不可能 ("this is impossible"), 不可能的事 ("an impossible thing"). It is strong and absolute, unlike the softer 不太可能 ("unlikely") which leaves a sliver of chance. As an exclamation, 不可能！means "no way! / that can't be!"
不良率	bù liáng lǜ	defect rate — Percentage of defective units; 良品率 or 良率 (liánglǜ) is the yield, its complement. 不良品 (bùliángpǐn) and 次品 (cìpǐn) are defective goods. Agree an acceptable rate in the contract and what happens above it.
不论	bù lùn	no matter; regardless of — 不 bù (not) + 论. Functionally interchangeable with 无论 in most sentences, slightly less formal. Same grammar: an open element follows, and 都 or 也 marks the main clause.
不应该	bù yīng gāi	should not; ought not to — Negates the obligation: putting 不 before 应该 says something ought not to be done: 你不应该对她说谎 ("you shouldn't lie to her"). Often it voices regret about the past, "should not have": 我不应该那样说 ("I shouldn't have said that"). Do not confuse with 应该不, where 不 falls after and negates a guessed event instead.
不知道	bù zhī dào	don't know; I'm not sure; I have no idea — 不 bù (negation) + 知道. One of the most common phrases in conversational Mandarin: the honest, unadorned acknowledgment of not having found the path. As a standalone response, 不知道！is perfectly natural and socially acceptable (not rude) in most contexts.
蚕丝	cán sī	Silk thread , literally "silkworm thread — Silk thread , literally "silkworm thread." The raw fiber before weaving. 蚕 is the silkworm; 丝 is the thread.
差错	chā cuò	a slip, an error; a mishap going wrong — 差 chā (off, deviating) + 错 cuò (error). A small slip or accidental error, and by extension an unforeseen mishap: 万一出了差错 "in case something goes wrong." Both halves mean "off the mark," doubling the sense of deviation.
拆开	chāi kāi	to tear open; to unwrap
唱反调	chàng fǎn diào	to deliberately disagree; to sing a different tune — 唱 chàng (to sing) + 反调 fǎndiào (an opposing tune). To voice the contrary position on purpose, to be the one who always sings against the prevailing note. Colloquial and faintly critical.
唱歌	chàng gē	to sing — 唱 (sing) + 歌 (song). The social institution attached is KTV (卡拉OK), where 唱歌 is what colleagues, classmates, and business contacts do together; declining every song is borderline antisocial, and one rehearsed number is sound survival strategy.
唱片	chàng piàn	record; disc (vinyl, album) — 唱 chàng (to sing) + 片 piàn (a thin flat piece). A singing-disc: the physical record that holds recorded sound. 唱片公司 chàngpiàn gōngsī is a record label. The word predates and survives digital formats.
超越	chāo yu è	to surpass; to exceed; to transcend — 超 (chāo, to overtake; to exceed) + 越. The figurative summit of the family: surpassing a competitor, exceeding a limit, transcending a category. Used in business, sport, philosophy, and self-development contexts.
嘲笑	cháo xiào	to mock; to ridicule; to jeer at — 嘲 cháo (to taunt; to deride) + 笑 xiào (laugh). To laugh at someone with scorn. This is the cruel edge of 笑: the same sound aimed downward at a target. Takes a direct object, the person mocked.
车次	chē cì	train number — G-trains are the fastest, D-trains next. 检票口 (jiǎnpiàokǒu) is the boarding gate, shown on the screens next to the train number; 车厢 (chēxiāng) is the carriage, 座位号 the seat number.
诚	chéng	sincerity; genuine
诚信	chéng xìn	integrity; honesty and trustworthiness; good faith — 诚 chéng (sincerity; genuine; true to one's heart , 诚 is itself 言 + 成 chéng, words that have been completed, words that have come to pass; sincerity as the alignment of word with reality) + 信 xìn (trustworthiness).
充电	chōng diàn	to charge (device)
出错	chū cuò	to go wrong; to make an error — 出 chū (to come out) + 错 cuò (error). For an error to surface, often in a process or system: 系统出错了 "the system threw an error," 计算出错 "a miscalculation came up." Common in technical and procedural contexts.
出问题	chū wèn tí	a problem occurs; something goes wrong — Here 问题 is unambiguously "problem": 出 chū (to emerge, arise) + 问题 = "a problem crops up, something breaks down." Used for malfunctions, complications, and things going wrong. Contrast 解决问题 (solve a problem) and 发现问题 (spot a problem).
出现	chū xiàn	to appear; to emerge; to come into view — 出 chū (emerge) + 现 xiàn (appear; manifest; current). To emerge into visibility , applicable to everything from a rainbow appearing after rain to a new problem appearing in a system. One of the most common verbs in news language and everyday description.
穿越	chuān yu è	to pass through; to cut across; (popular fiction) to time-travel — Cutting through a region. Also the standard verb for popular time-travel fiction (穿越剧, 穿越小说) where a modern character is thrown into a past dynasty.
传播	chuán bō	to disseminate; to spread; to broadcast; communication — 传 chuán (to transmit) + 播 bō (to sow, to broadcast). To spread information, ideas, or culture widely: 传播知识 "to disseminate knowledge," 大众传播 "mass communication." Also the academic field name, 传播学 "communication studies."
传承	chuán chéng	to inherit and carry on
传达	chuán dá	to convey; to pass on (a message); to communicate — 传 chuán (to convey) + 达 dá (to reach, to arrive). To deliver a message, an order, or a feeling so that it reaches its target: 传达指示 "to convey instructions," 传达上级的精神 "to pass on the leadership's intent." Stresses that the content arrives intact.
传递	chuán dì	to pass; to relay; to convey; to transmit — 传 chuán (to pass on) + 递 dì (to hand over). To move something from one party to the next in a chain: 传递信息 "to relay information," 传递火炬 "to pass the torch." Stresses the act of handing along, often through several hands or steps.
传记	zhuàn jì	biography; a life story — 传 zhuàn (a handed-down record) + 记 jì (to record). A written account of a person's life: 名人传记 "celebrity biographies," 人物传记 "biographies of notable figures." Note the reading shifts to zhuàn here, never chuán.

词语	cí yǔ	words and phrases; expressions — 词 cí (word) + 语 yǔ (speech; phrase). A cover term for words and set phrases together, broader than 单词. Used when talking about vocabulary as a body of expressions.

大概	dà gài	approximately; probably; roughly — 大 dà (large, rough) + 概 gài (general outline; to level off). A rough outline: approximately, probably. One of the most natural hedges in spoken Mandarin: 大概三点 "around three o'clock."

单词	dān cí	a single word; a vocabulary word — 单 dān (single) + 词 cí (word). One word taken on its own, the unit you memorize when studying a language. Common in 背单词 bèi dāncí, to memorize vocabulary.

In interrogative sentences, 到底 intensifies the question and demands a genuine response , it presses the interlocutor to stop evading and give a straight answer.
道理	dào lǐ	reason; logic; principle; the way things work — 道 dào (way; path) + 理 lǐ (principle; pattern; grain of wood, the inherent structure of things). "The logic of the path." The way things make sense: the reason behind an argument, the principle underlying a situation, the basic logic that should govern behavior.

调度	diào dù	to dispatch; to schedule; a dispatcher — 调 diào (to move, to transfer) + 度 dù (to measure, to manage). To dispatch and coordinate trains, vehicles, crews, or tasks; also the person who does it. 列车调度 (train dispatching), 调度中心 (control center). The work of directing many moving parts.

调节	tiáo jié	to regulate; to adjust; to balance — 调 tiáo (to adjust) + 节 jié (to control, to moderate). 调解	tiáo jiě	to mediate; to reconcile; mediation — 调 tiáo (to harmonize) + 解 jiě (to resolve, to untie). To step between two sides and settle their dispute. 调解纠纷 (mediate a dispute), 调解员 (a mediator). The social form of tiáo: bringing people, not flavors, into accord.

调整	tiáo zhěng	to adjust; to regulate; adjustment — 调 tiáo (to adjust) + 整 zhěng (to put in order). To set something right by careful change: 调整计划 (adjust the plan), 调整心态 (adjust one's attitude), 价格调整 (a price adjustment). The everyday word for fine-tuning anything.
调子	diào zi	tune; melody; tone (of voice or mood) — 调 diào (melody, pitch) + 子 zi (noun suffix). A tune or melody, and figuratively the pitch or mood of speech or writing. 唱调子 (carry a tune), 定调子 (set the tone, decide the direction). The everyday word for the shape of a sound.
懂得	dǒng de	to know how, to appreciate — 懂 (understand) plus the resultative 得 (de). It reaches past facts toward practical wisdom: knowing how to do a thing, or appreciating its value.

台词	tái cí	an actor lines; stage dialogue; script lines — 台 tái (stage) + 词 cí (words). The lines a performer speaks in a play, film, or show. 背台词 bèi táicí is to learn one lines by heart.
谈	tán	to talk; to chat
讨论	tǎo lùn	to discuss; to talk over — 讨 tǎo (to seek out, to ask for) + 论. The everyday word for discussing a matter: a group talking something through to reach understanding or a decision. Cooperative by default, two or more people pooling views rather than fighting over them.
讨论会	tǎo lùn huì	a discussion forum; a symposium
提问	tí wèn	to ask a question; to raise a question — 提 tí (to raise; to bring up) + 问 wèn (to ask). To put a question forward, typically in a class or meeting. 老师提问 "the teacher asks a question," often of a student called on to answer.
题目	tí mù	exam question; title; subject (of an essay) — 题 tí (heading) + 目 mù (item; entry). The item set at the head of a test, or the title given to a composition: 考试题目 "exam questions," 作文题目 "the essay prompt." It points at the specific thing to be answered or written about.
题诗	tí shī	to inscribe a poem (on a painting, wall, or scroll) — 题 tí (to inscribe) + 诗 shī (poem). To write a poem directly onto a surface, a classic gesture in Chinese painting where verse and image share one scroll.
题字	tí zì	to inscribe characters; an inscription — 题 tí (to inscribe) + 字 zì (characters). To brush words onto a painting, plaque, or wall, or the inscription itself. Often done by a respected figure as a mark of honor.
听懂	tīng dǒng	to understand what one hears; to follow spoken language — 听 (to listen) + 懂 dǒng (to understand). The resultative complement 懂 marks successful comprehension , listening that reached understanding. Constant in language learning contexts: 你听懂了吗？ (Did you understand what you heard?) contrasts with 你听到了吗？ (Did you hear it? — did the sound reach you?).
听话	tīng huà	obedient; to do as one is told — 听 (to listen) + 话 huà (words; speech). Obedient in the sense of listening to what is said and acting accordingly. The standard word for a well-behaved child: 这孩子很听话 (this child is very obedient).
听说	tīng shuō	to have heard (that); reportedly; I've heard that — 听 (to hear) + 说 shuō (to say; to speak). A hedging marker for secondhand information: "I've heard it said that…" Placed at the start of a clause to flag that the speaker did not witness the information directly. 听说他去北京了 (I heard he went to Beijing).
停电	tíng diàn	power outage
通常	tōng cháng	usually; ordinarily; generally; as a rule — 通 (general; holding throughout) + 常 cháng (constant; usual; frequent). The case that holds across most instances: usually, as the general rule. 通常 sets up the ordinary expectation against which exceptions are measured: 我通常七点起床 (I usually get up at seven).
通车	tōng chē	to be open to traffic; to begin service (of a road or line) — 通 (to open; to be through) + 车 chē (vehicle). To be through to vehicles: the moment a new road, bridge, railway, or subway line opens and traffic can run along it. A staple of infrastructure news, where the opening of a line is itself an event: 正式通车 (officially opened to traffic).
通过	tōng guò	to pass through; to get past; by means of; via — 通 (to go through) + 过 guò (to cross; to pass). The everyday verb for getting through something: passing through a gate, passing an exam, a motion passing a vote.
通信	tōng xìn	to correspond; to communicate; telecommunications — 通 (to connect; to convey) + 信 xìn (a letter; a message; trust). To get messages through to one another. Historically it meant to exchange letters: 我们通了多年信 (we corresponded for many years).
通用	tōng yòng	general-purpose; in common use; interchangeable — 通 (general; running throughout) + 用 yòng (to use). Usable throughout, across cases: not specialized to one situation but serving generally. 通用 describes something accepted everywhere or applicable across the board: 全国通用 (valid nationwide), 通用规则 (a general rule that applies in all cases).
通知	tōng zhī	to notify; to inform; a notice; a notification — 通 (to get through; to convey) + 知 zhī (to know; to make known). To make something known by getting word through: the standard word for an official notification and the act of giving it. Works as a verb (请通知大家, please notify everyone) and a noun (一份通知, a notice).
童话	tóng huà	fairy tale; children's story — 童 tóng (child) + 话 huà (story). A tale told for children. Its kin 神话 shénhuà (god-story) means a myth, and 神话 and 童话 together cover the world of fabulous stories.

外表	wài biǎo	outward appearance: the way someone or something looks from outside — 外 wài (outer; outside) + 表 biǎo (surface). The outward appearance of a person or thing, especially how they look to others. Where 表面 leans toward physical surfaces and abstract superficiality, 外表 is most natural for the looks of people and objects, the impression they give at first sight.
玩笑	wán xiào	a joke; a prank; banter — 玩 wán (to play) + 笑 xiào (laugh). Play-laughter: a joke made between people who are at ease together. 开玩笑 kāi wánxiào (to crack a joke, to be kidding) is one of the highest-frequency phrases in spoken Mandarin.
玩笑话	wán xiào huà	words said in jest

网络	wǎng luò	internet; network
网民	wǎng mín	netizen; internet user
网友	wǎng yǒu	online friend; internet friend — A friend made online, from 网 wǎng (net) + 友 yǒu (friend). A thoroughly modern coinage and an extremely common one. Note that 友 contracts here from 朋友, a regular pattern: 友 alone serves as the bound "friend" element in compounds (校友 alumnus, 战友 comrade-in-arms, 笔友 pen pal).
网站	wǎng zhàn	website — 网 (wǎng, net) + 站 (station): a website, a fixed point on the internet. The everyday word for any site you visit, from a shop to a search engine.
往年	wǎng nián	former years
忘记	wàng jì	to forget
微笑	wēi xiào	to smile; a smile — 微 wēi (faint; slight; minute) + 笑 xiào (laugh/smile). A small, soundless laugh: the smile. The word that names the warm, closed-lipped expression, distinct from the open-mouthed 大笑.
尾号	wěi hào	last digits (of a phone number) — Drivers confirm the passenger by the last four digits of the phone number: 手机尾号多少? Answer with the digits one by one.
为了	wèi le	in order to; for the sake of; for the purpose of — 为 wèi (for; toward) + 了 le (grammatical completion particle; here a suffix to 为). The standard purpose marker in modern Chinese. 为了 introduces the goal or beneficiary of an action and can appear at the start of a sentence or clause.
为了 + 目的	wèi le + go a l	in order to; for the sake of — When the real answer is a purpose rather than a cause, use 为了, not 因为. 为了 names the goal the action serves: 为了健康，他每天跑步 ("for the sake of health, he runs every day"). Asking specifically about purpose, you can use 为了什么 ("to what end").

问候	wèn hòu	to send regards; to greet; to ask after someone — 问 wèn (to ask after) + 候 hòu (to wait on; to attend). To inquire warmly after another person, in speech or in writing: 致以亲切的问候 "to extend warm regards."
无论	wú lùn	no matter (what, who, how); regardless — 无 wú (without) + 论. A conjunction introducing an exhaustive condition: whatever the case, the main clause still holds. Pairs with 都 or 也 in the second clause. More formal than 不管.

物理	wù lǐ	physics; the principles of matter — 物 wù (thing; matter) + 理 lǐ (principle). Literally "the principles of things," the modern name for physics. A clear case of 理 meaning the lawful pattern running through the physical world.

习题	xí tí	practice exercises
下班	xià bān	to finish work; to get off work; end of shift — 下 xià (to descend; to leave the higher platform) + 班 bān (shift; work roster). To come down from the work platform , to leave one's post at the end of the day. The indispensable pair of 上班. 下班后 (after work) is the default framing for evening activities in Chinese urban conversation.
下载	xià zài	To download — To download: carry down. Paired with 上传 (upload). 载 also reads zǎi meaning "year" in classical texts, a polyphone trap for advanced readers.
先	xiān	first
先富起来	xiān fù qǐ lai	let some get rich first — permission for inequality as a stage — 先 (first, beforehand) + 富 (wealthy, to become rich) + 起来 (resultative complement indicating a process coming into effect). The condensed form of Deng's proposition: 让一部分人先富起来 (ràng yī bùfen rén xiān fù qǐlai, let some people get rich first).

现成	xiàn chéng	ready-made; off the shelf; already prepared — 现 xiàn (on hand) + 成 chéng (completed). Already finished and available, needing no further making: 现成的答案 "a ready-made answer," 现成的衣服 "off-the-rack clothing."
现代化	xiàn dài huà	modernization; to modernize — 现代 xiàndài (modern; the modern era) + 化 huà (the -ize suffix). The textbook example of the 化 suffix at work: "to make modern." 现代化 is one of the central words of twentieth-century Chinese politics, most famously in 四个现代化 (the Four Modernizations: agriculture, industry, defense, science and technolog…
现金	xiàn jīn	cash; physical currency — 现 xiàn (present; to appear) + 金 jīn (gold; money). Cash in hand, as opposed to digital payments, credit, or promises. 付现金 "to pay in cash." In a society where 微信支付 and 支付宝 (WeChat Pay and Alipay) dominate, 现金 is increasingly rare.
现实	xiàn shí	reality; actual; (of a person) realistic, practical — 现 xiàn (present) + 实 shí (real; solid). What is actually the case here and now, set against 理想 lǐxiǎng (the ideal). As an adjective for a person it can mean clear-eyed, or pointedly materialistic.

小声	xiǎo shēng	quiet; softly; in a low voice — 小 xiǎo (small) + 声 shēng (voice). A small voice, the opposite of 大声: 小声说 "to speak quietly," often so others do not overhear.
小说	xiǎo shuō	novel; fiction — 小 xiǎo (small; minor) + 说 shuō (speech; theory). Literally "minor talk" , the genre of fiction was long considered a lower form of literature compared to history and philosophy. The term was coined to contrast with 大道 "the Great Way" of classical learning.
笑话	xiào hua	a joke; to laugh at (someone) — 笑 xiào (laugh) + 话 huà (speech; words). Laugh-words: a joke, a funny story. As a verb it flips to "to laugh at, to make a fool of." 讲笑话 = to tell a joke; 闹笑话 = to make an embarrassing blunder that others laugh at.
笑脸	xiào liǎn	smiling face; a smile worn for others — 笑 (laugh, smile) + 脸 (face). A face shaped into a smile, especially the welcoming one you put on for guests or customers. The opposite expression is 板着脸 (bǎnzhe liǎn), to hold the face stiff and stern.
笑容	xiào róng	a smile; a smiling expression — 笑 xiào (to smile; to laugh) + 容 róng (facial expression). The smile as a look worn on the face, treated as a noun: something one has, shows, or loses. Where 笑 is the act of smiling, 笑容 is the expression itself, the way the face holds a smile.
协助	xié zhù	to assist (cooperatively)
新婚	xīn hūn	newly married; newlywed — 新 (new) + 婚 (hūn, marriage). Newly married , in the fresh state of the new relationship. 新婚快乐 (congratulations on the marriage , Happy Newlywed); 新婚夫妇 (newlywed couple); 新郎 xīnláng (groom , new-man) and 新娘 xīnniáng (bride , new-woman) are the standard terms for a wedding couple.
新年	xīn nián	New Year — 新 (new) + 年 (year). The new year , the most basic New Year compound, usable for both the Gregorian New Year (January 1) and the Chinese Lunar New Year. 新年快乐 (Happy New Year) and 新年好 (New Year greetings) are the standard phrases.
新闻	xīn wén	news
新鲜	xīn xiān	fresh; novel; new and interesting — 新 (new) + 鲜 (xiān, fresh , especially of food; vivid; rare). Fresh in the sensory sense: 新鲜蔬菜 (fresh vegetables), 新鲜空气 (fresh air). Also used for novel experiences: 对我来说这是个新鲜事物 (this is something new to me). 鲜 alone means fresh-vivid; 新鲜 combines freshness with newness.
信封	xìn fēng	envelope — 信 (xìn, letter) plus 封 (the sealing/wrapper): the paper sleeve that closes around a letter. The most common everyday word built on 封.
信号	xìn hào	signal; indicator — 信 xìn (trustworthy communication; word) + 号 hào (sign; designation; mark; sound used as a signal). A signal is a word that has been stripped of language and reduced to a reliable mark: it carries only its presence or absence, its strength or weakness, rather than semantic content.
信任	xìn rèn	trust; to trust; to have confidence in — 信 xìn (trustworthiness; to trust) + 任 rèn (to appoint; to entrust; to allow , 任 carries the sense of delegating responsibility to someone). To trust is to appoint a person as worthy of reliance. 信任危机 xìnrèn wéijī = a crisis of trust (institutional or personal). 建立信任 jiànlì xìnrèn = to build trust.
信息	xìn xī	information; news; data — 信 xìn (trustworthy word; communication) + 息 xī (breath; rest; news , 息 contains 自 zì, nose, over 心 xīn, heart: breath as the sign of life; by extension, news and tidings). A word that travels and carries meaning , information as a form of 信. 信息时代 xìnxī shídài = the information age.
信用	xìn yòng	creditworthiness; credit; trustworthiness in financial conduct — 信 xìn (trustworthiness) + 用 yòng (use; to use; practical application). Trustworthiness made actionable , the record of a person or institution reliably standing by its commitments. 信用卡 xìnyòng kǎ = credit card. 信用评级 xìnyòng píngjí = credit rating.
修改	xiū gǎi	to revise, to amend, to edit — 修 (mend, trim) + 改 (alter). To go back over a text, plan, or law and revise it. The standard word for editing a document or amending a draft.
宣传	xuān chuán	to publicize; to promote; publicity; propaganda — 宣 xuān (to proclaim) + 传 chuán (to spread). To put out a message deliberately so it reaches the public: 宣传海报 "a promotional poster," 宣传活动 "a publicity campaign." Carries a sense of organized, purposeful spreading, and in political contexts the meaning of propaganda.
旋律	xuán lǜ	melody
学问	xu é wèn	learning; knowledge; scholarship — 学 xué (to study) + 问 wèn (to ask). Knowledge built by studying and asking, the deep, earned kind. 很有学问 "highly learned." The word itself teaches that real knowledge grows from questions, not answers alone.
询问	xún wèn	to inquire; to ask about; to seek information — 询 xún (to inquire) + 问 wèn (to ask). A formal verb for asking in order to gather information, common in writing and service contexts: 询问情况 "to inquire about the situation," 向某人询问 "to make inquiries of someone."
训练	xùn liàn	to train; training — 训 (to instruct, to discipline) + 练 (to drill). Organized, directed training: athletes, soldiers, dogs, new staff. Heavier and more institutional than 练习.
押解	yā jiè	to escort (a prisoner) under guard — 押 yā (to detain; to hold) + 解 jiè. The clearest home of the second reading jiè. To escort a prisoner or valuables under guard from one place to another: 押解犯人 = escort a prisoner; the related 解送 jièsòng = send under escort.

语法	yǔ fǎ	grammar — 语 yǔ (language; speech) + 法 fǎ (rule; law). The "law of language," the system of rules by which a language is built. The same 法 of fixed standard, here governing how words combine.
语言	yǔ yán	language (as a system) — 语 yǔ (language; speech; to tell) + 言 yán (speech; words). The word for language as a structured system , grammar, vocabulary, and everything that makes communication possible across a community. 语言 is used in academic, formal, and everyday contexts alike. 语言学 yǔyánxué = linguistics.
语音	yǔ yīn	voice message — Factory staff often send long voice messages. 转文字 (zhuǎn wénzì) converts a voice message to text inside WeChat (press and hold the message), and the result is easy to paste into a translator. Asking for text is acceptable when phrased as your limitation.
预报	yù bào	forecast
元素	yuán sù	Element — Element: "original plain-stuff." Beyond the lab it means element in the design sense too: 中国元素 (Chinese elements) is the stock phrase for Chinese motifs in fashion or film.
原来	yuán lái	originally; as it turns out; so that's how it is — 原 yuán (original; the root; where something came from) + 来 lái (came from; arrived from). "From the original state," used in two senses. Temporal: originally, at first (他原来住在北京 = He originally lived in Beijing). Revelatory: "so that's how it is / now I understand" (原来如此！= So that's the reason!).
原因	yuán yīn	cause; reason — 原 yuán (origin; source) + 因 yīn (cause). The standard noun for the cause or reason behind something: 失败的原因 "the reason for the failure." Its pair is 结果 jiéguǒ, the result.
原因分析	yuán yīn fēn xī	cause analysis — The first thing to ask for. 根本原因 (gēnběn yuányīn) is the root cause; an answer like 工人操作不当 (worker error) usually is not one. Ask why the process allowed the error.
援助	yuán zhù	aid; relief; assistance
愿意	yuàn yì	to be willing; to want to — 愿 yuàn (to wish) + 意 yì (intent). The willingness of the heart to do something: 我愿意帮你 "I'm willing to help you." It expresses consent and readiness, not mere ability.
越过	yu è guò	to cross over; to get past — 越 + 过 (guò, to pass). The most basic verb of crossing: a fence, a stream, a hurdle, a difficulty. Highly productive in both literal and figurative senses.
在一起	zài yì qǐ	together (in one place); as a couple — With 在, 一起 marks being gathered in one place: 我们住在一起 ("we live together"), 把书放在一起 ("put the books together"). It also carries the romantic sense of being a couple: 他们在一起了 ("they're together now / they're dating").
造句	zào jù	to make a sentence; to compose a sentence — 造 zào (to make; to build) + 句 jù (sentence). The classroom exercise of building a sentence to practice a new word, a verb-object compound: 用 "因为" 造句 "make a sentence with 'because'."
噪音	zào yīn	noise — 噪 (clamor, wearing three mouths) + 音. The urban complaint word: construction 噪音, traffic 噪音, and the neighbor's renovation drill. 噪音污染 (noise pollution) is a recognized legal complaint category in Chinese cities.
责任	zér èn	responsibility
怎么 + 动词	zěn me + ve r b	how (to do something); in what way — The core use: placed before a verb, 怎么 asks about the method or manner of an action. 怎么走 ("how to get there"), 怎么说 ("how to say it"), 怎么用 ("how to use it").
怎么了 · 怎么办	zěn me le zěn me bàn	what's wrong · what to do — Two fixed expressions every learner needs. 怎么了 asks "what's wrong / what happened", reacting to a change you notice, the 了 marking the new state: 你脸色怎么了？("what's up with your face / you look unwell").
怎么样	zěn me yàng	how is it; how about it — The evaluating cousin of 怎么. It asks about a state or opinion rather than a method: 这家餐厅怎么样？("how's this restaurant?"), 你最近怎么样？("how have you been?"). It also floats a suggestion: 我们去看电影，怎么样？("let's see a movie, how about it?"). Negated, 不怎么样 means "nothing special, so-so".

找工作	zhǎo gōng zuò	to look for a job; to job-hunt — The set phrase for job-hunting. 找 (to look for) plus 工作. Common in life and conversation, especially among graduates and people changing roles: 毕业以后我要找工作 ("after I graduate I'll look for a job"). The result of a successful search is 找到工作 ("found a job"), with 到 marking the achieved result.

知道了	zhī dào le	got it; understood; okay — The everyday acknowledgement that you have received and registered information or an instruction. Extremely common as a one-word reply. Beware the tone: a flat or clipped 知道了 can read as impatient, like "yeah, I know already." 好的 (okay) or 明白了 (understood) are gentler alternatives.

Factual, storable, transmissible knowledge , what fills textbooks, databases, and examinations. 知识分子 zhīshi fènzǐ (intellectual; educated person , lit.
职业	zhí yè	occupation; profession; vocational — 职 zhí (post; duty) + 业 yè (work). The line of work a person practices for a living: 职业选择 "choice of profession." As a modifier it means professional or vocational: 职业运动员 "professional athlete," 职业学校 "vocational school."
质量	zhì liàng	quality: how good something is (also physical mass) — 质 zhì (substance, nature) + 量 liàng (measure). In everyday speech 质量 is quality, the standard or grade of a product or piece of work: 质量好 (good quality), 提高质量 (to raise quality). In physics the same word is the technical term for mass. Context separates the two cleanly.

珠心算	zhū xīn suàn	Abacus mental arithmetic — Abacus mental arithmetic: performing suanpan calculations by visualizing the instrument in the mind. Neurologically distinct from verbal mental arithmetic: brain imaging shows practitioners use visuospatial rather than language regions.

主题	zhǔ tí	theme; central subject; motif — 主 zhǔ (main; principal) + 题 tí (theme). The central idea that organizes a work, an event, or a piece of music: 电影的主题 "the theme of the film," 主题公园 "a theme park." It is the heading that everything else serves.

自传	zì zhuàn	autobiography — 自 zì (self) + 传 zhuàn (a life record). A biography written by its own subject, the story of a life told in the first person: 写自传 "to write an autobiography." Like 传记, it takes the zhuàn reading.
总算	zǒng suàn	at last (with relief)
最后	zuì hòu	finally; in the end; last — 最 zuì (most; superlative) + 后 (behind; last). The furthest back position in a sequence: the final item, the last moment, the ultimate outcome. Functions as both an adverb before a verb (最后他同意了 , in the end he agreed) and as an adjective modifying a noun (最后一页 , the last page).
