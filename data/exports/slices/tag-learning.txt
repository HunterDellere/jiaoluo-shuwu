//角落書屋/Slices/Learning & school
读	dú	to read aloud, to study — Reading as the act of voicing what is written: the everyday verb for studying, declaiming a text, and (figuratively) interpreting a person, a situation, or a sign.
考试	kǎo shì	examination — China ran the world's longest experiment in testing, thirteen centuries of imperial exams, and the vocabulary still sits every June for the gaokao.
历史	lì shǐ	history — The character for historian shows a hand holding a brush over a vessel: in ancient China, the scribe who held the brush held power.
明白	míng bai	to understand; clear — Understanding as illumination: sun-and-moon brightness plus plain white. The clarity verb, and its siblings 懂, 知道, and 了解.
不求甚解	bù qiú shèn jiě	not seeking deep understanding — reading for pleasure, not to dissect — From Tao Yuanming's self-portrait Mr. Five Willows (五柳先生传): 好读书，不求甚解 "He loved to read, but did not press for fine-grained interpretation." The classical defense of reading widely and lightly rather than scholastically.
东施效颦	Dōng Shī xiào pín	to imitate blindly without understanding, and make things worse — A homely woman copies Xi Shi's famous frown and clears the street: Zhuangzi's parable about form without essence.
读书破万卷	dú shū pò wàn juàn	read until ten thousand scrolls are worn through — saturate yourself in reading — From Du Fu's "Twenty-Two Rhymes Presented to Vice-Director Wei" (奉赠韦左丞丈二十二韵): 读书破万卷，下笔如有神 "Read until ten thousand scrolls are worn through, and your pen will move as if inspired." The classical statement of how literary skill is built.
读万卷书，行万里路	dú wàn juàn shū , xíng wàn lǐ lù	read ten thousand scrolls of books, walk ten thousand li of road — learn from text and from travel both — A Ming-dynasty saying attributed to the painter and theorist Dong Qichang. The pairing of 读 and 行 as the two halves of an educated life: book-knowledge and lived experience as equally necessary, neither sufficient alone.
邯郸学步	hán dān xu é bù	losing your own way while trying to imitate another's — ending up with neither — From the Zhuangzi (秋水 chapter). The problem: imitation so total that it erases the imitator's original competence. Used for people who pursue a foreign or fashionable style so completely that they lose their own footing.
画虎不成反类犬	huà hǔ bù chéng fǎn lèi quǎn	tried to paint a tiger, ended up with a dog — Colloquial proverb for the same failure: aiming high, imitating without understanding, producing something worse than the original and worse than doing nothing.
恍然大悟	huǎng rán dà wù	suddenly, greatly awakened — the flash of realization — 恍然 (in a flash) + 大悟 (great awakening, 悟 being the enlightenment verb of Chan Buddhism and the Monkey King's name 悟空). The eureka moment: the detective's final chapter, the student when the proof clicks. Chinese lets you borrow the vocabulary of enlightenment for algebra.
金榜题名	jīn bǎng tí míng	one's name inscribed on the golden list — exam success — The 金榜 was the yellow placard of palace-exam graduates posted outside the examination compound. Classical culture counted it among life's four great joys, alongside the wedding night, rain after drought, and meeting an old friend far from home. Today it captions every gaokao congratulations post.
科举	kē jǔ	the imperial examination system — The system of selecting officials by competitive written examination, which the Sui began and later dynasties developed into the central institution of Chinese government.
临时抱佛脚	lín shí bào fó jiǎo	clasping the Buddha's feet at the last minute — cramming — From a proverb about neglecting incense in ordinary times and embracing the statue's feet only when disaster looms (平时不烧香，临时抱佛脚). The universal student condition, named without mercy. Usable for any deferred preparation: visas, taxes, wedding speeches.
名落孙山	míng luò Sūn Shān	one's name fell behind Sun Shan's — to fail an examination — Sun Shan, a Song-dynasty wit, passed the provincial exam in last place. Asked by a neighbor whether the neighbor's son had passed, he answered in verse: "The last name on the list was Sun Shan; your worthy son came after Sun Shan." The gentlest failure notice in the language, still the standard lite…
难得糊涂	nán dé hú tu	rare and precious is muddleheadedness — wise to sometimes not see clearly — The famous inversion, from the Qing painter-official Zheng Banqiao, who inscribed it as a scroll: for a clear-sighted person, letting some things stay blurry is the harder and wiser art. Hung in studies and offices ever since as a license not to litigate every wrong.
十年寒窗	shí nián hán chuāng	ten years at a cold window — long, austere years of study — The image of the exam candidate reading through winters without heat. The full couplet adds the payoff: ten years at the cold window unknown, one exam success and the world knows your name (十年寒窗无人问，一举成名天下知). Modern parents invoke it during gaokao season with full sincerity.
熟读唐诗三百首	shú dú Táng shī sān bǎi shǒu	read three hundred Tang poems until you know them by heart — and you'll be able to compose poetry yourself — From the preface to the famous Qing-dynasty anthology Three Hundred Tang Poems: 熟读唐诗三百首，不会作诗也会吟. The locus classicus for the Chinese theory of learning by saturated recitation. The full saying ends "even if you can't compose, you'll be able to chant" , competence emerges from soaking in the corpus.
万丈高楼平地起	wàn zhàng gāo lóu píng dì qǐ	A tower ten thousand zhang tall rises from flat ground — The folk proverb that answers impatience: every immeasurable height begins on ordinary earth.
心知肚明	xīn zhī dù míng	the heart knows and the belly is clear — everyone understands, nobody says it — Knowledge distributed through the torso: heart knowing, belly bright. The phrase for open secrets: the office 心知肚明 about why the manager left; both negotiators 心知肚明 about the real bottom line. Understanding at its most silent.
真相大白	zhēn xiàng dà bái	the true face comes fully to light — 真相 (the true state of affairs) + 大白 (made fully plain, the white of 明白). The headline phrase when a mystery resolves or a cover-up collapses. Its verb form 水落石出 ("the water recedes and the stones emerge") describes the same moment hydrologically.
半	bàn	Half — Half. Follows the measure word when added to a whole quantity (一个半, one and a half) and precedes it when alone (半个小时, half an hour). Also in 半天 (bàntiān, "half a day"), the standard exaggeration for "ages."
背诵	bèi sòng	to recite from memory
本质	běn zhì	essence
朝代	cháo dài	dynasty: a reigning house and its era — 朝 cháo (court, reign) + 代 dài (generation, age). The standard word for a dynasty, the span during which one ruling house held the throne. Chinese history is told as a sequence of 朝代, each with its own name, capital, and character.
打折	dǎ zhé	to give a discount — 折 counts tenths of the price you pay, not the amount taken off. 八折 means you pay eight tenths, a 20% discount. Learners hear "eight" and think "80% off." In a negotiation 点 (diǎn, a percentage point) is more common than 折 for small moves.
道德经	dào dé jīng	the Daodejing
第一	dì yī	first; number one — 第 dì (ordinal) + 一 yī (one). The ordinal "first." Note the live tone-sandhi: 一 yī shifts to second tone before a fourth-tone syllable, so it is read dì-yí in 第一个 but stays dì-yī before a pause or another tone.
读懂	dú dǒng	to read-and-understand; to truly grasp — 读 dú + 懂 dǒng (to understand). A resultative compound: read-to-the-point-of-understanding. Used both literally (read a difficult text and grasp it) and metaphorically (read a person and truly know them).
读书	dú shū	to read; to study; to attend school — 读 dú + 书 shū (book). Three overlapping senses: (1) the literal act of reading, (2) studying for school or self-improvement, (3) attending an educational institution. 他在北京大学读书 "He's a student at Peking University." Closer to "to be in school" than English "to read books" suggests.
读音	dú yīn	pronunciation (of a character or word) — 读 dú + 音 yīn (sound). Literally "the read-sound" , the conventional pronunciation of a written character. The standard reference in dictionaries: 这个字的读音是 zhège zì de dúyīn shì… "the pronunciation of this character is…"
读者	dú zhě	reader (of a publication); readership — 读 dú + 者 zhě (one-who). The standard term for the reader of a book, magazine, or newspaper. 亲爱的读者 "Dear reader" is the conventional opening for a letter from the editor.
翻	fān	to flip through (a book)
方程	fāng chéng	Equation — Equation. The term is two thousand years old, taken from the chapter of the Nine Chapters that solved systems of linear equations with counting rods. 解方程 (jiě fāngchéng) is to solve an equation.
复习	fù xí	to review, revise — 复 (again) + 习 (practice, whose traditional form 習 shows wings over the sun: a fledgling's repeated flights). The exam-season verb, echoing the Analects' opening line about the pleasure of timely review, which every Chinese student can quote and few believe during finals.
高楼	gāo lóu	tall building, tower
工程	gōng chéng	engineering
公式	gōng shì	Formula — Formula: "public pattern," a form that holds generally. 套公式 (tào gōngshì, "slot into the formula") is the study-hall phrase for plug-and-chug problem solving.
攻读	gōng dú	to pursue (a degree, a field of study); to read-for — 攻 gōng (to attack; to apply oneself to) + 读. The verb for actively pursuing advanced study , a master's, a doctorate, a specialty. The 攻 conveys the sense of going at it, attacking the subject rather than passively absorbing.
积累	jī lěi	to accumulate, accumulation
基础	jī chǔ	foundation; base
及格	jí gé	to pass (an exam or standard) — 及 (reach) + 格 (the standard grid): to reach the mark, customarily 60 out of 100. 不及格 is failing, and the near-miss score of 59 is proverbial for agony: 59分 is one point short of relief.
几	jǐ	how many (small number); a few — Asks about a small, countable number, typically under ten, and must be followed by a measure word: 几个 ("how many"), 几本 ("how many volumes"), 几位 ("how many people," polite). It assumes the answer is small, so it is the word for guests, family members, hours, or days.
几何	jǐ hé	Geometry — Geometry. Classical Chinese for "how much?", repurposed by Xu Guangqi and Matteo Ricci in their 1607 translation of Euclid. Both senses live on: one in the classroom, one in Cao Cao's poetry.
计算	jì suàn	To calculate, to compute — To calculate, to compute. The formal verb behind 计算机 (jìsuànjī, computer, "calculating machine") and 计算器 (jìsuànqì, calculator). Colloquial speech often just says 算 (suàn).
记忆	jì yì	memory; to remember (formal) — 记 jì (to remember) + 忆 yì (to recall, recollect). The faculty of memory and its contents, more formal and abstract than 记得: 童年的记忆 "childhood memories," 记忆力 jìyìlì "the power of memory."
解读	jiě dú	to interpret; to construe; to make sense of — 解 jiě (to untie; to dissolve; to explain) + 读. The standard verb for interpretive reading , of a text, a result, a signal, a person's behaviour. Where 阅读 is comprehending what is plainly there, 解读 is going after what is implicit.
九层之台	jiǔ céng zhī tái	a nine-story platform
考古	kǎo gǔ	archaeology; to study antiquity through remains — 考 kǎo (to examine, to investigate) + 古 gǔ (the ancient). To investigate the past through its physical remains: 考古学 "archaeology (the discipline)," 考古发现 "an archaeological discovery." The verb of unearthing and reading 古迹, the traces the past left in the ground.
科学	kē xu é	science; scientific — 科 kē (branch; category; department) + 学 xué (learning; -ology). Lit. "categorized learning" , the systematic division of knowledge into disciplines. Coined as a translation for Western "science" in the late 19th century.
课文	kè wén	text (in a textbook)
朗读	lǎng dú	to read aloud (clearly, with expression) — 朗 lǎng (clear; bright; resonant) + 读. The school-room and literary register for reading aloud , the kind of reading where pronunciation, rhythm, and feeling matter. Standard exercise in Chinese language classes.
老师	lǎo shī	teacher — the most common honorific in Chinese education — 老 lǎo (venerable) + 师 shī (master; teacher; expert). The standard term for teacher at all levels, from elementary school through university. Used as a title of address (王老师 "Teacher Wang") and as a term of respect for anyone with expertise to transmit.
了解	liǎo jiě	to understand thoroughly; to have rounded knowledge of — 了 liǎo (clear-sighted; complete) + 解 jiě (to unravel; to untie). The full unraveling of something until it is entirely clear , understanding that goes all the way through, not just surface knowledge. More thorough than 知道 zhīdao (to know a fact). Common across casual speech and formal registers.
历法	lì fǎ	lunisolar calendar
历史观	lì shǐ guān	historical worldview; philosophy of history — 历史 + 观 (view; perspective; outlook , the same 观 as in 世界观 shìjièguān, worldview, and 人生观 rénshēngguān, outlook on life). 历史观 is the framework through which one interprets history: its patterns, its forces, its meaning.
历史性	lì shǐ xìng	historic; of historical significance — 历史 + 性 (the suffix forming abstract quality nouns: 重要性 importance, 可能性 possibility). 历史性 modifies nouns to mean "of historic significance" , a moment or event that will be recorded as consequential. 历史性会议 (a historic meeting), 历史性协议 (a historic agreement).
历史学	lì shǐ xu é	historiography; the academic discipline of history — 历史 + 学 (the suffix marking a field of systematic study: 哲学 philosophy, 科学 science, 心理学 psychology). 历史学 is history as an academic discipline , the methods, debates, and institutions of scholarly historical inquiry, as distinct from history as the accumulated record of events.
历史学家	lì shǐ xu é jiā	historian — 历史学 + 家 (the suffix for a person who is expert in or practises something: 科学家 scientist, 艺术家 artist, 音乐家 musician). The formal word for a professional historian. The classical equivalent, 史官 (shǐguān, court historian), referred specifically to the official record-keeper of a dynasty.
模仿	mó fǎng	to imitate, to mimic
默读	mò dú	to read silently — 默 mò (silent) + 读. The marked term for silent reading , marked precisely because the unmarked sense of 读 is voiced. A reading classroom may instruct students 先默读，再朗读 "first read silently, then aloud."
起步	qǐ bù	to take the first steps, starting point
千里之行	qiān lǐ zhī xíng	a journey of a thousand li
清楚	qīng chu	clear, distinct — 清 (clear water) + 楚 (distinct). The clarity of things and statements: 说清楚 (say it clearly), 看清楚 (see it distinctly). Pairs with 明白 across the divide of world and mind: the map is 清楚, the reader is 明白.
诗	shī	poem; regulated verse
史记	Shǐ jì	Records of the Grand Historian (Sima Qian, c. 94 BCE) — 史 (historical record) + 记 (to record; to note). The foundational work of Chinese historiography, written by Sima Qian (司马迁, c. 145–86 BCE) over decades and completed after his castration by order of Emperor Wu of Han.
史料	shǐ liào	historical materials; primary sources — 史 (historical record) + 料 (materials; raw material , the same 料 as in 材料 cáiliào, material/ingredient). 史料 refers to primary source materials: documents, inscriptions, court records, archaeological finds , the raw evidence from which historical accounts are constructed.
史书	shǐ shū	historical texts; history books (classical register) — 史 (historical record) + 书 (book; written text). The classical compound for historical writings, used in literary and academic contexts. 史书记载 (shǐshū jìzǎi, "according to historical records") is a standard phrase for citing the written tradition.
始于足下	shǐ yú zú xià	begins beneath the feet
数学	shù xu é	Mathematics — Mathematics: number-study. 数学家 (shùxuéjiā) is a mathematician; 数学课 (shùxué kè) is math class, the collocation every student uses daily.
数字	shù zì	Number, numeral, digit — Number, numeral, digit. The everyday word for a figure, whether spoken, written, or on a screen. 数字化 (shùzìhuà) extends it to "digitization," the same move English made with "digit."
数字系统	shù zì xì tǒng	the number system
四大美人	sì dà měi rén	the Four Beauties of ancient China
算盘	suàn pán	The abacus, literally "calculating tray — The abacus, literally "calculating tray." The standard Chinese bead-frame instrument with 2 upper beads and 5 lower beads per rod, in use in its canonical form since at least the 16th century.
物理	wù lǐ	physics; the principles of matter — 物 wù (thing; matter) + 理 lǐ (principle). Literally "the principles of things," the modern name for physics. A clear case of 理 meaning the lawful pattern running through the physical world.
西施	Xī Shī	Xi Shi, one of the Four Beauties
效仿	xiào fǎng	to imitate, to follow the example of
形式	xíng shì	form; format: the outward shape, as opposed to content — 形 xíng + 式 shì (pattern; format). The form or format of something, set against its 内容 nèiróng (content). 各种形式 = various forms; 形式上 = in form only, nominally. 形式主义 = formalism, prizing appearance over substance. Do not confuse with 形势 (the situation): see the 辨析 section above.
宣读	xuān dú	to read out formally (a statement, verdict, decree) — 宣 xuān (to announce; to proclaim) + 读. To read aloud in a formal setting , a court verdict, a wedding vow, an official document at a ceremony. The 宣 marks the public, declarative force.
学生	xu é sheng	student — 学 xué (to study; to learn) + 生 shēng (one who is still "green"; a junior person). The student as someone still growing, not yet ripe. One of the most common nouns in Chinese , every school context relies on it.
学习	xu é xí	to study; to learn (ongoing process) — 学 xué (to learn) + 习 xí (to practice; to review; to get accustomed to). The compound captures the Confucian dual process: 学 (receiving new knowledge) + 习 (practicing and consolidating it). Not just acquisition but repeated reinforcement. The most common word for "to study" in general.
学校	xu é xiào	school
揠苗助长	yà miáo zhù zhǎng	pulling up seedlings to help them grow
亿	yì	One hundred million, the great unit above 万 — One hundred million, the great unit above 万. China's population is 十四亿 (shísì yì, 1.4 billion), a figure every learner should be able to parse and produce.
圆周率	yuán zhōu lǜ	Pi — Pi: "circle-circumference ratio," usually written π and read pài in class. Zu Chongzhi's 355/113 approximation is taught to every Chinese student as a point of scientific heritage.
阅读	yu è dú	to read (with comprehension); reading (as a skill) — 阅 yuè (to inspect; to review) + 读. The formal, written-register word for reading-with-understanding. The standard term in education: 阅读理解 yuèdú lǐjiě "reading comprehension," 阅读量 yuèdú liàng "amount one has read." More deliberate than 看 (to look-at) and more comprehension-focused than 读 alone.
重读	chóng dú / zhòng dú	to re-read (chóng); to stress (a syllable, zhòng) — 重 + 读 , but 重 is a polyphone. chóngdú = "again-read," to re-read a book or article. zhòngdú = "heavy-read," to stress or accent a syllable in pronunciation. Same characters, different reading, different meaning entirely.
庄子	Zhuāng zǐ	Zhuangzi, the Daoist text and its author
状元	zhuàng yuán	top scorer; the imperial exam's first graduate — The palace examination's number one, personally ranked by the emperor, paraded through the capital on horseback. The word now crowns any field's best: 高考状元 (top gaokao scorer), 行行出状元 ("every trade produces its zhuangyuan," the proverb that dignifies all work).
作弊	zuò bì	to cheat (on an exam) — 作 (do) + 弊 (fraud, malpractice). Exam cheating is a criminal offense in China for national exams, policed with metal detectors and signal jammers, which measures how much the tests are worth. The honest sibling 打小抄 (crib notes) covers the classroom misdemeanor.
