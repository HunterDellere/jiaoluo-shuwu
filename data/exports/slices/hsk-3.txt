必	bì	certainly; must; necessarily — A character rooted in the image of a blade that divides without exception: certainty as the sharpness that leaves no room for maybe.
变	biàn	to change, to transform — The character at the heart of the I Ching, the dynastic cycle, and the modern vocabulary of reform: change as the one constant.
茶	chá	tea — The character that gave the world its word for tea: a grass over wood over man, a plant that shaped civilization, and the locus of China's richest material culture.
产	chǎn	to produce, give birth, property, output — From birth to bounty: a character that spans biological generation and industrial output, linking the miracle of birth to the logic of production and wealth.
朝	cháo	dynasty, imperial court, to face toward; (zhāo) morning — The sun rising among grasses with the moon still in the sky. From that dawn scene grow two readings: zhāo, the morning and its youthful vigor, and cháo, the court that met at daybreak, the dynasty it served, and the preposition toward.
城	chéng	city, city wall, town — A rampart of earth raised around a settlement: the character where the wall and the city it protects are one and the same word.
传	chuán	to pass on, transmit, spread; (zhuan) a biography, a commentary — The person radical carrying something onward: one figure handing a thing to the next. From that image grow the senses of 传, read chuan when it means to pass down, transmit, and spread, and read zhuan when it names a biography or a classical commentary.
代	dài	generation, era, to substitute, on behalf of — The person radical carrying the sense of succession: one figure standing in the place of another. From that single image grow the four faces of 代: to replace or act for someone, to do a thing on their behalf, the generation that succeeds the one before, and the era or dynasty in which a life is set.
道	dào	way, path — The character that opens the Dàodéjīng and names Daoism: way, path, principle, and the measure word for rivers and light.
低	dī	low; to lower; to bow down — A person stooping: the phonetic 氐 gives the sound while the human radical supplies the posture, and together they built one of the most culturally loaded words in modern Chinese.
调	diào	(diào) to transfer, tune, accent; (tiáo) to adjust, to blend, to mediate — A high-frequency polyphone with two readings. As diào it means to transfer, a melody or key, a tone, an accent; as tiáo it means to adjust, to blend, to mediate. The reading tells you whether 调 is moving things or harmonizing them.
风	fēng	wind; style; custom — The character that moves crops, carries seeds, and became the Chinese word for cultural character itself.
夫	fū	husband, a grown man, laborer; the literary particle fú — A grown man pictured with a hairpin marking adulthood: the husband and the married couple (丈夫 夫妻 夫人 夫妇), the adult man and laborer (农夫 渔夫 大夫), skill and effort (功夫 工夫), and the classical literary particle 夫 fú that opens a sentence.
福	fú	fortune, blessing, good luck — The most recognized Chinese character in the world: fortune, blessing, and the art of the upside-down 福.
父	fù	father, male elder, household head — A hand gripping a stone axe: the tool of the one who heads the household, drawn as the figure of the father himself.
富	fù	rich, wealthy, abundant, to enrich — A full wine jar tucked under a roof: the picture of a household storehouse packed to its rafters with plenty.
告	gào	to tell, to announce, to report — A mouth aimed upward: the character for delivering words to authority, and the root of advertising, accusations, and warnings.
功	gōng	merit, achievement, skill, effort — Merit earned through effort: from meritorious service and success to the patient skill the Chinese call gongfu, and even work in physics.
古	gǔ	ancient, old, classical — The mouth radical holding what ten generations have spoken down the years: 古 is what is old because it has been handed on. From that single image grow the senses of antiquity (古代 古老 远古), the classical and the antique (古典 古文 古董 古迹 考古), and the quaint or unconventional (古怪 古板).
害	hài	to harm, injury, calamity, to fear — Under the roof a mouth and a blade build the sense of injury done; the same glyph also means to fear, as in 害怕.
化	huà	to change, to transform, to melt — The character of transformation itself: melting, digesting, civilizing, and the productive -ize suffix that builds half of modern technical Chinese.
价	jià	price, value, worth — A person standing beside 介 jiè: the worth a thing takes on only once two people sit down to trade for it.
简	jiǎn	simple, brief, terse; a bamboo writing slip — Before paper, a letter was a strip of bamboo; from that narrow slip 简 grew into the words for simple, brief, and a short note.
脚	jiǎo	foot, base — The everyday word for foot, and by extension the base of any standing thing: a mountain, a wall, a script. The flesh-and-bone counterpart to literary 足.
解	jiě	to untie, to solve, to explain, to dissolve — The character for cutting apart and undoing: untying and liberating, solving and explaining, dissolving and relieving. A polyphone read jiě, jiè, and xiè, pictured as a hand prying an ox horn from the ox.
金	jīn	gold, metal, money — Nuggets of gold beneath the earth, and one of the Five Phases that governs autumn, the west, and sharpness.
紧	jǐn	tight, taut, tense, urgent — Thread drawn taut over the silk radical; the same word carries a tight knot, a tense nerve, an urgent deadline, and a tight wallet.
近	jìn	near; close; recent — Walking toward something as close as a hatchet strike, and the character that covers both spatial proximity and the specific historical era Chinese scholars call the modern period.
救	jiù	to rescue, to save, to relieve — A hand wields a stick to pull someone out of danger; the phonetic 求 once meant a desperate plea for help.
空	kōng	empty, sky, void; in vain — A cave with the work of hollowing inside it. The character that names the open sky, the empty room, the wasted afternoon, and the Buddhist śūnyatā at the heart of Chan.
口	kǒu	mouth, opening, entrance — A simple rectangle for an open mouth: one of the most productive radicals in the script, present in hundreds of characters for speaking, eating, and openings.
苦	kǔ	bitter, hardship, suffering, painstaking — The grass radical over 古 names a bitter herb on the tongue, then stretches to every flavor of hardship and effort.
困	kùn	to trap, hard-pressed, tired — A tree boxed inside four walls and unable to grow: the picture of being hemmed in, the source of both hardship and sleepiness.
历	lì	to experience, history, calendar — One simplified glyph that absorbed two traditional characters: 歷 (to pass through, history) and 曆 (calendar). The word that holds both lived experience and reckoned time.
连	lián	to link, to join, even — Carts moving in a line: a character that joins things in space, in sequence, and in emphasis, from 连接 to the famous 连...都 construction.
龙	lóng	dragon, the imperial symbol — China's most powerful mythic creature: benevolent, not fearsome; the emperor's emblem and the Chinese people's self-described totem.
慢	màn	slow, unhurried — Pace becomes a moral register: 慢 màn carries the same heart radical as 快, and extends from physical slowness into social neglect and arrogance.
民	mín	the people, a citizen, folk — The common people as a collective subject: the populace, the citizen, the folk and the nation, and the civilian set against the military. From 人民 the people to 民族 ethnic nation to 民主 democracy.
念	niàn	to recite, to keep in mind, to miss — 念 holds the present moment 今 over the heart 心: to keep something in mind, to recite aloud, to long for what is absent. The 念 of 念佛 recitation, 想念 longing, and the single thought-moment of Buddhist psychology.
农	nóng	agriculture, farming, farmer, rural — A character compressed from a graph that showed working the fields at the time of the stars, the whole cycle of growing crops.
品	pǐn	article, grade, character, to taste — Three mouths stacked: a character about multiplicity, evaluation, and the discerning assessment of quality, from product grades to moral character.
气	qì	breath, vital energy — From weather to anger to the cosmic force underlying TCM and martial arts.
认	rèn	to recognize, to acknowledge — The act of recognition: knowing something for what it is, owning up to it, and the small set of compounds that turn this on the world, on others, and on oneself.
容	róng	to contain, to allow, appearance — A roof over a valley: the space that holds. To contain, to tolerate, the face one shows, and the easy in rongyi.
社	shè	society, an association, the god of the soil — The altar radical beside the earth: a shrine to the god of the soil and the community that gathered around it. From that image grow society itself, the many kinds of association and agency, and the ancient soil-and-grain spirits that once stood for the state.
神	shén	spirit; deity; divine — The character that names both the gods of Chinese folk religion and the focused attention of the human mind, because in Chinese thought they belong to the same category of phenomenon.
世	shì	world, age, generation, lifetime — A pictograph traditionally read as three tens joined together, the span of thirty years that makes one generation. From that root grow the four faces of 世: the generation that succeeds the one before, the lifetime or the life to come, the era stacked from many lives, and the wider world of human society in which all of it unfolds.
首	shǒu	head, first, chief, a measure word for songs and poems — A drawn head, with hair above an eye, and from it the senses of first, foremost, chief, leader, and the classifier you count songs and poems with. 首 is its own radical.
书	shū	book, writing, letter — Book, writing, calligraphy: the character that holds civilization together.
思	sī	to think, to miss, thought — The field of the mind above the heart: thinking as cultivation, longing as thought turned inward.
图	tú	map, diagram, to plan, to seek — A character for diagrams, maps, intentions, and plots: anything that represents the world at one remove, or seeks to reshape it.
土	tǔ	earth, soil, local, native — A mound of soil with a base: the earth phase of the Five Elements, and the root of hundreds of characters for land, construction, and cultural belonging.
王	wáng	king — Three horizontals joined by one vertical: the ruler who connects heaven, earth, and humanity. Also the most common Chinese surname, and a radical that is secretly 玉 (jade) in disguise.
望	wàng	to gaze afar, to hope, reputation — A person on a mound looking into the distance, with the moon above: from that raised gaze grow three senses. To gaze and look afar (眺望, 望远镜, 张望), to hope and expect (希望, 期望, 盼望, 失望), and reputation or prestige (名望, 威望, 声望). As a coverb it can even mean toward, like 朝 or 向.
危	wēi	danger, peril, precarious, lofty height — A kneeling figure pinned beneath a cliff overhang spells danger, where the same overhang also carries the sound.
屋	wū	room, house, dwelling — Room, dwelling, and the human need for shelter: the character at home.
武	wǔ	martial, military, valiant — The foot radical 止 carrying a dagger-axe 戈: the soldier on the march, and the older reading that to wield arms well is finally to halt them. From 武力 armed force to 武术 martial arts to 威武 powerful and dignified, the character holds the whole world of strength wielded with discipline.
香	xiāng	fragrant, delicious, incense, popular — Grain stacked over a mouth of sweetness: 香 is the smell of ripe rice, and the word still means both perfume and a hot meal worth eating.
形	xíng	shape, form, to appear, to compare — The character for visible form: shape and figure, the body as form, the act of taking shape and being described, and the lay of a situation. A pictophonetic compound built on the pattern-strokes radical 彡.
性	xìng	nature, character, sex/gender, the -ness/-ity suffix — The heart radical carrying the sense of innate nature: what a thing is born to be. From that core grow the four faces of 性: a persons disposition or character, sex and gender, the property or quality of a thing, and the productive abstract suffix that turns adjectives into -ness and -ity nouns.
义	yì	righteousness, duty, justice — The Confucian virtue of doing what is morally right, and the simplification that erased a sheep, a self, and the entire picture of what righteous form looks like.
必须	bì xū	must, have to (the obligation modal) — 必须 is the strongest of the everyday modal verbs of obligation: a non-negotiable requirement that the speaker presents as not-up-for-discussion.
茶道	chá dào	the way of tea — From wild forest leaf to civilizational ritual: how tea became China's quintessential art of presence.
茶具	chá ju	Tea Implements — The vessels, tools, and objects of the tea table: from the Yixing purple clay teapot to the fairness pitcher, each implement with a name, a function, and an aesthetic logic.
产品	chǎn pǐn	product, manufactured good — The standard Chinese word for product: what production yields and commerce moves. From factory floor to digital marketplace, 产品 names the output of organized human effort.
传统	chuán tǒng	tradition; traditional — The thread passed down through generations: and the concept that sits at the center of every Chinese debate about modernity, identity, and what to keep.
风水	fēng shuǐ	wind and water — Geomancy, qi flow, and the cosmological logic of favorable siting.
风俗	fēng sú	customs, traditions, folk practices — The wind of culture and the ways of the people: how 风俗 differs from 习惯 and why the distinction matters.
工夫茶	gōng fu chá	Gongfu Tea — Tea prepared with skill and time: the Southern Chinese brewing method that defines serious tea culture, built on small vessels, multiple short steeps, and total attention.
功夫	gōng fu	effort, skill over time — Mastery earned through time: martial arts is the paradigm case, but the word covers any deeply cultivated skill.
节气	jié qì	the 24 solar terms — China's ancient agricultural calendar: 24 precise moments that mark the turning of the year, still shaping food, medicine, and daily life.
客气	kè qi	politeness, ceremony — The polite manners owed to guests: and the gentle dance of refusal, insistence, and ritual modesty that runs through every Chinese social interaction.
历史	lì shǐ	history — The character for historian shows a hand holding a brush over a vessel: in ancient China, the scribe who held the brush held power.
气功	qì gōng	qigong — Cultivating the vital breath: China's ancient practice of aligning body, breath, and mind.
认真	rèn zhēn	earnest, conscientious, taking it seriously — To recognize the 真 of a thing and engage with it on its own terms: the everyday word for taking work, study, and other people seriously rather than going through motions.
社会	shè huì	society — The word for society that China borrowed from Japan, which had borrowed the characters from China: a compound forged at the altar of the earth.
神明	shén míng	gods, spirits, deities; divine intelligence — The generic term for any deity in the Chinese religious world: and, in classical philosophy, the luminous intelligence that distinguishes the divine from the merely powerful.
生产	shēng chǎn	to produce, production, manufacturing — The foundational act of economic life: and, in China's Marxist political vocabulary, the act from which all social relations flow. Life gives rise to production; production sustains life.
时代	shí dài	era, age — The word for a historical era or age, built from 时 (time) and 代 (generation), and how it differs from 时期 a period, 年代 a decade, 时候 a moment, and 现代 modern times.
书法	shū fǎ	calligraphy — The art where writing becomes a performance of character: and why the Chinese brush is a philosophical instrument.
文化	wén huà	culture; civilization — The word that names the organizing project of this entire site: and a concept that Chinese thought built from the ground up differently than the West did.
武术	wǔ shù	martial arts — The art of stopping weapons: how China systematized combat into a living philosophy of the body.
希望	xī wàng	to hope — The everyday word for hope, both the verb and the noun, and how it differs from 想 (to want), 愿意 (to be willing), 盼望 (to long for), and 期待 (to look forward to).
现代	xiàn dài	modern times — The word for the modern era, built from 现 (present, manifest) and 代 (generation, age), how Chinese history slices time into 古代 / 近代 / 现代 / 当代, and how 现代 differs from 现在 now.
义	yì	Righteousness: The Second Constant — Doing what is right because it is right: 义 as the enacted path of Confucian ethics, the constant that gives 仁 its direction.
意思	yì si	meaning, intention — Meaning, interest, charm, and the gift-giving idiom 意思意思.
长城	Cháng chéng	the Great Wall — A series of fortification systems built across multiple dynasties, whose most visible sections are Ming-era brick: not the continuous ancient rampart of popular imagination.
知道	zhī dào	to know — The everyday verb for knowing a fact, and how it differs from 认识 (to be acquainted with) and 了解 (to understand deeply).
爱屋及乌	ài wū jí wū	love me, love my dog — love extends to all connected with the beloved — Lit: love-house reach-crow. If you love the house, you even love the crow on the roof. Love spreads to everything associated with the beloved, even people or things that would otherwise seem unremarkable.
八仙过海，各显神通	bā xiān guò hǎi , gè xiǎn shén tōng	"the Eight Immortals cross the sea, each showing their powers" — everyone uses their own talents — The Eight Immortals, crossing the sea, each rely on a different magical skill rather than a single method. The saying describes a group facing a shared task in which every member contributes through their own distinct ability, and is used to praise resourceful, varied teamwork.
败坏门风	bài huài mén fēng	to ruin the family's good name: to disgrace one's household — 败坏 (to corrupt, ruin) acting on 门风, a family's reputation and moral tradition. Said of conduct shameful enough to stain the whole family, a phrase out of old domestic and clan morality.
饱经风霜	bǎo jīng fēng shuāng	to have weathered wind and frost: hardened by hardship — 饱 here means having had a full share of something. The image is a face lined by years of exposure; used of people who have lived through long adversity. Common in literary and journalistic prose.
暴风骤雨	bào fēng zhòu yǔ	violent wind and sudden rain — a sudden, overwhelming storm of events — Lit: violent-wind-sudden-rain. 暴 means violent, sudden, savage; 骤 means abrupt, arriving all at once. The idiom describes any sudden, overwhelming onslaught: a torrential crisis, a savage attack, a rapid political upheaval.
北风怒号	běi fēng nù háo	"the north wind howls in fury" — biting cold; adversity — 北风 (north wind) + 怒 (furious) + 号 (to howl). A classical weather image used in poetry and prose to evoke bitter cold and desolation. Du Fu's famous poem 茅屋为秋风所破歌 uses the howling north wind as a metaphor for the hardships of his refugee years during the An Lushan Rebellion (755–763 CE).
笔走龙蛇	bǐ zǒu lóng shé	the brush moves like dragons and snakes: bold, flowing, vigorous calligraphy — Lit: brush-runs-dragon-snake. The line of cursive writing twists and surges with the energy of a dragon and a snake in motion. High praise for a calligrapher whose hand is alive and free.
变本加厉	biàn běn jiā lì	to alter the base and pile on more: to grow worse than before, to intensify — Lit: change-base-add-severity. Said when behavior that was already bad escalates rather than improving, the original measure exceeded and then redoubled.
变化莫测	biàn huà mò cè	change beyond prediction — unpredictable; mercurial; always surprising — 莫 mò = no one can; impossible to. 测 cè = measure; fathom; predict. Lit: "the changes cannot be measured." Used to describe weather, markets, political situations, and people whose behavior cannot be anticipated.
彪形大汉	biāo xíng dà hàn	a burly, powerfully built man — Lit: tiger-bodied big fellow. 彪 (a young tiger) lends its image of muscular bulk, so the phrase paints a tall, broad, imposing man, the bodyguard or strongman of a scene.
兵临城下	bīng lín chéng xià	troops at the foot of the wall: the enemy at the gates — 兵 bīng = soldiers, troops; 临 lín = to arrive at, to overlook; 城下 = below the city wall. The enemy army has reached the base of the wall, the last moment before a siege becomes an assault.
兵者，诡道也。	bīng zhě , guǐ dào yě .	Warfare is the way of deception. — A blunt rejection of chivalric ideals: success in conflict depends on shaping the enemy's perceptions, concealing one's own strength and intentions, and striking where one is not expected.
不到长城非好汉	bú dào cháng chéng fēi hǎo hàn	"he who has not reached the Great Wall is not a true hero" — From Mao Zedong's 1935 poem 清平乐·六盘山. Not technically a 成语 (it is seven characters), but functions as a fixed quotation. Often paired in modern usage with 越长城: to "cross the wall" is to prove oneself. The verb implied is 越 (to cross over) or 到 (to reach).
不紧不慢	bù jǐn bù màn	neither tight nor slow: unhurried, even-paced — A steady, measured tempo with no rush and no dawdling. Often admiring, describing someone who keeps composure under pressure.
不可思议	bù kě sī yì	inconceivable; unimaginable — Literally "cannot be thought or discussed," beyond comprehension, originally a Buddhist term for the ineffable. Here 可 ("can / possible"), the first character of 可能, takes its sense of "able to be": what cannot possibly be grasped by thought.
不可一世	bù kě yī shì	insufferably arrogant: to think no one in the whole age is one's equal — Lit: cannot be matched by anyone of the age. Said of someone so overweeningly proud that they look down on everyone alive, behaving as though the entire era holds no rival worthy of them. Strongly critical in tone.
不求甚解	bù qiú shèn jiě	not seeking deep understanding — reading for pleasure, not to dissect — From Tao Yuanming's self-portrait Mr. Five Willows (五柳先生传): 好读书，不求甚解 "He loved to read, but did not press for fine-grained interpretation." The classical defense of reading widely and lightly rather than scholastically.
不容置疑	bù róng zhì yí	leaving no room for doubt: beyond question; indisputable — 不容 = leaves no room for; 置疑 = to cast doubt. A fact or conclusion so certain that no doubt can be entertained. The same 不容 pattern as 义不容辞: there is simply no space for the doubt to enter. 这个事实不容置疑。(This fact is beyond dispute.)
不违农时	bù wéi nóng shí	do not violate the farming seasons: act in step with the right time — From the Mencius (孟子). The ruler who does not drag farmers off to other work during planting and harvest will have grain enough to feed the realm. Now a general caution against wrong timing.
才思敏捷	cái sī mǐn jié	quick-witted; rapid and agile in thought and creative expression — 才思 cáisī = literary talent and flow of ideas (才 here is the noun "talent," not the adverb). 敏捷 = swift and agile. A high compliment for writers, poets, and scholars who produce brilliant work quickly.
茶馆酒肆	chá guǎn jiǔ sì	teahouses and wine-shops: the everyday public houses of a town — A four-character set phrase pairing 茶馆 with 酒肆 (the wine-shop) to stand for the street-level places where ordinary people gathered, drank, and talked.
朝令夕改	zhāo lìng xī gǎi	issue an order at dawn and change it by dusk — From debates on Han fiscal policy. A sharp criticism of fickle, unstable rule whose constant reversals leave people unable to plan.
朝气蓬勃	zhāo qì péng bó	full of youthful vigor: brimming with life and energy — Lit: morning-vigor, luxuriant-thriving. 朝气 is the spirit of a new day, 蓬勃 the look of growth in full flush. Said of people, teams, or whole societies that are young and full of drive.
朝秦暮楚	zhāo Qín mù Chǔ	Qin in the morning, Chu at night — serving different masters; inconsistency and opportunism — From the reality of Warring States advisors who moved between states as circumstances changed. The phrase originally described border regions whose allegiance shifted with the military tide. Now a common idiom for fickleness, opportunism, or having no fixed loyalties.
朝三暮四	zhāo sān mù sì	three in the morning, four at dusk: fickle, forever changing one's mind — From a fable in which a keeper offers his monkeys three nuts in the morning and four at night; they protest, so he swaps it to four and three, and they are content though the total is the same. Now it names someone fickle, who keeps switching with no real difference.
朝思暮想	zhāo sī mù xiǎng	to think of morning and night; to long for ceaselessly — 朝 zhāo (morning; dawn) + 思 sī (to think of; to long for) + 暮 mù (dusk; evening) + 想 xiǎng (to think of; to miss). Thinking at dawn and thinking at dusk: longing that fills the entire arc of a day. Used for intense, sustained yearning: romantic longing, homesickness, or grief for the absent.
城隍爷	Chéng huáng yé	City God (respectful address) — The suffix 爷 yé is an honorific for senior male figures, divine and human: 土地爷, 老天爷. It conveys respectful familiarity rather than formal distance. Worshippers address the City God as 城隍爷 in prayer and casual speech; formal temple documents may use the full title with rank designation.
吃苦耐劳	chī kǔ nài láo	to endure hardship and persevere in labor — a core work-ethic virtue — The defining work-ethic phrase in Chinese culture. Applied to individuals, generations, and national character. Eating bitterness + tolerating labor = the formula for achievement.
吃亏是福	chī kuī shì fú	suffering loss is a blessing — short-term disadvantage leads to long-term benefit — A proverb attributed to Zheng Banqiao (Qing dynasty). The Daoist-inflected wisdom: accepting being at a disadvantage cultivates humility and patience, which eventually bring greater good. Widely quoted in Chinese business and parenting contexts.
愁眉苦脸	chóu méi kǔ liǎn	knitted brows and a bitter face: a picture of gloom and worry — 愁 (worry) eyebrows over a 苦 (bitter) face. The whole head sags with anxiety. A vivid, everyday way to say someone looks miserable.
出神入化	chū shén rù huà	to leave the mundane and enter the magical: superb; consummate; masterly — 出 chū = to leave, 神 shén = the divine, 入 rù = to enter, 化 huà = the transformed, transcendent state.
传宗接代	chuán zōng jiē dài	to carry on the family line: to continue the ancestral lineage through descendants — Lit: pass on the clan, link the generations. The traditional duty of producing heirs so that the family line does not end. 宗 is the ancestral lineage and 代 the generations; 传 and 接 are the passing-on and the picking-up that keep the chain unbroken.
春风得意	chūn fēng dé yì	riding the spring wind, full of pride: flush with success — From Mèng Jiāo's Tang poem on passing the imperial exam. Describes someone on a winning streak, often with a hint that the pride may be premature.
从容不迫	cóng róng bù pò	calm and unhurried: composed under pressure — 从容 = calm, leisurely; 不迫 = not pressed, not rushed. To act with poise and no sign of haste even when stakes are high. Here 容 sits in 从容, the word for unflustered composure: someone with room inside to spare. 他从容不迫地回答了所有问题。(He answered every question calmly and without haste.)
大功告成	dà gōng gào chéng	the great work is accomplished — mission complete — Lit: great-achievement-announce-accomplished. A formal declaration that a major undertaking (a construction project, a campaign, a long creative work) has been completed. Often used with a tone of proud announcement: 大功告成！(We did it!).
大失所望	dà shī suǒ wàng	to be greatly let down: bitterly disappointed in what one had hoped for — Literally "greatly to lose what one hoped for." Used when reality falls far short of expectation and the disappointment is severe, as when an eagerly awaited result turns out badly. The intensified form of plain 失望.
大手大脚	dà shǒu dà jiǎo	big hands and big feet — extravagant; throwing money around — Lit: big-hand-big-foot. Said of someone who spends lavishly without thinking, makes large gestures, lives beyond their means. Mildly disapproving, the opposite of 节俭 jiéjiǎn (frugal).
大义凛然	dà yì lǐn rán	full of the greatest righteousness — awe-inspiring moral authority; unshakeable in the face of evil — 大义 dàyì (the greater righteousness; overriding moral principle) + 凛然 lǐnrán (awe-inspiring; commanding; cool and stern; the quality of moral authority that makes others straighten up).
大有希望	dà yǒu xī wàng	very promising; full of hope — Literally "to greatly have hope," said of a person or prospect with bright chances: 这个项目大有希望 ("this project is very promising"). A warm, encouraging phrase built directly on 希望, the bright mirror of 大失所望.
荡气回肠	dàng qì huí cháng	stirring the breath and coiling the gut: deeply moving, soul-stirring — Lit: surging-breath-coiling-innards. Said of music, writing, or a story so moving that it seems to turn one's very insides. 回 keeps its coiling sense here.
道不同不相为谋	dào bù tóng bù xiāng wéi móu	"those who follow different ways cannot work together" — fundamental value divergence ends cooperation — From the Analects: when the fundamental principles (道) that govern a person's life diverge, strategic cooperation becomes impossible. Used when parting ways with a colleague, organization, or ally over principle , not personal dislike but incompatible directions.
道可道，非常道。	dào kě dào , fēi cháng dào .	The Way that can be spoken is not the constant Way. — The opening line of chapter 1, and the most famous sentence in the entire text. It establishes at once that the 道 lies beyond the reach of fixed language: any 道 that can be fully captured in words is already a limited, conditioned thing, not the constant, nameless source.
道听途说	dào tīng tú shuō	"heard on the road, told on the path" — hearsay; rumor; unverified gossip — 途 tú = road; path (in transit). Things overheard on the road, passed on without verification. The road is a place of unfiltered information, strangers, and half-truths. 不要相信道听途说。(Don't believe hearsay.) Used to dismiss rumors or unverified claims.
得道多助	dé dào duō zhù	"the righteous have many helpers" — moral legitimacy generates real-world support — From Mencius (孟子). Full phrase: 得道多助，失道寡助 (Those who have the Way have many helpers; those without the Way have few). Moral legitimacy is not merely symbolic , it creates tangible coalitions. Those who govern well attract support; those who govern badly find themselves isolated.
得意忘形	dé yì wàng xíng	so pleased with oneself that one forgets propriety — overweening pride — Lit: obtain-pleasure-forget-form/shape. 得意 = satisfied, pleased (here 得 is the full verb "to get/obtain," not the particle). 忘形 = to forget one's bearing/form. Used for someone carried away by success or compliments.
低声下气	dī shēng xià qì	"lower the voice, lower the breath" — to speak with extreme humility; to be submissive in order to placate someone — 低声 (lower the voice) + 下气 (bring down one's breath/spirit). The physical image is precise: a person who speaks quietly and exhales slowly is physiologically in a submissive, de-escalated state.
点石成金	diǎn shí chéng jīn	to touch stone and turn it to gold: to transform the ordinary into the priceless — Lit: touch-stone-become-gold. From Daoist immortal lore, the touch that turns base rock to gold. Now used of an editor, teacher, or craftsman who makes something dull suddenly shine.
东张西望	dōng zhāng xī wàng	"looking east and west" — glancing around nervously or curiously; gawking — Lit: east-look-west-gaze. Used for someone whose eyes dart around, whether out of nervousness, suspicion, or simple distraction. The four-character structure vividly enacts the scanning motion itself. 他在街上东张西望，好像在找人。"He was looking all around the street as if searching for someone."
动手动脚	dòng shǒu dòng jiǎo	to move hands and feet: to get physical, to grope or rough up — Lit: move-hand-move-foot. To put one's hands on someone, either to start a fight or to touch improperly. The doubled 动 spreads the action across hands and feet alike.
读书破万卷	dú shū pò wàn juàn	read until ten thousand scrolls are worn through — saturate yourself in reading — From Du Fu's "Twenty-Two Rhymes Presented to Vice-Director Wei" (奉赠韦左丞丈二十二韵): 读书破万卷，下笔如有神 "Read until ten thousand scrolls are worn through, and your pen will move as if inspired." The classical statement of how literary skill is built.
法不容情	fǎ bù róng qíng	the law allows no room for sentiment: justice must not bend to feeling — Lit: law-not-tolerate-feeling. The principle that the law must be applied evenly, without softening for personal ties or sympathy. The level standard of the water radical made into a maxim.
分久必合，合久必分。	fēn jiǔ bì hé , hé jiǔ bì fēn .	What is long divided must unite; what is long united must divide. — The opening philosophy of the Romance of the Three Kingdoms, stating the cyclical rhythm of Chinese political history between unity and fragmentation. It is one of the most quoted sentences about Chinese history as a whole, used to frame any long swing between consolidation and breakup.
分秒必争	fēn miǎo bì zhēng	to fight for every minute and second: to lose not a moment — Lit: minute-second-must-contend. Said when time is so pressing that each unit counts, in a race, an emergency, a deadline. 争分夺秒 is the common variant.
焚书坑儒	fén shū kēng rú	burn the books and bury the Confucians — state censorship and intellectual repression — The four-character condemnation of the Qin cultural purges of 213–212 BCE. Still the standard Chinese phrase for any government suppression of thought or culture. Note that 坑 kēng (pit) also functions in modern colloquial Chinese as a verb meaning "to trap" or "to screw over someone" , e.g.
风调雨顺	fēng tiáo yǔ shùn	favorable weather: good conditions for a harvest — Lit: the wind is well-tuned and the rain comes in due course. A set wish for a year of mild, timely weather and a good harvest, often paired with 国泰民安 (the country at peace and the people content). Here 调 takes the tiáo reading, "to harmonize," the weather brought into balance.
风平浪静	fēng píng làng jìng	the wind is calm and the waves are still: calm seas, a tranquil situation — Lit: wind-level-waves-quiet. The sea at perfect rest, used both literally and for any situation that has settled into calm after trouble has passed.
风水轮流转	fēng shuǐ lún liú zhuǎn	feng shui rotates in turn — fortunes change; cycles of luck; the wheel keeps turning — The most common figurative use of 风水. Used to express acceptance of change, comfort in adversity, or (more pointedly) schadenfreude when an opponent's luck runs out. 三十年河东，三十年河西，风水轮流转 (thirty years east of the river, thirty years west , the wheel of fortune keeps turning).
风雨同舟	fēng yǔ tóng zhōu	to weather a storm in the same boat — Literally "wind and rain, the same boat", to stand together through hardship, sharing the danger. From the Sunzi Bingfa, which notes that even old enemies in one storm-tossed boat will help each other. The deepest form of being 在一起, bound by shared trial.
烽火连三月，家书抵万金	fēng huǒ lián sān yu è , jiā shū dǐ wàn jīn	beacon fires for three months; a letter from home worth ten thousand gold — Du Fu's couplet from 春望 (Spring View), written during the An Lushan Rebellion while imprisoned in rebel-held Chang'an (756 CE). Among the most quoted lines in Chinese literature: a complete world of wartime separation compressed into fourteen characters.
夫唱妇随	fū chàng fù suí	the husband sings and the wife follows: a harmonious married couple — Literally "the husband leads, the wife accompanies." A traditional image of marital harmony in which the couple act in accord, one taking the lead and the other in step.
福星高照	fú xīng gāo zhào	the star of fortune shines from above — to be under a lucky star — 福星 fúxīng = the star of fortune (one of the three auspicious stars 福禄寿). 高照 = shining from on high. A blessing used at New Year, weddings, and new ventures.
父慈子孝	fù cí zǐ xiào	the father kind, the son filial: the reciprocal duty between generations — A Confucian formula for the father-son relationship, paired in the classics with 兄友弟恭 (elder brother caring, younger respectful). Authority and obedience are framed as mutual obligations, not one-way command.
父债子还	fù zhài zǐ huán	the father's debt, the son repays: a debt or wrong passes down the line — Once a literal expectation in family law, now used figuratively for any burden inherited from a previous generation. Carries a tone of grim inevitability.
富国强兵	fù guó qiáng bīng	"enrich the country, strengthen the army" — national power as a policy goal — 富国 (enrich the state) + 强兵 (strengthen the army). The paired goals of realist statecraft, traceable to the Legalist ministers of the Warring States period (especially Shāng Yāng of Qin, died 338 BCE).
富甲一方	fù jiǎ yī fāng	wealthiest in the whole region: the richest man around — 甲 here means "to rank first." Said of a local magnate whose fortune tops everyone in the district. Admiring, slightly old-fashioned register.
富可敌国	fù kě dí guó	wealth that can rival a state: fabulously rich — 敌 means "to match." A private fortune so vast it equals a kingdom's treasury, applied to tycoons past and present.
富丽堂皇	fù lì táng huáng	rich, beautiful, grand and imposing: sumptuous and magnificent — Describes a palace, mansion, or banquet hall dazzling with luxury. Common in travel writing and architecture description today.
改朝换代	gǎi cháo huàn dài	to change dynasty and era: a change of regime — Lit: change-dynasty, swap-era. 朝 and 代 are the dynasty and its age. The phrase names the fall of one ruling house and the rise of another, and by extension any sweeping change of who holds power.
高低不就	gāo dī bù jiù	"neither high nor low will do" — too picky; unable to settle; rejecting both good and mediocre options — 高低 (high and low, all options across the spectrum) + 不就 (will not take, will not accept).
高风亮节	gāo fēng liàng jié	lofty bearing and shining integrity: noble character of the highest order — Lit: high-wind-bright-node. Here 节 is integrity, the firm node that does not bend. High praise for a person of unbending principle and selfless conduct.
个人主义	gèr én zhǔ yì	individualism — 个人 (the individual) + 主义 (-ism). The modern term for individualism, the doctrine that puts the single person ahead of the collective. Its standing contrast is 集体主义 jítǐ zhǔyì (collectivism).
功成名就	gōng chéng míng jiù	achievement complete and fame attained: to have made it — Lit: merit-accomplished-name-attained. The classical picture of a fulfilled career: the work is done and the reputation is secured. Often paired with the idea of retiring gracefully afterward (功成身退, to withdraw once the work is achieved).
功夫不负有心人	gōng fu bù fù yǒu xīn rén	effort does not fail the determined person — hard work pays off; persistence wins — One of the most commonly cited Chinese sayings about effort. 有心人 = a person with resolve and intention. The saying frames determination as the prerequisite: effort pays off for those who truly commit to it. 只要坚持，功夫不负有心人。
功亏一篑	gōng kuī yī kuì	to fail for the lack of one final basketful: to fall short at the very end — Lit: achievement-short-one-basket. From the image of building a mound and stopping one last basket of earth before completion. From the Book of Documents (尚书). The lesson: do not abandon a near-finished effort, or all the work before it is wasted.
古色古香	gǔ sè gǔ xiāng	of antique flavor: redolent of the past in look and feel — Literally "ancient color, ancient scent." Praises a building, room, or object that has a tasteful, antique character, steeped in the atmosphere of an older age. A favorite for describing a classical garden, a tea house, or an old street that has kept its period charm.
古往今来	gǔ wǎng jīn lái	from ancient times to the present — Literally "the ancient goes, the present comes," spanning all of history up to now. Used to introduce a truth that has held across the ages: 古往今来，人人都怕死 ("from ancient times to now, everyone fears death"). Here 今 is the classical single character for "the present, now."
古为今用	gǔ wéi jīn yòng	make the past serve the present — To draw on the heritage of antiquity for present-day purposes, using old wisdom, forms, or techniques in modern life. Often paired with 洋为中用 ("make the foreign serve China"), the two together forming a well-known formula for selectively adapting the old and the foreign to modern Chinese needs.
谷贱伤农	gǔ jiàn shāng nóng	cheap grain hurts the farmer: low crop prices ruin the people who grow them — A piece of classical economics still quoted in policy debates: when a bumper harvest crashes prices, the farmer who grew it earns less, not more.
骨肉相连	gǔ ròu xiāng lián	bone and flesh joined together: bound by blood kinship — The image is that bone and flesh cannot be pulled apart. Used for the inseparable bond between family members, and figuratively for a homeland and its people.
官逼民反	guān bī mín fǎn	corrupt officials drive the people to revolt — The phrase that captures the whole moral logic of Water Margin: rebellion springs not from the people's wickedness but from the oppression of corrupt officials who leave them no other choice.
国泰民安	guó tài mín ān	the country is prosperous and the people are at peace — The classical blessing for a nation: 泰 tài (great peace) + 民安 (the people are secure). Used in toasts, New Year wishes, and political rhetoric. The Confucian ideal of good governance.
海阔天空	hǎi kuò tiān kōng	the sea is wide and the sky is open: boundless; (of talk) freewheeling and unconstrained — Lit: sea-wide sky-empty. It paints limitless space, hence both a feeling of total freedom and a way to describe rambling, anything-goes conversation: 海阔天空地聊 "to chat about everything under the sun."
含辛茹苦	hán xīn rú kǔ	to hold pungency and swallow bitterness: to endure every hardship — 茹 (rú) means to eat. Almost always said of parents, especially mothers, who suffer silently for years to raise their children.
寒窗苦读	hán chuāng kǔ dú	cold window, bitter study — The archetypal image of examination preparation: the scholar sitting through winter nights by a cold window, studying by lamplight, without heat or comfort.
喝西北风	hē xī běi fēng	to drink the northwest wind: have nothing to eat, go hungry — A vivid colloquial idiom: with no food, all you can swallow is the cold wind. Said wryly of poverty or a failed livelihood, "我们都去喝西北风了".
合纵连横	hé zòng lián héng	vertical alliance and horizontal linkage — coalition against a dominant power, or accommodation with it — The two grand strategic options of the Warring States endgame. Used today in geopolitics, business competition, and organizational dynamics to describe the choice between forming a coalition against a dominant player and making separate deals with it.
和气生财	hé qì shēng cái	harmony brings wealth — a gentle manner attracts prosperity — 和气 héqi (harmony of manner; friendliness) + 生 shēng (to generate) + 财 cái (wealth). The principle that amicable dealings create the trust and goodwill that sustain business. Posted in shops across the Chinese-speaking world.
厚古薄今	hòu gǔ bó jīn	to favor the past over the present: to esteem antiquity and slight the modern — Literally "thick toward the ancient, thin toward the present." Criticizes the habit of prizing everything old while dismissing the contemporary. Its mirror is 厚今薄古, valuing the present over the past. A pointed term in debates over tradition and modernity.
化险为夷	huà xiǎn wéi yí	to turn danger into safety: to avert a crisis; to come through unscathed — 化 huà = to transform, 险 xiǎn = danger, peril, 为 wéi = into, 夷 yí = level ground, flatness, safety. Lit: "transform the perilous into the level." Describes turning a dangerous, threatening situation into a safe one, often through skill, calm, or good fortune. 在关键时刻，他冷静地化险为夷。
化整为零	huà zhěng wéi líng	"turn the whole into pieces" — to break something large into small scattered parts — 化 (to transform) + 整 (the whole) + 为 (into) + 零 (the scattered pieces). To disperse one large thing into many small ones: a big force split into small units that move unseen, a large task divided into manageable bits, a lump sum paid in installments.
画龙点睛	huà lóng diǎn jīng	drawing a dragon and dotting the eye — the small finishing touch that brings the whole alive — From a Tang anecdote: the painter Zhang Sengyou painted dragons but refused to add eyes; when pressed, he dotted the eyes of two , and they immediately flew off the wall. The dot is what brings the form to life. Used today for the small but decisive move that makes a piece of work click.
火眼金睛	huǒ yǎn jīn jīng	fiery golden eyes — seeing through deception — The Monkey King's eyes, hardened in the furnace of the Daoist god Laojun, that let him see through every demon's disguise to the true form beneath. The chengyu now means sharp, penetrating discernment, the ability to see through falsehood and recognise the real nature of a person or situation.
货真价实	huò zhēn jià shí	genuine goods at a fair price: the real thing, no fakery — Lit: goods-genuine-price-real. A merchant's pledge that the wares are authentic and honestly priced. By extension it certifies anything as the genuine article, not a counterfeit or an exaggeration.
急功近利	jí gōng jìn lì	eager for quick merit and near-term gain: shortsighted opportunism — Lit: anxious-merit-near-profit. A criticism of those who chase immediate results and quick profit at the expense of long-term, sustainable success. The opposite of the 功夫 ethic of patient, foundational effort.
价值连城	jià zhí lián chéng	worth a string of cities: of immense value — From the Warring States tale of the 和氏璧 jade, which King Zhao of Qin offered fifteen cities to obtain. To be worth 连城 is to command a value beyond ordinary reckoning.
间不容发	jiān bù róng fà	the gap admits not a hair: a situation of extreme danger or urgency — Lit: the-gap-does-not-allow-a-hair. The interval is so narrow not even a single hair could fit, so the margin for error is none at all. Here 间 takes the jiān reading for the gap itself.
简练揣摩	jiǎn liàn chuǎi mó	to study and refine through relentless, focused effort — From the Zhanguo Ce account of Su Qin reading until he was exhausted, jabbing his own thigh with an awl to stay awake. 练 carries the sense of working material over and over until it is honed.
简明扼要	jiǎn míng è yào	simple, clear, and gripping the essentials: succinct and pointed — 简 simple, 明 clear, 扼要 to grip the crux. Common in formal evaluation of summaries and briefings, marking a text that wastes no words.
见义勇为	jiàn yì yǒng wéi	see righteousness and act with courage — to step up when others need help — 见 jiàn (to see; to perceive) + 义 yì (righteous action; what is right) + 勇 yǒng (courage; bravery) + 为 wéi (to do; to act). From the Analects (论语): 见义不为，无勇也 , "To see what is right and not do it is a want of courage" (Confucius, 2.24).
江山社稷	jiāng shān shè jì	the rivers, mountains, and altars of soil and grain: the whole realm, the state — Literally "rivers and mountains, the altars of soil and grain." 江山 the land and 社稷 the altars together name the entire country a ruler holds in trust. A grand, classical phrase for the state as a whole, the territory and the legitimacy that come with it.
江山易改，本性难移	jiāng shān yì gǎi , běn xìng nán yí	easy to change the landscape, hard to change one's nature; a leopard can't change its spots — The full proverb. 易 yì (easy) + 改 gǎi (to change; to alter) / 难 nán (difficult) + 移 yí (to shift; to move; to transplant). The contrast is between the external world , rivers and mountains, the landscape of history , and the internal: 本性, innate character, the temperament you were born with.
江山易改本性难移	jiāng shān yì gǎi běn xìng nán yí	territory is easy to change; fundamental nature is hard to move — a leopard cannot change its spots — Classic proverb about human nature used in resignation or rueful acknowledgment. 他就是这样的人，江山易改本性难移。(He's just that kind of person; a leopard can't change its spots.) Often invoked by someone who has tried to change another person and given up.
脚踏实地	jiǎo tà shí dì	feet planted on solid ground: down-to-earth, steady and practical — Lit: feet-tread-solid-ground. High praise for someone who works steadily and realistically rather than chasing empty schemes. The 实 here is the solid earth underfoot.
接二连三	jiē èr lián sān	one after another: coming in quick succession — Literally "joining the second, linking the third," counting events as they pile up. Describes things happening in rapid, repeated succession, most often a run of incidents or problems: 接二连三地出事 (one mishap after another).
接连不断	jiē lián bù duàn	linked and unbroken: continuous, without interruption — 接连 doubles the sense of joining; 不断 says the chain never breaks. Describes rain, applause, or news that keeps coming.
借风使船	jiè fēng shǐ chuán	borrow the wind to sail the boat: turn outside conditions to your own ends — A close cousin of 顺水推船, stressing the borrowed force: you let someone else's momentum carry your own plan forward.
借古讽今	jiè gǔ fěng jīn	use the past to satirize the present — A classic mode of Chinese writing: cloak criticism of present rulers in a story about an ancient dynasty. Long the safest way to speak truth to power.
金榜题名	jīn bǎng tí míng	one's name inscribed on the golden list — exam success — The 金榜 was the yellow placard of palace-exam graduates posted outside the examination compound. Classical culture counted it among life's four great joys, alongside the wedding night, rain after drought, and meeting an old friend far from home. Today it captions every gaokao congratulations post.
金屋藏娇	jīn wū cáng jiāo	to keep a beauty in a golden house — to keep a mistress in luxury — From a Han dynasty story: Emperor Wu as a child said he would build a golden house for the girl Ah Jiao (阿娇) if she became his wife. Now means to keep a mistress in a secret dwelling. A literary idiom that appears in Tang poetry and classical fiction.
紧锣密鼓	jǐn luó mì gǔ	tight gongs and dense drums: intense, busy preparation — From the loud, crowded percussion that builds before an opera's climax. Modern usage describes a project being driven forward at full, urgent pace behind the scenes.
紧要关头	jǐn yào guān tóu	a crucial, critical juncture — The pivotal moment when everything hangs in the balance. Common in news and narrative for the turning point of a crisis or contest.
近朱者赤，近墨者黑	jìn zhū zhě chì , jìn mò zhě hēi	those near red become red; those near ink become black — environment shapes character — Two parallel 者 constructions: 近朱者 = the one(s) near vermilion / 近墨者 = the one(s) near ink. The argument is that proximity to good or bad influences changes a person's nature, just as a white object held near ink absorbs its color.
近朱者赤近墨者黑	jìn zhū zhě chì , jìn mò zhě hēi	near vermilion turns red, near ink turns black — you become like the company you keep — 近 jìn (close to) + 朱 zhū (vermilion) + 者 zhě (one who) + 赤 chì (red); 近 + 墨 mò (ink) + 者 + 黑 (black). Whoever stays near red dye turns red; whoever stays near ink turns black. A classic statement that environment and company shape character, attributed to the Jin-dynasty writer Fu Xuan 傅玄.
救苦救难	jiù kǔ jiù nàn	to relieve suffering and rescue from peril — A Buddhist phrase, the standing epithet of the bodhisattva 观音 (Guānyīn), 救苦救难 (one who relieves the suffering and saves from calamity). Now also figurative for any savior figure.
居安思危	jū ān sī wēi	dwelling in safety, think of danger: stay alert in good times — From the Zuǒ Zhuàn. The classic counsel of prudent statecraft: when secure, prepare for the threat that has not yet come. A staple of formal and editorial Chinese.
举世闻名	jǔ shì wén míng	world-famous: renowned throughout the whole world — Lit: the whole world has heard the name. 举世 means the entire world taken together, 闻名 means to be known by name. Said of a person, place, or work whose fame reaches everywhere, as in the Great Wall or a celebrated master.
举头三尺有神明	jǔ tóu sān chǐ yǒu shén míng	three feet above your head there are divine spirits — you are always watched — A foundational folk-religion moral warning: the gods are always observing human conduct; no action goes unseen. Functionally identical to the Western "God is watching." Used to caution against wrongdoing, especially in private. The full proverb: 举头三尺有神明，不畏人知畏己知。
开元盛世	Kāi yuán Shèng shì	the Glorious Era of Kaiyuan — the High Tang at its zenith — The reign-era name 开元 (713–741 CE) under Emperor Xuanzong gave its name to the period of Tang's greatest cultural and economic flourishing. Population reached approximately 50 million; the examination system produced its greatest talents; Chang'an was at its most cosmopolitan.
康乾盛世	Kāng Qián shèng shì	the flourishing era of Kangxi and Qianlong — the Qing golden age — The compound reign-name shorthand 康乾 (Kangxi-Qianlong) with 盛世 (flourishing era, golden age).
可望不可即	kě wàng bù kě jí	can be seen but not reached — within sight but out of grasp — 可望 (can be hoped for/seen) + 不可即 (cannot be approached). Used for goals, dreams, and people that seem close but remain permanently out of reach. Often carries a wistful rather than bitter tone.
空城计	kōng chéng jì	the Empty Fort Strategy — bluffing strength from weakness — Zhuge Liang, holding an undefended town against a vastly superior army, flings the gates open and sits calmly playing the zither on the wall. The enemy commander, certain so bold a display must conceal an ambush, retreats.
空前绝后	kōng qián ju é hòu	"empty before, cut off behind" — unprecedented and unrepeated; without equal in all of history — 空 kōng (empty; void) + 前 (before; the past) + 绝 jué (cut off; without) + 后 (after; the future). Nothing like it has existed in the past (the front is empty of precedent) and nothing like it will exist in the future (the back is cut off from repetition).
口是心非	kǒu shì xīn fēi	the mouth says yes but the heart says no — saying one thing while thinking another — Lit: mouth-yes-heart-no. The classic expression for hypocrisy or false agreement: when outward words and inner feelings are opposites. Used for people who agree to your face while disagreeing inside, or who say what they think you want to hear rather than what they actually believe.
苦尽甘来	kǔ jìn gān lái	bitterness ends, sweetness comes: hardship gives way to better days — 苦 (bitter) and 甘 (sweet) frame a full turn of fortune. The standard consolation offered to anyone enduring a long hard stretch; the reward is built into the saying.
苦口婆心	kǔ kǒu pó xīn	bitter mouth, grandmotherly heart: to urge earnestly and patiently — Describes someone who repeats hard advice tirelessly out of genuine care, the way an old woman nags her grandchild for their own good.
困兽犹斗	kùn shòu yóu dòu	a cornered beast still fights: desperate last-ditch resistance — From the Zuǒ Zhuàn. A trapped animal will fight on even with no hope, so the phrase warns that a cornered opponent is dangerous. Used of enemies making a final stand.
来历不明	lái lì bù míng	of unknown origin: provenance unclear, background not established — 来历 láilì = origin, background, where something or someone came from (历 here is the path-walked-through that produced the present state); 不明 bù míng = not clear. Said of a person whose background cannot be verified, or an object whose provenance is suspect. It carries a note of suspicion.
劳民伤财	láo mín shāng cái	to exhaust the people and drain the treasury — The chengyu that names exactly what brought the Sui down: undertaking projects so costly in labour and money that they ruin both the population and the state's finances.
老调重弹	lǎo diào chóng tán	to rehash old ideas: to bring out a tired theme again — Lit: to pluck the old tune once more. A dismissive phrase for repeating a stale argument or worn-out idea as if it were new. Here 调 takes the diào reading, "a tune, a melody."
乐不思蜀	lè bù sī shǔ	so happy as to forget Shu: too content to think of home — Lit: happy-not-miss-Shu. From the deposed ruler of the kingdom of Shu, who, kept in comfort by his conquerors, said he no longer missed his homeland. Now said of anyone so absorbed in present pleasure that they forget where they came from.
离经叛道	lí jīng pàn dào	to depart from the classics and rebel against the Way: heterodox, unorthodox — From the Confucian frame: to stray from the canonical texts (经) and turn against orthodox doctrine (道). Modern usage is broad, describing any rebel who breaks with convention, sometimes admiringly.
理直气壮	lǐ zhí qì zhuàng	bold with right on your side: speaking firmly because the principle is clearly yours — Lit: principle-straight-spirit-strong. When the 理 is on your side, your 气 (spirit, breath) is strong, so you can speak up with full confidence and no guilt.
历尽艰辛	lì jìn jiān xīn	to endure every hardship — to pass through countless trials to reach a goal — 历 (to pass through) + 尽 (exhaust, to the full) + 艰辛 (hardship). The phrase fits Xuanzang's journey exactly: deserts, mountains, thirst, and danger endured over sixteen years for the sake of the texts.
历久弥新	lì jiǔ mí xīn	grows better with time: the longer it lasts, the fresher and more valued it becomes — 历久 lìjiǔ = to pass through a long time, to endure; 弥 mí = all the more, increasingly; 新 xīn = new, fresh. Lit: "having lasted long, all the more new." Said of friendships, classic works, traditions, and well-made things that do not fade but gain in worth as the years accumulate. 这部经典作品历久弥新。
利害关系	lì hài guān xi	"interest-harm relationship" — a relationship involving mutual stakes and risks — Lit: benefit-harm-relationship. Describes a situation where parties are bound by shared interests and shared risks , where what benefits or harms one affects the others. 他们之间有复杂的利害关系，不能轻易分开。"There are complex stakes between them , they can't be easily separated."
连绵不断	lián mián bù duàn	unbroken and continuous — stretching on without interruption — 连绵 liánmián = continuous, uninterrupted (as mountains rolling one into the next, or rain falling without pause). 不断 = without cessation. Used for mountain ranges, rain, difficulty, sorrow, and any phenomenon that continues without obvious end.
连篇累牍	lián piān lěi dú	page after page, tablet after tablet — excessively wordy, verbose — 篇 = a piece of writing; 累牍 = pile of writing tablets (classical). Together: pieces of writing linked in succession and accumulated into piles. A pejorative idiom for excessive verbosity: writing that goes on and on without saying much.
怜香惜玉	lián xiāng xī yù	pity the fragrance, cherish the jade: to be tender toward women — 香 (fragrance) and 玉 (jade) both stand for a beautiful woman. To treat women with gentleness and care. Literary, sometimes ironic.
良药苦口	liáng yào kǔ kǒu	good medicine is bitter to the mouth: honest advice is hard to hear — From the Kǒngzǐ Jiāyǔ. Often completed by 忠言逆耳 (loyal words grate on the ear). Used to soften unwelcome but useful criticism.
流连忘返	liú lián wàng fǎn	so enthralled as to forget to leave: lingering, reluctant to go home — 流连 liúlián = to linger, to be unable to tear oneself away; 忘返 wàng fǎn = to forget to return.
六亲不认	liù qīn bù rèn	to disown all six kinships — utterly cold-blooded, recognizing no family bond — Lit: six-relatives-not-recognize. The classical six kinships are father, mother, elder brother, younger brother, wife, child. Said of someone who in the pursuit of power or self-interest will not even acknowledge their own kin.
龙飞凤舞	lóng fēi fèng wǔ	dragon flying, phoenix dancing — describes bold, unrestrained calligraphy — Lit: dragon-fly phoenix-dance. Originally a description of grand, sweeping calligraphy where the strokes move with the energy of mythic creatures. Now used both admiringly (fluid, powerful brushwork) and ironically (illegible scrawl that only the writer understands).
龙争虎斗	lóng zhēng hǔ dòu	dragon and tiger fighting — two powerful forces in fierce competition — 龙 lóng + 争 zhēng (to contend; to compete) + 虎 hǔ (tiger) + 斗 dòu (to fight; to contend). Used when two equally formidable opponents , companies, athletes, armies, political factions , clash with serious intensity. Neither side is diminished; both are worthy adversaries. 这场决赛简直是龙争虎斗，双方势均力敌。
率性而为	shuài xìng ér wéi	to act following ones nature: to do as one's disposition prompts — Literally "to follow nature and act." To behave according to one's true temperament rather than calculation or constraint. It can praise an honest, unaffected spirit or, in a cooler reading, describe someone who simply acts on impulse without weighing the consequences.
马到成功	mǎ dào chéng gōng	immediate success upon arrival — "when the cavalry arrives, victory follows" — Lit: horse-arrive-success. Originally a wish for military campaigns, deeply resonant with the Mongol cavalry tradition that shook the known world. Used today as a congratulatory phrase for new endeavors. 祝你马到成功！ "Wishing you swift success!"
埋头苦干	mái tóu kǔ gàn	to bury the head and toil hard: to work diligently without distraction — Lit: bury-head-bitter-work. To lower one's head and labor away, absorbed in the task and indifferent to praise or notice. The picture is of someone bent over their work, head down.
满面春风	mǎn miàn chūn fēng	a face full of spring breeze: beaming, radiant with joy — Lit: full-face-spring-wind. The whole face glowing like a warm spring day, said of someone visibly pleased, content, or flush with good fortune.
慢工出细活	màn gōng chū xì huó	slow work produces fine goods — Lit: slow-work-produce-fine-goods. The Chinese counterpart to "haste makes waste." A proverb rather than a classical four-character 成语 in the strict sense, but widely used and culturally important. Invoked to defend careful, unhurried craftsmanship against pressure for speed.
慢条斯理	màn tiáo sī lǐ	slow and methodical; unhurried and in proper order — Lit: slow-stitch-this-manage. Describes someone who does everything at an unhurried, measured pace and in proper sequence. Can be neutral (a composed, methodical person) or slightly pejorative (someone frustratingly slow to act).
孟姜女哭长城	Mèng Jiāng nü kū Cháng chéng	"Meng Jiangnu weeping at the Great Wall" — grief strong enough to move stone — One of China's four great folk legends (四大民间故事 sì dà mínjiān gùshì). Meng Jiangnu's husband Fan Xiliang was conscripted to build the Qin wall and died of exhaustion.
民不聊生	mín bù liáo shēng	the people can scarcely survive: a populace plunged into misery — Literally "the people have no means to make a living." A grim phrase for times of misrule, war, or disaster when ordinary people cannot even keep themselves alive. 聊 liáo here is the classical sense "to rely on, to depend on for," so 不聊生 means having nothing to depend on for life.
民以食为天	mín yǐ shí wéi tiān	to the people, food is heaven: nothing matters more than feeding the people — Literally "the people take food as heaven." An ancient maxim of statecraft: the first and highest concern of any ruler is that the people have enough to eat. Still quoted constantly in everyday life, often half-jokingly, to defend the seriousness of a good meal.
名不虚传	míng bù xū chuán	the reputation is not falsely spread: to live up to one's name; to be as good as people say — Lit: the name is not emptily passed around. Said of a person or thing whose fame turns out to be well deserved, the praise that circulated was no exaggeration. Here 传 keeps the chuán reading, the spreading of a reputation.
南腔北调	nán qiāng běi diào	a jumble of accents: speech mixing many regional tongues — Lit: southern resonance and northern tones. Said of speech that mixes accents from all over, or of a gathering where many dialects are heard at once. Here 调 takes the diào reading, "accent, manner of speech."
难分难解	nán fēn nán jiě	hard to divide and hard to untangle: locked together, evenly matched — Lit: hard-to-separate-hard-to-loosen. Of a contest too close to call, or of people so entwined, whether fighting or in love, that they cannot be pulled apart.
难解难分	nán jiě nán fēn	hard to untie, hard to part: locked together — Lit: hard 难 to untie 解, hard 难 to separate 分. Two sides so entangled they cannot be pulled apart: a deadlocked contest, a fierce struggle, or two people inseparably close. 双方打得难解难分 = the two sides fought to a standstill.
内忧外困	nèi yōu wài kùn	troubles within, pressures without: beset on every side — A state or organization squeezed simultaneously by internal worry and external pressure. Common in political and business commentary; a near twin of 内忧外患.
年富力强	nián fù lì qiáng	rich in years and strong in force — in the prime of life — Lit: year-rich-strength-strong. Used to describe someone in their prime: still young enough to have full physical and mental energy, experienced enough to have skill and judgment. The ideal working age. 他正值年富力强之时 "He is at the prime of his powers." A compliment to someone in their 30s–50s.
念念不忘	niàn niàn bù wàng	to keep thinking of without ever forgetting — to hold something constantly in mind — Lit: thought-after-thought, never forgetting. 念念 doubles the character to mean "moment after moment of mind." Used for an attachment, a wish, or a person one cannot let go of: 他对故乡念念不忘 (he never stops thinking of his hometown).
念念有词	niàn niàn yǒu cí	to mutter words under one breath: to chant or murmur incantations — Lit: muttering-and-having-words. Originally of a priest or sorcerer murmuring a spell, now of anyone muttering steadily to themselves, whether praying, reciting, or grumbling.
念兹在兹	niàn zī zài zī	to think of it and dwell on it — to keep a matter constantly before the mind — A classical phrase from the Book of Documents (书经): 兹 zī means "this." To think of this and be present to this , to give a duty or concern unbroken attention. More literary than the others, used in formal or written contexts to describe devotion to a cause or responsibility one never sets aside.
鸟语花香	niǎo yǔ huā xiāng	birds singing, flowers fragrant: a beautiful spring scene — A stock phrase for an idyllic countryside in spring, the birds talking and the blossoms in scent. Warm and common in description.
庖丁解牛	Pāo dīng jiě niú	Cook Ding carves the ox; acting in perfect accord with natural structure — 庖丁 Pāodīng (Cook Ding , 庖 páo = kitchen, cook; 丁 Dīng = a name/title) + 解 jiě (to separate; to cut apart; to resolve; to understand , the character shows a horn + knife + cow) + 牛 niú (ox; cattle). Zhuangzi's most celebrated parable, from the third chapter (养生主 , The Secret of Caring for Life).
跑龙套	pǎo lóng tào	"to run the dragon costume" — to play a minor role; to be a bit-part player — From traditional Chinese opera. 龙套 lóngtào is the costume worn by the chorus of attendants (soldiers, servants, courtiers) who fill out a scene without lines. To 跑龙套 is to be one of these background figures: present, busy, but never central.
平心静气	píng xīn jìng qì	a level heart and quiet breath: calm and composed — Lit: level-heart-quiet-breath. To approach something with a settled mind and even temper, neither agitated nor hasty. 平心静气地谈 "to talk it over calmly."
平易近人	píng yì jìn rén	"level and easy, close to people" — approachable; unpretentious; accessible — 平易 (flat and easy , without obstacles or pretension) + 近 (close to; approachable) + 人 (people). A person who is 平易近人 does not stand on ceremony, does not use their position as a barrier, and can be approached by ordinary people without formality or anxiety.
七十二变	qī shí èr biàn	seventy-two transformations — endless versatility — Sun Wukong's power to transform himself into seventy-two different forms, from a fly to a temple. Used as a chengyu for boundless adaptability and resourcefulness, the capacity to change shape to fit any situation, and applied admiringly to anyone strikingly versatile or quick to adapt.
气急败坏	qì jí bài huài	breath urgent, composure ruined: flustered and furious, beside oneself with rage — Pictures someone so panicked or enraged that their composure has fallen apart. 败坏 doubles "defeated" and "ruined." Common in narration for a person who has lost their cool and is lashing out.
气吞山河	qì tūn shān hé	spirit that could swallow mountains and rivers — magnificent, all-encompassing force — Lit: qi-swallow-mountain-river. A classical idiom for heroic grandeur: the qi of a great army, a historical figure, or a piece of calligraphy or poetry so powerful it seems to contain all of nature within it. Often used in literary and historical commentary.
气壮山河	qì zhuàng shān hé	magnificent spirit that fills the mountains and rivers — Lit: qi-strong-mountain-river. Similar to 气吞山河 but with a sense of steadiness rather than swallowing, the qi standing as firm and vast as mountains and rivers. Used for patriotic spirit, heroic resolve, or great art. A phrase from Wen Tianxiang's 正气歌 tradition.
千变万化	qiān biàn wàn huà	a thousand changes, ten thousand transformations: endlessly changing; protean — 千 qiān = thousand, 万 wàn = ten thousand, the two largest round numbers paired for the sense of "countless." Lit: "a thousand changes and ten thousand transformations." Describes anything in ceaseless, dazzling flux: clouds, markets, a magician's tricks, the moves of a skilled fighter. 舞台上的灯光千变万化。
千夫所指	qiān fū suǒ zhǐ	pointed at by a thousand men: condemned by everyone — Literally "what a thousand men point their fingers at." It describes a person reviled by the whole public, the target of universal blame and reproach. To be 千夫所指 is to stand utterly condemned in the eyes of the crowd, a heavy figure for public disgrace.
千古绝唱	qiān gǔ ju é chàng	a peerless masterpiece: a work unmatched through the ages — Literally "a song unmatched in a thousand ages." Here 千古 means all the ages, and 绝唱 the supreme, unrepeatable performance. Reserved for a poem, song, or work of art so fine that nothing across the centuries can equal it.
千辛万苦	qiān xīn wàn kǔ	"a thousand pains, ten thousand toils" — untold hardships; every kind of suffering — 千辛 (a thousand bitternesses) and 万苦 (ten thousand toils): hardship piled on hardship beyond counting. Used to describe the immense effort and suffering endured to reach a goal: 历尽千辛万苦 (to have gone through every kind of hardship).
钱可通神	qián kě tōng shén	money can reach the gods — money can accomplish anything — A cynical proverb about the power of money to overcome any obstacle. 通神 = to reach the divine, suggesting even the sacred can be bought. The Tang-dynasty origin makes this one of the oldest commentaries on corruption in Chinese literature.
潜移默化	qián yí mò huà	to shift unseen and transform in silence: to influence imperceptibly over time — 潜 qián = hidden, submerged, 移 yí = to shift, 默 mò = silent, 化 huà = to transform. Lit: "shifting while submerged, transforming in silence." Describes the slow, unnoticed way an environment, a teacher, or a habit gradually changes a person's character or thinking without any single dramatic moment.
锲而不舍，金石可镂	qiè ér bù shě , jīn shí kě lòu	Persevere without giving up, and you can engrave even metal and stone — From 劝学. The full passage contrasts: if you chisel but give up, you cannot even cut rotten wood; if you persevere without stopping, you can engrave metal and stone.
倾国倾城	qīng guó qīng chéng	to topple states and cities: a woman of overwhelming beauty — 倾 qīng = to overturn, to topple; 国 guó = state; 城 chéng = city. The image is hyperbolic: a beauty so dazzling that rulers neglect their duties and whole cities and states fall because of her.
穷兵黩武	qióng bīng dú wǔ	to exhaust the troops in endless war: reckless militarism — Literally "drain the soldiers, abuse the martial." A sharp condemnation of a ruler who wages war without end, squandering lives and resources in aggression for its own sake. 黩 dú means to defile or abuse, so 黩武 is the abuse of military force.
全神贯注	quán shén guàn zhù	fully absorbed: to concentrate every bit of attention on one thing — Lit: whole-spirit-pour-into. With one's entire mind poured into a single focus, oblivious to all else. The phrase for deep, undivided concentration.
热火朝天	rè huǒ cháo tiān	heat and fire toward the sky: a buzzing, full-tilt scene — The image is a fire roaring up at the heavens. Used for a worksite, campaign, or rehearsal humming with energy. Common in news and everyday speech.
人困马乏	rén kùn mǎ fá	men worn out and horses spent: an army exhausted — The classic picture of a column at the end of a long march, fighters and mounts alike drained. Frequent in historical novels such as the Sānguó Yǎnyì.
人神相通	rén shén xiāng tōng	humans and spirits communing — the fundamental premise of shamanism — Lit: human-spirit-mutually-communicate. The cosmological axiom that makes shamanism possible: the boundary between the human world and the spirit world is permeable, and certain human specialists can cross it.
人往高处走，水往低处流	rén wǎng gāo chù zǒu , shuǐ wǎng dī chù liú	"people move toward high places; water flows to low places" — the natural human drive for self-improvement; ambition as gravity — A folk proverb rather than a classical four-character chengyu, but one of the most widely cited sayings about ambition and social mobility in Mandarin. Water follows physical gravity downward; people follow social gravity upward.
认贼作父	rèn zéi zuò fù	to take a thief for a father: to serve one's enemy as a benefactor — A harsh condemnation, aimed at a traitor or collaborator who submits to the very people who wronged him. The image inverts the sacred father-son bond into betrayal.
认祖归宗	rèn zǔ guī zōng	to acknowledge one's ancestors and return to the lineage — Lit: recognize-ancestor-return-lineage. Used for those who were separated from their family (by adoption, diaspora, or historical disruption) and formally reconnect with their ancestral line. A profoundly significant act in Chinese social life.
任重道远	rèn zhòng dào yuǎn	"the burden is heavy, the road is far" — great responsibility with a long journey ahead — From the Analects of Confucius (论语): 仁以为己任，不亦重乎？死而后已，不亦远乎？(Is taking benevolence as one's charge not heavy? Does the journey end only at death , is that not far?) Used to describe serious long-term undertakings: social reform, academic work, national development. Conveys gravitas without complaint.
荣华富贵	róng huá fù guì	glory and splendor, wealth and rank: a life of luxury and high station — Four near-synonyms stacked for weight. Often used wistfully or critically, the worldly prize that classical heroes are urged to see through.
入土为安	rù tǔ wéi ān	entering the earth brings peace — may they rest in peace — Lit: enter-earth-become-peaceful. The traditional Chinese expression for the peace of burial: entering the earth is how the deceased find rest. Used in discussions of funerary customs and in consoling the bereaved.
三民主义	Sān mín Zhǔ yì	the Three Principles of the People — nationalism, democracy, livelihood — Sun Yat-sen's political program, still the official ideology of the Republic of China (Taiwan). The phrase is so embedded in Chinese political vocabulary that it functions almost as a set term.
删繁就简	shān fán jiù jiǎn	cut the complex and move toward the simple: prune the excess — 删 to delete, 繁 the complicated, 就 to go toward, 简 the simple. An editor's motto, applied to prose, procedures, and design alike.
伤天害理	shāng tiān hài lǐ	to wound heaven and harm reason: utterly immoral — An act so cruel it offends both heaven and natural law. Strong condemnation of conscienceless deeds.
深思熟虑	shēn sī shú lǜ	deep thought, mature deliberation — to think long and carefully before acting — 深 shēn (deep) + 思 sī (think) + 熟 shú (mature; thoroughly cooked) + 虑 lǜ (to deliberate; to consider). A four-character description of the deliberative ideal: thought that has gone deep and has been given time to mature, like food that has cooked through completely.
生龙活虎	shēng lóng huó hǔ	living dragon, active tiger — bursting with energy and vitality — 生 shēng (alive; living) + 龙 lóng + 活 huó (active; lively) + 虎 hǔ (tiger). Used to describe a person , especially a child, an athlete, or anyone at the peak of their physical vitality , who radiates robust, irrepressible energy. 这些孩子个个生龙活虎，根本停不下来。
士农工商	shì nóng gōng shāng	scholar, farmer, artisan, merchant: the four classes of old society — The traditional ranking of occupations, with the farmer placed second only to the scholar and above the merchant, a clue to how high agriculture stood in the Confucian moral order.
世界第八大奇迹	shì jiè dì bā dà qí jì	the eighth wonder of the world — The popular epithet attached to the terracotta army since foreign dignitaries applied it after the discovery.
世世代代	shì shì dài dài	generation after generation: for all the generations to come — A doubling of 世 (age) and 代 (generation), each repeated for emphasis. Describes something passed down or continuing without break across every generation, as in a craft, a duty, or a family living on the same land.
世外桃源	shì wài táo yuán	a haven of peace: an idyllic refuge cut off from the troubled world — Lit: a peach-blossom spring beyond the world. From Tao Yuanming's fable of a fisherman who stumbles on a hidden valley untouched by war and time. Now any tranquil place sheltered from the strife and bustle of ordinary life.
事半功倍	shì bàn gōng bèi	half the work, double the result: maximum output from minimum effort — Lit: work-half-achievement-double. The ideal of efficiency: accomplishing more by working smarter. From Mencius (孟子). Its mirror image is 事倍功半 shì bèi gōng bàn (double the work, half the result), wasted, inefficient effort.
事倍功半	shì bèi gōng bàn	double the effort, half the result — A related but distinct phrase: where 事与愿违 says the outcome diverged from the wish without specifying the ratio, 事倍功半 specifically describes disproportionate effort yielding inadequate results.
是古非今	shì gǔ fēi jīn	to praise the ancient and disparage the present — Lit: right-ancient-wrong-present. The bias of treating old things as inherently superior to contemporary ones. Appears in debates about tradition versus modernity, canonical texts versus living practice. Qin Shihuang's advisors warned him about scholars guilty of this very tendency.
手忙脚乱	shǒu máng jiǎo luàn	hands busy, feet disordered — flustered and disorganized — Lit: hands-busy-feet-chaotic. The image of someone scrambling in a panic, hands and feet moving in disordered haste. Used to describe being overwhelmed and disorganized: the opposite of calm competence. 他一到过年就手忙脚乱 "Around New Year, he's always running around in a flustered state."
守望相助	shǒu wàng xiāng zhù	to watch out for and help one another — From the Mencius: neighbors keeping watch and lending aid to each other, the ideal of a community that protects its own. 守望 (to keep watch) + 相助 (to help one another). Invoked for solidarity in hard times.
首屈一指	shǒu qū yī zhǐ	the first finger you bend to count: second to none, the very best — Lit: first-bend-one-finger. When you count off the best on your fingers, this is the one you bend down first. Used to rank a person or thing as the foremost in its field. 他在这个行业首屈一指 "He is second to none in this industry."
书同文，车同轨	shū tóng wén , chē tóng guǐ	unified script, standardized axle widths — the rhetorical formula of systemic national unity — Still quoted in policy arguments about national standards: language standardization, infrastructure compatibility, technical regulation. The pairing condenses the Qin unification logic: physical connectivity (roads) plus cultural connectivity (writing) equals empire.
书香门第	shū xiāng mén dì	a family with a fragrance of books — a family of scholars and learning — Lit: book-fragrance-door-lineage. A family where books are the tradition and education runs through the generations. High cultural prestige in Chinese society. 书香 shūxiāng "the fragrance of books" is itself a vivid compound.
熟读唐诗三百首	shú dú Táng shī sān bǎi shǒu	read three hundred Tang poems until you know them by heart — and you'll be able to compose poetry yourself — From the preface to the famous Qing-dynasty anthology Three Hundred Tang Poems: 熟读唐诗三百首，不会作诗也会吟. The locus classicus for the Chinese theory of learning by saturated recitation. The full saying ends "even if you can't compose, you'll be able to chant" , competence emerges from soaking in the corpus.
水火不容	shuǐ huǒ bù róng	water and fire cannot coexist — irreconcilably opposed — Water extinguishes fire; fire evaporates water. The two simply cannot occupy the same space. Used for people, factions, or ideas in absolute opposition. 他们两人水火不容 "Those two are completely incompatible."
顺风顺水	shùn fēng shùn shuǐ	with the wind, with the water — smooth sailing; everything going well without obstruction — A wish and a description: wind at your back, current in your favor , nothing impeding progress. 祝你顺风顺水！(Wishing you smooth sailing!) is a common departure blessing. The 风水 imagery is natural: when wind and water cooperate, travel is easy; when they oppose, nothing moves.
太平盛世	tài píng shèng shì	an age of great peace and prosperity — the ideal the Taiping name invoked, and failed to deliver — 太平 (great peace) + 盛世 (flourishing age, era of prosperity). The four-character compound describes the classical Chinese political ideal: an era in which the realm is at peace, governance is just, harvests are abundant, and the common people live without fear.
谈笑风生	tán xiào fēng shēng	to chat and laugh with verve: lively, easy conversation — Lit: talk-laugh wind-rises, as if a fresh breeze sprang up from the talk. Describes a person who holds a room with relaxed, witty conversation. A warm compliment to a host or a speaker.
桃园结义	táo yuán jié yì	the Oath of the Peach Garden — sworn brotherhood — The scene in which Liu Bei, Guan Yu, and Zhang Fei swear an oath of brotherhood in a peach orchard, vowing to live and die for one another and their shared cause.
讨价还价	tǎo jià huán jià	ask a price, return a price: to haggle, bargain — Acts out the back-and-forth of the marketplace, one side naming a figure and the other countering. Extended to any drawn-out negotiation over terms.
体大思精	tǐ dà sī jīng	grand in structure and fine in thought: a work both vast and meticulous — Lit: body-large-thought-fine. Praise for a major work, a book or system, whose overall framework is grand while its detailed reasoning is precise. Here 体 is the structure or framework of the whole.
天经地义	tiān jīng dì yì	self-evident truth; axiomatic — too obvious to argue about — Lit: heaven's warp and earth's weft. 经 jīng = warp threads (the vertical structural threads in a loom); 义 yì = weft and righteous pattern. Together they constitute the fabric of moral reality , what is simply given. "孩子要孝顺父母，这是天经地义的事。
天下大势分久必合	tiān xià dà shì , fēn jiǔ bì hé	the great tendency of all-under-heaven: long divided, must unite; long united, must divide — The opening principle of the Romance of the Three Kingdoms (三国演义). A cyclical theory of political history: unity and division alternate as inevitably as the seasons. Endlessly cited in Chinese political discourse to frame any current period as part of a longer historical rhythm.
天下兴亡，匹夫有责	tiān xià xīng wáng , pǐ fū yǒu zé	the rise and fall of the realm is the responsibility of every common person — Attributed to the late Ming / early Qing Confucian scholar 顾炎武 Gù Yánwǔ (1613–1682), though the current form was popularized by Liang Qichao in the late Qing. A founding text of Chinese civic patriotism.
同甘共苦	tóng gān gòng kǔ	to share joys and hardships — Literally "together the sweet, shared the bitter", to go through good times and bad side by side. It captures the deepest sense of 跟 as "with", a togetherness that holds through difficulty. Here 同 ("together") plays the accompanying role 跟 carries in speech.
痛心疾首	tòng xīn jí shǒu	pain in the heart and ache in the head: bitterly grieved and resentful — Lit: pain-heart-ache-head. To feel grief and indignation so deep that heart and head both ache. Used for bitter regret or anger over a wrong or a loss. 对这种浪费，他感到痛心疾首 "He was bitterly grieved at such waste."
头头是道	tóu tóu shì dào	clear and logical at every point: well-reasoned and articulate — Lit: every-head-is-the-way. Said of speech or analysis that is orderly and convincing from start to finish, with each point landing soundly. 说得头头是道 "to argue every point convincingly."
头重脚轻	tóu zhòng jiǎo qīng	heavy on top and light on the feet: top-heavy and unstable — Lit: head-heavy-feet-light. Used of anything badly balanced, with too much weight or emphasis at the top: a wobbling structure, a lopsided plan, or a dizzy, unsteady feeling.
万古长青	wàn gǔ cháng qīng	evergreen through ten thousand ages — enduring forever — 万古 (ten-thousand ages, all of history) + 长青 (ever-green, perennially fresh). Used for ideas, institutions, or relationships that are wished to last beyond ordinary time.
万里长城	wàn lǐ Cháng chéng	the ten-thousand-li Great Wall — symbol of colossal collective effort — 万里 wànlǐ (ten thousand li) is figurative hyperbole. The wall's actual total length across all dynasties is approximately 21,196 km. Used metaphorically for any enormous undertaking requiring sustained collective effort.
万事俱备，只欠东风	wàn shì jù bèi , zhǐ qiàn dōng fēng	all ten thousand things are ready; only the east wind is lacking — From the Romance of the Three Kingdoms (三国演义). Before the Battle of Red Cliffs (208 CE), Zhuge Liang tells Zhou Yu that the plan is complete in every respect , except that the wind is blowing in the wrong direction to set the enemy fleet alight.
万事俱备，只欠东风。	wàn shì jù bèi , zhǐ qiàn dōng fēng .	Everything is ready; we lack only the east wind. — From the eve of Red Cliffs, when the allies' fire-attack plan was fully prepared but needed a wind from the east to carry the flames into Cao Cao's fleet. The line is now a common chengyu for a situation in which all preparations are complete and only one final, decisive condition is missing.
往生净土	wǎng shēng jìng tǔ	rebirth in the Pure Land — the aspiration fulfilled at death — The central Pure Land aspiration: to depart this world and arrive in Amitābha's Pure Land at death. In eulogies and memorial contexts: "She has been reborn in the Pure Land" 她已往生净土 is the standard Buddhist formulation for death.
望尘莫及	wàng chén mò jí	to see only the dust and never catch up: to be hopelessly outdistanced — Literally "to gaze at the dust and be unable to reach." One can see only the dust kicked up by someone far ahead, with no hope of catching them. Used to admit that another is so far superior or so far in front that one cannot hope to match them.
望梅止渴	wàng méi zhǐ kě	to quench thirst by gazing at plums — From a tale of Cao Cao, who told thirsty troops that plum groves lay ahead, so the thought of sour plums made their mouths water and eased their thirst. It means consoling oneself with an empty hope or imagined prospect. The 望 of gazing-toward is the same root that gives 希望 its reach.
望女成凤	wàng nǚ chéng fèng	hoping your daughter will become a phoenix — parental ambition for daughters — The female counterpart to 望子成龙 wàng zǐ chéng lóng (hoping your son will become a dragon). 凤 fèng (phoenix) is the female auspicious creature paired with 龙 (dragon). The phoenix (凤凰 fènghuáng) represents feminine grace, beauty, and virtue.
望眼欲穿	wàng yǎn yù chuān	gazing till the eyes would bore through: aching with anticipation — Eyes straining at the road so hard they might pierce the distance. Describes longing for someone's return or news that will not come.
望子成龙	wàng zǐ chéng lóng	to hope ones son becomes a dragon: to long for ones child to succeed greatly — Literally "to hope the son becomes a dragon," the dragon being the emblem of supreme success and distinction. The classic expression of parental ambition: the deep hope that ones child will rise high in life. A close partner phrase, 望女成凤, hopes the daughter becomes a phoenix.
危在旦夕	wēi zài dàn xī	danger lies in dawn-and-dusk: peril is imminent, near death — Sānguó Zhì era usage. 旦夕 is the span of a single day; the danger is so close it could fall within hours. Used for a dying patient or a regime about to collapse.
为民除害	wèi mín chú hài	to rid the people of a scourge — To eliminate a menace for the public good, as a hero kills a man-eating beast. Praising tone, used for someone who removes a real threat.
为学日益，为道日损。	wéi xu é rì yì , wéi dào rì sǔn .	In pursuit of learning, every day something is gained. In pursuit of the Dao, every day something is dropped. — Chapter 48. The contrast with Confucius's emphasis on continuous learning and accumulation of knowledge is probably deliberate. The Daodejing does not reject learning but insists that the Dao requires a different movement: subtraction rather than addition, unlearning rather than accumulation.
文武双全	wén wǔ shuāng quán	accomplished in both letters and arms: master of both pen and sword — Literally "civil and martial, both complete." The classic praise for a complete person who excels at both scholarship and fighting, the brush and the blade. The ideal whole human being in the traditional view, equally at home in the study and on the field.
文以载道	wén yǐ zài dào	writing carries the Way — literature serves moral truth — The classical Chinese theory of literature: writing is not merely aesthetic entertainment but a vehicle for moral truth (道 Dào). Proposed by Han Yu (韩愈) in the Tang Dynasty and central to the Chinese literary tradition.
屋漏偏逢连夜雨	wū lòu piān féng lián yè yǔ	the roof leaks, and then it rains all night — misfortunes come in clusters — The Chinese equivalent of "when it rains, it pours." A leaking roof is already misfortune; continuous rain compounds it. Often quoted as half-proverb with the paired line: 船迟又遇打头风 (the boat is delayed and then hits a headwind). Very natural in everyday speech.
武松打虎	wǔ sōng dǎ hǔ	Wu Song fights the tiger — fearless strength against great odds — The most famous single episode in the novel: the hero Wu Song, drunk and alone on Jingyang Ridge, kills a man-eating tiger with his bare hands.
武艺高强	wǔ yì gāo qiáng	highly skilled in martial arts: a formidable fighter — Literally "martial skill high and strong." Describes a person of outstanding fighting ability, a master whose technique is far above the ordinary. 武艺 is the craft of arms, the practised techniques of combat; 高强 marks it as superior.
物极必反	wù jí bì fǎn	when things reach their extreme, they necessarily reverse — the law of cyclical transformation — 物 wù = things; 极 jí = extreme, apex; 必 bì = necessarily; 反 fǎn = to reverse. This is the fundamental law of Yin-Yang cosmology, applicable to all phenomena: the hottest summer carries the seed of winter; the most powerful dynasty carries the seed of its decline; a pendulum at its extreme cannot stay…
物美价廉	wù měi jià lián	the goods fine and the price cheap: excellent value for money — The four-character verdict every shopper hopes to pronounce: good quality and a low price at once. Standard praise in advertising and casual recommendation alike.
喜出望外	xǐ chū wàng wài	joy beyond what one hoped — overjoyed by surprise — 喜 (delight) arriving from outside (外) the range of expectation (望). The precise word for good news you did not dare hope for: the scholarship, the recovered wallet, the friend at the airport gate. Stronger and more specific than 很高兴.
喜气洋洋	xǐ qì yáng yáng	brimming with joy: radiant with a festive, happy spirit — Lit: festive air spilling over. 洋洋 paints joy overflowing in every direction. Said of a face, a household, or a whole town lit up with celebration, a stock phrase at weddings and the New Year.
喜形于色	xǐ xíng yú sè	joy takes shape in the face: visibly delighted — Lit: joy 喜 takes form 形 upon 于 the complexion 色. Happiness that cannot be hidden and shows plainly on the face. Here 形 is verbal, "to take shape, to appear," a clear window onto the verb sense of the character.
香火	xiāng huo	incense smoke; the vitality of a temple's active veneration — Literally "incense fire" or "incense smoke," but 香火 carries meanings beyond the physical act of burning. A temple with 香火鼎盛 xiānghuo dǐngshèng (incense burning vigorously) is a temple with a healthy, active congregation, good reputation, and real spiritual efficacy.
小孩子气	xiǎo hái zi qì	childish, immature in manner — Describes adult behavior that is petty or babyish. The 气 (qì) suffix turns a noun into a temperament. Everyday spoken register: "他这样太小孩子气了。"
心快口直	xīn kuài kǒu zhí	quick heart, straight mouth — frank and direct by nature — Lit: heart-fast-mouth-straight. A description of someone whose inner state and outer speech are perfectly aligned: no strategic filtering, no diplomatic softening. Used as a compliment among people who value directness, and occasionally as an excuse after saying something pointed.
心旷神怡	xīn kuàng shén yí	heart vast, spirit at ease — expansive peace and contentment in beautiful surroundings — 旷 kuàng = open; vast (like an open plain). 怡 yí = pleased; comfortable. The specific feeling of contentment and openness experienced in a beautiful landscape, after a burden lifts, or at a moment of rare clarity. Common at scenic spots: 站在山顶，令人心旷神怡。
心领神会	xīn lǐng shén huì	"heart receives, spirit understands" — to understand without words; to grasp intuitively — Lit: heart-receive-spirit-comprehend. The ideal of Chinese social understanding: no explicit statement is needed. You see, you understand, you nod. This is 意思 operating at its highest level , the meaning is fully communicated without ever being spoken. 他点了点头，心领神会。
心神不定	xīn shén bù dìng	heart and mind unsettled: distracted and uneasy — Lit: heart-spirit-not-settled. Said of a restless, distracted state in which the mind cannot come to rest, often from worry or anticipation. The opposite of 镇定.
心心念念	xīn xīn niàn niàn	with one's whole heart and every thought — to dwell on something constantly — A doubled, rhythmic phrase stacking 心 (heart) and 念 (thought): every beat of the heart, every flicker of mind, bent on one object. 她心心念念想回家 (her every thought is set on going home).
新陈代谢	xīn chén dài xiè	the new replaces the old: metabolism; constant renewal — Lit: new and old, replacing and discharging. The scientific term for metabolism, the body taking in the new and expelling the spent. Figuratively, the natural cycle by which fresh things supersede worn-out ones in any system.
信口开河	xìn kǒu kāi hé	to open the mouth like a river — to speak carelessly; to say whatever comes to mind without regard for truth — 信 xìn here shifts sense: 信 kǒu = to trust one's mouth, to follow wherever the mouth goes (the archaic sense of 信 as "to follow freely"). 开河 kāi hé = to open a river, to let it flow unobstructed.
形单影只	xíng dān yǐng zhī	a lone form, a single shadow: solitary and without companions — Lit: the form is alone, the shadow is single. The mirror image of 形影不离: here there is only one form and its one shadow, no one alongside. A literary, somewhat melancholy phrase for a person who is utterly on their own.
形形色色	xíng xíng sè sè	all shapes and colors: of every kind, all sorts — Lit: shape-shape, color-color. The reduplication piles up variety: people, goods, schemes, opinions of every imaginable kind. 形形色色的人 = all sorts of people. Often carries a faint edge of "a mixed bag," not all of it admirable.
形影不离	xíng yǐng bù lí	form and shadow never part: inseparable — Lit: form and shadow do not separate. A shadow follows its form everywhere; two people who are 形影不离 are constant companions, always together. Warm and affectionate, used for close friends, devoted couples, or a child who will not leave a parent's side.
学富五车	xu é fù wǔ chē	"learning fills five carts" — immensely learned; erudite — 学 (learning) is 富 (rich enough) to fill 五车 (five cartloads of books). The image comes from an age when books were bamboo strips, so that a serious library was measured by the cartload. Originally said of the philosopher Hui Shi, whose writings were said to fill five carts.
血肉相连	xu è ròu xiāng lián	blood and flesh linked: tied as closely as flesh to blood — A near-twin of 骨肉相连, stressing the living, circulating closeness between people, or between an individual and a cause they cannot separate from.
扬眉吐气	yáng méi tǔ qì	to feel proud and elated; to finally breathe out after long suffering — Lit: raise-eyebrows-exhale-qi. The physical sensation of relief and pride after enduring hardship or injustice. The eyebrows lift, the breath that was held in tension is finally released. Used for vindication, promotion after years of struggle, or national pride after historical humiliation.
养家活口	yǎng jiā huó kǒu	to support the family and feed its mouths: to provide for one's household — Lit: support-family-keep-alive-mouths. Here 活 is causative, to keep alive by feeding. The duty of the breadwinner to put food on the table and sustain everyone under the roof.
要风得风，要雨得雨	yào fēng dé fēng , yào yǔ dé yǔ	want wind, get wind; want rain, get rain — getting whatever one wishes — Used to describe someone who gets everything they ask for: a pampered child, a powerful person, someone in a position where nothing is refused. The parallelism of 要…得… (want…obtain…) is the construction backbone. Can be admiring or envious. 他在家里要风得风，要雨得雨，什么都不缺。
叶公好龙	Yè gōng hào lóng	Lord Ye Loves Dragons — He decorated his palace with dragons, until a real one appeared. The idiom for loving what you only love in the abstract.
一步一个脚印	yī bù yī gè jiǎo yìn	"one step, one footprint" — steady, methodical progress — Lit: each step leaves exactly one footprint; nothing rushed, nothing skipped. A modern proverb for diligent, unhurried work that builds on itself. Often used in career advice, study guidance, and praise for craftsmen.
一成不变	yī chéng bù biàn	fixed and unchanging; rigidly the same — set in stone; never varying — 成 chéng here carries its classical sense of "established; fixed; accomplished." Lit: "once established, not changing." The phrase carries a critical edge, describing something or someone that has refused all adaptation.
一夫当关	yī fū dāng guān	one man guards the pass: a position so strong that a single defender holds off an army — From the line about the road to Shu, 一夫当关，万夫莫开, "one man holding the pass, ten thousand cannot force it open." It praises a place of such natural strength that a lone defender can block a whole host, and by extension any well-held strategic point or a person stubbornly guarding a position.
一口气	yī kǒu qì	in one breath; without stopping; in one go — Lit: one-mouth-breath. Describes doing something continuously, without pause, in one uninterrupted exhalation of effort. 他一口气跑了十公里 "He ran ten kilometers without stopping." 一口气读完这本书 "Read the whole book in one sitting." The most common and versatile 气 idiom in everyday speech.
一连串	yī lián chuàn	a string of / a series of — multiple linked events — 一连 = one stretch of consecutive events; 串 chuàn = a string of threaded items (the classifier for strings of things: 一串葡萄 a bunch of grapes). Together: a chain of multiple connected events. 一连串的问题 "a string of problems." 一连串的失败 "a series of failures." Extremely common in news and commentary.
一路顺风	yí lù shùn fēng	"the whole road with following winds": bon voyage, may your trip go smoothly — A maritime image (favorable wind for the sails) generalized to all travel. Ubiquitous farewell phrase, slightly less formal than 一路平安.
一诺千金	yī nuò qiān jīn	one promise is worth a thousand gold — a promise that is absolute — A promise from a person of integrity is as valuable as a thousand pieces of gold. From the story of Ji Bu 季布 in the Shiji: "It is better to have one promise from Ji Bu than a hundred gold pieces from another." Used to praise reliability and personal honor.
一知半解	yì zhī bàn jiě	to know a little, to half-understand: superficial knowledge — Lit: one part 一 known 知, half 半 understood 解. A shallow, fragmentary grasp of something, knowing a bit but not the whole. 对这门学问他只是一知半解 = he has only a superficial grasp of this subject. Usually mildly critical of half-baked understanding.
一字千金	yī zì qiān jīn	one character, a thousand gold pieces — words of immense value — 字 here is a classifier for characters/words. 金 (gold pieces) is the unit of price. From the story of Lü Buwei's 吕不韦 text 《吕氏春秋》: he challenged anyone to change a single character and offered a thousand gold pieces if they could improve it. Used to praise literary excellence.
以心传心	yǐ xīn chuán xīn	mind transmitted by mind — the direct transmission of awakening from master to student — The second Chan axiom, paired with 不立文字. Awakening cannot be transferred via words or rituals; only by a direct, living encounter between an awakened mind and a genuinely seeking student.
义不容辞	yì bù róng cí	duty leaves no room to decline: an obligation one cannot refuse — 义 = duty, righteousness; 不容 = brooks no, leaves no room for; 辞 = to decline, to refuse. A moral duty so clear that refusing it is not an option. Note the 不容 (will brook no) construction: 容 in the negative means "to leave room for." 帮助他是我义不容辞的责任。
义正词严	yì zhèng cí yán	righteous in cause and stern in speech: to speak with upright authority — Lit: principle-upright-words-stern. Said of someone whose argument rests on a just cause and whose wording is firm and severe, leaving no room for doubt that they hold the moral high ground.
异口同声	yì kǒu tóng shēng	different mouths, the same sound — unanimous — Everyone says the same thing at once: the committee that answers in chorus, the witnesses whose accounts match. Neutral to positive in tone; suspicious unanimity would be phrased differently. The mirror image of 众说纷纭, where every mouth says something else.
因陋就简	yīn lòu jiù jiǎn	make do with the crude and the simple: get by with what little there is — 因 to rely on, 陋 crude, 就 to settle for, 简 the simple. Said of working within tight means, building or running something on a shoestring without waiting for better tools.
音容笑貌	yīn róng xiào mào	voice and smiling face: the remembered presence of someone now gone — Lit: sound-face-smile-appearance. The way a person's voice and warm expression linger in memory, used above all of the departed. 音容宛在 "the voice and face seem still present" is a common condolence.
迎刃而解	yíng rèn ér jiě	to split apart at the meeting of the blade: solved with ease — Lit: meeting 迎 the blade 刃, it splits 解. Like bamboo that splits cleanly the moment the knife touches it: once the hardest part is cracked, everything else falls apart of its own accord. Used when solving one key difficulty makes all the rest resolve effortlessly.
忧国忧民	yōu guó yōu mín	worried about the country and the people — the Confucian official's moral burden — The phrase encodes the Confucian ideal of public-spirited concern: not personal ambition but sorrow at the state's condition and compassion for the people's suffering.
有朝一日	yǒu zhāo yī rì	should there come a day: one day, someday — Lit: there-is morning one-day. 朝 here is zhāo, a morning standing for some future day. A set way of saying "one day," usually pointing to a hope or ambition: 有朝一日我会成功 "one day I will succeed."
有求必应	yǒu qiú bì yìng	every request met with response — to never refuse a reasonable ask — Lit: have-request-must-respond. Said of a generous, responsive person or deity (Guanyin is described this way). 她对学生有求必应，从不拒绝。"She responds to every student's request and never refuses." High praise for helpfulness and generosity.
鱼跃龙门	yú yu è lóng mén	fish leaping Dragon Gate — passing a crucial test and transforming one's destiny — 鱼 yú (fish) + 跃 yuè (to leap) + 龙门 lóngmén (Dragon Gate). The legend: a carp that braves the current and clears the great waterfall at Dragon Gate on the Yellow River is transformed into a dragon.
寓兵于农	yù bīng yú nóng	to lodge soldiers among farmers: keep a militia by having peasants farm in peacetime and fight in war — A classical statecraft principle: soldiers feed themselves from the land in quiet years, so the army costs little until it is needed. Used today of any scheme that hides one function inside another.
原形毕现	yuán xíng bì xiàn	the original form is fully revealed: a true nature laid completely bare — Lit: original-shape-completely-appears. Said when a disguise drops and someone's real, usually unflattering, character or identity becomes plain for all to see.
远亲不如近邻	yuǎn qīn bù rú jìn lín	"a distant relative is no match for a close neighbor" — proximity creates community — 远亲 (distant kin) + 不如 (not as good as) + 近邻 (near neighbor). A proverb rather than a classical chengyu with a single attributed source, but documented in Song dynasty collections and widely cited since.
远水救不了近火	yuǎn shuǐ jiù bù liǎo jìn huǒ	distant water cannot put out a nearby fire — From the Hán Fēizǐ. Help too far away is useless against an urgent crisis; a homely proverb on the gap between resources and immediate need.
月黑风高	yu è hēi fēng gāo	moonless and windy — a dark night fit for crime or treachery — 月黑 (the moon is black, that is, no moon) + 风高 (the wind is high). A set scene-setting phrase for a night when something sinister is afoot. The full classical line is 月黑风高杀人夜 (a moonless, windy night, a night for killing). Storytellers use it to signal that the next thing to happen will be dark.
越俎代庖	yu è zǔ dài páo	"to overstep the sacrificial vessel and take the cook's place" — to do someone else's job, to overstep one's role — From the Zhuangzi (庄子). 俎 zǔ is the sacrificial chopping block , the priest's tool , and 庖 páo is the kitchen's domain. The original image is of a priest abandoning the altar to do the cook's work.
站不住脚	zhàn bù zhù jiǎo	cannot stand on its feet: untenable — Literally "the feet cannot hold their stand." Used of an argument, excuse, or theory that collapses under scrutiny. Colloquial and very frequent.
站稳脚跟	zhàn wěn jiǎo gēn	to stand firm; to gain a foothold — Literally "to stand steady on one's heels." A common set phrase, not a classical four-character idiom: to establish oneself securely in a place, job, or market before making a move. Everyday modern usage.
站在风口浪尖	zhàn zài fēng kǒu làng jiān	to stand where the wind and waves meet: at the eye of the storm — To be exposed at the most dangerous, contested forefront of events, where pressure and scrutiny are fiercest. Modern journalistic register, common in commentary.
长风破浪	cháng fēng pò làng	a long wind breaking through the waves — bold ambition and perseverance — From the Tang poet Li Bai: 长风破浪会有时，直挂云帆济沧海. "There will come a time when the long wind breaks through the waves; I shall set my cloud-sail straight across the vast sea." The image is unstoppable ambition riding the great winds, no obstacle too large to overcome.
枝连叶合	zhī lián yè hé	branches linked, leaves joined — close-knit family or unity — Lit: branches-connect-leaves-join. A literary phrase for family solidarity or any close-knit organic unity, like branches of a tree that are all connected at the root. Uses 连 in its original botanical/linking sense.
知恩图报	zhī ēn tú bào	knowing a kindness and planning to repay it — grateful; not forgetting a debt of gratitude — 知 zhī (to know; to be aware of) + 恩 ēn (kindness; grace; favor) + 图 tú (to plan; to intend) + 报 bào (to repay; to respond). The compound 知恩 , knowing that a kindness has been done to you , is the first act; 图报, planning how to return it, is the second.
指手画脚	zhǐ shǒu huà jiǎo	pointing with hands and gesturing with feet — bossing others around with showy gestures — Lit: point-hand-draw-foot. Said of someone who criticises and orders others about while making theatrical gestures. Carries a strong note of irritation: the speaker is performing authority rather than exercising it.
志同道合	zhì tóng dào hé	sharing the same goals and ideals — kindred spirits — Describes friends or partners who share the same ambitions (志 zhì) and follow the same path (道 dào). The classical ideal of friendship: not mere companionship but a meeting of values and direction. Used for close friends, allies, and collaborators who are truly aligned in what they want and believe.
志在必得	zhì zài bì dé	determined to get it; fully committed to winning — 志 zhì (will; ambition) + 在 (located in) + 必 bì (certainly) + 得 dé (to obtain). The ambition is so fixed that obtaining the goal is taken as given, not from arrogance but from total commitment. Used to describe someone who has made a full decision and will not waver. 他对这个项目志在必得，不成功不罢休。
众口铄金	zhòng kǒu shuò jīn	many mouths melt gold — From the Guoyu and other classical texts: collective speech is so powerful it can melt metal. A more extreme formulation of the same principle as 三人成虎: the force of accumulated voices is overwhelming, even against something as solid as reputation or fact.
众志成城	zhòng zhì chéng chéng	"when wills unite, a city wall is formed" — collective will is an impregnable defense — From the Guoyu (国语 Guóyǔ), a pre-Qin historical text. 众 (the many; the people) + 志 (will; resolve) + 成 (form; become) + 城 (city wall). When a people are of one mind, they become as strong as a city's walls. Used in political speeches, team motivation, and times of national crisis. 面对困难，我们众志成城，一定能克服。
转危为安	zhuǎn wēi wéi ān	turn danger into safety: pull through a crisis — A four-character mirror of 居安思危, read in the other direction. The relief phrase after a patient stabilizes or a company survives. Common in news and speeches.
子承父业	zǐ chéng fù yè	the son carries on the father's trade: inheriting the family calling — Said when a child takes up the parent's profession or business, from a craftsman's shop to a family firm. Neutral to approving, depending on whether the inheritance is seen as continuity or constraint.
罪魁祸首	zuì kuí huò shǒu	the ringleader of the crime and the chief of the disaster: the main culprit — Lit: crime-chief-disaster-head. The principal wrongdoer, the one most to blame for an offense or calamity. Here 首 is the head, the ringleader. 他是这场事故的罪魁祸首 "He is the chief culprit behind this accident."
左思右想	zuǒ sī yòu xiǎng	thinking left and right — to turn something over and over in the mind; to deliberate from all angles — 左 zuǒ (left) + 思 sī (to think) + 右 yòu (right) + 想 xiǎng (to think). The spatial metaphor makes tangible what overthinking feels like: the mind moving left, then right, finding no ground to rest on.
连…都/也	lián … dōu / yě	even X — The intensifier construction: raising the stakes of a claim.
"可以从太空看见"	kě yǐ cóng tài kōng kàn jiàn	"visible from space" — The wall cannot be seen from space with the naked eye. It is long but only four to eight metres wide, narrower than most highways. The claim was a 1932 invention from Ripley's Believe It or Not! — published before any human had reached orbit.
"秦始皇修了长城"	qín shǐ huáng xiū le cháng chéng	"Qin Shihuang built the Great Wall" — He connected and extended pre-existing Warring States walls. He did not build from nothing, and what he did build is mostly gone. The visual wall most people picture is Ming, fifteen centuries later.
"一道连续的墙"	yī dào lián xù de qiáng	"a continuous wall" — The Great Wall is not, and never was, a single continuous line. Even the Ming wall has parallel branches, doubled-back sections, gaps where rivers and mountains made building unnecessary, and stretches where it thins to a chain of isolated beacon towers.
*把书看	* bǎ shū kàn	WRONG — verb must have a resultative element — The most fundamental 把 error: using a bare verb with no resultative or directional complement. The construction requires that the action have a clear outcome. The sentence is ungrammatical because it doesn't tell us what happened to the book as a result.
*把一本书看完	* bǎ yī běn shū kàn wán	WRONG (usually) — object must be definite — The object of 把 must be definite , a specific, known thing. An indefinite object (a book you haven't mentioned before, an unspecified amount) generally cannot be used. The construction implies you are taking hold of a particular thing and doing something to it.
安危	ān wēi	safety and danger; well-being — 安 (safety) bound to 危 (danger) as one word: the whole question of whether someone is safe or in peril. A fixed pair where the two opposites name a single concern, often someone's fate or welfare.
昂首	áng shǒu	to hold the head high; head raised — 昂 áng (to raise, to lift up) + 首 shǒu (head). To carry the head high, in pride or defiance. 昂首挺胸 "head high and chest out" is the stock image of someone walking with proud, confident bearing. Literary in tone; the spoken equivalent uses 头 tóu.
傲慢	ào màn	arrogant; haughty; condescending — 傲 ào (proud, lofty, unbending) + 慢 màn (slow to respond, dismissive of others). The most common Chinese word for arrogance: deliberate condescension, the attitude of someone who considers themselves above the normal courtesies. Clearly pejorative.
把书放好	bǎ shū fàng hǎo	to put the book away properly — 放 fàng (to place; to put) + 好 hǎo (well; properly; in the right state). The 好 signals the object reaches a satisfactory, correct, or complete state. 放好 is not just "put down" , it's "place in a way that is done properly." 好 as a resultative complement implies care, correctness, and readiness.
把书拿出来	bǎ shū ná chū lái	take the book out (bring it out into the open) — 拿 ná (to take; to hold in the hand) + 出来 chūlái (out-coming; out toward the speaker). The book is extracted from some container or location and brought into the present space. 出来 implies emergence from a hidden or enclosed place toward an open, accessible position.
霸道	bà dào	hegemony; domineering; the way of force — 霸 bà (hegemon; overlord, one who rules by force rather than virtue) + 道 dào (way; path). Classically: governing through coercion rather than moral example, the antithesis of 王道. Colloquially in modern Mandarin: overbearing, domineering (of a person's behavior).
白茶	bái chá	white tea — minimally processed; wither and dry
白莲社	Bái lián Shè	White Lotus Society (Huiyuan's)
拜神明	bài shén míng	to venerate the gods; to pay respect at a deity's altar — 拜 bài (to bow; to worship; to pay respects) + 神明. The standard verb for the act of ritual veneration , burning incense, bowing, presenting offerings. Common in Taiwan, Fujian, and diaspora communities. The word 拜 is secular enough (拜访 = to visit) that 拜神明 does not sound archaic or overly formal.
绊脚石	bàn jiǎo shí	stumbling block; obstacle — 绊 bàn (to trip) + 脚 + 石 shí (stone). The foot-tripping stone. Both literal (a stone in the path) and figurative (an obstacle to progress).
报道	bào dào	news report; to report (news) — 报 bào (to announce; to report; newspaper) + 道 dào (way; path). To map and announce the course of events: journalism as the act of charting what has happened. Used both as a noun (a report, a piece of coverage) and as a verb (to report on something).
报告	bào gào	to report; a report — 报 (to announce) plus 告 (gào, to tell): to deliver an account formally, and the document that delivers it. Works as both the act of reporting and the written or spoken report itself.
报价	bào jià	quotation
报价单	bào jià dān	quotation sheet — 报价 (bàojià) is both the verb "to quote" and the noun "quote." Ask for it in writing, line by line: 分项报价 (fēnxiàng bàojià) is an itemised quote that breaks out the product, packaging, tooling, and printing.
报社	bào shè	newspaper office; press — 报 bào (newspaper) + 社 shè (an agency, office). The organization that produces a newspaper, both the institution and its premises: 在报社工作 "to work at a newspaper." The 社 here is the institutional body, not a club; the same slot gives 出版社 "publishing house" and 杂志社 "magazine office."
北港朝天宫	Běi gǎng Cháo tiān Gōng	Beigang Chaotian Temple — Taiwan's great Mazu shrine — The Chaotian Temple (朝天宫) in Beigang, Yunlin County, Taiwan, is one of the oldest and most revered Mazu temples on the island, traditionally dating its founding to 1694 CE. With Dajia's Zhenlangong, it is one of Taiwan's two dominant Mazu pilgrimage centers.
被代表	bèi dài biǎo	to have one's views "represented" without consent — 代表 dàibiǎo (to represent; a representative). 被代表 = someone else speaks for you without your authorization and claims to represent your interests or views.
被误解了	bèi wù jiě le	was misunderstood (adversarial — not one's intention) — Misunderstanding, misrepresentation, and mistreatment are quintessential 被 territory. The speaker's intention or the subject's nature is distorted by someone else's interpretation. The adversarial quality is strong , no one wants to be misunderstood.
本土	běn tǔ	native; local; indigenous; domestic — 本 běn (root; origin; this) + 土 tǔ (soil; native place). Native, local, domestic, contrasted with 外来 wàilái (from outside) or 进口 jìnkǒu (imported). 本土品牌 = domestic brand. 本土文化 = local culture.
本性	běn xìng	innate nature; one's true nature — 本 běn (root, origin) + 性 xìng (nature). The nature one is born with, deep and hard to change: 本性善良 "good-natured at the core." Often invoked when surface behavior gives way to what a person really is, as in 本性难移, that one's nature is hard to alter.
必定	bì dìng	certainly; definitely; without a doubt — 必 bì (certainly) + 定 dìng (settled; fixed; determined). A speaker's confident prediction about the future or about something not yet observed. The certainty is in the speaker's judgment, not a rule or law. Closer to "I am certain that" than to "you are required to."
必然	bì rán	inevitable; necessarily; of necessity — 必 bì (certainly; without exception) + 然 rán (so; thus; naturally so , the word for "naturally" or "as it must be"). Together: that which is naturally and necessarily the case, which could not have been otherwise. The register is philosophical, historical, or scientific.
必胜	bì shèng	certain to win; victory is assured — 必 bì (certainly; without exception) + 胜 shèng (to win; to overcome). A morale-building formula rather than a measured prediction. Used in sports, military contexts, and competitive settings where the declaration of certainty is itself motivational. The certainty is willed, not merely predicted.
必须承认	bì xū chéng rèn	one must admit; it must be acknowledged that — A common rhetorical move in essays and serious conversation, conceding ground before pivoting. The 必须 here is not commanding the listener; it is the speaker acknowledging an inescapable point. English equivalent: "it has to be said that," "one must concede that."
必须遵守	bì xū zūn shǒu	must comply with; must abide by (rules, laws) — The standard pairing in legal, regulatory, and policy language. 必须遵守法律 (must comply with the law). The phrase carries the formal-rules register that 必须 lives in: every Chinese workplace handbook, public notice, and company memo will use this collocation rather than its softer alternatives.
必需	bì xū	essential (thing); necessity
必要	bì yào	necessary; required; essential (for a given purpose) — 必 bì (certainly; without exception) + 要 yào (to want; to need; required). Necessity defined by a purpose or goal: if you want the outcome, this is what the outcome requires. The conditionality is built in.
变成	biàn chéng	to turn into; to become — 变 biàn (to change) + 成 chéng (to become; to complete; to succeed), the directional complement. The standard compound for completed transformation: X has fully become Y. 成 is the result complement, indicating the transformation has landed somewhere definite.
变法	biàn fǎ	to change the laws; political-legal reform (historical term) — 变 biàn (to change) + 法 fǎ (law; method; the Dharma, depending on context). The classical Chinese term for fundamental legal and institutional reform: changing the very rules by which a state operates.
变革	biàng é	transformation; systemic reform; institutional change — 变 biàn (to change) + 革 gé (to reform; leather, the image of tanning a hide and stripping away the old surface). 变革 carries more structural weight than 变化: it implies change to systems, institutions, or fundamental arrangements, not merely surface conditions.
变卦	biàng uà	changing hexagram; colloquial: to change one's mind, go back on one's word — In the 易经, 变卦 biànguà is the technical term for the resultant hexagram, the second hexagram produced when changing lines (爻 yáo that have reached their extreme) transform. The 变卦 reveals where the present situation is moving.
变化	biàn huà	change; transformation; variation — 变 biàn (to change) + 化 huà (to transform; to dissolve into something new). The most general and most frequent word for change in modern Mandarin. Where 变 alone is the verb of transformation, 变化 is the noun: change as a thing that happens, can be observed, measured, discussed.
变坏	biàn huài	to go bad, to deteriorate — 变 (to change) + 坏 (bad). To turn for the worse, whether food spoiling in the heat, weather souring, or a person's character degrading over time.
辨认	biàn rèn	to identify; distinguish
别客气	bié kè qi	don't stand on ceremony
濒危	bīn wēi	endangered (species)
补救	bǔ jiù	to remedy; to make good; remediation
不必	bù bì	not necessary; need not
不好意思	bù hǎo yì si	embarrassed; sorry to trouble you; excuse me — Lit: not-good-meaning/face. The phrase used when imposing on someone, asking a favor, or causing mild embarrassment. One of the most important social lubricants in Mandarin. Not a strong apology, more like "I feel awkward about this" or "pardon the imposition."
不客气	bú kè qi	"you're welcome"; impolite, blunt
不认真	bú rèn zhēn	not taking it seriously; phoning it in
不用客气	bú yòng kè qi	no need to be polite
不知道	bù zhī dào	don't know; I'm not sure; I have no idea — 不 bù (negation) + 知道. One of the most common phrases in conversational Mandarin: the honest, unadorned acknowledgment of not having found the path. As a standalone response, 不知道！is perfectly natural and socially acceptable (not rude) in most contexts.
部首	bù shǒu	radical (the section head of a character) — 部 bù (section, division) + 首 shǒu (head). The radical of a character: the head-element under which a dictionary groups it. 首 itself is a 部首. To look a character up in a paper dictionary, you find its 部首 first, then count the remaining strokes.
财富	cái fù	wealth; riches — 财 (money, property) plus 富 (riches). The accumulated stock of wealth itself, the noun for what a rich person holds. Often paired abstractly, as in 精神财富 (spiritual wealth).
财神	Cái shén	the Wealth God — most actively invoked deity in commercial life — Cáishén is not one deity but a cluster of wealth-associated gods, the most popular being 赵公明 Zhào Gōngmíng (a dark-faced general riding a black tiger) and the Five Roads Gods (五路财神 Wǔlù Cáishén). His image greets visitors at the entrances of shops, restaurants, and banks across China.
草书	cǎo shū	cursive script — the most expressive and wild form — 草 cǎo (grass; rough; draft) + 书 shū. "Grass writing" , like wild grass, cursive is fast, flowing, and irregular. Individual strokes merge; some characters are radically abbreviated. Legible mainly to trained readers. The style most associated with artistic expression and emotional intensity.
插一脚	chā yī jiǎo	to butt in; to insert oneself into someone else's affair — 插 chā (to insert) + 一 yī (one) + 脚. To stick a foot in where it wasn't invited , usually meddlesome but sometimes opportunistic. Cousin of English "to put one's foot in." Often pejorative.
茶餐厅	chá cān tīng	Cha chaan teng — Cha chaan teng: the quintessential Hong Kong casual restaurant, serving a fusion menu of Cantonese and Western-influenced dishes: milk tea, pineapple buns (菠萝包 bōluóbāo), egg tarts, congee, rice dishes, and Hong Kong-style French toast.
茶禅一味	chá chán yī wèi	tea and Chan: one flavor
茶馆	chá guǎn	Teahouse — Teahouse: a social institution especially important in Sichuan and Guangdong, where one goes not just to drink tea but to play mahjong, listen to storytelling, or simply spend a morning reading the paper.
茶毫	chá háo	tea down — fine white hairs on young leaves
茶荷	chá hé	tea display dish — for showing the dry leaf
茶壶	chá hú	Teapot — Teapot. The Yixing purple clay teapot (紫砂壶) is the prestige form, prized because it absorbs tea oils over time and improves with use.
茶黄素	chá huáng sù	theaflavins — the brightness compounds of black tea

茶马古道	Chá Mǎ Gǔ Dào	Ancient Tea-Horse Road — the trade route that carried tea to Tibet

产能	chǎn néng	production capacity — Output per day or month. 排产 (páichǎn) is scheduling an order onto the line; 旺季 (wàngjì) is peak season, when capacity disappears. In the months before Chinese New Year lines fill up and many workers leave early, so lead times stretch.
产品认证	chǎn pǐn rèn zhèng	product certification — 认证 rènzhèng (to certify, certification , 认 recognize + 证 proof/certificate). The system by which products are officially verified to meet standards. 3C认证 (China Compulsory Certification) is the mandatory certification for electronics, appliances, and vehicles sold in China.
产品质量	chǎn pǐn zhì liàng	product quality — 质量 zhìliàng (substance + quantity → quality) is the standard word for quality in both the everyday and technical sense. 产品质量 is the fixed phrase for product quality , appearing in consumer protection law (产品质量法), manufacturing standards, and marketing.
产前样	chǎn qián yàng	pre-production sample (PPS) — Made on the production line with production materials, after tooling and before bulk starts. Hand samples can be perfect while line samples are not; insisting on a 产前样 catches the difference before thousands of units exist.
产生	chǎn shēng	to produce; to give rise to; to emerge — 产 chǎn (to produce; output) + 生 shēng (arise). For abstract and concrete creation , ideas, problems, emotions, consequences arising. 产生影响 "to have an effect" · 产生误会 "to give rise to a misunderstanding."
唱反调	chàng fǎn diào	to deliberately disagree; to sing a different tune — 唱 chàng (to sing) + 反调 fǎndiào (an opposing tune). To voice the contrary position on purpose, to be the one who always sings against the prevailing note. Colloquial and faintly critical.
朝代	cháo dài	dynasty: a reigning house and its era — 朝 cháo (court, reign) + 代 dài (generation, age). The standard word for a dynasty, the span during which one ruling house held the throne. Chinese history is told as a sequence of 朝代, each with its own name, capital, and character.
朝气	zhāo qì	youthful spirit: vigor, the lively air of a new day — 朝 zhāo (morning) + 气 qì (air, energy). The energy of morning seen in people, a fresh, lively spirit. Said of the young or of any group full of drive: 充满朝气 "brimming with vigor." Its opposite is 暮气 mùqì, the listlessness of dusk.
朝前	cháo qián	forward; toward the front; facing ahead — 朝 cháo (toward) + 前 qián (front). Directed at the space ahead: 朝前走 "walk forward," 朝前看 "look ahead." A close everyday counterpart to 向前.
朝圣	cháo shèng	pilgrimage
朝廷	cháo tíng	the imperial court: the ruler and the central government — 朝 cháo (court) + 廷 tíng (court hall). The ruler together with the ministers and offices around the throne, the seat of central power. To act 代表朝廷 is to act in the name of the throne itself.
朝夕	zhāo xī	morning and evening: every hour, day after day — 朝 zhāo (morning) + 夕 xī (evening). The two ends of the day standing for all of it: from dawn to dusk, every hour. 朝夕相处 "to be together morning and evening," to live in close daily company; 只争朝夕 "to seize every moment."
朝向	cháo xiàng	to face toward; the aspect or facing of (a building) — 朝 cháo (to face) + 向 xiàng (toward). To face a given way, or the facing itself. Common in describing buildings: 这套房子朝向南 "this flat faces south," a prized aspect for sunlight in Chinese homes.
朝阳	zhāo yáng	the morning sun: the rising sun, dawn light — 朝 zhāo (morning) + 阳 yáng (sun). The sun as it rises, and the fresh light it brings. Used figuratively for anything new and full of promise: 朝阳产业 "a sunrise industry," a sector on the rise.
朝着	cháo zhe	toward; in the direction of (with sustained motion) — 朝 cháo (toward) + 着 zhe (the durative marker). The preposition with a sense of ongoing facing or movement toward something: 朝着大海 "facing the sea," 朝着目标努力 "to work toward a goal." Common in figurative aims as well as physical ones.
成功	chéng gōng	success: to succeed — 成 chéng (to accomplish; to become) + 功 gōng (achievement). Literally "to complete the achievement." Both verb (to succeed) and noun (success). One of the most common words in modern Chinese, the natural opposite of 失败 shībai (failure). 成功人士 = a successful person. 取得成功 = to attain success.
承认	chéng rèn	to admit, to acknowledge, to recognize formally — 承 chéng (to bear; to receive; to assume) + 认 rèn. To shoulder the recognition of something , a fact, a fault, a state's legitimacy. Spans personal admission ("I admit I was wrong") and formal recognition (one country recognizing another diplomatically).
城堡	chéng bǎo	castle; fortress — 城 chéng (wall; fortified place) + 堡 bǎo (fort; stronghold). A castle or fortress, the walled stronghold of a lord. 城堡 is the standard translation for the European castle, and the word most readers meet first in fairy tales and fantasy.
城隍	Chéng huáng	the City God — divine magistrate of the underworld — 城隍 (lit. "city wall and moat") is the divine magistrate of a city or county, presiding over both the living city and its deceased residents in the underworld. He holds court in his temple (城隍庙 Chénghuáng miào), trying the souls of the recently dead to assign their afterlife fate.
城隍庙	Chéng huáng miào	City God Temple
城门	chéng mén	city gate — 城 chéng (city; wall) + 门 mén (gate; door). The controlled opening in the wall, the single point where the inside and outside of a city legally met. City gates were closed at night, manned by guards, and named individually; a walled capital might have a dozen, each a landmark in its own right.
城墙	chéng qiáng	city wall; rampart — 城 chéng (city; wall) + 墙 qiáng (wall). The original sense of 城 made explicit. Where 城 alone once meant the wall, modern Mandarin pairs it with 墙 to specify the physical structure as opposed to the city it encloses.
城区	chéng qū	urban district
城市	chéng shì	city — 城 chéng (walled settlement) + 市 shì (market). The most ordinary modern word for "city," and a fossil of how cities formed: a wall (城) for defense, a market (市) for exchange. The two together make the thing.
城镇	chéng zhèn	towns and cities; urban areas — 城 chéng (city) + 镇 zhèn (town). A collective term spanning the whole range of urban settlement, from large cities down to market towns, used especially in contrast to the countryside (农村 nóngcūn).
吃苦	chī kǔ	to endure hardship; to eat bitterness — 吃 chī (eat) + 苦 kǔ (bitter; hardship). To eat bitterness , to endure difficulty without complaint. One of the most culturally loaded compounds in Chinese. 能吃苦 (able to endure hardship) is a major virtue. 吃苦耐劳 = endure hardship and labor without complaint.
吃香	chī xiāng	to be popular, sought-after
赤脚	chì jiǎo	barefoot — 赤 chì (red; bare; naked) + 脚. Bare-footed. Famously the term in 赤脚医生 chìjiǎo yīshēng, the "barefoot doctors" of the Mao-era rural healthcare program , village paramedics with minimal training who made primary care available across the countryside.
出版社	chū bǎn shè	publishing house
出产	chū chǎn	to produce; to yield; output — 出 chū (emerge; put out) + 产 chǎn (produce; give birth to; output). To yield products from a source , a region produces goods, a field yields a crop, a factory puts out units. Used for natural resources, agricultural yield, and manufactured goods. As a noun: the output, the produce.
出口	chū kǒu	to export; an exit; to speak — 出 chū (exit; emerge) + 口 kǒu (mouth; opening; port). Three interlocking meanings from the same compound. As trade: to export , goods going out through the port. As architecture: the exit of a building or tunnel. As speech: 出口成章 (words emerge perfectly formed , to speak with eloquence).
出口退税	chū kǒu tuì shuì	export VAT rebate — The refund of input VAT on exported goods, claimed by whoever exports them. A factory with its own export licence (自营出口 zìyíng chūkǒu) claims it directly; one without exports through a trading company (外贸公司 wàimào gōngsī), which adds a layer of margin.
出神	chū shén	to enter trance; to be rapt
传播	chuán bō	to disseminate; to spread; to broadcast; communication — 传 chuán (to transmit) + 播 bō (to sow, to broadcast). To spread information, ideas, or culture widely: 传播知识 "to disseminate knowledge," 大众传播 "mass communication." Also the academic field name, 传播学 "communication studies."
传承	chuán chéng	to inherit and carry on
传达	chuán dá	to convey; to pass on (a message); to communicate — 传 chuán (to convey) + 达 dá (to reach, to arrive). To deliver a message, an order, or a feeling so that it reaches its target: 传达指示 "to convey instructions," 传达上级的精神 "to pass on the leadership's intent." Stresses that the content arrives intact.
传递	chuán dì	to pass; to relay; to convey; to transmit — 传 chuán (to pass on) + 递 dì (to hand over). To move something from one party to the next in a chain: 传递信息 "to relay information," 传递火炬 "to pass the torch." Stresses the act of handing along, often through several hands or steps.
传记	zhuàn jì	biography; a life story — 传 zhuàn (a handed-down record) + 记 jì (to record). A written account of a person's life: 名人传记 "celebrity biographies," 人物传记 "biographies of notable figures." Note the reading shifts to zhuàn here, never chuán.
传染	chuán rǎn	to infect; to be contagious; to spread (a disease) — 传 chuán (to transmit) + 染 rǎn (to dye, to be tainted). The spread of an illness from one person to another: 传染病 "infectious disease." Figuratively, an emotion or a mood that catches: 他的笑很有传染力 "his laugh is contagious."
传染病	chuán rǎn bìng	infectious disease — 传染 (chuánrǎn, to infect, to pass on) + 病: a disease that spreads from one person to another. It is the headline word during any epidemic and the category label on public-health notices.
传授	chuán shòu	to impart (knowledge or skill)
传说	chuán shuō	legend; folklore; that which is passed down — 传 chuán (to transmit; to pass on) + 说 shuō (speech; to speak; theory). A legend or oral tradition , stories that have been passed down through generations without fixed written form.
传统节日	chuán tǒng jié rì	traditional festival; heritage holiday — 传统 (traditional) + 节日 (festival, holiday). The inherited ritual calendar: 春节, 元宵节, 清明节, 端午节, 七夕节, 中元节, 中秋节, 重阳节. These are 传统节日 as opposed to 法定节假日 (statutory public holidays) like National Day.
传统文化	chuán tǒng wén huà	traditional culture; cultural heritage — 传统 (tradition) + 文化 (culture). The received deposit of Chinese civilization: classical literature, Confucian ethics, folk festivals, traditional arts and crafts, medicine, cuisine, and everything else that is "Chinese" by inheritance rather than by innovation.
传统医学	chuán tǒng yī xu é	traditional medicine; TCM — 传统 (traditional) + 医学 (medicine). The formal name for the system of Chinese medicine (中医 zhōngyī) as a body of inherited knowledge: acupuncture, herbal medicine, moxibustion, massage (推拿 tuīná), and the diagnostic frameworks of qi, yin-yang, and the five phases.
春风	chūn fēng	spring breeze; warmth, favor — 春 (spring) + 风 (fēng, wind). The mild wind of spring, and by extension any warm, reviving influence, a kind teacher, good news, a stroke of fortune. The phrase 春风得意 (chūnfēng déyì) describes riding high on success.
从容	cóng róng	calm and unhurried; composed
打破传统	dǎ pò chuán tǒng	to break with tradition; to challenge convention — 打破 (to break, to smash) + 传统 (tradition). The act of violating inherited norms deliberately. In modern usage, 打破传统 can be positive (innovative, forward-thinking) or negative (disrespectful, reckless) depending on context.
大道	dà dào	main road; grand way; the broad path — 大 dà (big; main; grand) + 道 (way; path). Physically: a wide, main thoroughfare. Philosophically: the grand way of moral order or heaven's intention.
大夫	dài fu	doctor; physician (colloquial) — 大 dà + 夫 fū, but in this sense read dàifu with a neutral tone. A common, slightly informal word for a doctor, especially in northern speech: 看大夫 to see a doctor, 张大夫 Dr. Zhang. Read 大夫 dàfū instead and the same characters name a senior official of the old imperial court, a different word entirely.
大义	dà yì	the greater cause; moral principle; overriding righteousness — 大 dà (great; large) + 义 yì (righteousness; moral duty). The righteousness that supersedes personal ties, individual interest, or clan loyalty. 大义灭亲 dàyì mièqīn = to put righteousness above family (a set phrase for reporting or condemning a family member for the sake of the greater moral order).
代办	dài bàn	to handle on behalf of; to do for another — 代 dài (on behalf of) + 办 bàn (to handle; to manage). To take care of a task or piece of business in someone else's stead: 代办手续 "to handle the formalities for someone," 代办签证 "to arrange a visa on another's behalf." The errand done in your name.
代表	dài biǎo	to represent; a representative; to stand for — 代 dài (on behalf of) + 表 biǎo (to express; outward). As a verb, to represent or stand for: 红色代表喜庆 "red stands for celebration." As a noun, the person who represents others: 班代表 "class representative," 人民代表 "people's deputy."
代工	dài gōng	contract manufacturing, OEM
代购	dài gòu	to buy on behalf of; a purchasing proxy — 代 dài (on behalf of) + 购 gòu (to purchase). To buy something for another person, often goods from abroad: 海外代购 "overseas purchasing proxy." A familiar modern word from the rise of cross-border shopping, where one traveler buys for many at home.
代价	dài jià	price paid, cost (in effort or loss) — 代 (to stand in for, substitute) + 价 (price). The cost something exacts, not in money but in effort, sacrifice, or loss. You pay a 代价 for a mistake, a victory, or a hard choice.
代理	dài lǐ	to act as agent; acting (in a post); agency — 代 dài (on behalf of) + 理 lǐ (to manage). To handle affairs in an official capacity for someone else: 代理人 "an agent," 代理商 "a distributor," 代理校长 "acting principal." As a modifier it marks a temporary or proxy holder of a post.
代替	dài tì	to replace; to substitute for; in place of — 代 dài (to stand in for) + 替 tì (to replace). The general verb for one thing or person taking another's place: 用机器代替人工 "to replace manual labor with machines." Often follows the pattern A 代替 B, "A in place of B."
怠慢	dài màn	to neglect; to slight a guest through inattention — 怠 dài (lazy, inattentive, slack) + 慢 màn (slow). To fail to attend to someone with the care and promptness they deserve. The standard word for hosting someone poorly or neglecting a guest's needs. Often appears in apologies: 怠慢了您, please forgive my poor hospitality.
单价	dān jià	unit price — The price per piece; 总价 (zǒngjià) is the total, 金额 (jīn'é) the amount on a line. The usual follow-up is what the unit price includes: 包括 (bāokuò) and 不包括 are the words to listen for.
弹性	tán xìng	elasticity; flexibility; springiness — 弹 tán (to spring, to bounce) + 性 xìng (property). The quality of stretching and springing back, literal in materials and figurative in arrangements: 弹性材料 "an elastic material," 弹性工作时间 "flexible working hours." Names a built-in capacity to flex without breaking.
当代	dāng dài	contemporary era
道谛	dào dì	the truth of the path — the Noble Eightfold Path — 道 dào = way, path. Sanskrit magga. The prescription: the 八正道 bā zhèng dào (Noble Eightfold Path): right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration.
道法自然	dào fǎ zì rán	the Way models nature; the Way follows its own spontaneous pattern — 道 (the Way) + 法 (to model; to follow as a pattern) + 自然 (self-thus; spontaneous; natural). The culminating principle of Daodejing chapter 25. The Way does not follow anything outside itself , its law is its own spontaneous nature.
道观	dào guàn	a Daoist temple; a Daoist monastery — 道 dào (the Way; Daoism) + 观 guàn (temple). The temple of a Daoist community, where 观 takes its fourth-tone reading. Set against the Buddhist 寺 sì (monastery) and the general 庙 miào (shrine).
道家	Dào jiā	Daoism — the way of naturalness and non-action — The Daodejing 道德经 (attributed to Laozi, c. 4th–3rd century BCE) and Zhuangzi 庄子. Central concept: 无为 wú wéi, effortless action aligned with the natural order. The sage ruler governs so lightly that the people believe they govern themselves.
道教	dào jiào	Daoism
道理	dào lǐ	reason; logic; principle; the way things work — 道 dào (way; path) + 理 lǐ (principle; pattern; grain of wood, the inherent structure of things). "The logic of the path." The way things make sense: the reason behind an argument, the principle underlying a situation, the basic logic that should govern behavior.
道路	dào lù	road; path; way — 道 dào (way; path) + 路 lù (road; route). The most standard, neutral word for a road or path. Used freely in both spatial and metaphorical senses. 人生道路 (the road of life) is one of the most common metaphorical applications, at home in political speeches and personal memoirs alike.
道歉	dào qiàn	to apologize
道上的人	dào shàng de rén	underworld figures
道统	dào tǒng	orthodox transmission of the Way
道义	dào yì	moral duty; principle of right conduct; the Way and righteousness — 道 dào (the Way; the correct path; principle) + 义 yì (righteousness; moral duty). The pairing of the Way as principle and righteousness as conduct , what a person owes to the moral order. 道义上的责任 dàoyì shàng de zérèn = moral responsibility (as opposed to legal obligation).
得气	dé qì	Arrival of qi, the sensation confirming correct needling — Arrival of qi, the sensation confirming correct needling: heaviness, distension, or a spreading warmth at the needled point. Classical and modern acupuncturists both treat déqì as the essential marker of effective treatment.
地图	dì tú	map — 地 dì (earth; ground) + 图 tú (diagram; picture; plan). Lit. "earth diagram." A transparent compound , the earth drawn out as a picture. 地图 is the standard word for any kind of map, from a hand-drawn sketch to a satellite navigation app.
地形	dì xíng	terrain; topography; the shape of the land — 地 dì (ground; land) + 形 xíng. The shape of the land: terrain, topography, the rise and fall of the ground. 地形复杂 = the terrain is complex; 地形图 = a topographic map. The most literal of the situation-words, where the shape being read is the physical landscape itself.
地狱道	dì yù dào	the hell realm; the path of extreme suffering — The lowest of the six realms: extreme suffering as the karmic consequence of severe negative actions , violence, cruelty, deliberate harm.
低矮	dī ǎi	low and short; squat; close to the ground — 低 (low) + 矮 (short, of stature or height). Used for structures rather than people: low-ceilinged rooms, squat walls, single-story buildings hugging the ground. 低矮的平房 (low-slung single-story houses) is a recurring image in descriptions of older hutong neighborhoods or rural architecture.
低沉	dī chén	low and deep (of sound); somber; heavy; low-spirited — 低 (low) + 沉 (to sink, heavy, deep). The compound sits at the intersection of acoustic and emotional: a 低沉的声音 is a deep, resonant voice that seems to come from somewhere down in the chest; a 低沉的心情 is a mood that has sunk below normal , subdued, heavy, not quite depressed but nowhere near cheerful.
低处	dī chù	low place; lower ground; a lower spot — 低 (low) + 处 (place, location). A low-lying spot or lower area. Practical and descriptive in geography, architecture, and flood-risk contexts: 低处积水 (water pooling in low-lying areas) is a standard phrase in weather reports.
低调	dī diào	low-key; understated; keeping a low profile — 低 (low) + 调 (key, register, tone , musical or social). Originally the low musical key; now the social posture of deliberate understatement. 低调做人 (carry yourself with a low profile) and 低调行事 (act without fanfare) are standard phrases in professional and political discourse.
低级	dī jí	low-level; crude; vulgar; base — 低 (low) + 级 (grade, level, rank). At the lower end of a scale , which in practice means either genuinely entry-level (低级职员, junior staff; 低级别, low rank) or, more pejoratively, crude or vulgar in taste and behavior: 低级趣味 (vulgar tastes), 低级错误 (dī jí cuòwù, a basic mistake , the kind you shouldn't mak…
低人一等	dī rén yī děng	one rank below others; to consider oneself inferior — 低 (lower) + 人 (person, others) + 一等 (one grade, one rank). To be placed one step below others in status , or to place oneself there through shame or social conditioning.
低声	dī shēng	in a low voice; softly; in a whisper — 低 (low) + 声 (sound, voice). Speaking quietly, in a lowered voice. Appears as an adverb (低声说 , to say in a low voice) or a verb complement.
低头	dī tóu	to lower one's head; to bow; to yield — 低 (low, lower) + 头 (head). The single most loaded compound on this page. Physically, it is simply to look down , at a phone, at work, at the ground while walking.
低音	dī yīn	low pitch; bass; bass register — 低 (low) + 音 (sound, musical tone). The low end of the pitch spectrum , the bass register in music, the bottom frequencies in acoustics. 低音炮 (dīyīn pào, "bass cannon") is the colloquial term for a subwoofer. 低音提琴 (dīyīn tíqín) is the double bass. In vocal music, 低音歌手 is a bass singer.
调查	diào chá	to investigate; to survey; investigation — 调 diào (to direct, to send out) + 查 chá (to check, to examine). To look into a matter systematically, whether a police inquiry or a market survey. 市场调查 (market survey), 调查结果 (the findings of an investigation). Both verb and noun.
调动	diào dòng	to transfer; to mobilize; to arouse — 调 diào (to transfer) + 动 dòng (to move). To move a person to a new post, or to mobilize forces and feelings. 调动工作 (transfer to a new job), 调动积极性 (arouse enthusiasm). The sense is of marshalling people or energy toward a purpose.
调度	diào dù	to dispatch; to schedule; a dispatcher — 调 diào (to move, to transfer) + 度 dù (to measure, to manage). To dispatch and coordinate trains, vehicles, crews, or tasks; also the person who does it. 列车调度 (train dispatching), 调度中心 (control center). The work of directing many moving parts.
调和	tiáo hé	to harmonize; to blend
调节	tiáo jié	to regulate; to adjust; to balance — 调 tiáo (to adjust) + 节 jié (to control, to moderate). To keep a level or rhythm steady: 调节温度 (regulate the temperature), 空调 (air conditioning, literally "air regulator"), 自我调节 (self-regulation). The sense is ongoing balance rather than a single change.
调解	tiáo jiě	to mediate; to reconcile; mediation — 调 tiáo (to harmonize) + 解 jiě (to resolve, to untie). To step between two sides and settle their dispute. 调解纠纷 (mediate a dispute), 调解员 (a mediator). The social form of tiáo: bringing people, not flavors, into accord.
调理	tiáo lǐ	to regulate; to tonify
调料	tiáo liào	seasoning
调试	tiáo shì	to debug; to tune up
调停	tiáo tíng	to mediate a dispute
调味	tiáo wèi	to season; to flavor — 调 tiáo (to blend) + 味 wèi (flavor, taste). To season food, to bring its flavors into balance. The seasoning itself is 调料 tiáoliào or 调味品 tiáowèipǐn. The kitchen sense of tiáo: mixing tastes until they are right.
调整	tiáo zhěng	to adjust; to regulate; adjustment — 调 tiáo (to adjust) + 整 zhěng (to put in order). To set something right by careful change: 调整计划 (adjust the plan), 调整心态 (adjust one's attitude), 价格调整 (a price adjustment). The everyday word for fine-tuning anything.
调子	diào zi	tune; melody; tone (of voice or mood) — 调 diào (melody, pitch) + 子 zi (noun suffix). A tune or melody, and figuratively the pitch or mood of speech or writing. 唱调子 (carry a tune), 定调子 (set the tone, decide the direction). The everyday word for the shape of a sound.
订金	dìng jīn	advance payment
东道主	dōng dào zhǔ	host (of a banquet, event, or country) — 东 (east) + 道 (road; way) + 主 (master, host). From the Zuǒzhuàn (5th century BCE): the state of Zhèng, lying east of the Qin army's road, offered to supply and host the Qin troops. "East-road-master" became the word for host.
动功	dòng gōng	moving qigong; forms with coordinated movement and breath — 动 dòng (moving; active) + 功 gōng (practice). Qigong with movement, from slow and gentle forms like 八段锦 Bāduànjǐn (Eight Brocades) and 五禽戏 Wǔqínxì (Five Animals) to more vigorous practices. Movement is synchronized with breath and guided by awareness.
动气	dòng qì	to get riled up; to let anger stir (colloquial) — 动 dòng (to move; to stir) + 气 qì. For qi to be "moved," to let irritation become active anger. Often used in negative advice: 别动气 "don't get worked up." The qi-as-fluid logic is explicit: something causes the qi to shift and agitate.
毒害	dú hài	to poison, harm
毒性	dú xìng	Toxicity, one of the key properties documented for each drug in the Bencao Gangmu. Li distinguished between non-toxic (无毒), slightly toxic (小毒), moderately toxic (有毒), and highly toxic (大毒) substances, with corresponding cautions about dosage, preparation, and contraindications.
独立思考	dú lì sī kǎo	independent thinking; critical thought
读书	dú shū	to read; to study; to attend school — 读 dú + 书 shū (book). Three overlapping senses: (1) the literal act of reading, (2) studying for school or self-improvement, (3) attending an educational institution. 他在北京大学读书 "He's a student at Peking University." Closer to "to be in school" than English "to read books" suggests.
队形	duì xíng	formation, marching shape — 队 (formed group) + 形 (shape). The geometric pattern a group holds while moving: a wedge of soldiers, a flying V of geese, a defensive line on the pitch.
凡夫	fán fū	ordinary being (primary audience)
犯困	fàn kùn	to get drowsy; to feel sleepy — 犯 (to come on, to have a bout of) + 困 (sleepy). A spell of drowsiness creeping over you, often at the wrong time. Colloquial and very common: the after-lunch slump, the late-night struggle to stay awake.
房价	fáng jià	housing price — 房 (house) plus 价 (jià, price). The purchase price of property. A constant topic in contemporary Chinese life, where 房价 in big cities is a daily-news obsession.
房屋	fáng wū	housing, buildings
放慢	fàng màn	to slow down; to ease up the pace — 放 fàng (to release, to let go) + 慢 màn (slow). To deliberately release speed. Used for vehicles, speech, breathing, economic growth. The verb of conscious deceleration.
费功夫	fèi gōng fu	to take effort; to be laborious; to be time-consuming — 费 fèi (to spend; to cost; to consume) + 功夫 (effort). Where 下功夫 emphasizes voluntarily investing effort, 费功夫 emphasizes the effort a task demands of you , the cost side of the equation. A task that is 费功夫 is demanding, painstaking, time-intensive.
丰富	fēng fù	abundant, rich, varied; to enrich — 丰 (lush, plentiful) plus 富 (abundant). As an adjective, rich in variety or quantity. As a verb, to enrich or broaden, as in 丰富知识 (to broaden one's knowledge).
风度	fēng dù	grace; bearing; manner
风格	fēng gé	style; manner; character; the distinctive way something is done — 风 fēng (wind; manner) + 格 gé (standard; grid; manner). The characteristic way someone or something presents itself , artistic style, architectural style, speaking style, leadership style.
风景	fēng jǐng	scenery; landscape; a scenic view — 风 fēng (wind; atmosphere) + 景 jǐng (view; scene; spectacle). The atmospheric scene , the view that catches the eye and stirs the spirit. Not just a geographic description but an aesthetic one: 风景 implies the view is worth seeing.
风水宝地	fēng shuǐ bǎo dì	a feng shui treasure land; an auspiciously sited place — 宝 bǎo (treasure; precious) + 地 dì (land; place). A site so well positioned in the landscape , with mountains, water, orientation, and dragon veins all aligning , that it is considered to bring fortune to whoever inhabits it or is buried there.
风水相生	fēng shuǐ xiāng shēng	wind and water mutually generate — the core principle of feng shui energy management — The operative logic behind 风水: when wind is blocked by mountains and water is present to create a boundary, 气 accumulates rather than dispersing. Wind and water are the agents , qi is what they manage.
风水择地	fēng shuǐ zé dì	geomantic grave siting — fortune for the living through the dead — The location and orientation of a grave (阴宅 yīnzhái, "yin dwelling") directly affects the fortune of the descendants, a doctrine that made grave geomancy (阴宅风水 yīnzhái fēngshuǐ) one of the most important applications of feng shui.
风险	fēng xiǎn	risk; danger
风向	fēng xiàng	wind direction; (figurative) which way the wind blows — 风 fēng (wind) + 向 xiàng (direction). The direction the wind comes from. Used figuratively for the drift of opinion or events: 看风向 "to see which way the wind is blowing," to read the mood before acting.
封口	fēng kǒu	to seal an opening; to keep quiet
否认	fǒu rèn	to deny, to refuse to acknowledge — 否 fǒu (negation; to deny) + 认 rèn. The structural opposite of 承认. To formally refuse the recognition: to deny an accusation, deny an affair, deny a relationship.
夫妇	fū fù	a married couple; husband and wife (formal) — 夫 fū (husband) + 妇 fù (married woman). A married couple, close in meaning to 夫妻 but more formal and bookish. Often attached to a surname: 张氏夫妇 the Zhang couple, 新婚夫妇 newlyweds. Where 夫妻 feels everyday and warm, 夫妇 reads as written or ceremonious.
夫妻	fū qī	husband and wife — the household foundation — The marital relationship orders the household 家 jiā, which is the structural unit between the individual and the state. The husband is obligated to provide and to lead with moral example; the wife is obligated to manage the inner household and to support the family's moral life.
夫妻肺片	fū qī fèi piàn	Husband and Wife Beef Slices
夫人	fū rén	madam; lady; another man's wife (polite) — 夫 fū (man) + 人 rén (person). A respectful term for a married woman, especially the wife of a person of standing: 总统夫人 the first lady, 王夫人 Madam Wang. It honors the woman through her husband's status, so it is formal rather than intimate; you would not call your own wife 夫人 in casual speech.
福气	fú qi	blessed fortune; a life touched by luck — 福 fú + 气 qì (atmosphere; quality; energy). 教书	jiāo shū	to teach (as a profession); to be a teacher — 教 jiāo (to teach) + 书 shū (books). Literally "to teach books," the standard way to say one teaches for a living. Note the first-tone jiāo here, the colloquial verb reading.
阶梯价	jiē tī jià	tiered pricing — "Staircase price": the unit price falls as quantity rises. Asking for a tier table is the cleanest way to learn the factory's real cost curve, and it moves the conversation from "lower the price" to "at what quantity."
接近	jiē jìn	to approach; to be close to; to approximate — 接 jiē (to connect; to receive; to meet) + 近 (near). To connect by closing the gap , a more formal and abstract verb than 靠近.
接连	jiē lián	one after another; in succession; repeatedly — 接 jiē (to join; to connect) + 连 lián (in a series). An adverb meaning that events occur in close, repeated succession, each new one arriving before the last has finished registering. Often reinforced as 接连不断 (continuously, without a break).
街道	jiē dào	street; (urban) subdistrict — 街 (street) + 道 (road, way). The everyday word for a city street. In administrative use it also names the smallest urban division, the 街道办事处 (jiēdào bànshìchù), a neighborhood subdistrict office.
节日祝福	jié rì zhù fú	holiday greetings — A short greeting at Chinese New Year and the Mid-Autumn Festival maintains the relationship between orders. 新年快乐 or 中秋快乐 with a line of thanks is enough.
姐夫	jiě fu	elder sister's husband; brother-in-law — 姐 (elder sister) plus 夫 (husband) names the man married to one's older sister. Chinese kinship marks the relation precisely, distinct from 妹夫 (mèifu), a younger sister's husband.
解除	jiě chú	to remove; to lift; to terminate (a state or obligation) — 解 jiě + 除 chú (to remove; to do away with). To untie and clear away. Used for lifting a binding state or formal tie: 解除警报 = lift an alert; 解除合同 = terminate a contract; 解除武装 = disarm; 解除疲劳 = relieve fatigue. More formal than 解开, it dissolves an arrangement or condition rather than a physical knot.
解答	jiě dá	to answer; to solve and explain; a solution — 解 jiě + 答 dá (to answer; to reply). To unravel and answer. Used for working out and explaining the answer to a question or problem, especially in study: 解答疑问 = answer a question; 标准解答 = the standard solution.
解读	jiě dú	to interpret; to construe; to make sense of — 解 jiě (to untie; to dissolve; to explain) + 读. The standard verb for interpretive reading , of a text, a result, a signal, a person's behaviour. Where 阅读 is comprehending what is plainly there, 解读 is going after what is implicit.
解放	jiě fàng	to liberate; to free; liberation — 解 jiě + 放 fàng (to release; to set free). To untie and let go. The standard word for liberating people from bondage, oppression, or restraint: 解放思想 = free up one's thinking; 解放双手 = free one's hands (from drudgery).
解封	jiě fēng	to unseal; to lift a lockdown
解雇	jiě gù	to dismiss; to fire (an employee) — 解 jiě + 雇 gù (to hire; to employ). To undo the hiring. The formal word for dismissing someone from a job, the legal counterpart to the colloquial 炒鱿鱼 chǎo yóuyú (lit. stir-fry the squid). 解雇 separates the worker from the employment tie, the same "undoing a bind" logic applied to a contract of work.
解决	jiě ju é	to solve; to resolve; to settle — 解 jiě + 决 jué (to decide; to settle). To undo and settle. The all-purpose verb for solving a problem or settling a matter: 解决问题 = solve a problem; 解决困难 = overcome a difficulty; 想办法解决 = find a way to settle it.
解开	jiě kāi	to untie; to undo; to unfasten — 解 jiě + 开 kāi (to open). The most literal 解-word: to untie a knot, undo a button, unfasten a rope. By extension it covers loosening any bind, even a metaphorical one: 解开心结 = untie the knot in one's heart, resolve an inner tension. The picture of separating the bound thing is right on the surface.
解渴	jiě kě	to quench thirst — 解 jiě + 渴 kě (thirst; thirsty). To undo thirst. The everyday verb-object phrase for slaking thirst: 喝水解渴 = drink water to quench thirst; 西瓜很解渴 = watermelon is very thirst-quenching.
解释	jiě shì	to explain; to interpret; an explanation — 解 jiě + 释 shì (to release; to elucidate). To open a thing up and let its sense out. The everyday word for explaining: 解释原因 = explain the reason; 做出解释 = give an explanation. It also covers defending oneself: 解释误会 = clear up a misunderstanding. Where 解决 settles a problem, 解释 makes a meaning plain.
解脱	jiě tuō	liberation; release; to be freed from bondage or constraint — 解 jiě (to untie; to release; to dissolve) + 脱 tuō (to escape; to shed; to free oneself from). In Buddhist context: liberation from 轮回, the shedding of karmic bondage and conditioned existence.
借口	jiè kǒu	excuse, pretext — 借 (to borrow) + 口 (mouth). A "borrowed mouth," words you reach for that are not the true reason. As a noun it is the excuse itself; as a verb, "to use as a pretext."
今世	jīn shì	this life; the present life; this age — 今 jīn (present; now) + 世 shì (life; age). The life one is living now, set against 前世 (a former life) and 来世 (the next): 今生今世 "this very life." Can also mean the present age in a more general sense.
金刚经	Jīng āng jīng	the Diamond Sutra; the foundational text of Chan Buddhism — The full title is 金刚般若波罗蜜经 (Jīngāng bōrě bōluómì jīng), the Diamond Prajñāpāramitā Sutra. The diamond image is the cutting power of 般若 that severs every clinging.
金花	jīn huā	golden flowers — the prized mold colonies in Fu Brick
金黄	jīn huáng	golden yellow
金钱	jīn qián	money (formal/literary) — 金 jīn (gold) + 钱 qián (coin; money). A slightly elevated or literary term for money. Used in formal writing and in philosophical contexts: 金钱不是万能的 "Money is not omnipotent." Compare: 钱 (everyday "money"), 金钱 (money as an abstraction or moral question), 财富 cáifù (wealth/riches).
金石学	jīn shí xu é	Epigraphy — Epigraphy: the study of inscriptions on bronze and stone. The scholarly discipline that underlies serious seal carving: to carve seal script well, one must have studied the ancient bronzes and stone inscriptions from which the script derives.
金芽	jīn yá	golden bud — the premium single-bud grade
紧跟	jǐng ēn	follow closely; keep up with — 紧 (close, tight) + 跟 (gēn, to follow). To follow with no gap. Physical (stay right behind) or figurative (紧跟时代, keep pace with the times).
紧急	jǐn jí	urgent; emergency — 紧 (pressing) + 急 (jí, hurried, anxious). Two words for urgency stacked together, intensifying each other. The register of sirens, evacuations, and red-banner notices: 紧急出口 (emergency exit), 紧急情况 (emergency situation).
紧紧	jǐn jǐn	tightly; closely; firmly — The doubled form intensifies the grip. 紧紧 describes an action done with full, unrelenting tightness, often a hold or an embrace that won't let go.
紧张	jǐn zhāng	nervous; tense; in short supply — 紧 (taut) + 张 (zhāng, to stretch open). A surface pulled tight from both ends. Used for nerves before an exam, the tension in a thriller, and resources stretched thin (时间紧张, time is short).
近代	jìn dài	early modern era (1840–1949)
近景	jìn jǐng	foreground; near scene
近来	jìn lái	recently; lately; in recent times — 近 jìn (near; close; recent) + 来 lái (coming; approaching the present). "What has come near," the recent past approaching the present. A slightly more formal or literary synonym of 最近 zuìjìn. Common in formal speech, writing, and news contexts.
近年来	jìn nián lái	in recent years; over recent years — 近 (recent) + 年 (year) + 来 (coming from , a directional particle marking elapsed duration). Time that has been coming toward the present moment across recent years. A standard phrase in news, academic writing, and formal speech: 近年来，中国的城市化速度加快 (in recent years, China's urbanization has accelerated).
近期	jìn qī	in the near future; recently; in the near term — 近 (near) + 期 qī (a period of time; a term; a scheduled point). A time period that is near , used for both the recent past and the near future depending on context. 近期内 (within the near term); 近期计划 (near-term plans). The period implied is typically weeks to a few months, not years.
近视	jìn shì	nearsighted; myopic — 近 (near) + 视 shì (to see; vision). Seeing only what is near , the clinical and colloquial term for myopia. 近视眼镜 (myopia glasses , corrective lenses); 高度近视 (high myopia, severe nearsightedness).
进城	jìn chéng	to go into town; to go to the city — 进 jìn (to enter) + 城 chéng (city; town). A verb-object phrase: to go into the city. The expression preserves the old sense of the city as a bounded place one literally enters, crossing from outside to inside.
进化	jìn huà	Evolution — Evolution: advance-transformation. 进化论 is Darwin's theory; gamers use 进化 for level-ups, which Darwin might have minded. The neutral scholarly variant is 演化.
进口	jìn kǒu	to import; imports; entrance (of a place) — 进 jìn (to take in) + 口 kǒu (mouth; opening). To bring goods in across a border, the opposite of 出口 chūkǒu (to export). The same word also names a physical "entrance," the opening you go in through.
进香	jìn xiāng	to make a pilgrimage offering incense
经历	jīng lì	to experience; experience; what one has been through — 经 jīng (to pass through; warp threads; to undergo) + 历 lì (to go through). The most common everyday word for living through something and for the sum of what one has lived through. As a verb it takes events as objects: you 经历 a war, a hardship, a change.
精简	jīng jiǎn	to cut down, trim, pare — 精 (refined, essential) + 简 (pared down). To trim away the excess and keep only what is needed, used of staff, budgets, text, and organizations. Sharper and more deliberate than plain 简化.
精神	jīng shén	spirit; vitality; morale; mental state
精神胜利法	jīng shén shèng lì fǎ	The method of spiritual victory , Ah Q psychological mechanism of reframing defeats as victories. Lu Xun intended this as a portrait of a national psychological defense mechanism: the substitution of internal narrative for genuine change.
精神文明	jīng shén wén míng	spiritual civilization; cultural-moral development as a policy domain — A signature term of post-1978 reform-era political vocabulary, paired with 物质文明 (wùzhì wénmíng, material civilization).
净土	jìng tǔ	Pure Land
净土宗	Jìng tǔ Zōng	Pure Land Buddhism — the most widely practiced school in China — Pure Land centers on faith in Amitābha Buddha 阿弥陀佛 Āmítuófó and the aspiration to be reborn in his Western Pure Land 西方极乐世界, from which enlightenment is easy to attain. The primary practice is 念佛 niànfó (recitation of Amitābha's name).
静功	jìng gōng	still-form qigong; seated or standing meditation — 静 jìng (still; quiet) + 功 gōng (practice). Qigong performed without external movement: seated meditation, standing post (站桩 zhàn zhuāng), or lying-down practices. The body is still while breath and attention cultivate interior movement of qi.
九龙	Jiǔ lóng	Kowloon, "Nine Dragons," the peninsula ceded to Britain in 1860. The name comes from a legend that eight mountains in the area represented eight dragons; a Qing emperor passing through added himself as the ninth.
救护车	jiù hù chē	ambulance — 救 (rescue) + 护 (hù, protect) + 车 (chē, vehicle). The "rescue-and-protect vehicle." Hear a siren in a Chinese city and this is the word.
救活	jiù huó	to save someone's life; to revive (the dying) — 救 jiù (to rescue) + 活 huó (alive) as a resultative ending. To rescue with the result of being alive: 活 here marks the outcome, that the rescue succeeded and the person lives. The same pattern builds 养活 yǎnghuó (to support, to keep alive by feeding).
救火	jiù huǒ	to fight a fire — 救 (rescue) + 火 (huǒ, fire). Counterintuitively this means "to put out a fire," not to save the fire; here 救 keeps its Shuōwén sense of 止, halting the disaster. The same logic gives 救灾 (jiùzāi, disaster relief).
救济	jiù jì	to relieve, give relief — 救 (rescue) + 济 (jì, to aid, to cross over). Material relief for the poor or disaster-struck: food, money, shelter. This is 救 in its social-welfare register.
救人	jiù rén	to save / rescue someone — 救 (rescue) + 人 (rén, person). The general act of pulling a person out of danger, whether from water, fire, illness, or ruin.
救援	jiù yuán	to rescue; rescue operation — 救 (rescue) + 援 (yuán, to aid, to reach a hand). A coordinated, often large-scale rescue effort: teams, equipment, logistics. The default word for earthquake and mountain rescue.
救灾	jiù zāi	disaster relief
就近	jiù jìn	nearby; at a convenient location close by — 就 jiù (then; right there; to conform to; to make use of what is present) + 近 (near). Making use of what is nearby , going to the closest available option rather than traveling farther.
就这么简单	jiù zhè me jiǎn dān	it's just that simple; merely this easy — 就 in this usage minimizes or dismisses: it is merely, just, only this , simpler or smaller than one might have expected. 就这样 "just like this, that's all" and 就这么 "it's simply this" are extremely common in casual speech for dismissing complexity or closing a matter.
居民	jū mín	resident; inhabitant (of a place) — 居 jū (to dwell, to reside) + 民. A person defined by where they live rather than by their nationality: the residents of a building, a neighbourhood, a city, a region. The everyday administrative word, as in 居民区 jūmínqū (a residential district) and 城市居民 (urban residents).
君君，臣臣，父父，子子	jūn jūn , chén chén , fù fù , zǐ zǐ	let the ruler rule, minister minister, father father, son son — The Analects 12.11 formula for 正名 in practice, given when Duke Jing of Qi asked Confucius about governance. 开口	kāi kǒu	to open one's mouth — to speak (especially after a silence, or to ask for something)
楷书	kǎi shū	regular script — the foundation script — 楷 kǎi (model; standard) + 书 shū (writing). The standard script , the foundation that every calligrapher must master before attempting other styles. Print fonts are based on regular script. 不会楷书，不学草书 "Don't attempt cursive without mastering regular script" , the traditional maxim.
砍价	kǎn jià	to haggle
看书	kàn shū	to read (a book); to study — 看 (to look at) + 书 (book). The standard way to say "to read" in Mandarin , specifically reading for comprehension or leisure. 读书 dúshū overlaps but emphasizes reading aloud or formal study; 看书 is the unmarked everyday verb for sitting with a book.
考古	kǎo gǔ	archaeology; to study antiquity through remains — 考 kǎo (to examine, to investigate) + 古 gǔ (the ancient). To investigate the past through its physical remains: 考古学 "archaeology (the discipline)," 考古发现 "an archaeological discovery." The verb of unearthing and reading 古迹, the traces the past left in the ground.
靠近	kào jìn	to approach; to draw near; to get close to — 靠 kào (to lean against; to approach; to rely on) + 近 (near). To lean close , the verb of physical approach. 靠近 carries the movement more explicitly than 接近: it often describes the final stage of approach, closing the last stretch of distance.
可能性	kěn éng xìng	possibility; likelihood — 可能 kěnéng (possible) + 性 xìng (-ness). The abstract noun "possibility," built by attaching the -ity suffix to the adjective 可能: 有可能性 "there is a possibility," 很大的可能性 "a strong likelihood." A model case of how 性 turns a quality into a countable, discussable thing.
渴望	kě wàng	to crave; to yearn for
刻苦	kè kǔ	hardworking, painstaking, assiduous — 刻 (kè), to carve or engrave, joins 苦 (kǔ) to describe effort that bites in: study or training pursued through self-denial. A 刻苦 student drills past comfort, the way a blade is sharpened against stone.
空白	kòng bái	blank space; gap
空话	kōng huà	empty talk

空姐	kōng jiě	flight attendant (female) — 空 (sky, air) plus 姐 is the colloquial word for a female flight attendant, literally a "sky sister." It is a clipped, affectionate everyday term beside the formal 空中小姐 (kōngzhōng xiǎojiě).
空气	kōng qì	air; atmosphere (physical) — 空 kōng (empty; void; sky) + 气 qì. The physical substance of the atmosphere, what you breathe. Also used metaphorically for social atmosphere: 房间里的空气很紧张 "the air in the room was tense." The same word covers oxygen and social mood.
空闲	kòng xián	leisure; idle, free

口才	kǒu cái	eloquence; a gift for speaking — 口 kǒu (mouth) + 才 cái (talent). Literally "mouth-talent," the ability to speak well and persuasively: 他很有口才 "he is very eloquent." One of a family of 才 compounds naming a specific gift, alongside 文才 wéncái (literary talent).
口渴	kǒu kě	thirsty (mouth dry)

口头禅	kǒu tóu chán	pet phrase; catchphrase — Mouth-Chan: enlightenment talked rather than lived, generalized into anyone's verbal habit. 他的口头禅是"没问题" means "no problem" is his catchphrase. One of the most useful conversation words about conversation.

口吻	kǒu wěn	manner of speech; implied standpoint (literary) — 口 kǒu (mouth) + 吻 wěn (lips; to kiss, the form of the mouth). More literary than 口气, used in written analysis, narration, and formal commentary.
口音	kǒu yīn	accent; regional way of speaking — 口 kǒu (mouth) + 音 yīn (sound). The sound a particular mouth gives a language, marking where the speaker is from: 南方口音 "a southern accent," 带口音 "to have an accent."

In everyday business usage: productivity in the simpler sense , output per unit of input.

世家	shì jiā	Hereditary Houses — the great families — Thirty chapters on the feudal lords and hereditary houses of the Zhou and after. 世间	shì jiān	the world of sentient beings; this life; among the world — More literary than 世界, similar in range to 人间, but cooler and more doctrinally precise in Buddhist usage. 世界	shì jiè	world; the world — 世 shì (the living age) + 界 jiè (boundary; realm). The world as a whole, both the physical globe and the human sphere within it: 全世界 "the whole world," 世界和平 "world peace," 世界观 "worldview." One of the most common nouns in the language.
世界第一	shì jiè dì yī	first in the world; the world's best — 世界 shìjiè (world) + 第一 dì-yī (first). The top of a global ranking, by extension the finest there is. Used of athletes, products, and achievements that lead the field.

世俗	shì sú	secular; worldly; common; mundane — 世 shì (the human world) + 俗 sú (custom; common). Can describe the secular as a neutral domain or carry a mild note of the common and unrefined.
世袭	shì xí	hereditary; to inherit by succession — 世 shì (generation) + 袭 xí (to inherit; to succeed to). Passed down by birth from one generation to the next: 世袭制度 "the hereditary system," 世袭爵位 "a hereditary title." Marks a right or rank inherited through bloodline rather than earned or appointed.

Both speakers know that someone told this person something , the unknown is who. 谁 occupies the focus position between 是 and the verb phrase. This is one of the most natural everyday uses of 是…的: interrogating the agent of a known past action.

首都	shǒu dū	capital city — 首 shǒu (head, chief) + 都 dū (metropolis, city). The head-city of a country, its seat of government. 中国的首都是北京 "China's capital is Beijing." Note the reading: here 都 takes its first tone dū (city), not the toneless dōu (all).

首先	shǒu xiān	first of all; firstly; in the first place — 首 shǒu (first) + 先 xiān (before, prior). The standard opener for an ordered list of points, often paired later with 其次 qícì (next) and 最后 zuìhòu (finally). Marks the very first item in a sequence of reasons, steps, or arguments.
首相	shǒu xiàng	prime minister (of a modern foreign government) — 首 shǒu (head; chief) + 相 xiàng (minister). The modern title for the head of government in countries such as Britain and Japan: 英国首相 "the British Prime Minister."
首要	shǒu yào	of prime importance; the foremost — 首 shǒu (first) + 要 yào (important). 英武	yīng wǔ	valiant and impressive; heroic in bearing — 英 yīng (heroic, outstanding) + 武 wǔ (martial). Of a person, valiant and impressive in look and manner, the brave, spirited bearing of a hero or a fine soldier: 英武不凡 "valiant and out of the ordinary." A literary, admiring word, often used of a young officer or commander.
用功	yòng ōng	to study hard; to work diligently; to apply oneself — 用 yòng (to use; to deploy) + 功 gōng (effort; merit; achievement). To deploy effort , the literal meaning maps exactly onto the English "to apply oneself." Used particularly of students and academic work, but extends to any disciplined sustained effort.
优质产品	yōu zhì chǎn pǐn	high-quality product; premium product — 优质 yōuzhì (excellent quality , 优 excellent + 质 substance/quality) is the premium tier modifier. 打造优质产品 (to build premium products) is a common mission statement. Contrasts with 劣质产品 (lièzhì chǎnpǐn, inferior-quality products) , counterfeit or substandard goods.
有道理	yǒu dào lǐ	to make sense; to be reasonable; that's a fair point — 有 yǒu (to have; there is) + 道理 dàolǐ (reason; logic). "To have reason," to possess the quality of making sense. One of the most common spoken responses to an argument you find convincing, used the way "fair point," "that makes sense," and "good point" function in English.
有害	yǒu hài	harmful, noxious — 有 (to have) + 害 (harm): possessing the capacity to wound. Its antonym 无害 (wúhài) marks a thing as harmless. Frequent on labels and in science writing.
有空	yǒu kòng	to have free time
有空再联系	yǒu kòng zài lián xì	"Keep in touch when you have time — "Keep in touch when you have time." Slightly more open-ended than 改天再聊. Suggests that contact will resume when both schedules permit. Polite and standard.
有希望	yǒu xī wàng	to have hope; to be promising — "There is hope," used of a situation that may yet turn out well: 病人还有希望 ("the patient still has hope"). It can also mean "promising" of a person or prospect: 这孩子很有希望 ("this child is very promising"). The negative 没有希望 ("there's no hope") and the verb 失望 ("to be let down") sit on the other side.
有意思	yǒu yì si	interesting; fun; entertaining; appealing — Lit. "has meaning/appeal." The go-to word for "interesting" in casual spoken Chinese. Used for books, films, people, topics, situations , anything with engaging appeal.
渔夫	yú fū	fisherman — 渔 yú (to fish) + 夫 fū (man). A man who makes his living by fishing. Like 农夫 it has a timeless, story-book ring, and it features in the famous proverb about the fisherman who profits while the snipe and the clam are locked in struggle.
渔民	yú mín	fishermen; Mazu's core devotees
羽化升天	yǔ huà shēng tiān	ascension to Heaven — her death and deification — In 987 CE, at age twenty-seven or twenty-eight, Lin Mo climbed Meizhou Island's highest point. Witnesses reported hearing celestial music (仙乐 xiānyuè) and seeing her surrounded by colored clouds as she ascended into the sky.
语气	yǔ qì	tone of voice; grammatical mood; modal register — 语 yǔ (language; speech) + 气 qì. The qi of language, how something is said rather than what. In grammatical analysis, 语气 = mood (indicative, imperative, interrogative).
语气词	yǔ qì cí	modal particle
语义	yǔ yì	semantic meaning (linguistics)
元朝	Yuán cháo	Yuan dynasty
元气	yuán qì	primordial qi; vitality; the reserves of life force — 元 yuán (origin; primary; first) + 气 qì. In cosmology: the undifferentiated qi before heaven and earth separated, the primordial substrate of existence. In medicine: the constitutional qi inherited from one's parents, stored in the kidneys, spent by illness and restored by rest.
元首	yuán shǒu	head of state
原形	yuán xíng	original form; true shape (often the one revealed) — 原 yuán (original) + 形 xíng. The original form a thing had before it was disguised or altered. The vivid idiom 原形毕露 yuánxíng bìlù means "the true shape is fully exposed," said when a deception finally collapses and someone is seen for what they really are.
袁世凯	Yuán Shì kǎi	military strongman who hijacked the republic
远古	yuǎng ǔ	remote antiquity; the distant ancient past — 远 yuǎn (far, remote) + 古 gǔ (ancient). The very distant past, the dim and often legendary beginnings before clear historical record: 远古时代 "remote antiquity," 远古传说 "ancient myths and legends." Reaches further back than 古代, toward the edge of memory.
愿望	yuàn wàng	wish; aspiration
阅历	yu è lì	life experience; seasoned knowledge of the world — 阅 yuè (to read; to review; to inspect) + 历 lì (what one has gone through). The accumulated worldly experience a person builds up over the years: the breadth of life seen and understood, not just events lived but their lessons absorbed.
运气	yùn qi	luck; fortune (good or bad) — 运 yùn (fortune in motion) + 气 qì (vital energy; breath; atmosphere). Fortune as a flow of qi: luck circulates rather than sitting still. 好运气 = good luck; 坏运气 = bad luck. 运气好 = to be lucky; 靠运气 = to rely on luck.
灾害	zāi hài	disaster, calamity
灶神	Zào shén	the Kitchen God — divine household reporter — The Kitchen God (also 灶王 Zàowáng or 灶君 Zàojūn) resides in the kitchen and observes the family's behavior throughout the year. On the 23rd of the 12th lunar month (one week before the new year) he ascends to Heaven to make his annual report to the Jade Emperor.
灶王爷	zào wáng yé	The Kitchen God , the divine household official who observes the family year-round and reports to the Jade Emperor before the Spring Festival. Propitiated with sweet foods before his image is burned and he ascends to make his report. A new image is installed when he returns on New Year's Eve.
展望	zhǎn wàng	to look ahead; outlook
张望	zhāng wàng	to look around; to peer about — 张 zhāng (to open, to spread) + 望 wàng (to look). To look around searchingly, the eyes opened wide and turning in every direction: 四处张望 "to look all around." It carries a note of uncertainty or expectation, as of someone scanning a crowd for a face.
长城内外	cháng chéng nèi wài	inside and outside the Great Wall; the agricultural vs. steppe world — A geographic and cultural formula that names the two worlds the wall divided: the settled, grain-farming Han civilization inside, and the pastoral, nomadic cultures of the steppe outside. 内外 encodes not just geography but an entire worldview about civilization and its boundaries.
涨价	zhǎng jià	to raise the price
丈夫	zhàng fu	husband — 丈 zhàng (an honorific for an adult man) + 夫 fū (man). The ordinary, neutral word for one's husband, the man one is married to. In older usage 大丈夫 dà zhàngfu also means a true man, a man of character, but on its own 丈夫 simply names the spouse.
这倒未必	zhè dào wèi bì	"That's not necessarily so — "That's not necessarily so." A more literary pushback, often used in discussions or debates. 倒 marks the contrary tone; 未必 means "not necessarily." Slightly considered, signals careful disagreement.
真不容易	zhēn bù róng yì	that's really not easy; well done — Standard expression of admiration or sympathy for someone who has overcome difficulty: a graduation, a long project, raising children. The 真 makes it heartfelt rather than perfunctory.
真空保温	zhēn kōng bǎo wēn	vacuum insulation — Double-wall insulation: the air between liner and shell is pumped out, 抽真空 (chōu zhēnkōng). 保温 is keeping warm, 保冷 keeping cold; 保温测试 is the thermal retention test, and 保温效果 the result.
阵脚	zhèn jiǎo	battle-line foundation; one's footing in a contest — 阵 zhèn (battle formation) + 脚. The foot of the battle line , the structural integrity of a position. The common idiom 乱了阵脚 luàn le zhènjiǎo "to lose one's footing" describes the moment a debater, athlete, or general is rattled and the formation collapses.
拯救	zhěng jiù	to save, to deliver — 拯 (zhěng, to lift up out of) + 救 (rescue). A weightier, more literary "save," used for deliverance on a grand or moral scale: saving a soul, a people, a planet.
正念	zhèng niàn	right mindfulness
正气	zhèng qì	upright qi; righteous energy; moral integrity — 正 zhèng (upright; correct; proper) + 气 qì. In TCM: the body's defensive qi that resists pathogenic invasion. In moral discourse: the upright character and courage that resists corruption and injustice.
正义	zhèng yì	justice; righteousness; the just cause — 正 zhèng (correct; upright; proper , the character shows a foot 止 walking toward a target 一, straight on) + 义 yì (righteousness; moral rightness). Together: righteousness that is properly aligned , justice as it should be, not merely as claimed. 正义感 zhèngyìgǎn = a sense of justice.
知道了	zhī dào le	got it; understood; okay — The everyday acknowledgement that you have received and registered information or an instruction. Extremely common as a one-word reply. Beware the tone: a flat or clipped 知道了 can read as impatient, like "yeah, I know already." 好的 (okay) or 明白了 (understood) are gentler alternatives.
知识产权	zhī shi chǎn quán	intellectual property
指示代词	zhǐ shì dài cí	demonstrative pronoun (这/那/哪)
制品	zhì pǐn	manufactured article; processed product
致富	zhì fù	to get rich; to make a fortune — 致 (to bring about, to arrive at) plus 富 (wealth). To become wealthy, especially through one's own effort. 勤劳致富 (qínláo zhìfù), getting rich through diligence, is a stock phrase.
中华民国	Zhōng huá Míng uó	Republic of China
重要性	zhòng yào xìng	importance
周朝	Zhōu Cháo	the Zhou dynasty
竹简	zhú jiǎn	bamboo writing slip
注脚	zhù jiǎo	footnote; gloss; annotation — 注 zhù (to annotate; to gloss) + 脚. Annotation at the foot of the page. A more elegant alternative to the modern compound 脚注 jiǎozhù (which has the same components in reverse order).
祝福	zhù fú	to bless; to wish someone well; best wishes — 祝 zhù (to wish; to pray for) + 福 fú (blessing). To send blessings to someone, as a verb or noun. 送上我的祝福 = "I send you my best wishes." Standard in holiday messages, weddings, and farewells.
抓紧	zhuā jǐn	grasp firmly; make the most of (time) — 抓 (zhuā, to grab) + 紧 (tight). To grab and hold with no slack. Literally to grip something hard, figuratively to seize time and not waste it: 抓紧时间 (zhuājǐn shíjiān), make the most of the time.
转化	zhuǎn huà	to transform; to convert; to change from one form into another — 转 zhuǎn (to turn; to shift) + 化 huà (to transform). 转化 names a conversion across a boundary: one form, type, or state turning into another. It is the standard word in science, philosophy, and economics for systematic conversion, the conditions under which A becomes B.
转口港	zhuǎn kǒu gǎng	Entrepôt , a port where goods are imported, stored, and re-exported without significant processing. Hong Kong's entire colonial economic logic was entrepôt trade, and it retains this function today as a major transshipment hub alongside its role as a financial center.
转世	zhuǎn shì	reincarnation; transmigration; rebirth into a new form — 转 zhuǎn (to turn; to shift; to transmit) + 世 shì (lifetime; generation; world). The specific act of being reborn into a new form, the individual instance of the larger cycle of 轮回.
篆书	zhuàn shū	seal script
桌上有书	zhuō shàng yǒu shū	The existential pattern: place + 有 + thing — The canonical existential 有 structure. Place word or place phrase comes first, followed by 有, followed by the indefinite thing being asserted to exist. The place functions as the topic , the frame within which existence is being asserted.
紫禁城	Zǐ jìn chéng	Forbidden City
自传	zì zhuàn	autobiography — 自 zì (self) + 传 zhuàn (a life record). A biography written by its own subject, the story of a life told in the first person: 写自传 "to write an autobiography." Like 传记, it takes the zhuàn reading.
自古	zì gǔ	since ancient times
自然灾害	zì rán zāi hài	natural disaster
自首	zì shǒu	to turn oneself in; to confess to the authorities — 自 zì (oneself) + 首 shǒu (to bow the head, to confess). To give oneself up to the law of one's own accord, before being caught. A legal term: voluntary surrender that often earns a lighter sentence. The head bows itself before justice.
走道	zǒu dào	corridor; aisle; walkway — 走 zǒu (walk; go) + 道 (way; path). The path you walk: a corridor between rooms, an aisle on a plane or train, a narrow walkway between structures. Practical and architectural: the space designated for movement through an enclosed or constrained area.
祖父	zǔ fù	paternal grandfather (formal) — 祖 zǔ (ancestor, forebear) + 父 fù (father). The father's father, in formal register. Spoken Mandarin uses 爷爷 yéye for the same man, the way 爸爸 stands in for 父亲.
最近	zuì jìn	recently; lately; nearest
左传	Zuǒ Zhuàn	the Zuo Commentary — the primary narrative source for the Spring and Autumn period — Traditionally attributed to Zuo Qiuming (左丘明 Zuǒ Qiūmíng) and covering 722–468 BCE, the 左传 is the great narrative expansion of the Chūnqiū chronicle.
左道旁门	zuǒ dào páng mén	heterodox sects and methods; unorthodox ways — 左道 (the left way, the deviant path) + 旁门 (a side gate, a door off to the side). 道 is the orthodox central path; 左道 is the path that veers left of it. 旁门 extends the spatial metaphor , not the main gate, but a side entrance used by those who cannot or will not use the front.
作风	zuò fēng	work style; conduct
作品	zuò pǐn	a work (of art, literature, music) — 作 zuò (to compose) + 品 pǐn (article; item). A finished creative work: 文学作品 "a literary work," 这是他最好的作品 "this is his finest work."
