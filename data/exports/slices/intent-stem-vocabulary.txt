//角落書屋/Slices/STEM Vocabulary
方	fāng	square, direction, method — One glyph that runs from the literal square to the cardinal directions, to the place a thing sits, to the method by which it is done. The geometry of orientation and approach.
化	huà	to change, to transform, to melt — The character of transformation itself: melting, digesting, civilizing, and the productive -ize suffix that builds half of modern technical Chinese.
角	jiǎo	corner, horn, angle — Corner, horn, angle: the character that opens this site's name (角落書屋).
零	líng	zero; raindrops, the fractional remainder, and the late invention of nothing — Zero began as falling raindrops and the leftover odd remainder, taking on the meaning of mathematical nothing only late, the placeholder that Chinese numerals long managed without.
生	shēng	life, birth, raw, student — A sprouting plant: life emerging from the earth. The most generative radical in Chinese.
十	shí	ten; complete; cross — 十 is written as a cross (a vertical stroke cutting through a horizontal one), the point where all four cardinal directions meet, making ten the numeral of total completion.
物	wù	thing, object, matter, being — An ox under a phonetic, once the word for varicolored cattle, that broadened into the whole world of things: every object, all matter, the myriad creatures, and even a person as a figure.
星	xīng	star; celestial body; spark — Born of the sun radical and a sprout, 星 names the stars, the planets, the week, movie fame, and the spark that starts prairie fires.
形	xíng	shape, form, to appear, to compare — The character for visible form: shape and figure, the body as form, the act of taking shape and being described, and the lay of a situation. A pictophonetic compound built on the pattern-strokes radical 彡.
出神入化	chū shén rù huà	to leave the mundane and enter the magical: superb; consummate; masterly — 出 chū = to leave, 神 shén = the divine, 入 rù = to enter, 化 huà = the transformed, transcendent state.
大大方方	dà dà fāng fāng	generous and unaffected; poised and at ease in manner — The reduplicated form of 大方 dàfang (generous; gracious; not stingy or awkward). Said in praise of someone whose bearing is relaxed and dignified rather than petty or self-conscious.
待人接物	dài rén jiē wù	to treat people and handle affairs: the way one conducts oneself with others — Lit: treat-people-engage-things. The whole manner of dealing with others and managing matters; said in praise of someone whose conduct is gracious and well judged.
地大物博	dì dà wù bó	vast land, abundant resources — used of China's territory — Lit: land-great-things-abundant. The 地 here is the full noun "land, earth" (dì, 4th tone), not the adverbial particle de. Appears frequently in descriptions of China's natural endowment. A reminder: 地 has a full semantic life beyond its particle function.
凋零	diāo líng	"to wither and fall" — to fade and drop away; (of people) to pass away — 凋 (to wither) + 零 (to fall in drops). Strictly a two-character word rather than a four-character chengyu, but a fixed literary expression worth knowing.
独占鳌头	dú zhàn áo tóu	to come in first; to stand at the apex — Lit: to stand alone on the giant turtle's head. 鳌 áo is the mythical sea turtle whose head is the highest point, corresponding to the top of the imperial examination standings. Metaphor: to take first place.
感激涕零	gǎn jī tì líng	"so grateful the tears fall" — moved to tears by gratitude; deeply thankful — 感激 (deep gratitude) + 涕 (tears) + 零 (to fall in drops). Here 零 returns to its literal verb sense, "to fall in drops," applied not to rain but to tears: gratitude so overwhelming that the tears come down. The phrase describes being moved to weeping by another's kindness or favor.
勾心斗角	gōu xīn dòu jiǎo	to scheme and intrigue against each other — hooks, hearts, and clashing horns — Originally described the complex interlocking eaves of palace architecture: each bracket hooking into another, each corner clashing. Now means: workplace backstabbing, political maneuvering, scheming rivalry.
花甲之年	huā jiǎ zhī nián	the year of the flowery cycle — age sixty
化险为夷	huà xiǎn wéi yí	to turn danger into safety: to avert a crisis; to come through unscathed — 化 huà = to transform, 险 xiǎn = danger, peril, 为 wéi = into, 夷 yí = level ground, flatness, safety. Lit: "transform the perilous into the level." Describes turning a dangerous, threatening situation into a safe one, often through skill, calm, or good fortune. 在关键时刻，他冷静地化险为夷。
化整为零	huà zhěng wéi líng	"turn the whole into pieces" — to break something large into small scattered parts — 化 (to transform) + 整 (the whole) + 为 (into) + 零 (the scattered pieces). To disperse one large thing into many small ones: a big force split into small units that move unseen, a large task divided into manageable bits, a lump sum paid in installments.
零零星星	líng líng xīng xīng	"scattered like stray stars" — sparse and scattered; bits and pieces here and there — A reduplication of 零星 (scattered, sporadic), itself 零 (scattered drops) plus 星 (stars, scattered points of light).
六十而耳顺	liù shí ér ěr shùn	at sixty, the ear follows — one can hear without resistance
民以食为天	mín yǐ shí wéi tiān	to the people, food is heaven: nothing matters more than feeding the people — Literally "the people take food as heaven." An ancient maxim of statecraft: the first and highest concern of any ruler is that the people have enough to eat. Still quoted constantly in everyday life, often half-jokingly, to defend the seriousness of a good meal.
披星戴月	pī xīng dài yu è	cloaked in stars, capped with the moon — toiling from before dawn until after dark — To wear the stars as a cape and the moon as a hat is to be outdoors working while they are up. Said admiringly of farmers, delivery riders, and anyone on the early-and-late grind: 他披星戴月地工作，就为了孩子上大学。He works from starlight to moonlight, all so his child can attend university.
七上八下	qī shàng bā xià	"seven up, eight down" — nervous and unsettled; anxious about the outcome — Seven and eight are the neighbors of ten who cannot agree on direction. Seven goes up, eight goes down: the image is of something that cannot find rest, oscillating without resolution. The idiom describes the feeling of anxiety before a result, the inner turbulence of waiting without knowing.
起死回生	qǐ sǐ huí shēng	to bring back from the dead — to revive a desperate situation — Lit: raise-dead-return-life. Originally medical (miraculous cures); now used for companies, projects, and relationships revived against all odds.
千变万化	qiān biàn wàn huà	a thousand changes, ten thousand transformations: endlessly changing; protean — 千 qiān = thousand, 万 wàn = ten thousand, the two largest round numbers paired for the sense of "countless." Lit: "a thousand changes and ten thousand transformations." Describes anything in ceaseless, dazzling flux: clouds, markets, a magician's tricks, the moves of a skilled fighter. 舞台上的灯光千变万化。
千方百计	qiān fāng bǎi jì	a thousand methods and a hundred schemes: by every conceivable means — Lit: thousand-methods-hundred-plans. Stronger than 想方设法, often with a hint that the effort is dogged or even scheming. 千方百计地想办法 "to try every trick in the book."
潜移默化	qián yí mò huà	to shift unseen and transform in silence: to influence imperceptibly over time — 潜 qián = hidden, submerged, 移 yí = to shift, 默 mò = silent, 化 huà = to transform. Lit: "shifting while submerged, transforming in silence." Describes the slow, unnoticed way an environment, a teacher, or a habit gradually changes a person's character or thinking without any single dramatic moment.
生生不息	shēng shēng bù xī	life generating life without ceasing — the endless renewal of existence — A Daoist cosmological phrase: life perpetually generating more life. Used in nature writing, philosophical prose, and speeches about cultural continuity.
生死与共	shēng sǐ yǔ gòng	to share life and death together — a bond through the deepest adversity — Lit: life-death-with-together. Used for close comrades, soldiers, and couples who have endured extreme hardship. The deepest statement of loyalty.
十年树木，百年树人	shí nián shù mù , bǎi nián shù rén	"ten years to grow a tree, a hundred years to cultivate a person" — education takes far longer than any other cultivation — From the classical text 管子 Guǎnzi. Growing a tree requires ten years of patience: planting, watering, waiting. Cultivating a human being , educating, civilizing, forming character , requires ten times longer. The century-scale horizon is a deliberate rebuke to impatience.
十全十美	shí quán shí měi	perfect in every way — ten-complete and ten-beautiful, with nothing lacking — The most-used idiom of perfection in Chinese. The doubling of 十 means completeness is measured twice , in wholeness and in beauty , so that neither a technical nor an aesthetic standard is left unmet.
四面八方	sì miàn bā fāng	"four faces, eight directions" — from every direction; from everywhere — The most common chengyu using 方. 四面 (the four faces of a square; the four sides) + 八方 (the eight directions). Used for crowds, news, demands, attacks , anything converging from every direction. 来自四面八方的客人 (guests from all over).
天涯海角	tiān yá hǎi jiǎo	the ends of the earth — sky-edge, sea-corner — Lit: sky-edge sea-corner. The farthest, most remote reaches of the world. High frequency in romantic poetry and pop songs about separation and longing.
物换星移	wù huàn xīng yí	things change, stars shift — time passes and the world turns over — From Wang Bo's Tang-dynasty Pavilion of Prince Teng poem: 物换星移几度秋, "things change, stars shift, how many autumns now?" The stars' slow precession as the clock of dynasties. Used for nostalgia at scale: returning to a transformed hometown, rereading an old diary.
物美价廉	wù měi jià lián	the goods fine and the price cheap: excellent value for money — The four-character verdict every shopper hopes to pronounce: good quality and a low price at once. Standard praise in advertising and casual recommendation alike.
物以类聚	wù yǐ lèi jù	things gather by kind: like attracts like, birds of a feather flock together — Lit: things-by-kind-gather. From the idea that creatures of a sort come together; often paired with 人以群分 "people divide into groups," and used, sometimes pointedly, of who keeps company with whom.
喜形于色	xǐ xíng yú sè	joy takes shape in the face: visibly delighted — Lit: joy 喜 takes form 形 upon 于 the complexion 色. Happiness that cannot be hidden and shows plainly on the face. Here 形 is verbal, "to take shape, to appear," a clear window onto the verb sense of the character.
想方设法	xiǎng fāng shè fǎ	to think of squares and devise methods: to try every possible means — Lit: think-method-set-up-method. To rack one's brains for a way through, doing whatever it takes. 方 and 法 here both carry the "method" sense, doubled for emphasis.
星罗棋布	xīng luó qí bù	arrayed like stars, spread like chess pieces — dotted everywhere — 星罗 (netted like stars) + 棋布 (laid out like go stones). The standard descriptor for anything scattered densely across a landscape: lakes on a delta, islands in an archipelago, convenience stores in Shanghai. The two images, night sky and go board, are both grids the classical eye knew intimately.
星星之火，可以燎原	xīng xing zhī huǒ , kě yǐ liáo yuán	a single spark can set the prairie ablaze — 星星 here is the spark sense of 星. The image of fire racing across a plain is ancient (the Book of Documents already warns of fire on the grassland), but the phrase's modern fame comes from Mao Zedong's 1930 essay taking it as a title, arguing a small revolutionary force could ignite the country.
形单影只	xíng dān yǐng zhī	a lone form, a single shadow: solitary and without companions — Lit: the form is alone, the shadow is single. The mirror image of 形影不离: here there is only one form and its one shadow, no one alongside. A literary, somewhat melancholy phrase for a person who is utterly on their own.
形形色色	xíng xíng sè sè	all shapes and colors: of every kind, all sorts — Lit: shape-shape, color-color. The reduplication piles up variety: people, goods, schemes, opinions of every imaginable kind. 形形色色的人 = all sorts of people. Often carries a faint edge of "a mixed bag," not all of it admirable.
形影不离	xíng yǐng bù lí	form and shadow never part: inseparable — Lit: form and shadow do not separate. A shadow follows its form everywhere; two people who are 形影不离 are constant companions, always together. Warm and affectionate, used for close friends, devoted couples, or a child who will not leave a parent's side.
崭露头角	zhǎn lù tóu jiǎo	to show one's emerging talent — horns beginning to protrude — The image is a young animal whose horns are just breaking through the hide. Used for a young talent beginning to stand out. Very common in Chinese writing about emerging professionals or artists.
阿是穴	ā shì xu é	Ashi points — Ashi points: tender spots with no fixed location, needled wherever the patient says "Ah, yes, that is it!" when pressed. Not on any meridian map, but recognized in classical texts as clinically valid.
艾灸	ài jiǔ	Moxibustion , burning dried mugwort at or near acupoints to warm and tonify. Paired with needling in the term 针灸 (zhēnjiǔ), which names the two techniques together. Used specifically for cold-type and deficiency-type conditions where warming stimulation is indicated.
办法	bàn fǎ	a way; a means; a solution — 办 bàn (to handle; to manage) + 法 fǎ (way). The practical way to get a concrete thing done or to solve a problem at hand: 想办法 "to think of a way," 没办法 "there's nothing to be done."
本草	běn cǎo	Materia medica — Materia medica: the traditional term for the pharmacological literature and its subject matter.
本草纲目	běn cǎo gāng mù	the materia medica
边角料	biān jiǎo liào	offcuts; scraps; odds and ends
变化	biàn huà	change; transformation; variation — 变 biàn (to change) + 化 huà (to transform; to dissolve into something new). The most general and most frequent word for change in modern Mandarin. Where 变 alone is the verb of transformation, 变化 is the noun: change as a thing that happens, can be observed, measured, discussed.
病毒	bìng dú	virus — 病 + 毒 (dú, poison): a "sickness poison," the standard word for a biological virus and, by extension, a computer virus. The same term covers the flu and the malware on your hard drive.
产生	chǎn shēng	to produce; to give rise to; to emerge — 产 chǎn (to produce; output) + 生 shēng (arise). For abstract and concrete creation , ideas, problems, emotions, consequences arising. 产生影响 "to have an effect" · 产生误会 "to give rise to a misunderstanding."
成长	chéng zhǎng	to grow up; to mature; personal development — 成 chéng (to become; to complete) + 长 zhǎng (to grow). The compound for the full arc of development: physical, psychological, and social maturation. 成长故事 = coming-of-age story. 成长经历 = formative experiences. Also used for adult development: 这段经历让我很成长 "this experience helped me grow a lot."
宠物	chǒng wù	pet
出生	chū shēng	to be born; birth — 出 chū (emerge) + 生 shēng (life; to be born; raw). Emergence into life: the original threshold crossing. 出生 is used for persons: 他在北京出生 (he was born in Beijing). The date is 出生日期 chūshēng rìqī (date of birth).
诞生	dàn shēng	to be born (grand things)
得气	dé qì	Arrival of qi, the sensation confirming correct needling — Arrival of qi, the sensation confirming correct needling: heaviness, distension, or a spreading warmth at the needled point. Classical and modern acupuncturists both treat déqì as the essential marker of effective treatment.
地方	dì fang	place; spot; (also) local, regional — 地 dì (earth; ground) + 方 fang. The all-purpose word for "place," from a physical spot to an abstract point in an argument (这个地方我不同意 "on this point I disagree"). With full tone, 地方 dìfāng means "local" as opposed to "central": 地方政府 local government.
地形	dì xíng	terrain; topography; the shape of the land — 地 dì (ground; land) + 形 xíng. The shape of the land: terrain, topography, the rise and fall of the ground. 地形复杂 = the terrain is complex; 地形图 = a topographic map. The most literal of the situation-words, where the shape being read is the physical landscape itself.
地支	dì zhī	Earthly Branches
东方	dōng fāng	the east; the Orient — 东 dōng (east) + 方 fāng (direction). The eastern direction, and by extension "the East" as a cultural sphere. Its pair is 西方 xīfāng, the West. The four directions all take 方: 南方 the south, 北方 the north.
东西南北	dōng xī nán běi	the four directions
冬至	dōng zhì	Winter Solstice — the turning of yin into yang — Winter Solstice, the most important of the twenty-four solar terms, celebrated as a major festival throughout Chinese history. Marks the shortest day and the nadir of the yin/yang cycle, from which point yang begins its return. Dumplings are eaten in the north; tangyuan in the south.
动物	dòng wù	animal — 动 dòng (to move) + 物 wù (thing). The "moving things," the animals, set against 植物 the "planted things," the plants. 动物园 dòngwùyuán is the zoo, the "animal garden."
毒性	dú xìng	Toxicity, one of the key properties documented for each drug in the Bencao Gangmu. Li distinguished between non-toxic (无毒), slightly toxic (小毒), moderately toxic (有毒), and highly toxic (大毒) substances, with corresponding cautions about dosage, preparation, and contraindications.
对方	duì fāng	the other party; the opposite side — 对 duì (facing; opposite) + 方 fāng (party). The other side in a conversation, negotiation, contest, or relationship. Its pairs are 双方 shuāngfāng (both parties) and 我方 wǒfāng (our side).
二十八宿	èr shí bā xiù	The 28 Lunar Mansions — The 28 Lunar Mansions: the primary coordinate system of Chinese astronomy, dividing the ecliptic and celestial equator into 28 unequal stations corresponding to the moon's daily progress around the sky.
方便	fāng biàn	convenient; to make things easy for; (euphemism) to use the toilet — 方 fāng + 便 biàn (convenient; suitable). Easy and convenient, of a time, a place, or an arrangement. 方便面 fāngbiànmiàn is instant noodles, literally "convenient noodles." As a polite euphemism it also means to relieve oneself.
方程	fāng chéng	Equation — Equation. The term is two thousand years old, taken from the chapter of the Nine Chapters that solved systems of linear equations with counting rods. 解方程 (jiě fāngchéng) is to solve an equation.
方法	fāng fǎ	method; means; way of doing something — 方 fāng (square; direction) + 法 fǎ (way). A method or procedure considered in the abstract, the systematic way to approach a task: 学习方法 "study methods," 科学方法 "the scientific method."
方面	fāng miàn	aspect; side; area; regard — 方 (direction; side) + 面 (face; surface). A particular "side" or "face" of a topic , one of the aspects of a multi-sided issue. Extremely common in academic, journalistic, and analytical Chinese. 在这方面 (in this regard), 各个方面 (all aspects), and 经济方面 (in terms of economics) are standard expressions.
方式	fāng shì	way; manner; mode; style — 方 fāng (way) + 式 shì (form; pattern). The manner or mode in which something is done, broader and more abstract than 方法: 生活方式 lifestyle, 付款方式 payment method.
分子	fēn zǐ	Molecule — Molecule: divided unit. The same word, differently stressed, means "member of a group" in political vocabulary (知识分子, intellectuals), a coincidence of compounding worth knowing before it confuses.
干支	gān zhī	Stems and Branches — Stems and Branches: the shorthand for the combined system of Ten Heavenly Stems and Twelve Earthly Branches used to name years, months, days, and two-hour periods.
高铁	gāo tiě	high-speed rail — Trains are booked on the official 12306 app or site (it has an English version) or through travel apps, and your passport is the ticket: it is checked at the station entrance and at the gate. 动车 (dòngchē) are slightly slower bullet trains. Arrive early; large stations have security checks.
工程	gōng chéng	engineering
工程师	gōng chéng shī	engineer — 工程 gōngchéng (engineering; project , lit. "work + process") + 师 shī (master; teacher; professional). Engineer , one of the most respected professional titles in China, associated with the nation's modernization drive. 工程 alone means a large project or undertaking: 三峡工程 = Three Gorges Project.
公式	gōng shì	Formula — Formula: "public pattern," a form that holds generally. 套公式 (tào gōngshì, "slot into the formula") is the study-hall phrase for plug-and-chug problem solving.
光速	guāng sù	The speed of light — The speed of light. Colloquially any blinding speed: 光速下班 is leaving work the second the clock strikes, a usage every office knows.
海角	hǎi jiǎo	cape; headland; sea-corner
航天	háng tiān	Spaceflight — Spaceflight: "sailing the heavens," on the model of 航海 (seafaring). 航天员 is the official word for China's astronauts; the industry is 航天工程, space engineering.
红十字	Hóng Shí zì	the Red Cross — 红 hóng (red) + 十字 shízì (cross). The International Committee of the Red Cross, established 1863. The Chinese translation renders the emblem directly: the red 十-shaped cross. The full institutional name is 红十字国际委员会 Hóng Shízì Guójì Wěiyuánhuì.
花甲	huā jiǎ	60th birthday; one full cycle
化为	huà wéi	to turn into; to become; to be reduced to — 化 huà (to transform) + 为 wéi (to be; to become), a literary verb of becoming. 化为 marks a complete turning of one thing into another, often dramatic and frequently with a sense of dissolution or loss.
化学	huà xu é	Chemistry — Chemistry: transformation-study. 化学反应 is a chemical reaction; 化学式 (huàxuéshì) is a chemical formula, read left to right like grammar.
黄历	huáng lì	The almanac — The almanac: literally "yellow calendar," named for the imperial yellow of the official calendar. An annual publication (now also a mobile app feature) that gives day-by-day auspiciousness ratings based on the traditional calendrical system.
火星	huǒ xīng	Mars; a spark — The fire star twice over: the red planet of the five-phase planet names, and the everyday spark from a fire or a grinder's wheel. Internet slang added a third life: a 火星人 (Martian) is someone hopelessly behind on the news.
货物	huò wù	goods; cargo; merchandise — 货 huò (goods; commodity) + 物 wù (thing). Goods as freight or stock in trade, the things that move through commerce: 运送货物 "to transport goods," 货物清单 "a manifest of cargo."
基因	jī yīn	Gene — Gene: sounds like the English, means "basic cause." 转基因 (zhuǎnjīyīn, transgenic/GMO) is the charged supermarket word built on it.
几何	jǐ hé	Geometry — Geometry. Classical Chinese for "how much?", repurposed by Xu Guangqi and Matteo Ricci in their 1607 translation of Euclid. Both senses live on: one in the classroom, one in Cao Cao's poetry.
计算	jì suàn	To calculate, to compute — To calculate, to compute. The formal verb behind 计算机 (jìsuànjī, computer, "calculating machine") and 计算器 (jìsuànqì, calculator). Colloquial speech often just says 算 (suàn).
甲乙丙丁	jiǎ yǐ bǐng dīng	Stems 1–4 (Wood &amp; Fire)
甲子	jiǎ zǐ	the 60-year cycle; first combination
建筑	jiàn zhù	Architecture, building — Architecture, building. 建筑师 (jiànzhùshī) is an architect, distinct from the 工程师 who makes the building stand up. As a verb, 建 alone means to build: 建一座桥, build a bridge.
角度	jiǎo dù	angle; perspective
角落	jiǎo luò	corner; nook; out-of-the-way place — 角 jiǎo (corner) + 落 luò (to settle, to land). The place where things come to rest in the corner, a nook. Both literally (a room's corner) and figuratively (a quiet, tucked-away place in the world). The name of this site: 角落書屋.
角色	ju é sè	role; character
教化	jiào huà	to civilize; to educate morally
街角	jiē jiǎo	street corner — 街 jiē (street) + 角 jiǎo (corner). The physical corner where two streets meet. In narrative, the street corner is a classic site of waiting, meeting, and chance encounter.
进化	jìn huà	Evolution — Evolution: advance-transformation. 进化论 is Darwin's theory; gamers use 进化 for level-ups, which Darwin might have minded. The neutral scholarly variant is 演化.
经络	jīng luò	meridians; the qi channel system — 经 jīng (warp thread; main channel) + 络 luò (network thread; connecting channel). Together they describe the body's energy network: 12 primary 经 channels + 8 extraordinary 奇经八脉 vessels + an extensive network of 络 branch channels. The basis of acupuncture, acupressure, and qigong.
九九表	jiǔ jiǔ biǎo	The multiplication table — The multiplication table: memorized in China as a chant of 45 compressed phrases from 一一得一 (1×1=1) to 九九八十一 (9×9=81). The chant begins with the smaller number, which is why it runs from 1×1 up rather than 9×9 down as in some other traditions.
君臣佐使	jūn chén zuǒ shǐ	Ruler, minister, assistant, envoy — Ruler, minister, assistant, envoy: the four functional roles within a compound herbal formula.
科学	kē xu é	science; scientific — 科 kē (branch; category; department) + 学 xué (learning; -ology). Lit. "categorized learning" , the systematic division of knowledge into disciplines. Coined as a translation for Western "science" in the late 19th century.
客星	kè xīng	Guest star — Guest star: a star that appears briefly and then fades, i.e., a nova or supernova. Chinese court astronomers maintained careful records of guest stars for astrological-political interpretation: a guest star in a particular mansion was a portent for specific ministers, regions, or dynasties.
礼物	lǐ wù	gift; present — 礼 lǐ (ritual offering) + 物 wù (thing; object; matter). Lit. "a ritual-offering made material." A gift in Chinese culture is always, at some level, a ritual object that carries social meaning exceeding its material value.
历法	lì fǎ	lunisolar calendar
量子	liàng zi	Quantum — Quantum. 量子力学 is quantum mechanics; 量子纠缠 (entanglement, "quantum entwining") supplies metaphors for codependent relationships in internet slang.
零件	líng jiàn	spare part; component part — 零 líng (fragmentary; odd; loose) + 件 jiàn (a piece). The small individual parts that make up a machine, especially replaceable ones. 零 carries the sense of scattered, loose pieces, so 零件 is the loose hardware: screws, gears, the bits kept in a parts drawer.
零落	líng luò	to wither and fall; scattered; declined and broken up — 零 líng (to fall in drops) + 落 luò (to fall; to drop). Closest to the character's root: things falling and scattering. Of flowers and leaves, to wither and drop; of people, families, or fortunes, to scatter and decline (家道零落, the household fallen on hard times).
零钱	líng qián	small change; loose coins — 零 líng (zero; scattered; odd bits) + 钱 qián. Change, small bills and coins. Increasingly rare in mainland China where mobile payment (微信支付/支付宝) dominates. 找钱 zhǎo qián = to give change. 整钱 zhěng qián = a whole bill (no change needed).
零食	líng shí	snacks; between-meal food — 零 líng (small, piecemeal) + 食 shí (food). The bits eaten between whole meals: snacks, nibbles, treats. 吃零食 is to snack. The word reflects 零's sense of the scattered and fractional, food taken in small odd amounts rather than as a proper sit-down meal (整餐).
零下	líng xià	below zero; sub-zero (temperature) — 零 líng (zero) + 下 xià (below). Below zero degrees, the standard way to state freezing temperatures: 零下十度 (minus ten degrees). Its counterpart 零上 (above zero) is less often said, since positive temperatures are usually given as a bare number.
龙脉	lóng mài	dragon vein; underground energy channel — 龙 lóng + 脉 mài (vein; pulse; channel). In feng shui 风水 theory, mountains are the visible backs of buried dragons; the ridgelines and peaks are the spine of a living creature moving through the earth. The 龙脉 are the underground channels through which this vital energy flows.
罗盘	luó pán	Geomantic compass — Geomantic compass: a multi-ring instrument centered on a magnetic needle, encoding the eight trigrams, Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, 24 solar term directions, 28 lunar mansions, and multiple other cosmological correspondence systems in concentric rings.
美化	měi huà	to beautify; to prettify; to whitewash — 美 měi (beautiful) + 化 huà (the -ize suffix). Literally "to make beautiful." 美化 covers both the literal beautifying of a place (planting flowers, cleaning streets) and the figurative dressing-up of something unattractive, that is, putting a flattering gloss on a person, a record, or a history.
明堂	míng táng	Bright hall — Bright hall: the open space in front of a geomantically ideal site, where qi gathers before the building. The concept appears in both palace architecture and burial site theory.
明星	míng xīng	star (celebrity) — 明 (bright) + 星: the bright star, classical Chinese for Venus before it became the word for screen and stage fame. 追星 ("chasing stars") is fandom, and a 粉丝 (fěnsī, fan, a sound-loan that also means glass noodles) does the chasing.
陌生	mò shēng	unfamiliar; strange; unknown — 陌 mò (a stranger; a path between rice fields, where you encounter people you don't know) + 生 shēng (unprocessed; unfamiliar). For unfamiliar places, strangers, and new experiences. 陌生人 = stranger. 对我来说很陌生 = "It's unfamiliar to me."
内容	nèi róng	content; what is contained
能量	néng liàng	Energy — Energy. Physics term and self-help term alike: 正能量 (positive energy) is the government-endorsed phrase for uplifting content, a usage physicists did not authorize.
牛角	niú jiǎo	cow horn
农历	nóng lì	the lunar calendar — 农 (farming) plus 历 (calendar). The traditional lunisolar calendar, named for farmers because its solar terms tell you when to plant and harvest. 春节 (chūnjié) falls on 农历 New Year.
配角	pèi ju é	supporting role
桥梁	qiáo liáng	bridge; bridging structure — Bridge, in the formal engineering register (spoken Chinese just says 桥). Used figuratively exactly as in English: a person or institution can be a 桥梁 between two sides.
钦天监	qīn tiān jiàn	Imperial Observatory / Bureau of Astronomy — Imperial Observatory / Bureau of Astronomy: the government department responsible for astronomical observation, calendar production, and astrological consultation in imperial China.
清明	qīng míng	Clear and Bright — Clear and Bright: the solar term around April 4–6, one of the few solar terms that is also a public holiday in China (and Taiwan, Hong Kong). The Tomb-Sweeping Festival (清明节 Qīngmíngjié) falls on or near this term: families visit ancestral graves, sweep them, leave offerings, and burn paper goods.
情况	qíng kuàng	situation; circumstances; condition — 情 qíng (circumstance) + 况 kuàng (condition). The standard word for a situation or set of circumstances: 了解情况 "to understand the situation," 紧急情况 "an emergency."
情形	qíng xíng	situation; circumstances; state of affairs — 情 qíng (circumstance; feeling) + 形 xíng. The plain, everyday word for a situation or set of circumstances. 实际情形 = the actual situation; 这种情形 = this kind of case. Close to 情况 qíngkuàng and often interchangeable, with 情形 leaning slightly more descriptive.
人物	rén wù	figure; personage; character (in a story) — 人 rén (person) + 物 wù (thing). A notable person, a "somebody," and also a character in a novel, film, or play: 历史人物 "a historical figure," 主要人物 "the main character."
壬癸	rén guǐ	Stems 9–10 (Water)
融化	róng huà	to melt; to thaw — 融 róng (to melt; to blend; to merge) + 化 huà (to dissolve). The standard verb for a solid melting into a liquid: snow, ice, butter, chocolate. Here 化 supplies its concrete physical sense, the turning of one physical state into another.
闰月	rùn yu è	Intercalary month, the extra month inserted approximately every 2–3 years (7 times in 19 years) to reconcile the lunar and solar cycles. A year with a 闰月 has 13 months.
三伏	sān fú	The Three Hottest Periods — The Three Hottest Periods: the three intervals of approximately 10 days each during Major Heat and surrounding terms, traditionally considered the most intensely hot and humid days of the year.
三角	sān jiǎo	triangle; triangular
三角形	sān jiǎo xíng	triangle — 三 sān (three) + 角 jiǎo (corner; angle) + 形 xíng. The three-corner shape. 形 is the standard suffix that turns a description into the name of a geometric shape: 正方形 zhèngfāngxíng (square), 圆形 yuánxíng (circle), 长方形 chángfāngxíng (rectangle).
设计	shè jì	To design; a design — To design; a design. 设计师 (shèjìshī) covers designers from fashion to industrial. In engineering documents, 设计院 (design institute) is the state-descended firm that produces the drawings.
生词	shēng cí	new word; unfamiliar vocabulary item — 生 shēng (raw; unfamiliar) + 词 cí (word). A word you have not yet learned, the staple of every textbook vocabulary list. Its opposite is 熟词 shúcí, a word already mastered.
生存	shēng cún	to survive; to subsist — 生 shēng + 存 cún (to exist; to preserve). Survival , continuing to exist under difficult conditions. 生存竞争 "survival competition" · 生存危机 "survival crisis." A more pressured, primal register than 生活.
生活	shēng huó	life; to live; daily living — 生 shēng (to be born, to grow) + 活 huó (to live). The pairing of two life-words covers both the verb (to live, to make a life) and the noun (life as it is lived day to day). This is the everyday word for "life," distinct from 命 mìng, the life that can be lost.
生命	shēng mìng	life (the abstract concept; the fact of being alive) — 生 shēng (to be born; to live) plus 命. The standard modern word for life in the broad sense: biological life, the human life-course, life as a value worth protecting. Distinct from 生活 shēnghuó (daily life, way of living): 生命 is the precious thing you have once, 生活 is what you do with it.
生物	shēng wù	living thing; organism; biology — 生 shēng (to live) + 物 wù (thing). A living organism, and also the name of the school subject, biology. 微生物 wēishēngwù is a microorganism, a "tiny living thing."
生肖	shēng xiào	zodiac animals; birth sign
施工	shī gōng	To carry out construction — To carry out construction: "execute the work." The word on every fence and detour sign. 施工中 means "under construction," on streets and, borrowed, on websites.
十二生肖	shí èr shēng xiào	The twelve zodiac animals
十分	shí fēn	very; extremely; completely (lit. "ten parts") — 十 shí (ten; complete) + 分 fēn (part; portion; to divide). Ten out of ten parts: the full measure of something. The most natural intensifier in formal and written Mandarin, sitting between 很 hěn (colloquial "very") and 非常 fēicháng (extremely, emphatic).
十干	shí gān	the Ten Heavenly Stems — 十 shí (ten) + 干 gān (stem; heavenly stem). The cycle of ten: 甲 jiǎ, 乙 yǐ, 丙 bǐng, 丁 dīng, 戊 wù, 己 jǐ, 庚 gēng, 辛 xīn, 壬 rén, 癸 guǐ.
十年	shín ián	a decade; ten years — 十 shí + 年 nián (year). A decade. In Chinese cultural memory, ten years functions as the basic unit of historical reckoning: the Cultural Revolution lasted "ten years" (1966-1976, the 十年浩劫 shínián hàojié , ten-year catastrophe); a generation is roughly one decade; the proverb 十年树木，百年树人 places educati…
十月	shí yu è	October; the tenth month — 十 shí + 月 yuè (moon; month). The standard Mandarin name for October and for the tenth month of any lunar calendar. Chinese month names are purely numerical: 一月 through 十二月, January through December. The naming system makes 十月 the last single-digit-ten month before 十一月 (November) and 十二月 (December).
十字	shí zì	cross shape
十字路口	shí zì lù kǒu	crossroads; intersection (lit. "ten-character road opening") — 十字 shízì (cross shape) + 路 lù (road) + 口 kǒu (opening; mouth). The point where two roads cross to form the shape of 十: a four-way intersection. Widely used both literally (traffic intersections) and metaphorically (a crossroads in life, a moment of decision between multiple paths).
时辰	shí chen	two-hour period (traditional)
实验	shí yàn	Experiment — Experiment: to test against reality. 做实验 is to run an experiment; 实验室 is the laboratory; 实验中学 is the "experimental high school" found in nearly every Chinese city.
食物	shí wù	food — 食 shí (to eat; food) + 物 wù (thing). Food as a category of substance, the general written term: 健康食物 "healthy food." For "a meal" you would say 饭 fàn, and for cooked dishes 菜 cài.
势	shì	momentum; force; tendency
数学	shù xu é	Mathematics — Mathematics: number-study. 数学家 (shùxuéjiā) is a mathematician; 数学课 (shùxué kè) is math class, the collocation every student uses daily.
数字	shù zì	Number, numeral, digit — Number, numeral, digit. The everyday word for a figure, whether spoken, written, or on a screen. 数字化 (shùzìhuà) extends it to "digitization," the same move English made with "digit."
数字系统	shù zì xì tǒng	the number system
四大发明	sì dà fā míng	the Four Great Inventions
四气五味	sì qì wǔ wèi	Four temperatures and five flavors — Four temperatures and five flavors: the two-axis system for classifying drug properties. The four temperatures: cold (寒), cool (凉), warm (温), hot (热). The five flavors: sour (酸), bitter (苦), sweet (甘), pungent (辛), salty (咸). Each flavor has specific organ affinities and physiological effects.
四象	sì xiàng	The Four Symbols — The Four Symbols: the four mythological animal guardians of the cardinal directions: Azure Dragon (east, spring), Vermilion Bird (south, summer), White Tiger (west, autumn), Black Tortoise (north, winter).
酸	suān	Acid; sour — Acid; sour. One word covers the chemical class and the taste, plus muscle soreness (腿酸, sore legs) and the sting of envy (心里酸酸的). Chemistry students never forget which taste an acid has.
算筹	suàn chóu	Counting rods — Counting rods: the predecessor to the suanpan. Small bamboo, bone, or ivory sticks arranged in patterns on a flat surface to represent numbers in a base-10 positional system. Used in China from at least the Warring States period (475–221 BCE) through the Tang dynasty.
算盘	suàn pán	The abacus, literally "calculating tray — The abacus, literally "calculating tray." The standard Chinese bead-frame instrument with 2 upper beads and 5 lower beads per rod, in use in its canonical form since at least the 16th century.
太阳	tài yáng	the sun — 太 tài (supreme; ultimate; the greatest) + 阳 yáng (yang force; light). The Supreme Yang , the ultimate source of yang energy in the cosmos. In the 五行 framework, the sun is the source of Fire phase energy. 太阳能 (solar energy) uses exactly this logic.
天干	tiāng ān	Heavenly Stems
天文	tiān wén	Astronomy — Astronomy: heaven's patterns. 天文数字 ("an astronomical figure") is the standard idiom for an unthinkably large sum, same metaphor as English.
铜人	tóng rén	Bronze man — Bronze man: the life-sized bronze figure commissioned in 1026 CE with channel lines and point locations engraved on the surface and a hollow interior. Filled with water and wax for examination purposes, it was the world's first standardized anatomical teaching model for acupuncture instruction.
图形	tú xíng	figure; graphic; geometric shape — 图 tú (drawing; diagram) + 形 xíng. A drawn figure or graphic: shapes on a page, icons on a screen, the figures of geometry. 几何图形 = geometric figures; 图形界面 = a graphical interface. Where 形状 is a thing's shape, 图形 is a shape that has been drawn as such.
完全	wán quán	completely; entirely; fully — 完 wán (finished; whole) + 全 quán (complete). The standard adverb for "completely," to the full degree with nothing left over: 完全同意 "completely agree," 完全没问题 "no problem at all." Negated, it gives "not at all": 完全不对.
万物	wàn wù	all things; the myriad phenomena; everything that exists — 万 (myriad) + 物 wù (thing, object, phenomenon). The standard classical and literary expression for "all of existence." In Daoist thought, the ten-thousand things emerge from the interplay of yin and yang, and the Dao underlies them all.
卫生	wèi shēng	hygiene; health
卫星	wèi xīng	satellite (natural or artificial) — 卫 (guard) + 星: the guard star that attends a planet. Covers the moon, the GPS constellation, and the dish antenna (卫星天线). The Great Leap Forward idiom 放卫星 ("launching a satellite") still means announcing an inflated achievement.
位置	wèi zhi	position; location
五行	wǔ xíng	the five phases; the five agents — 五 wǔ + 行 xíng (to move; phase; agent). Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water, understood as five phases of cyclical change rather than fixed substances. The generating cycle (相生) runs Wood feeds Fire, Fire makes Earth (ash), Earth bears Metal, Metal carries Water, Water nourishes Wood.
戊己庚辛	wù jǐ gēng xīn	Stems 5–8 (Earth &amp; Metal)

物理	wù lǐ	physics; the principles of matter — 物 wù (thing; matter) + 理 lǐ (principle). Literally "the principles of things," the modern name for physics. A clear case of 理 meaning the lawful pattern running through the physical world.
物品	wù pǐn	article; goods; item — 物 wù (thing) + 品 pǐn (article; kind). The formal word for goods or articles, the kind you see on a shipping label or in a store inventory: 贵重物品 "valuable items," 日用物品 "everyday articles."
物体	wù tǐ	object; physical body — 物 wù (thing) + 体 tǐ (body). A physical object regarded as a body occupying space, the term used in physics and plain description alike: 不明飞行物 "an unidentified flying object."
物质	wù zhì	matter; substance; material — 物 wù (thing) + 质 zhì (substance). Matter in the scientific sense, and the material as opposed to the spiritual: 化学物质 "a chemical substance," 物质需求 "material needs."
细胞	xì bāo	Cell — Cell: fine wrap. 干细胞 (gànxìbāo) is a stem cell, and 细胞分裂 is cell division, vocabulary every 生物课 student recites before exams.
先生	xiān sheng	Mr.; teacher; husband; gentleman — 先 xiān (first; ahead; earlier) + 生 shēng (one born; person). "One born earlier" , originally a teacher or Confucian master; now the standard polite term for "Mr." and also "husband" in formal contexts (我先生 "my husband"). A word whose meaning has drifted beautifully from teacher → gentleman → Mr.
现代化	xiàn dài huà	modernization; to modernize — 现代 xiàndài (modern; the modern era) + 化 huà (the -ize suffix). 消化	xiāo huà	to digest; to absorb (food, or by extension, information) — 消 xiāo (to dissolve; to dissipate; to consume) + 化 huà (to transform). The verb for digestion: food broken down and turned into something the body can absorb. 化 again names the physical turning of one substance into another.
星期	xīng qī	week; day of the week — 星 (star) + 期 (period): the star period, from the seven-luminary week. Days attach numbers: 星期三 is Wednesday. Asking the day is 今天星期几? , one of the few questions where 几 needs no measure word.
星宿	xīng xiù	the lunar mansions
星星	xīng xing	Star, in the affectionate reduplicated form used in speech and songs. Single 星 does the compounding work: 星空 (starry sky), 星系 (galaxy, "star system"), 星座 (constellation, and the zodiac sign small talk runs on).
星座	xīng zuò	constellation; zodiac sign — 星 + 座 (seat): the seats of the stars. In young urban small talk it means the Western zodiac sign: 你是什么星座的? has largely displaced asking one's Chinese zodiac animal among the under-forty, an ironic import given the antiquity of native Chinese astral lore.
形成	xíng chéng	to form; to take shape; to come into being — 形 xíng + 成 chéng (to become; to complete). To shape-into-being. The all-purpose verb for something forming through a process: 形成习惯 form a habit; 形成共识 reach (form) a consensus; 逐渐形成 gradually take shape. Used constantly in writing about how systems, opinions, and conditions arise.
形容	xíng róng	to describe; to characterize in words — 形 xíng + 容 róng (appearance; countenance). To give a thing its look in words. The verb for describing, especially feelings and impressions that resist plain statement. 难以形容 = hard to describe; 无法形容的美 = indescribable beauty.
形式	xíng shì	form; format: the outward shape, as opposed to content — 形 xíng + 式 shì (pattern; format). 形势	xíng shì	the situation; the lay of forces; circumstances — 形 xíng + 势 shì (momentum; force). The configuration of forces at a given moment, weightier than 情形. 形体	xíng tǐ	physical form; body; bodily figure — 形 xíng + 体 tǐ (body). 形象	xíng xiàng	image; figure; vivid

学生	xu é sheng	student — 学 xué (to study; to learn) + 生 shēng (one who is still "green"; a junior person). The student as someone still growing, not yet ripe. One of the most common nouns in Chinese , every school context relies on it.
