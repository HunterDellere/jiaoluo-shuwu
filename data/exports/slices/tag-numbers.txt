//角落書屋/Slices/Numbers
多少	duō shao	how much, how many — Chinese builds its "how much" by welding many to few, part of a system where opposites fuse into the scale between them.
不计其数	bú jì qí shù	countless; too many to count — Literally "not to reckon their number," so many that counting is hopeless. Used for vast, uncountable quantities, the far end of the scale that 多少 stretches toward. The answer to 多少 when the true reply is "beyond counting."
多多益善	duō duō yì shàn	the more the better — Literally "more and more is the more beneficial," the more the merrier. From a remark attributed to the general Han Xin, who said that as a commander he could lead troops 多多益善 ("the more the better"). Built on the 多 ("many") of 多少.
积少成多	jī shǎo chéng duō	accumulate the few and it becomes the many — The thrift-and-study chengyu: small savings, daily vocabulary reviews, and minor improvements compound. The financial-app era has given it a second life in savings-product marketing, and teachers deploy it against the student who dismisses ten minutes of practice as pointless.
面面俱到	miàn miàn jù dào	every aspect covered; thorough in all directions — 面 miàn (face; surface; aspect) + 面 + 俱 jù (all; together) + 到 (arrived at). Arrival has reached every face, every surface, every angle of the matter. Used to praise comprehensive coverage , a plan that misses nothing, a speech that addresses every concern, an analysis that leaves no gap.
屈指可数	qū zhǐ kě shǔ	countable on bent fingers, a mere handful — 屈指 (bend the fingers) + 可数 (can be counted). The vivid opposite of 不计其数: so few you can tally them on one hand. 像他这样的人才屈指可数 "Talents like him can be counted on one hand." Note 数 here is shǔ (to count), the verb reading, against shù (number) in 不计其数.
僧多粥少	sēng duō zhōu shǎo	many monks, little porridge — too many claimants for scarce resources — The monastery breakfast as economic model. Used for job markets (百个人抢一个岗位，僧多粥少), housing, research funding, and concert tickets. The pictorial economy of the phrase, one queue and one pot, is why it beats the abstract word for scarcity in daily speech.
凶多吉少	xiōng duō jí shǎo	much ill fortune, little good — the outlook is grim — 多 and 少 as a probability report: on the scale of outcomes, the bad end is heavy. The stock phrase of thriller narration and worried families when someone has gone missing; expect it in every wuxia novel's darkest chapter.
一无所有	yī wú suǒ yǒu	to have nothing at all — Literally "one/whole (一) lack (无) that-which-is-had (所有)": the components of 所有 in their original possessive sense. Cuī Jiàn's 崔健 1986 song 《一无所有》, often called the founding track of Chinese rock, turned the idiom into a generational catchphrase: 我曾经问个不休，你何时跟我走 sung by a narrator who owns nothing.
一言为定	yì yán wéi dìng	one word and it is settled: a deal is a deal — Lit: one-word-makes-settled. Said to seal an agreement on the spot: a single word given, and the matter is fixed, with no going back. The everyday way to say "deal, it's settled."
应有尽有	yīng yǒu jìn yǒu	everything one could expect is there — 应有 (what ought to be there) + 尽有 (is exhaustively there). The stock phrase for a well-provisioned shop or feature-complete product: 这家超市商品应有尽有 "This supermarket has everything you could want." The structural mirror of 一无所有: total presence against total absence.
都	dōu / měi / suǒ yǒu	每 · 所有 · quantifiers — All, every, some, many, none: 都 carries the whole system, and the other quantifiers lean on it.
半	bàn	Half — Half. Follows the measure word when added to a whole quantity (一个半, one and a half) and precedes it when alone (半个小时, half an hour). Also in 半天 (bàntiān, "half a day"), the standard exaggeration for "ages."
报价单	bào jià dān	quotation sheet — 报价 (bàojià) is both the verb "to quote" and the noun "quote." Ask for it in writing, line by line: 分项报价 (fēnxiàng bàojià) is an itemised quote that breaks out the product, packaging, tooling, and printing.
便宜一点	pián yi yì diǎn	a bit cheaper — The basic request, best softened. 价格有点高 (the price is a bit high) states the problem without accusing; 还有空间吗 (is there still room?) invites a counteroffer without naming one.
称	chēng	To weigh — To weigh. 称一下 ("weigh it, please") hands the fruit to the vendor. The same character read chèn means "to fit," as in 称心如意, suiting one's heart's wishes.
成本	chéng běn	cost — What the factory pays, as opposed to 价格 (jiàgé), what you pay. 原材料 (yuáncáiliào, raw materials) and 人工 (réngōng, labour) are the two cost lines you will hear cited when a price goes up. 利润 (lìrùn) is profit.
出口退税	chū kǒu tuì shuì	export VAT rebate — The refund of input VAT on exported goods, claimed by whoever exports them. A factory with its own export licence (自营出口 zìyíng chūkǒu) claims it directly; one without exports through a trading company (外贸公司 wàimào gōngsī), which adds a layer of margin.
打折	dǎ zhé	to give a discount — 折 counts tenths of the price you pay, not the amount taken off. 八折 means you pay eight tenths, a 20% discount. Learners hear "eight" and think "80% off." In a negotiation 点 (diǎn, a percentage point) is more common than 折 for small moves.
大多数 · 少数	dà duō shù shǎo shù	the majority · the minority — 大多数 (the great majority) and 少数 (the few) partition a set by proportion: 大多数人都同意 (most people agree), with the majority phrase taking 都 like any near-universal. 少数 appears in the fixed civic vocabulary 少数民族 (minority nationalities, China's official term for its 55 recognized non-Han groups).
大小	dà xiǎo	size — Big-small: the size scale, the template for the whole antonym-compound family. 这双鞋大小正好 means the shoes fit exactly. Its siblings 长短 (length), 轻重 (weight), and 高矮 (height) work identically, and 轻重 doubles as "relative importance," what a tactless person 不知轻重 fails to weigh.
单价	dān jià	unit price — The price per piece; 总价 (zǒngjià) is the total, 金额 (jīn'é) the amount on a line. The usual follow-up is what the unit price includes: 包括 (bāokuò) and 不包括 are the words to listen for.
第一	dì yī	first; number one — 第 dì (ordinal) + 一 yī (one). The ordinal "first." Note the live tone-sandhi: 一 yī shifts to second tone before a fourth-tone syllable, so it is read dì-yí in 第一个 but stays dì-yī before a pause or another tone.
电汇	diàn huì	wire transfer (T/T) — "Electric remittance," usually just called T/T. 信用证 (xìnyòngzhèng) is a letter of credit (L/C), slower and more paperwork, with the bank standing between the parties.
订单	dìng dān	order
订金	dìng jīn	advance payment
多多少少	duō duō shǎo shǎo	more or less; to some extent — The question word reduplicated into a hedge: some amount on the scale, unspecified but nonzero. It softens claims politely, which makes it a favorite of careful speakers: everyone at the meeting 多多少少 had objections, though few voiced them.
多少钱	duō shao qián	how much (money); what's the price — The universal way to ask a price. Because cost can be any amount, you use 多少, never 几: 这件衣服多少钱？("how much is this piece of clothing?"). The answer uses money measure words: 块 kuài (yuan, colloquial), 毛 máo (ten cents), 分 fēn (cent). 一共多少钱 ("how much altogether") totals a bill.
发票	fā piào	official receipt: an invoice; a tax receipt — 发 fā (to issue; to send out) + 票 piào (slip; note). The official, tax-registered receipt a seller issues for a purchase. In China the 发票 is a regulated document used for reimbursement and tax, very different from a plain shop receipt (小票 xiǎopiào).
各	gè	each; every (distributive)
工厂	gōng chǎng	factory
公斤	gōng jīn	kilogram — 公 gōng (metric) + 斤 jīn (a traditional unit of weight, about half a kilo). The standard kilogram. The 公 again marks the international unit against the older 斤.
含税	hán shuì	tax included — 含 is "to contain." The pair 含税 / 不含税 appears on almost every RMB quotation; 含运费 (hán yùnfèi) is freight included, 含包装 packaging included. The same 含 works for anything the price might or might not cover.
合同	hé tong	Contract — Contract. 签合同 (qiān hétong) is to sign one. The parties inside it, 甲方 and 乙方 (Party A and Party B), have become everyday slang for client and vendor.
很多 · 不少	hěn duō bù shǎo	many, a lot: plain and understated — 很多 is the everyday "many, a lot": 很多人 (many people), 很多问题 (many questions). 不少, literally "not few", says the same thing through understatement, and often carries a note of mild surprise: 他认识不少人 (he knows quite a few people).
汇率	huì lǜ	exchange rate — A factory that quotes in dollars but pays its costs in RMB carries the exchange risk and may ask to reprice when the rate moves. 汇率波动 (huìlǜ bōdòng) is fluctuation. Agree a reference rate, or a band within which the price stays fixed.
会谈	huì tán	business meeting
几	jǐ	how many (small number); a few — Asks about a small, countable number, typically under ten, and must be followed by a measure word: 几个 ("how many"), 几本 ("how many volumes"), 几位 ("how many people," polite). It assumes the answer is small, so it is the word for guests, family members, hours, or days.
几 + 量词	jǐ + M W	a few, a handful (under ten) — Outside questions, 几 + measure word means "a few": 几个朋友 (a few friends), 几本书 (a few books), 前几天 (a few days ago). The same word asks "how many?" when the expected answer is small; the statement and question readings are told apart by context and intonation.
价格	jià gé	price — 价 (price) + 格 (a fixed standard, a slot). The settled, stated price of a good, the figure printed on the tag or quoted by the seller. The everyday word for what something costs.
阶梯价	jiē tī jià	tiered pricing — "Staircase price": the unit price falls as quantity rises. Asking for a tier table is the cleanest way to learn the factory's real cost curve, and it moves the conversation from "lower the price" to "at what quantity."
斤	jīn	The market half-kilo — The market half-kilo. 一斤多少钱 ("how much per jin?") is the essential market question. Originally a pictograph of an axe, which is still its job as a radical.
砍价	kǎn jià	to haggle
块 · 毛 · 分	kuài máo fēn	units of currency, spoken — Spoken prices use 块 for the main unit, 毛 for tenths, 分 for hundredths; written prices use 元, 角, 分. The trailing unit is often dropped: 四块一 is 4.10, 三块八五 is 3.85. Factories apply the same words to dollars, so add 美金 (měijīn) or 人民币 (rénmínbì) when it could be either.
离岸价	lí àn jià	FOB price — "Leave-shore price." Most export quotes are FOB, always with a port: FOB宁波, FOB深圳. In speech people simply say the letters, F-O-B. The port matters, since inland trucking to a distant port is part of the factory's cost.
利润	lì rùn	profit
每	měi	every, each one of an identical series — 每 walks through a set member by member: 每个人 (every person), 每本书 (every book). It requires a measure word before the noun, with a short list of exceptions that are themselves measure-like: 每天, 每年, 每次 (day, year, time).
米	mǐ	Meter, borrowed onto the rice character purely for sound — Meter, borrowed onto the rice character purely for sound. 一米八 is the shorthand for an enviable height; 百米 is the hundred-meter dash.
亩	mǔ	The farmland unit, about 667 square meters — The farmland unit, about 667 square meters. 一亩三分地 is everyone's metaphorical private turf, from an emperor's ceremonial plot.
目标价	mù biāo jià	target price — Naming your target moves the discussion from "lower" to "can you reach this." Give it with a reason, such as a retail price point or a competing quote (另一家的报价 lìng yì jiā de bàojià), without naming the other factory.
全 · 整	quán zhěng	whole vs. entire-and-unbroken — 全 fuses directly onto nouns to mean "the whole": 全家 (the whole family), 全国 (the whole country), 全世界 (the whole world). 整 zhěng insists the unit is complete and uncut: 整个 (the entire), 整天 (all day long), 整整三年 (three full years).
让价	ràng jià	to concede on price — 让 is "to yield." 让利 (rànglì) is giving up margin; 降价 (jiàngjià) is simply lowering the price, 涨价 (zhǎngjià) raising it, 还价 (huánjià) making a counteroffer. 底价 (dǐjià) is the floor price a side will not go below.
人民币	rén mín bì	renminbi, RMB
任何	rèn hé	any, no matter which — 任何 means "any whatsoever" and, true to the system, needs 都 at the verb: 任何人都可以参加 (anyone may take part), 任何问题都欢迎 (any question is welcome).
生意	shēng yì	doing business
数词	shù cí	numeral
数学	shù xu é	Mathematics — Mathematics: number-study. 数学家 (shùxuéjiā) is a mathematician; 数学课 (shùxué kè) is math class, the collocation every student uses daily.
数字	shù zì	Number, numeral, digit — Number, numeral, digit. The everyday word for a figure, whether spoken, written, or on a screen. 数字化 (shùzìhuà) extends it to "digitization," the same move English made with "digit."
数字系统	shù zì xì tǒng	the number system
水单	shuǐ dān	bank remittance slip — The bank's proof that you sent the money, also 汇款水单. The factory will ask for it the moment you say you have paid, and production often waits for it. Send it promptly; it is the fastest way to start the lead-time clock.
算盘	suàn pán	The abacus, literally "calculating tray — The abacus, literally "calculating tray." The standard Chinese bead-frame instrument with 2 upper beads and 5 lower beads per rod, in use in its canonical form since at least the 16th century.
所有 · 全部	suǒ yǒu quán bù	all of, the entirety of a set — 所有(的) + noun names the complete set up front: 所有的学生都来了 (all the students came). 所有 began as "that which is possessed" (所 + 有, what one has), and the possessive flavor survives in 我所有的钱 (all the money I have).
尾款	wěi kuǎn	balance payment — "Tail money." What triggers it is the real negotiation: 出货前 (before shipment), 验货后 (after inspection passes), or 见提单副本 (jiàn tídān fùběn, against a copy of the bill of lading). The later the trigger, the more leverage you keep.
无数	wú shù	countless; innumerable
一点儿	yì diǎn r	a little; a bit (neutral, often comparative) — After an adjective, 一点儿 makes a neutral comparison, "a little more X": 大一点儿 (a bit bigger), 快一点儿 (a bit faster), 便宜一点儿 (a little cheaper). After a verb, it means "a little / some": 喝一点儿水 (drink a little water), 说一点儿 (say a bit).
一点儿 · 部分	yī diǎn r bù fen	a little (mass) · a part (of a whole) — 一点儿 measures out a small amount of an uncountable substance: 一点儿水 (a little water), 会一点儿中文 (speaks a little Chinese). It pairs with mass nouns where 几 pairs with countables. 部分 bùfen slices a defined whole: 一部分学生 (a portion of the students), 大部分时间 (most of the time). The trap of 一点儿 vs.
一些 · 有的 · 有些	yī xiē yǒu de yǒu xiē	some: indefinite vs. some-of-a-set — 一些 introduces an indefinite amount: 我买了一些水果 (I bought some fruit). 有的 and 有些 pick out a subset of a known or generic whole, and they live at the front of the sentence: 有的人喜欢，有的人不喜欢 (some people like it, some don't). The classic restriction: 有的 phrases resist the object position.
亿	yì	One hundred million, the great unit above 万 — One hundred million, the great unit above 万. China's population is 十四亿 (shísì yì, 1.4 billion), a figure every learner should be able to parse and produce.
有效期	yǒu xiào qī	validity period — How long a quote holds. Factories tie it to material prices, especially steel, and to the exchange rate (汇率 huìlǜ). A quote with no date can be reopened at any time.
运费	yùn fèi	freight charge — 海运 (hǎiyùn) is sea freight, 空运 (kōngyùn) air freight, 快递 (kuàidì) express courier for samples. 货代 (huòdài) is the freight forwarder. 整柜 (zhěngguì) is a full container load, 拼柜 (pīnguì) a shared one.
账户	zhàng hù	bank account — 收款账户 (shōukuǎn zhànghù) is the receiving account. Fraudsters impersonate suppliers by email and announce new bank details.
