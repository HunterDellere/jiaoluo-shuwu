高兴

gāoxìng

Happiness as elevation: spirits that rise. The everyday glad word, and how it divides the territory with 开心, 快乐, and 幸福.

~5 min read
字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
兴致高涨 xìngzhì gāozhǎng · Spirits Running High

高兴 (gāoxìng) is 高 (high) plus 兴 (xìng, aroused interest, animation): spirits raised high, the same vertical metaphor English uses in "high spirits" and "feeling down." 兴 is a polyphone whose two readings split one idea of rising. Read xīng, first tone, it is the verb to rise or flourish: 兴起 (rise up), 兴旺 (prosperous), 复兴 (revival, as in the official phrase about national rejuvenation). Read xìng, fourth tone, the rising moves indoors and becomes mood: 高兴 (glad), 兴趣 (xìngqù, interest), 兴奋 (xīngfèn is the exception that keeps first tone, excitement), and 尽兴 (jìnxìng, to enjoy oneself to the full, the host's parting wish at a banquet).

Grammatically 高兴 earns its keep. It takes degree adverbs like any adjective (很高兴, 特别高兴), and it takes a following verb phrase: 很高兴认识你 ("very glad to know you") is the fixed formula of introductions, and 我很高兴你来了 ("I'm glad you came") shows the pattern with a full clause. The negative 不高兴 understates displeasure the way English "not happy" does: 老板不高兴了 means storm clouds, not a neutral absence of joy.

近义词地图 jìnyìcí dìtú The Happiness Family
四种快乐 sì zhǒng kuàilè · Four Kinds of Happy 高兴 gāoxìng → glad, right now : reactive and momentary ; 见到你很高兴
开心 kāixīn → having a good time : "open-hearted" ; 玩得很开心
快乐 kuàilè → the wishing word : 生日快乐! 新年快乐! ; also sustained cheer
幸福 xìngfú → deep wellbeing : a good life, not a good mood ; 幸福的家庭
Test: you can 祝 (wish) someone 快乐 or 幸福, but not 高兴; gladness can't be scheduled
从心情到人生 · From Mood to Life

The four words ascend in depth. 高兴 and 开心 are the daily pair, close enough to swap in most sentences, though 开心 ("heart opened") leans toward enjoyment in progress (孩子们玩得很开心, the kids are having a great time) while 高兴 leans toward gladness at something (听到这个消息我很高兴, glad to hear the news). 快乐 (kuàilè, "swift joy," sharing the joy-reading of the music character 乐) owns the festival calendar: every holiday greeting ends in it. 幸福 (xìngfú) is different in kind: built from 幸 (good fortune) and 福 (the blessing character pasted on every door at New Year), it names the settled happiness of a life going well. Advertising sells 幸福; weddings promise it; surveys measure it. Announcing 我很幸福 is a life report, not a mood report.

The unhappy side of the ledger, for completeness: 难过 (nánguò, sad, "hard to get through"), 伤心 (shāngxīn, grieved, "wounded heart"), and 生气 (shēngqì, angry, "generating qi," the TCM physiology of rage preserved in the everyday verb). The heart radical 忄 or 心 anchors most of the emotion lexicon, filing feelings where classical China located them, in the heart that thinks and feels at once.

词汇 cíhuì Key Compounds
开心 kāixīn happy, having fun
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
开 (open) + 心 (heart): the heart opened up. The go-to word for enjoyment in progress and the standard well-wish for outings: 玩得开心! (have fun!). 开心果 ("open-heart fruit") is the pistachio, and by extension the friend who makes everyone laugh.
今天和老朋友聚会，玩得特别开心。
Jīntiān hé lǎo péngyou jùhuì, wán de tèbié kāixīn.
I got together with old friends today and had a wonderful time.
快乐 kuàilè happy (greetings and sustained cheer)
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
快 (swift) + 乐 (joy, the lè reading of the music character). The greeting suffix of the entire festival calendar: 生日快乐, 新年快乐, 中秋快乐. The philosophical register belongs to it too; Zhuangzi's famous debate about whether the fish are happy uses 乐.
祝你生日快乐，万事如意！
Zhù nǐ shēngrì kuàilè, wànshì rúyì!
Happy birthday, and may everything go as you wish!
幸福 xìngfú deep happiness; wellbeing
Adj/N 形容词/名词
幸 (good fortune) + 福 (blessing). Life-level happiness: 幸福的婚姻 (a happy marriage), 幸福感 (a sense of wellbeing, the survey researchers' term). A warm meal can make you 开心; only a good life makes you 幸福.
一家人健健康康地在一起，就是幸福。
Yì jiā rén jiànjiànkāngkāng de zài yìqǐ, jiùshì xìngfú.
The whole family together and healthy: that is happiness.
兴趣 xìngqù interest, hobby
N 名词 míngcí
兴 (aroused spirit, the xìng reading) + 趣 (inclination, delight). 对…感兴趣 ("to feel interest toward") is the essential pattern: 我对中国历史很感兴趣. 兴趣班 are the after-school enrichment classes filling Chinese childhood weekends.
他对书法很感兴趣，每天都练。
Tā duì shūfǎ hěn gǎn xìngqù, měitiān dōu liàn.
He is very interested in calligraphy and practices every day.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
兴高采烈 xìng gāo cǎi liè spirits high, expression ablaze — in jubilant high spirits Originally literary criticism: Liu Xie's Wenxin Diaolong used 兴高而采烈 to praise the poet Ji Kang's lofty inspiration and brilliant style. The phrase migrated from describing writing to describing faces, and now marks any scene of visible collective joy: children on the last day of school arrive 兴高采烈.
乐极生悲 lè jí shēng bēi joy at its extreme gives birth to sorrow From the Records of the Grand Historian: the wit Chunyu Kun warned King Wei of Qi, over wine, that drinking to the limit ends in disorder and joy at its limit turns to grief (酒极则乱，乐极则悲). The proverbial ceiling on celebration, cited whenever triumph gets careless: the champagne-soaked team that crashes the trophy.
喜出望外 xǐ chū wàng wài joy beyond what one hoped — overjoyed by surprise 喜 (delight) arriving from outside (外) the range of expectation (望). The precise word for good news you did not dare hope for: the scholarship, the recovered wallet, the friend at the airport gate. Stronger and more specific than 很高兴.
皆大欢喜 jiē dà huān xǐ all are greatly delighted — everyone ends up happy Originally the closing formula of Buddhist sutras, where the assembly rejoices at the teaching and departs; now the phrase for win-win endings, amicable settlements, and comedies. It is also the standard Chinese title of Shakespeare's As You Like It, a translation with better provenance than most.

常见问题chángjiàn wèntíFrequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between 高兴, 开心, 快乐, and 幸福?
All translate as happy, at different depths. 高兴 and 开心 are the everyday glad-right-now words, nearly interchangeable. 快乐 is the wishing word (生日快乐, happy birthday) and a more sustained cheerfulness. 幸福 is the deep one: life-level wellbeing, the happiness of a good marriage or a warm family, never a passing mood.
How do you say nice to meet you in Chinese?
很高兴认识你 (hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ), literally 'very glad to know you.' It is the standard polite formula at introductions, and the reply can simply echo it: 我也很高兴认识你.
Why does 兴 appear in both 高兴 (xìng) and 兴趣 (xìngqù)?
兴 read xìng is aroused interest or excitement, the sense in 高兴 (spirits raised high) and 兴趣 (interest). Read xīng, first tone, it means to rise or flourish, as in 兴起 (rise up) and 兴旺 (thriving). One character, the same idea of rising, split between mood and motion.
