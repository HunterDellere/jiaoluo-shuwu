医院

yīyuàn

The healing courtyard: how an arrow wound, a wine jar, and the institutional suffix 院 assembled the word for hospital.

~5 min read
字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
箭与酒 jiàn yǔ jiǔ · The Arrow and the Wine

The traditional form of the healing character, 醫, is a compressed scene of early medicine. The upper left 医 is an arrow (矢) in a quiver or wound-box; the upper right 殳 is a hand wielding an implement; and the bottom 酉 is a wine jar. Read together: extracting an arrow, working with instruments, and administering alcohol, which was antiquity's antiseptic, anesthetic, and solvent for herbal preparations at once. The Shuowen glosses the wine component directly: wine is what treats illness. Simplification kept only the arrow-box 医, which is why the modern character looks like a box with an arrow in it, a battlefield first-aid kit reduced to its emblem.

医 does the healing work across the lexicon: 医生 (yīshēng, doctor), 医学 (yīxué, the study of medicine), 中医 and 西医 (the traditional and biomedical systems), 医疗 (yīliáo, medical care), and 医保 (yībǎo, medical insurance). Paired with 院 (yuàn), the walled courtyard, it names the institution. Courtyards were how China built: houses, temples, government offices, and academies were all walled compounds, so 院 drifted from architecture into institution, and a hospital is literally the courtyard where medicine happens.

院的家族 yuàn de jiāzú The 院 Family
X + 院 · The Institution Suffix 医院 yīyuàn → hospital : the medicine courtyard
学院 xuéyuàn → college, institute : the study courtyard
法院 fǎyuàn → court of law : the law courtyard
电影院 diànyǐngyuàn → cinema : the electric-shadow courtyard
科学院 kēxuéyuàn → academy of sciences ; 国务院 → the State Council
四合院 sìhéyuàn → the Beijing courtyard house : the architectural original, still just a courtyard
从建筑到机构 · From Architecture to Institution

The 院 family is a map of Chinese institutional life, and its members share the flavor of the walled compound: a place you enter through a gate, with its own internal order. The hospital keeps the architecture in its verbs. To be hospitalized is 住院 (zhùyuàn, "live in the courtyard"), to be discharged is 出院 (chūyuàn, "leave the courtyard"), and the head of the institution is the 院长 (yuànzhǎng, "courtyard elder"), a title shared by hospital directors, college deans, and supreme court presidents, which occasionally requires clarification at dinner parties.

院内 yuàn nèi Inside the Hospital
白大褂 bái dàguà · The White Coats

The cast: 医生 (yīshēng, doctor; in the north also 大夫 dàifu, a Song-era official rank that settled onto physicians and stayed), 护士 (hùshi, nurse, "protecting officer"), and 病人 (bìngrén, patient, "ill person") or the more clinical 患者 (huànzhě). The geography: 门诊 (ménzhěn, outpatient clinic, "door consultations"), 急诊 (jízhěn, the ER, "urgent consultations"), 病房 (bìngfáng, the ward), 手术室 (shǒushùshì, the operating room, where 手术 "hand-technique" is surgery), and 药房 (yàofáng, the dispensary window where the visit ends). The whole route, from taking a queue number onward, is walked step by step in this guide's 看病 entry.

Practical modifiers matter when choosing where to go: hospitals are ranked 三甲 (sānjiǎ, "third-tier grade A") at the top, and 人民医院 (People's Hospital) plus a number is the standard municipal naming scheme, so 市一医院 ("Municipal Hospital No. 1") reads like an address rather than a brand. A 中医院 is a hospital of traditional medicine, and the 妇幼保健院 (women's and children's health center) rounds out the everyday institutional set.

词汇 cíhuì Key Compounds
医生 yīshēng doctor, physician
N 名词 míngcí
医 (heal) + 生 (person, the same suffix as in 学生). The address form doubles as the job title: patients say 医生 or 大夫 directly, with no surname needed. 主治医生 is the attending physician in charge of a case.
医生，我嗓子疼，还发烧。
Yīshēng, wǒ sǎngzi téng, hái fāshāo.
Doctor, my throat hurts, and I have a fever too.
住院 zhùyuàn to be hospitalized
V 动词 dòngcí
住 (live, stay) + 院 (the hospital courtyard). Separable: 住了三天院 is three days in the hospital. Its exit is 出院 (be discharged), the phone call every family waits for.
爷爷住院了，我们下午去看他。
Yéye zhùyuàn le, wǒmen xiàwǔ qù kàn tā.
Grandpa has been hospitalized; we're going to see him this afternoon.
急诊 jízhěn emergency department; emergency consultation
N 名词 míngcí
急 (urgent) + 诊 (examine a patient). 挂急诊 is to register at the ER, the after-hours route when the regular 门诊 windows have closed. The character 诊 carries the speech radical: diagnosis begins with talking.
孩子半夜烧到三十九度，我们只好挂急诊。
Háizi bànyè shāo dào sānshíjiǔ dù, wǒmen zhǐhǎo guà jízhěn.
The child's fever hit thirty-nine degrees in the middle of the night, so we had no choice but to go to the ER.
护士 hùshi nurse
N 名词 míngcí
护 (protect, the 护 of 保护) + 士 (officer, the scholar-gentleman suffix of 战士 soldier and 博士 PhD). Coined when modern nursing arrived in the late Qing; the profession's day, 护士节, falls on Florence Nightingale's birthday, May 12.
护士给他打了一针。
Hùshi gěi tā dǎ le yì zhēn.
The nurse gave him an injection.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
对症下药 duì zhèng xià yào prescribe according to the symptoms; tailor the remedy to the problem 对 (match) + 症 (symptoms) + 下药 (administer medicine). From the case lore of the Han physician Hua Tuo, who gave two patients with identical headaches different prescriptions because the causes differed. Now the standard metaphor for any correctly targeted solution: a policy, a lesson plan, a bug fix. 解决问题要对症下药。You must fit the remedy to the disease when solving a problem.
妙手回春 miào shǒu huí chūn miraculous hands bring back the spring; masterly healing 妙手 (wondrous hands) + 回春 (return spring, restore life's season). The stock phrase of gratitude to a doctor, embroidered on the silk banners (锦旗 jǐnqí) that patients present to hospital departments, a living custom: the walls of a respected clinic hang red with them.
病急乱投医 bìng jí luàn tóu yī when the illness is desperate, one runs to any doctor A proverb rather than a classical chengyu: desperation abandons judgment. Used far beyond medicine, for panicked job hunts, failing companies grasping at consultants, or students trying every cram school at once. The tone is sympathetic but critical; the flailing rarely works.
久病成医 jiǔ bìng chéng yī long illness makes the patient a doctor Chronic experience becomes expertise. Said of patients who know their condition better than junior doctors, and by extension of anyone whose long exposure to a problem has made them its reluctant expert: the veteran user who knows the software's bugs better than the developers.

常见问题chángjiàn wèntíFrequently Asked Questions
What does 医院 (yīyuàn) literally mean?
Medicine courtyard. 医 is the healing morpheme (its traditional form 醫 pictures an arrow being extracted and a jar of medicinal wine) and 院 is the walled courtyard that became the suffix for institutions, the same 院 as in 学院 (college) and 法院 (court).
What is the difference between 医院 and 诊所 (zhěnsuǒ)?
医院 is a full hospital with departments and inpatient beds; 诊所 is a small clinic. Chinese healthcare routes most complaints straight to the hospital's outpatient department (门诊), so 医院 does much of the everyday work a Western reader would associate with a GP's office.
How do you say doctor and nurse in Chinese?
医生 (yīshēng, 'healing person') is the doctor, addressed directly as 医生 or 大夫 (dàifu, an old official title that survived as northern spoken usage). The nurse is 护士 (hùshi, 'protecting officer').
