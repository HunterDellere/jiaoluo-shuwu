Vocabulary · 词汇 cíhuì

多少

duōshao

Chinese builds its "how much" by welding many to few, part of a system where opposites fuse into the scale between them.

~6 min read
反义合成 fǎnyì héchéng Opposites Welded
两极生一词 liǎng jí shēng yī cí · Two Poles, One Word

多少 (duōshao) is 多 (many) fused to 少 (few), and the fusion is systematic. Chinese regularly names a dimension by welding its two poles: 大小 (big-small) is size, 长短 (long-short) is length, 轻重 (light-heavy) is weight, 快慢 (fast-slow) is speed, 远近 (far-near) is distance, and 高矮 (tall-short) is stature. The logic runs: the pair of opposites defines a scale, and the compound names the scale itself. As a question word, 多少 asks where on the quantity scale the answer falls. The most famous member of the family has drifted furthest: 东西 (dōngxi, east-west) became the everyday word for "thing," the stuff one shops for in both directions of the market street.

The two characters are old pictographic arguments. 多 stacks two 夕 (moon/evening) forms: night after night, an accumulation. 少 is 小 (small) with a stroke shaved away: less than little. Their fusion is attested in classical texts, including the openings every learner eventually meets: Meng Haoran's dawn poem 春晓 ends 花落知多少, "who knows how many blossoms fell," using 多少 exactly as the modern language does, thirteen centuries ago.

几与多少 jǐ yǔ duōshao 几 versus 多少
选哪个? xuǎn nǎge · Which One to Use 几 + measure word → expects a small answer : 你家有几口人? (family size) ; 孩子几岁? (a child's age)
多少 (+ optional measure) → open-ended : 你们学校有多少学生? (could be thousands)
多少钱? → the price question : always 多少, never 几
电话号码是多少? → numbers read as digits ask 多少
几 in statements → "a few" : 几个朋友 (a few friends) ; 十几个 (ten-odd, between 11 and 19)
Rule of thumb: if you would be surprised by an answer over ten, ask 几
问与猜 wèn yǔ cāi · The Question Encodes the Guess

The choice between 几 and 多少 quietly tells the listener what scale of answer you expect, so using the wrong one is grammatical but socially odd: asking a metropolis 你们城市有几个人 sounds like you expect to count its residents on your fingers. 几 also moonlights outside questions. Before a measure word in a statement it means "a few" (说几句话, say a few words), and stacked after a round number it means "and some": 二十几岁 is twentysomething, 一百几十块 is a hundred-odd yuan. The date-and-time questions split the labor: 几月几号 asks the date, 星期几 asks the day of the week, and 几点 asks the hour, all small-range and therefore all 几.

多少 has one statement idiom worth owning early: reduplicated 多多少少, "more or less, to some degree" (他多多少少有点后悔, he somewhat regrets it). And in classical flavor 多少 can exclaim rather than ask, as in 花落知多少: not a request for a count but a sigh at uncountability, the same rhetorical slide English makes with "how many dreams were lost."

多字问句 duō zì wènjù The 多 Questions
多大、多高、多远 · How Big, How Tall, How Far

多 alone, prefixed to an adjective, builds the "how X" questions: 多大 (how old / how big), 多高 (how tall), 多远 (how far), 多长 (how long), 多重 (how heavy). Asking an adult's age is 你多大了, softened for elders into the honorific 您多大年纪. The answers recycle the adjective or drop it entirely for bare numbers: 我一米七五 (I'm 1.75 meters), 三十岁 (thirty years old). The pattern always picks the scale's larger pole, 大 not 小, 高 not 矮, 远 not 近: the unmarked direction of every dimension in Chinese, as in most languages, is up.

词汇 cíhuì Key Compounds
多少钱 duōshao qián how much money; what's the price
Phrase 短语 duǎnyǔ
The single most-used question in a learner's first year. Market fluency adds the unit: 多少钱一斤? ("how much per jin?"). The reply's follow-up move is 便宜一点 ("a little cheaper"), covered in this guide's bargaining notes.
老板，这个多少钱一斤？
Lǎobǎn, zhège duōshao qián yì jīn?
Boss, how much is this per jin?
东西 dōngxi thing, stuff
N 名词 míngcí
East-west, the antonym compound that outgrew its scale to mean "things" in general: 买东西 is shopping. Stressed differently (dōngxī) it still means the compass span. Applied to a person it is an insult or, negated, affection: 你真不是东西 (you're a real piece of work) versus a grandmother's 小东西 (little thing).
我去超市买点东西。
Wǒ qù chāoshì mǎi diǎn dōngxi.
I'm going to the supermarket to buy a few things.
大小 dàxiǎo size
N 名词 míngcí
Big-small: the size scale, the template for the whole antonym-compound family. 这双鞋大小正好 means the shoes fit exactly. Its siblings 长短 (length), 轻重 (weight), and 高矮 (height) work identically, and 轻重 doubles as "relative importance," what a tactless person 不知轻重 fails to weigh.
这件衣服大小合适，颜色也好看。
Zhè jiàn yīfu dàxiǎo héshì, yánsè yě hǎokàn.
This piece of clothing fits well, and the color is attractive too.
多多少少 duōduō shǎoshǎo more or less; to some extent
Adv 副词 fùcí
The question word reduplicated into a hedge: some amount on the scale, unspecified but nonzero. It softens claims politely, which makes it a favorite of careful speakers: everyone at the meeting 多多少少 had objections, though few voiced them.
在中国住了一年，他多多少少会说一点中文。
Zài Zhōngguó zhù le yì nián, tā duōduō shǎoshǎo huì shuō yìdiǎn Zhōngwén.
Having lived in China a year, he can speak at least some Chinese.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
多多益善 duō duō yì shàn the more the better From the Records of the Grand Historian: Liu Bang, founding emperor of the Han, asked his general Han Xin how many troops he himself could command. "Your majesty, ten thousand at most." And you? 多多益善, "the more the better." The emperor laughed and asked why such a genius served under him; Han Xin, thinking fast, replied that the emperor commanded generals, not soldiers. The phrase survives as the standard answer to "how much do you want?"
积少成多 jī shǎo chéng duō accumulate the few and it becomes the many The thrift-and-study chengyu: small savings, daily vocabulary reviews, and minor improvements compound. The financial-app era has given it a second life in savings-product marketing, and teachers deploy it against the student who dismisses ten minutes of practice as pointless.
僧多粥少 sēng duō zhōu shǎo many monks, little porridge — too many claimants for scarce resources The monastery breakfast as economic model. Used for job markets (百个人抢一个岗位，僧多粥少), housing, research funding, and concert tickets. The pictorial economy of the phrase, one queue and one pot, is why it beats the abstract word for scarcity in daily speech.
凶多吉少 xiōng duō jí shǎo much ill fortune, little good — the outlook is grim 多 and 少 as a probability report: on the scale of outcomes, the bad end is heavy. The stock phrase of thriller narration and worried families when someone has gone missing; expect it in every wuxia novel's darkest chapter.

常见问题chángjiàn wèntíFrequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between 多少 (duōshao) and 几 (jǐ)?
Both ask 'how many.' 几 expects a smallish answer (usually under ten) and requires a measure word: 几个人? 多少 has no size limit and the measure word is optional: 多少人? Prices ask 多少钱, never 几钱; a child's age asks 几岁.
Why does 多少 combine 'many' and 'few'?
Chinese regularly fuses antonym pairs into a word for the scale between them: 大小 (big-small) means size, 长短 (long-short) means length, 轻重 (light-heavy) means weight. 多少 (many-few) is the quantity scale, so as a question it asks where on that scale the answer sits.
Can 多少 appear in statements, not just questions?
Yes. 多多少少 means 'more or less, to some extent' (他多多少少懂一点中文, he understands some Chinese, more or less), and 多少 alone can soften a claim the same way: 多少有点贵, it's somewhat expensive.
