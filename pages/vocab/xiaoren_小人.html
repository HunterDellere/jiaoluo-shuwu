小人

xiǎorén

Confucius took a word that meant "commoner" and turned it into the permanent opposite of the gentleman: the small-hearted schemer of every office.

~6 min read
从身份到品格 cóng shēnfèn dào pǐngé From Commoner to Character
小与大 xiǎo yǔ dà · The Small and the Great

In the oldest usage, 小人 was sociology, not ethics: the small people, commoners, farmers, and servants, as against 大人, the great, the lords and officeholders. The pairing survives in fossil form: 大人 is the standard word for "adult" (and the "Your Honor" of every courtroom drama), while imperial-era commoners called themselves 小人 before officials, a humble first-person pronoun that period television keeps in every viewer's ear.

The Analects performed the semantic surgery that made the word permanent. Confucius systematically re-paired 君子 and 小人 by character rather than birth: the gentleman is whoever acts like one, and the petty man is whoever schemes like one, whatever his rank. It is one of the quiet radicalisms of the text, moving both words from the social register to the moral one, and the language never moved them back. Calling a duke's son a 小人 became possible, and calling a poor scholar a 君子 became praise worth having.

论语对举 Lúnyǔ duìjǔ The Analects Contrasts
君子…小人… · The Paired Verdicts 君子喻于义，小人喻于利 → the gentleman understands duty; the petty man understands profit
君子坦荡荡，小人长戚戚 → the gentleman is open and at ease; the petty man is forever fretting
君子和而不同，小人同而不和 → the gentleman harmonizes without conforming; the petty man conforms without harmony
君子求诸己，小人求诸人 → the gentleman demands of himself; the petty man demands of others
The 小人 is not a monster; he is ordinary self-interest without self-cultivation, which is why the warnings still land
影子的作用 yǐngzi de zuòyòng · The Work a Shadow Does

The Analects rarely defines the 君子 directly; it defines him against the 小人, in a long series of paired verdicts of which the table holds the most quoted. The rhetorical structure did lasting work: it made the petty man the gentleman's permanent shadow, the thing self-cultivation is cultivation away from. Note what the contrasts pick out: not crime but orientation. The 小人 calculates advantage (利), conforms to whoever is present, blames outward, and worries constantly, the last diagnosis (长戚戚, forever fretting) being the tradition's sharpest observation, that pettiness is exhausting from the inside. The full portrait of the other half of the pair lives in this guide's 君子 entry.

现代用法 xiàndài yòngfǎ The Office 小人
防小人 fáng xiǎorén · Guarding Against Petty People

Modern usage has narrowed the word to its sharpest edge: the 小人 is the backstabber, the colleague who smiles in the meeting and reports you afterward, the relative who stirs discord at the family banquet. 背后捅刀子的小人 ("the petty man who knifes you from behind") is the full indictment; 我们公司有小人 is the sighed diagnosis of a poisoned office. The accusation is serious enough that it is usually made about someone, rarely to them. Folk practice takes the threat literally: 防小人 (guarding against petty people) is a recognized category in temple culture and feng shui retail, from Villain Hitting (打小人), the Hong Kong street ritual of beating a paper figure of one's tormentor with a shoe, to cinnabar charms sold against workplace treachery.

One idiom rehabilitates the word with self-aware humor: 先小人，后君子 ("petty men first, gentlemen after") means settling money and terms explicitly up front so the friendship survives the deal, spoken while passing the contract across the table. It concedes the Confucian point while filing a practical amendment: a little scheduled pettiness now prevents the real kind later.

词汇 cíhuì Key Compounds
小气 xiǎoqì stingy; petty
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
Small qi: a constricted spirit, tight with money and with grace. The direct opposite is 大方 (dàfang, generous, poised). Milder than 小人; a friend can be teased as 小气 and remain a friend.
别这么小气，请大家喝杯奶茶吧。
Bié zhème xiǎoqì, qǐng dàjiā hē bēi nǎichá ba.
Don't be so stingy, treat everyone to milk tea.
小心眼 xiǎoxīnyǎn petty-minded; quick to take offense
Adj/N 形容词/名词
Small heart-aperture: a mind with a narrow gate, through which slights enter easily and never leave. The grudge-keeping cousin of 小气, aimed at temperament rather than wallet.
他有点小心眼，开玩笑要注意分寸。
Tā yǒudiǎn xiǎoxīnyǎn, kāi wánxiào yào zhùyì fēncun.
He's a bit thin-skinned; watch your step when joking with him.
小聪明 xiǎocōngming cheap cleverness; cunning without wisdom
N 名词 míngcí
Small cleverness: the shortcut-finding wit that games the rule and misses the point. 耍小聪明 (deploying petty cleverness) is what teachers accuse corner-cutting students of; its grown-up antonym is 大智慧, great wisdom.
做学问要下真功夫，别耍小聪明。
Zuò xuéwen yào xià zhēn gōngfu, bié shuǎ xiǎocōngming.
Real scholarship takes real work; don't rely on cheap tricks.
得罪 dézuì to offend, to cross someone
V 动词 dòngcí
To acquire guilt before someone: the verb of social danger. The folk warning 宁得罪君子，不得罪小人 advises that a crossed gentleman forgives and a crossed petty man files it away, which is the whole word-field in one proverb.
他说话太直，得罪了不少人。
Tā shuōhuà tài zhí, dézuì le bùshǎo rén.
He speaks too bluntly and has offended quite a few people.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
小人得志 xiǎorén dézhì the petty man gets his moment — power in unworthy hands 得志 is achieving one's ambitions; attached to 小人 it names the special insufferability of the schemer promoted. The stock description of the new supervisor swaggering through a department, and of every court favorite in the historical novels. Usually muttered, rarely printed on the congratulations banner.
以小人之心，度君子之腹 yǐ xiǎorén zhī xīn, duó jūnzǐ zhī fù measuring a gentleman's belly with a petty man's heart — projecting one's own baseness Adapted from a Zuozhuan phrase: assuming others' motives are as small as one's own. The polite deployment is self-accusation: 是我以小人之心度君子之腹了, "I misjudged you by my own petty standard," the graceful way to withdraw a suspicion that proved unfair.
先小人后君子 xiān xiǎorén hòu jūnzǐ petty men first, gentlemen after — settle terms up front The contract-signing proverb: state the money, the split, and the deadlines with un-gentlemanly explicitness now, so that everyone can afford to be a gentleman later. Spoken at the start of partnerships between friends precisely because friendship is what it protects.
亲贤臣，远小人 qīn xiánchén, yuǎn xiǎorén keep worthy ministers close and petty men far From Zhuge Liang's Chushibiao (出师表), the memorial he submitted before the northern campaigns, diagnosing why the Former Han rose and the Later Han fell. Memorized by generations of schoolchildren, it remains the classical statement of personnel policy as national destiny, quoted in every discussion of hiring and advisors.

常见问题chángjiàn wèntíFrequently Asked Questions
What does 小人 (xiǎorén) mean?
The petty person: small-minded, self-interested, quick to scheme and to resent. It is the fixed opposite of the 君子 (jūnzǐ, the gentleman of Confucian ethics). In modern speech, calling someone a 小人 accuses them of backstabbing and pettiness, not of small stature.
How did 小人 change meaning over time?
Originally it was a status word: the small people, commoners, as against 大人, the great. The Analects moralized the pair, redefining 君子 and 小人 by character rather than birth, so a noble could be a 小人 and a poor man a 君子. That redefinition is one of the hinges of Confucian ethics.
Why do characters in Chinese period dramas call themselves 小人?
As a humble first-person pronoun. In imperial-era speech, a commoner addressing an official called himself 小人 ('this lowly one') and the official 大人 ('great one'). Costume dramas preserve the usage, so viewers hear it constantly.
