离合词

líhécí

Words that unzip: 见面 is to meet, but meeting him is 跟他见面 and meeting once is 见一次面, because the "word" is secretly verb plus object.

~4 min read
词还是短语 cí háishi duǎnyǔ Word or Phrase?
离则合，合则离 · Apart When Together, Together When Apart

The name says it: 离合词, "separate-combine words." A large class of everyday two-syllable verbs look like single words in the dictionary but are internally verb + object: 睡觉 is sleep + a sleep, 吃饭 is eat + rice/meal, 唱歌 is sing + song, 见面 is see + face, 洗澡 is wash + bath, 结婚 is tie + marriage. Used whole, they behave like intransitive verbs: 我们明天见面 (we meet tomorrow). But the moment grammar needs a place to sit, the object half politely steps back and the machinery moves in between: 见了面 (met), 见过面 (have met before), 见个面 (meet up, casual), 睡了一觉 (had a sleep), 帮了我一个大忙 (did me a big favor).

Whether these are words or phrases is a genuine dispute in Chinese linguistics, and the honest answer is: both, which is what makes them a category. For the learner the practical rule is simpler. The dictionary lemma is a convenience; the grammar treats the two halves as verb and object, and everything else follows from that single fact.

怎么拆 zěnme chāi What Goes in the Gap
插入什么 chārù shénme · The Gap's Tenants 了 / 过 → 睡了觉 ; 见过面 (aspect attaches to the verb half)
个 (casualizer) → 见个面, 洗个澡, 帮个忙 : "grab a quick one"
duration / times → 睡了八个小时的觉 ; 见了三次面
possessive target → 生他的气 (angry at him) ; 帮我的忙 (help me)
reduplication → verb half only : 唱唱歌, 散散步 (never 唱歌唱歌)
Everything that would follow an ordinary verb slips inside the separable one
睡了一个好觉 · A Good Sleep, Slept

The gap's tenants are exactly the things that normally follow a verb: aspect particles, measure phrases, duration, and the beneficiary or target dressed as a possessor. That last one produces the constructions learners find most alien and natives find most natural: 生他的气, literally "generate his anger," means to be angry at him; 帮我的忙 is to help me; revenge dramas run on 报仇 splitting into 报了大仇. Duration goes inside because Chinese counts time on the object: eight hours of sleep is 八个小时的觉, a possessed sleep.

Reduplication, the softening doubling covered across this guide's grammar pages, copies only the verb half: 唱唱歌 (sing a little), 见见面 (meet briefly), 散散步 (take a stroll). And the casual 个, semantically almost empty, marks the activity as light and social: 改天见个面 ("let's grab a meet-up sometime") carries exactly the weight of its English translation, none.

宾语问题 bīnyǔ wèntí The Object Problem
跟他见面 · The 跟 Workaround

The signature error is bolting a second object onto a verb that already contains one: 见面他, 结婚她, 帮忙我 are all impossible, because 面, 婚, and 忙 occupy the object seat. The repair is a preposition fronting the person: 跟他见面 (meet with him), 跟她结婚 (marry her), 跟朋友聊天 (chat with friends), with 和 or 与 as register variants of 跟. For 帮忙 the alternative repair is to abandon the 忙 and use bare 帮, which takes objects freely: 帮我 (help me), 帮我买杯咖啡 (get me a coffee).

How do you know a verb is separable before it bites you? Heuristics: if the second syllable is a noun a dictionary lists independently (觉, 饭, 歌, 澡, 婚, 气), suspicion is warranted; if you have ever heard it split (见个面), the case is closed. Textbooks mark the class with a // in pinyin (jiàn//miàn), a notation worth learning to read as a warning label. When in doubt, front the person with 跟 and no harm can follow: the workaround is always grammatical, while the bolted-on object never is.

词汇 cíhuì The Everyday Set
见面 jiàn//miàn to meet (see-face)
V-O 离合词 líhécí
The class's poster child. First meeting: 第一次见面，请多关照 is the formal nice-to-meet-you. The person always arrives via 跟/和: 我明天跟客户见面.
我们好久没见面了，找时间见个面吧。
Wǒmen hǎojiǔ méi jiànmiàn le, zhǎo shíjiān jiàn ge miàn ba.
We haven't met in ages; let's find a time to get together.
帮忙 bāng//máng to help (lend-a-busy-hand)
V-O 离合词 líhécí
帮 (assist) + 忙 (busyness): to take on someone's busyness. The polite request is 帮个忙 or, upgraded, 帮我一个大忙 (do me a big favor). Direct objects go on bare 帮.
这件事你可帮了我一个大忙。
Zhè jiàn shì nǐ kě bāng le wǒ yí gè dà máng.
You really did me a huge favor with this.
生气 shēng//qì to get angry (generate-qi)
V-O 离合词 líhécí
Anger as qi production, TCM physiology fossilized in grammar. The target possesses the anger: 别生我的气 ("don't be angry at me"), the sentence every learner of Chinese eventually needs.
她还在生你的气，去道个歉吧。
Tā hái zài shēng nǐ de qì, qù dào ge qiàn ba.
She's still angry at you; go apologize.
结婚 jié//hūn to marry (tie-the-marriage)
V-O 离合词 líhécí
结 (tie, the knot exactly) + 婚 (marriage). One marries with someone: 跟她结婚. Having married twice is 结过两次婚, the split form carrying the count. Its opposite 离婚 separates identically.
他们认识十年，去年终于结婚了。
Tāmen rènshi shí nián, qùnián zhōngyú jiéhūn le.
They knew each other ten years and finally married last year.

常见问题chángjiàn wèntíFrequently Asked Questions
What is a separable verb (离合词) in Chinese?
A two-syllable verb that is internally verb + object and splits to admit grammar in the middle: 见面 (meet) becomes 见了面 (met), 见个面 (meet up), 见过一次面 (met once). Common members include 睡觉, 吃饭, 唱歌, 帮忙, 结婚, 生气, and 洗澡.
Why is 我见面他 wrong?
Because 见面 already contains its object: 面 (face). It cannot take another one. The person goes in front with a preposition: 跟他见面 ('meet with him'). The same fix serves 结婚 (跟她结婚) and 聊天 (跟朋友聊天).
How do you say 'help me' if 帮忙 is separable?
Either split it around the beneficiary, 帮我一个忙 ('do me a favor'), or drop the 忙 and use 帮 directly: 帮我 ('help me'). What you cannot say is 帮忙我, the classic separable-verb error.
