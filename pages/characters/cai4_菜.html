字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

菜 cài = 艹 (cǎo, the grass radical) + 采 (cǎi, to pick or gather). The top element 艹 is the grass radical, the two short strokes that mark a word as belonging to plants. Below it sits 采 cǎi, itself a picture of a hand (爫) reaching over a tree (木) to pluck its fruit. So 采 supplies both the sound (cǎi shifting to cài) and a fitting hint of meaning: 菜 is the plant you gather to eat, the greens picked for the pot.

From that picked plant the character branches into four everyday senses:

1. Vegetable, greens. The literal sense, the edible plant: 蔬菜 shūcài (vegetables), 青菜 qīngcài (green vegetables), 白菜 báicài (Chinese cabbage).
2. A dish, a course. Any prepared dish that reaches the table, not only the vegetable ones: 点菜 diǎn cài (to order dishes), 做菜 zuò cài (to cook), 菜单 càidān (menu), 一道菜 yī dào cài (one dish).
3. A cuisine, a cooking tradition. A whole regional style: 中国菜 Zhōngguó cài (Chinese food), 川菜 chuāncài (Sichuan cuisine), 粤菜 yuècài (Cantonese cuisine).
4. Colloquial: weak, lousy. Modern internet slang turns 菜 into an adjective for poor skill, as in 菜鸟 càiniǎo (a noob, literally a fledgling bird) or 太菜了 "so bad (at this)."

The thread running through every sense is the table. What starts as a plant in the field (vegetable) becomes a plate of cooked food (dish), then a named tradition of cooking it (cuisine), and finally, by a playful leap, a verdict on whether someone is any good. Wherever you meet 菜, the question is what is being served, or who is being served up.

蔬菜 shūcài Vegetables & Greens
蔬菜 shūcài vegetables (collectively)
N 名词 míngcí
蔬 shū (vegetables) + 菜 cài (greens). The formal, category word for vegetables as a food group, the term you see on nutrition labels and in recipes: 多吃蔬菜 "eat more vegetables."
这家店的蔬菜很新鲜。
Zhè jiā diàn de shūcài hěn xīnxiān.
The vegetables at this shop are very fresh.
白菜 báicài Chinese cabbage; bok choy family
N 名词 míngcí
白 bái (white) + 菜 cài (vegetable). The pale-stalked cabbage that anchors northern winter cooking. So cheap and plentiful that 白菜价 "cabbage price" means dirt cheap.
冬天我们常吃白菜。
Dōngtiān wǒmen cháng chī báicài.
In winter we often eat cabbage.
青菜 qīngcài green vegetables; leafy greens
N 名词 míngcí
青 qīng (green) + 菜 cài (vegetable). A general word for leafy green vegetables, and in the south the name of a specific small green cabbage. 一盘青菜 "a plate of greens."
医生说要多吃点青菜。
Yīshēng shuō yào duō chī diǎn qīngcài.
The doctor said to eat more greens.
辨析 biànxī · 菜 the plant vs. 菜 the dish When 菜 names a raw or growing plant it is the vegetable sense (蔬菜, 白菜). When it names what arrives at the table cooked, it is the dish sense (做菜, 一道菜). The same syllable covers both the ingredient in the field and the finished course on the plate, and only context tells you which is meant.
点菜 diǎn cài Dishes & Cooking
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

At the restaurant 菜 stops meaning the plant and starts meaning the plate. A 菜 here is any cooked dish, meat as readily as vegetable, and the verbs that surround it map a whole meal. You 点菜 diǎn cài (point at items to order them, the 点 of "to choose a dot on a list"), the kitchen 做菜 zuò cài (makes the dishes), and the food arrives as 一道菜 yī dào cài (one course) after another. To see the broader life of 点, from dots and o'clock to ordering, visit the 点 entry.

The list you order from is the 菜单 càidān, literally the "dish slip." Cook for guests at home and you serve 几个菜 jǐ ge cài (a few dishes); the unmarked staples a family eats day to day are 家常菜 jiāchángcài, "home-regular dishes." In every case 菜 has shifted from ingredient to outcome: not the greens in the basket, but the food on the table.

点菜 diǎn cài to order dishes (at a restaurant)
V 动词 dòngcí
点 diǎn (to choose; to dot) + 菜 cài (dish). To pick dishes off a menu and order them. The host usually does the 点菜 for the table. For the many senses of 点 see the 点 entry.
你来点菜吧。
Nǐ lái diǎn cài ba.
Why don't you order the dishes.
做菜 zuò cài to cook; to prepare dishes
V 动词 dòngcí
做 zuò (to make) + 菜 cài (dish). To cook, in the sense of making the dishes for a meal. 他很会做菜 "he is a good cook." Its near-neighbor 做饭 zuò fàn frames the same act around the staple rice.
妈妈正在厨房做菜。
Māma zhèngzài chúfáng zuò cài.
Mom is cooking in the kitchen.
菜单 càidān menu; (computing) a menu
N 名词 míngcí
菜 cài (dish) + 单 dān (list; slip). The list of dishes a restaurant offers, and by extension the drop-down 菜单 of a computer program. 看菜单 "to look at the menu."
服务员，请给我菜单。
Fúwùyuán, qǐng gěi wǒ càidān.
Waiter, please give me the menu.
川菜 chuāncài Regional Cuisines
文化洞见 wénhuà dòngjiàn · Cultural Insight

Stretch 菜 wider still and it names a whole cooking tradition. 中国菜 Zhōngguó cài is Chinese food as a category, and within it the regions divide into named schools, each a place-name plus 菜. The Eight Great Cuisines (八大菜系 bā dà càixì) include 川菜 chuāncài, the bold ma-la heat of Sichuan; 粤菜 yuècài, the light, fresh Cantonese style of the south; 鲁菜 lǔcài of Shandong; and 苏菜 sūcài of Jiangsu, among others. To call a restaurant a 川菜馆 is to promise Sichuan peppercorn and chili.

The same frame works for foreign food: 日本菜 Rìběn cài (Japanese food), 法国菜 Fǎguó cài (French food), 西餐 xīcān being the broader word for Western food. So 菜, which began as a single picked plant, ends up labeling the entire culinary map, one region or nation at a time.

川菜 chuāncài Sichuan cuisine
N 名词 míngcí
川 chuān (Sichuan) + 菜 cài (cuisine). The cooking of Sichuan, famous for the numbing-spicy 麻辣 málà of Sichuan peppercorn and chili. 麻婆豆腐 and 回锅肉 are classic 川菜 dishes.
我很喜欢吃川菜。
Wǒ hěn xǐhuan chī chuāncài.
I really like Sichuan food.
粤菜 yuècài Cantonese cuisine
N 名词 míngcí
粤 yuè (Guangdong; Canton) + 菜 cài (cuisine). The cooking of Guangdong, prized for lightness, freshness, and steaming that keeps each ingredient tasting of itself. Dim sum (点心) belongs to this tradition.
粤菜比较清淡。
Yuècài bǐjiào qīngdàn.
Cantonese food is relatively light.
辨析 biànxī · 菜 vs. 饭 A Chinese meal pairs 菜 cài (the dishes) with 饭 fàn (the staple, usually rice). 吃饭 chī fàn means to eat a meal in general, while the 菜 are the accompanying courses. To say a dinner had 四菜一汤 "four dishes and a soup" is to count the 菜 against the 饭 that underlies them.
菜鸟 càiniǎo (slang) novice; newbie; noob
N 名词 míngcí
菜 cài (here: weak, lousy) + 鸟 niǎo (bird). A fledgling, hence a beginner who is still bad at something, especially in gaming and the workplace. Self-deprecating or teasing: 我是个菜鸟 "I'm a total newbie."
别笑我，我还是个菜鸟。
Bié xiào wǒ, wǒ háishì ge càiniǎo.
Don't laugh at me, I'm still a newbie.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
面有菜色 miàn yǒu cài sè a face with the color of greens: gaunt and sallow from hunger Lit: face-has-vegetable-color. 菜色 is the wan, greenish pallor of someone who has eaten nothing but wild greens, with no grain or meat. The phrase paints a face worn down by famine or hardship.
小菜一碟 xiǎo cài yī dié a small dish on a plate: a piece of cake, an easy matter Lit: small-dish-one-saucer. A 小菜 is a minor side dish, trivial next to the main course, so 小菜一碟 means a task so easy it is hardly worth mentioning. 这对我来说是小菜一碟 "this is a piece of cake for me."
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

See a hand reaching over a plant: that is 采 cǎi, to pick, sheltered under the grass radical 艹. Together they are 菜, the greens gathered to be eaten. Hold that one image of picking plants and the senses fan out from it like dishes carried to a table.

Picked from the field, it is a vegetable (蔬菜, 白菜). Cooked and carried out, it is a dish (做菜, 点菜, 菜单). Named by its region, it is a cuisine (川菜, 粤菜). And turned into slang, it is the verdict on a fledgling who is still no good at the task (菜鸟). Whenever you meet 菜, picture the picked plant first, then ask whether it is still in the basket, on the plate, on the regional map, or aimed as a tease.
