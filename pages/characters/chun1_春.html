字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

Two stacked elements sit over a single anchor: 春 = the sprout-cluster over 屯 (the upper graph), with 日 (the sun) holding the base. 日 is the semantic anchor, sunlight as the engine of the season. The upper element traces back to a drawing of young shoots pushing up out of the ground, the same growth imagery that survives in 屯 (zhūn/tún), a graph of a seedling straining against hard earth as it breaks the surface.

Read the picture from the bottom up: the sun shines, and under that warmth the new shoots strain upward. The oracle-bone and bronze forms show plant strokes clustered around the seedling graph with a sun riding alongside or below, the whole composition saying one thing, the moment when light reaches the cold ground and growth begins. By the small-seal stage the components had compacted into the tight three-part stack standard today, the plant strokes fused into the flat top, 日 squared off at the foot.

The concrete sense (the growing season) extended early into the abstract. If spring is the surge of green after winter, then 春 becomes the surge of life in anything: youth (青春 qīngchūn, "green spring"), vigor, and from there the warmth of erotic feeling. Classical poetry uses 春 for desire so consistently that 春情 (chūnqíng) and 怀春 (huáichūn, "to harbor spring") read as longing without any further gloss. The same logic runs into the calendar, where 春 caps the year's first season and names its greatest festival.

Japanese keeps the graph as 春 (haru / shun), Korean as 춘 (chun), both carrying the season and the same overtone of youth and stirring feeling, a shared East Asian reading of one sun over one field of sprouts.

季节jìjiéThe Season on the Calendar
春天chūntiānspring; springtime
N 名词 míngcí
春 (spring) + 天 (tiān, day/season). The everyday word for the season, the one you reach for in conversation. 天 here marks a stretch of time rather than a single day, the same suffix in 夏天 (summer) and 冬天 (winter).
春天来了，树都绿了。
Chūntiān lái le, shù dōu lǜ le.
Spring has come, and the trees have all turned green.
我最喜欢北京的春天。
Wǒ zuì xǐhuan Běijīng de chūntiān.
Spring is my favorite season in Beijing.
辨析 biànxī · 春天 vs. 春季 春天 is the warm spoken word; 春季 (chūnjì) is the calendar-quarter term used in weather reports, schedules, and formal writing.
春节chūnjiéSpring Festival; Chinese New Year
N 名词 míngcí
春 (spring) + 节 (jié, festival/node). The lunar new year, the largest holiday in the Chinese world. The name frames the new year as the gate of spring, the festival that opens the growing season rather than just turning the calendar.
每年春节，我们全家都回老家。
Měi nián chūnjié, wǒmen quánjiā dōu huí lǎojiā.
Every Spring Festival, the whole family goes back to our hometown.
辨析 biànxī · 春节 vs. 新年 春节 is specifically the lunar new year and its festival; 新年 (xīnnián) can mean either the lunar or the January-1 new year depending on context.
立春lìchūnStart of Spring (solar term)
N 名词 míngcí
立 (lì, to stand/establish) + 春 (spring). The first of the twenty-four solar terms (节气 jiéqì), falling around February 4, when the year's farming cycle is reckoned to begin. 立 marks a season being set up, the same verb in 立夏, 立秋, 立冬.
过了立春，天气慢慢暖和起来。
Guò le lìchūn, tiānqì mànman nuǎnhuo qǐlái.
After the Start of Spring, the weather gradually warms up.
生机shēngjīVitality, Youth & Feeling
引申义 yǐnshēnyì · Extended Sense

Once spring means "the surge of new life," the character travels outward from the calendar. 春 stands in for youth, for vigor, and for the warmth of desire. The shoots breaking ground become the same image used for a young person's energy and for the first stir of love, which is why the cluster below reaches from 青春 (the green years) to 春风 (a spring wind that, by extension, means kindness and good fortune).

青春qīngchūnyouth; the prime of life
N 名词 míngcí
青 (qīng, green/blue, the color of new growth) + 春 (spring). "Green spring," the years of youth at their freshest. Used for the whole season of being young, often with a note of how briefly it lasts.
青春一去不复返。
Qīngchūn yī qù bù fù fǎn.
Youth, once gone, never returns.
他把青春献给了教育事业。
Tā bǎ qīngchūn xiàngěi le jiàoyù shìyè.
He gave his youth to the cause of education.
春风chūnfēngspring breeze; warmth, favor
N 名词 míngcí
春 (spring) + 风 (fēng, wind). The mild wind of spring, and by extension any warm, reviving influence, a kind teacher, good news, a stroke of fortune. The phrase 春风得意 (chūnfēng déyì) describes riding high on success.
老师的鼓励像一阵春风。
Lǎoshī de gǔlì xiàng yī zhèn chūnfēng.
The teacher's encouragement was like a spring breeze.
辨析 biànxī · literal vs. figurative Weather reports use 春风 for the literal wind; in praise and poetry it almost always carries the warmth-and-favor sense.
春意chūnyìthe feel of spring; signs of spring
N 名词 míngcí
春 (spring) + 意 (yì, sense/intent). The atmosphere of spring in the air, the green budding and softening light that signal the season has arrived. 春意盎然 (chūnyì àngrán) describes a scene brimming with spring.
公园里春意盎然，到处是花。
Gōngyuán lǐ chūnyì àngrán, dàochù shì huā.
The park is brimming with spring, flowers everywhere.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
春暖花开chūn nuǎn huā kāispring is warm and flowers bloom: the good season has comeThe stock image of spring at its best. Used literally for the season and figuratively for the right moment, a turn for the better after hardship.
春风得意chūn fēng dé yìriding the spring wind, full of pride: flush with successFrom Mèng Jiāo's Tang poem on passing the imperial exam. Describes someone on a winning streak, often with a hint that the pride may be premature.
雨后春笋yǔ hòu chūn sǔnspring bamboo shoots after rain: springing up everywhere at onceThe image of bamboo shoots erupting after spring rain. Modern usage describes new businesses, buildings, or trends multiplying rapidly.
妙手回春miào shǒu huí chūnskilled hands bring back spring: a doctor who restores the dying to healthHigh praise for a physician's skill, "to bring spring back" to a failing body. Often written on a banner gifted to a doctor.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture the morning after the last frost. A low sun (日, the base of the character) clears the horizon and lands on a bare field. Where the light touches, green shoots strain upward through the cold crust, that straining seedling is the 屯 wedged in the middle, and the sprout-strokes fan out across the top. The whole glyph is one frame, sun at the foot pushing growth up through the body of the character.

Hold that frame and the compounds fall into place. 春天 (chūntiān) is simply that field across a whole season. 青春 (qīngchūn) is the same shoots, now standing for a young life still rising. 春风 (chūnfēng) is the warm wind crossing the field, the breath that coaxes the green up, which is why it also means kindness and good fortune.

For the festival, see the field at dawn on lunar new year, red couplets (春联) on the doorframe, the household stepping out into the first light of the growing year. Sun under shoots: that single image carries the season, the youth, the warmth, and the holiday all at once.
