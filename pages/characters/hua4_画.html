字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

画 huà began as a hand holding a brush, drawing the boundaries of a field. The traditional form 畫 makes this vivid: at the top sits 聿 yù, the picture of a hand gripping a writing brush, and below it 田 tián, a field, framed by lines that mark out its edges. The original act was not art but demarcation: to take a brush and draw the limits of a plot of land, to delimit and bound. From "to draw a boundary line" the meaning broadened to drawing of every kind, then to the picture that results.

The simplified form 画 keeps the field 田 at its center and folds the brush and the bounding lines into the enclosing frame around it. The radical is 田 tián, the field whose edges were the first thing 画 ever drew. The 画 / 畫 pair is one glyph in two scripts: simplified 画 on the mainland and in Singapore, traditional 畫 in Taiwan, Hong Kong, and classical texts.

Three senses branch from the brush on the field:

1. To draw, to paint. The action of making a picture: 画画 huà huà (to draw, to paint), 画家 huàjiā (painter), 画图 huàtú (to draw a diagram).
2. A painting, a picture. The result of that action: 图画 túhuà (picture), 国画 guóhuà (Chinese painting), 漫画 mànhuà (comics, manga).
3. A stroke; to mark out. The single line of a written character, and by extension to delimit: 笔画 bǐhuà (the strokes of a character), 画线 huà xiàn (to draw a line).

The thread through all three is the brush laying down a line. Whether it bounds a field, shapes a landscape, or forms one stroke of a character, 画 is the deliberate mark a hand makes when it sets brush to surface.

画画 huà huà To Paint & Painters
画画 huà huà to draw; to paint
V 动词 dòngcí
画 huà (to draw) + 画 huà (a picture). A verb-object pair, literally "to draw a drawing," the everyday way to say one is drawing or painting. The first 画 is the action, the second is what it produces.
孩子喜欢画画。
Háizi xǐhuan huà huà.
Children love to draw.
她在纸上画了一只猫。
Tā zài zhǐ shàng huà le yī zhī māo.
She drew a cat on the paper.
画家 huàjiā painter; artist
N 名词 míngcí
画 huà (painting) + 家 jiā (an expert; a -ist). One whose vocation is painting. The suffix 家 marks a master of a field, as in 作家 zuòjiā (writer) and 音乐家 yīnyuèjiā (musician).
他是一位有名的画家。
Tā shì yī wèi yǒumíng de huàjiā.
He is a famous painter.
绘画 huìhuà painting; drawing (as an art form)
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
绘 huì (to depict) + 画 huà (to paint). A more formal word than 画画, naming painting as a discipline or art form: 绘画艺术 "the art of painting." Used in writing and in talk about fine art rather than casual sketching.
他对绘画很有天赋。
Tā duì huìhuà hěn yǒu tiānfù.
He has a real talent for painting.
辨析 biànxī · 画画 vs. 绘画 画画 is the everyday verb for the act, what a child or a hobbyist does. 绘画 is formal and names painting as an art or field, the word a museum or a textbook uses. You say 我喜欢画画 ("I like to draw"), but a wall label reads 中国绘画 ("Chinese painting").
图画 túhuà Pictures & the Painting Tradition
文化洞见 wénhuà dòngjiàn · Cultural Insight

The picture sense of 画 opens onto one of the oldest arts of the civilization. 国画 guóhuà, literally "national painting," is the name for traditional Chinese painting: ink and mineral colors on silk or paper, brushed rather than filled in, and sharing its tools and gestures with calligraphy. The same brush, the same ink, and the same trained wrist make both a character and a landscape, which is why the two arts are spoken of together as 书画 shūhuà (calligraphy and painting).

Set against 国画 is 油画 yóuhuà, "oil painting," the Western tradition that arrived later and is named for its medium rather than its nation. And at the popular end of the spectrum sits 漫画 mànhuà, the word that covers comics, cartoons, and the Japanese manga whose name is written with these very characters, read in Japanese as manga. One root, 画, reaches from the silk scroll in a museum to the comic page on a newsstand.

图画 túhuà picture; drawing; illustration
N 名词 míngcí
图 tú (chart; image) + 画 huà (painting). A general word for a picture or illustration, the kind in a children's book or on a wall. Broader and plainer than 国画 or 油画, which name specific traditions.
这本书里有很多图画。
Zhè běn shū lǐ yǒu hěn duō túhuà.
There are many pictures in this book.
国画 guóhuà traditional Chinese painting
N 名词 míngcí
国 guó (nation) + 画 huà (painting). The native tradition of brush-and-ink painting on silk or paper, kin to calligraphy. Short for 中国画 Zhōngguóhuà, and set against 油画 yóuhuà, the imported oil-painting tradition.
他从小就学国画。
Tā cóngxiǎo jiù xué guóhuà.
He has studied Chinese painting since childhood.
漫画 mànhuà comics; cartoon; manga
N 名词 míngcí
漫 màn (free; unrestrained) + 画 huà (drawing). A free, sketchy drawing: the word for comics and cartoons. The same two characters name Japanese manga, where they are read manga.
他每天都看漫画。
Tā měitiān dōu kàn mànhuà.
He reads comics every day.
笔画 bǐhuà Strokes & Marking: 画 vs. 划
辨析洞见 biànxī dòngjiàn · Distinction Insight

The third sense of 画 is the most concrete: a single stroke. 笔画 bǐhuà is the technical word for the strokes that make up a written character, the basic units of the writing system. To ask 这个字有几画 ("how many strokes does this character have") is to count its 画. Here the brush has narrowed all the way down to one line on the page.

This is where 画 huà brushes up against its near-twin 划 huà. The two share a sound and a root idea of a drawn line, and they overlap in some words, but they pull in different directions. 画 leans toward drawing and pictures: 画画, 画家, 笔画. 划 leans toward cutting, marking, and dividing a line through something: 划船 huá chuán (to row a boat, here read huá), 划分 huàfēn (to demarcate), 计划 jìhuà (a plan, a marked-out course). When you mean to depict or to picture, reach for 画; when you mean to slice, rule, or divide, reach for 划.

笔画 bǐhuà stroke (of a Chinese character)
N 名词 míngcí
笔 bǐ (pen; brush) + 画 huà (a stroke). One of the basic lines that compose a character. The number of 笔画 is how characters are counted and ordered in stroke-count dictionaries.
这个字有十二笔画。
Zhège zì yǒu shí'èr bǐhuà.
This character has twelve strokes.
油画 yóuhuà oil painting
N 名词 míngcí
油 yóu (oil) + 画 huà (painting). The Western art of painting in oils, named for its medium. Its native counterpart is 国画 guóhuà, named for its nation rather than its material.
墙上挂着一幅油画。
Qiáng shàng guà zhe yī fú yóuhuà.
An oil painting hangs on the wall.
画面 huàmiàn a scene; an image (on a screen or in a picture); a frame
N 名词 míngcí
画 huà (picture) + 面 miàn (surface; face). The visible surface of a picture or screen: the framed image you see in a film, a photo, or a painting. 画面很美 "the image is beautiful."
电影的画面非常漂亮。
Diànyǐng de huàmiàn fēicháng piàoliang.
The film's imagery is very beautiful.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
画蛇添足 huà shé tiān zú to draw a snake and add feet: to ruin a thing by overdoing it Lit: draw-snake-add-feet. From a contest to draw a snake fastest: the man who finished first kept going and gave his snake legs, so he lost. The lesson is that a needless addition spoils what was already complete.
画龙点睛 huà lóng diǎn jīng to paint a dragon and dot its eyes: the finishing touch that brings it to life Lit: paint-dragon-dot-eyes. A legendary painter left his dragons eyeless, saying they would fly off if completed; when he dotted the eyes, they did. The phrase praises the one small stroke that makes a whole work come alive.
画饼充饥 huà bǐng chōng jī to draw a cake to ease hunger: to comfort oneself with empty fantasy Lit: draw-cake-fill-hunger. A painted cake feeds no one. The idiom mocks any plan or promise that satisfies in imagination only, offering the picture of a thing in place of the thing itself.
指手画脚 zhǐ shǒu huà jiǎo to point with the hands and gesture with the feet: to boss others around or gesticulate wildly Lit: point-hands-draw-feet. It paints someone waving their limbs to give orders or commentary, the busybody who criticizes and directs others without doing the work themselves.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

See the traditional form 畫 whole: a hand grips a brush, 聿, and below it lies a field, 田, with lines drawn around its edges. The first thing 画 ever did was bound a plot of land. Hold that brush over that field and watch the line it draws do three jobs in turn.

Let the line trace a scene and you have painting (画画, 国画, 漫画). Let it settle into one mark on the page and you have a single stroke of a character (笔画). Let it rule a border and you have delimiting, the sense that hands off to its twin 划 (划分). The radical 田 keeps the memory honest: every 画 is a hand setting brush to a surface and drawing a deliberate line.
