字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

常 cháng = 尚 (shàng, the phonetic) + 巾 (jīn, cloth). A pictophonetic compound: 巾, the cloth radical at the base, supplies the meaning, while 尚 on top supplies the sound (shàng → cháng). The original referent was a long lower garment, a length of cloth that hung full and steady. From "the long, full-hanging cloth" the character generalized to its lasting modern senses, and the concrete garment meaning was later written with the separate character 裳.

Three senses grow from the idea of what hangs steady and endures:

1. Constant, lasting, unchanging. What persists rather than passing: 经常 jīngcháng (regularly), 常常 chángcháng (often), and the philosophical 无常 wúcháng (impermanence).
2. Ordinary, common, normal. What is usual rather than special: 平常 píngcháng (ordinary), 正常 zhèngcháng (normal), 通常 tōngcháng (usually).
3. The constant principle. The standing rule or shared ground: 常识 chángshí (common sense), and the classical 纲常 gāngcháng, the constant bonds of moral order.

The thread is endurance. Whether the subject is how often something happens, how ordinary it is, or what truth holds firm over time, 常 names what does not change: the steady cloth of the everyday.

经常 jīngcháng Often & Frequent
经常 jīngcháng often; frequently; regularly
ADV 副词 fùcí
经 jīng (to pass through; constant) + 常 cháng (constant). The standard adverb for an action that recurs as a regular habit: 我经常去那家店 "I go to that shop often." Stresses regularity, not just repetition.
他经常迟到。
Tā jīngcháng chídào.
He is often late.
我们经常一起吃饭。
Wǒmen jīngcháng yìqǐ chī fàn.
We often eat together.
辨析 biànxī · 经常 vs. 常常 经常 leans on settled regularity and reads slightly more written: 经常锻炼 "exercises regularly." 常常 is warmer and more colloquial, simply "often, time and again": 他常常忘事 "he often forgets things." In most everyday sentences they are interchangeable.
常常 chángcháng often; frequently; time and again
ADV 副词 fùcí
The reduplicated 常. A common, conversational adverb for frequent action. Its negative is usually 不常 bùcháng "not often," since 不常常 is avoided.
小时候我常常去外婆家。
Xiǎoshíhou wǒ chángcháng qù wàipó jiā.
As a child I often went to my grandmother's house.
平常 píngcháng Ordinary & Usual
平常 píngcháng ordinary; common; usually, ordinarily
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
平 píng (level; plain) + 常 cháng (ordinary). Both an adjective (unremarkable, common) and a time word (in ordinary times, usually): 平常的一天 "an ordinary day," 我平常六点起床 "I usually get up at six."
他平常很少说话。
Tā píngcháng hěn shǎo shuōhuà.
He usually says very little.
辨析 biànxī · 经常 vs. 平常 经常 measures how frequently an action happens (often). 平常 describes the ordinary state of things, or means "in ordinary times." 他经常运动 "he exercises often" describes frequency; 他平常不运动 "ordinarily he doesn't exercise" describes the usual baseline.
通常 tōngcháng usually; normally; in general
ADV 副词 fùcí
通 tōng (through; general) + 常 cháng (usual). What generally holds as a rule, slightly more formal than 平常: 通常情况下 "under normal circumstances."
我通常坐地铁上班。
Wǒ tōngcháng zuò dìtiě shàngbān.
I usually take the subway to work.
正常 zhèngcháng normal; regular
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
正 zhèng (proper; upright) + 常 cháng (ordinary). On the standard track, as things should be: 一切正常 "everything is normal." Its opposite is 反常 fǎncháng or 不正常.
他的反应很正常。
Tā de fǎnyìng hěn zhèngcháng.
His reaction is perfectly normal.
常识 chángshí The Constant & Common
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Beneath frequency and ordinariness lies the oldest sense of 常: the constant, the principle that holds firm. 常识 chángshí, "constant knowledge," is the common sense everyone is assumed to share. In classical thought the 五常 wǔcháng are the five constant virtues, and 纲常 gāngcháng names the enduring bonds of the moral order. Against this stands the Buddhist 无常 wúcháng, impermanence: the teaching that nothing in fact stays constant, that all things rise and pass.

This is why 常 can feel reassuring and unremarkable at once. What is 常 is what you can count on, the steady backdrop that does not surprise you. To say a thing is 反常 fǎncháng, against the constant, is to mark it as strange or alarming precisely because the 常 is what we expect the world to keep doing.

常识 chángshí common sense; general knowledge
N 名词 míngcí
常 cháng (constant; common) + 识 shí (knowledge). The basic knowledge that holds across a community, what one is expected to know: 生活常识 "everyday know-how," 缺乏常识 "to lack common sense."
这是基本的安全常识。
Zhè shì jīběn de ānquán chángshí.
This is basic safety common sense.
日常 rìcháng daily; everyday; day-to-day
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
日 rì (day) + 常 cháng (constant). What recurs as part of ordinary daily life: 日常生活 "daily life," 日常工作 "routine work."
运动已经成了我的日常。
Yùndòng yǐjīng chéng le wǒ de rìcháng.
Exercise has become part of my daily routine.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
习以为常 xí yǐ wéi cháng to grow so used to a thing that it seems ordinary Lit: practice-take-to-be-normal. What was once strange or hard becomes routine through repetition, until one no longer notices it. Often said of bad habits or hardships one has stopped questioning.
家常便饭 jiā cháng biàn fàn an everyday home meal: a common, unremarkable occurrence Lit: home-ordinary-simple-meal. Literally plain home cooking, used figuratively for anything that happens so routinely it is no big deal: 加班对他来说是家常便饭 "working late is routine for him."
反复无常 fǎn fù wú cháng to flip back and forth with no constancy: fickle and unpredictable Lit: repeatedly-without-constancy. A criticism of someone whose moods, promises, or positions keep changing, so that no one can rely on them.
老生常谈 lǎo shēng cháng tán an old scholar's stock talk: a hackneyed, oft-repeated remark Lit: old-scholar-often-says. A truism so worn from repetition that it carries little weight, the Chinese equivalent of a tired platitude.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a long banner of cloth, 巾, hanging full and steady in the same place day after day. That steadiness is the whole character. What is 常 is what stays: it hangs there always, unchanging, ordinary precisely because it is always there.

From that steady cloth, three readings follow. An action that recurs as steadily as the banner hangs is done 经常, often. A thing as unremarkable as the everyday backdrop is 平常, ordinary. And a truth that holds firm over time is the 常 of 常识, common sense. When something breaks the pattern, it is 反常, against the constant, and that is exactly why it startles.
