字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

全 quán shows 入 (rù, enter) standing over 玉 / 王 (yù, jade). The top element is 入, to enter or bring in, and below it sits the jade radical, written 王 in this position. Read together, the early sense is the jade brought in and found flawless, pure and without crack or flaw. From unblemished jade came the whole field of meaning: complete, intact, perfect, and from there simply whole or entire.

The traditional and simplified forms are identical, so 全 is one glyph in both scripts. The jade element appears as 王 here because the radical 玉 drops its dot when it sits as a component, the same shape that anchors 玉, 国, and 班.

Three senses branch from the flawless jade:

1. Whole, entire, all. The complete set with nothing left out: 全部 quánbù (all of it), 全体 quántǐ (the entire body of people), 全国 quánguó (the whole country), 全家 quánjiā (the whole family).
2. Complete, intact. Whole in the sense of unbroken and lacking nothing: 完全 wánquán (completely), 齐全 qíquán (complete in range), 保全 bǎoquán (to preserve intact).
3. Entirely (adverb). Sweeping up the whole of something at once: 全都 quándōu (all, every one), 全靠 quánkào (to rely entirely on).

The thread is wholeness with nothing missing and nothing flawed. Whether the field is a set of things (the entire country), a condition (intact and unbroken), or the reach of an action (relying wholly on one thing), 全 names the complete and unblemished whole.

构词 gòucí Word-Formation Patterns
构词规律 gòucí guīlǜ · Nothing Left Out 全 as whole / all → 全部 all · 全体 the entire group · 全国 the whole country · 全家 the whole family
全 as complete / intact → 完全 completely · 齐全 complete in range · 保全 to preserve intact · 健全 sound and whole
全 as entirely (adverb) → 全都 every one · 全靠 to rely wholly on · 全力 with full strength · 全新 brand new
全部 quánbù Whole & All
全部 quánbù all; the whole; entire
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
全 quán (whole) + 部 bù (part; section). The complete set of parts, every one included: 全部作品 "the complete works," 全部完成 "all finished." It points at the totality of a definite group of things.
这是我全部的钱。
Zhè shì wǒ quánbù de qián.
This is all the money I have.
他看完了全部的书。
Tā kàn wán le quánbù de shū.
He finished reading all the books.
辨析 biànxī · 全部 vs. 所有 全部 and 所有 suǒyǒu both translate as "all," but they sit differently. 所有 means "all that exist," sweeping over every member of a category: 所有的人 "everyone." 全部 stresses the whole of a definite, bounded set as one complete block: 全部的人 "the whole group of them." Often interchangeable, but 全部 can also stand alone as "all of it" while 所有 must lean on a following noun.
全体 quántǐ all (of a group of people); the entire body
N 名词 míngcí
全 quán (whole) + 体 tǐ (body). The entire body of people taken together, used of members, staff, or citizens: 全体学生 "all the students," 全体起立 "everybody stand up." Where 全部 counts things, 全体 gathers people.
全体员工都来了。
Quántǐ yuángōng dōu lái le.
All the staff have arrived.
全国 quánguó the whole country; nationwide
N 名词 míngcí
全 quán (whole) + 国 guó (country). The entire nation as one extent: 全国人民 "the whole nation's people," 全国各地 "all parts of the country." A model for 全 + place: 全省 the whole province, 全市 the whole city.
这个消息传遍了全国。
Zhège xiāoxi chuán biàn le quánguó.
The news spread across the whole country.
全家 quánjiā the whole family; the entire household
N 名词 míngcí
全 quán (whole) + 家 jiā (family; home). The family taken as a complete unit: 全家福 "a whole-family photo," 全家团圆 "the whole family reunited." A warm, everyday use of the whole sense.
我们全家去旅行。
Wǒmen quánjiā qù lǚxíng.
Our whole family is going on a trip.
完全 wánquán Complete & Intact
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The second sense reaches back to the flawless jade: not "all the members of a set," but "whole, intact, lacking nothing." When 全 pairs with 完 wán (finished) to make 完全, it becomes the everyday adverb for "completely," covering both the positive (完全正确, completely correct) and the negative (完全不懂, do not understand at all). The wholeness here is qualitative, a thing that is sound and entire rather than partial or broken.

The same sense drives 齐全 qíquán, said of a stock or a range that is complete with nothing missing, and 保全 bǎoquán, to keep something intact and unharmed. Listen for the contrast with 缺 quē (to lack) and 残 cán (broken, incomplete): wherever a thing is unbroken, sound, and entire, 全 is the word that names it.

完全 wánquán completely; entirely; fully
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
完 wán (finished; whole) + 全 quán (complete). The standard adverb for "completely," to the full degree with nothing left over: 完全同意 "completely agree," 完全没问题 "no problem at all." Negated, it gives "not at all": 完全不对.
我完全同意你的看法。
Wǒ wánquán tóngyì nǐ de kànfǎ.
I completely agree with your view.
齐全 qíquán complete (in range); all there; fully stocked
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
齐 qí (even; all present) + 全 quán (complete). Said of a set or selection where every needed item is on hand: 设备齐全 "fully equipped," 品种齐全 "a complete range of varieties." Stresses that nothing in the expected range is missing.
这家商店的商品很齐全。
Zhè jiā shāngdiàn de shāngpǐn hěn qíquán.
This shop has a very complete selection of goods.
保全 bǎoquán to preserve intact; to keep safe and whole
V 动词 dòngcí
保 bǎo (to protect) + 全 quán (intact). To keep something whole and unharmed through a threat: 保全性命 "to preserve one's life," 保全名誉 "to keep one's reputation intact." The intact sense made active.
他想办法保全了公司。
Tā xiǎng bànfǎ bǎoquán le gōngsī.
He found a way to keep the company intact.
全都 quándōu Entirely: the Sweeping 全
语法洞见 yǔfǎ dòngjiàn · Grammatical Insight

Standing before a verb, 全 works as an adverb meaning "entirely, all of it." It often teams with 都 dōu (all) to make 全都 quándōu, doubling the sense of totality: 他们全都来了 "every single one of them came," 钱全都花光了 "the money is all spent." The 全 sweeps over the whole subject or object, and 都 echoes it, so nothing escapes.

The adverbial 全 also fronts a verb to say "rely wholly on" or "act with the full of": 全靠 quánkào "to depend entirely on" (这件事全靠你了, "this whole thing rests entirely on you"), 全力 quánlì "with all one's strength." This is the temporal and dynamic cousin of the static whole: where 全部 gathers a set of things, the adverbial 全 gathers the whole force or reach of an action and throws it at one point.

全都 quándōu all; every one; without exception
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
全 quán (entirely) + 都 dōu (all). A reinforced "all," placed before the verb to stress that the whole group is included: 答案全都对 "every answer is right." The two totality words stack for emphasis.
他说的我全都听懂了。
Tā shuō de wǒ quándōu tīng dǒng le.
I understood everything he said.
全靠 quánkào to rely entirely on; to owe it all to
V 动词 dòngcí
全 quán (entirely) + 靠 kào (to lean on; to depend). To depend wholly on one person or thing, with nothing else to fall back on: 全靠大家的努力 "thanks entirely to everyone's effort."
这次成功全靠你帮忙。
Zhè cì chénggōng quánkào nǐ bāngmáng.
This success is owed entirely to your help.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
全力以赴 quán lì yǐ fù to go all out: to throw one's full strength at a task Lit: with-full-strength-to-go-toward. To commit every ounce of effort to something, holding nothing back. The everyday phrase for total commitment, whether to a contest, a project, or a rescue.
十全十美 shí quán shí měi perfect in every way: complete and flawless, ten parts whole and ten parts fine Lit: ten-whole-ten-beautiful. Describes something so complete and fine that nothing could be added or improved. Often used with a wry note, since few things truly are 十全十美.
全神贯注 quán shén guàn zhù fully absorbed: to concentrate every bit of attention on one thing Lit: whole-spirit-pour-into. With one's entire mind poured into a single focus, oblivious to all else. The phrase for deep, undivided concentration.
委曲求全 wěi qū qiú quán to compromise to keep the peace: to bend and yield so the whole holds together Lit: bend-crooked-seek-wholeness. To swallow grievances and make concessions in order to preserve a relationship or an arrangement intact. Here 全 is the intact whole one bends in order to save.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a piece of jade carried in through a doorway, 入 over 玉, and held up to the light: turn it, and there is no crack, no flaw, no missing corner. That flawless, complete stone is the whole of 全. Everything the character means is jade that lacks nothing.

On a set of things, that wholeness is "all of them" (全部, 全国, 全家). On a condition, it is "intact and unbroken" (完全, 齐全, 保全). On an action, it is the full force thrown at one point (全力以赴, 全靠). Whenever you meet 全, ask what whole is being held up to the light, and you will find it complete, with nothing left out.
