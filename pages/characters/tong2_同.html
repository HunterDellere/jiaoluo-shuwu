字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

同 tóng shows a 口 (kǒu, mouth) set under an enclosing top. It is an associative compound: the outer frame is read as a cover or a single rim, and beneath it sits the mouth. The image traditionally drawn from this is of many voices speaking from under one roof, sounding as one, hence the core sense of being together and the same. Whatever the precise origin of the upper element, the meaning that settled on the glyph is unity: things brought under a common cover, voices in unison.

From that unity the character spreads into three working senses:

1. Same, alike. Sharing one nature: 相同 xiāngtóng (identical), 同样 tóngyàng (the same, likewise), and the negative 不同 bùtóng (different).
2. Together, in common. Acting as one: 一同 yītóng (together), 共同 gòngtóng (jointly), 同时 tóngshí (at the same time).
3. The shared-role prefix. Marking people who share a station: 同学 tóngxué (fellow student), 同事 tóngshì (colleague), 同志 tóngzhì (comrade, one of the same aspiration).

同 also works as a quiet preposition meaning "with" or "and," interchangeable in many sentences with 跟 gēn and 和 hé. The thread through every use is shared ground: one nature, one action, one station, one company.

相同 xiāngtóng Same & Alike
相同 xiāngtóng identical; the same
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
相 xiāng (mutually) + 同 tóng (same). Two or more things being the same as each other: 完全相同 "exactly the same." More formal and written than the everyday 一样 yīyàng.
我们的看法完全相同。
Wǒmen de kànfǎ wánquán xiāngtóng.
Our views are exactly the same.
辨析 biànxī · 相同 vs. 一样 相同 is the more formal, written word, and it stands directly between the things compared: 颜色相同 "the colors are the same." 一样 yīyàng is the everyday spoken form and often takes 跟…一样: 这个跟那个一样 "this one is the same as that one." In conversation, reach for 一样; in writing, 相同.
不同 bùtóng different; not the same; difference
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
不 bù (not) + 同 tóng (same). The standard word for "different": 不同的意见 "differing opinions." As a noun it names the difference itself: 两者的不同 "the difference between the two."
每个人的想法都不同。
Měi ge rén de xiǎngfǎ dōu bùtóng.
Everyone thinks differently.
同样 tóngyàng same; equally; in the same way; likewise
ADJ/ADV 兼类 jiānlèi
同 tóng (same) + 样 yàng (kind; manner). Of the same kind, and adverbially "likewise, equally": 同样的问题 "the same problem," 同样重要 "equally important."
他们用同样的方法解决了问题。
Tāmen yòng tóngyàng de fāngfǎ jiějué le wèntí.
They solved the problem using the same method.
一同 yītóng Together & Shared
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

When 同 means "together," it also quietly does the work of a preposition. 我同你一起去 "I'll go with you" uses 同 exactly where many speakers would use 跟 gēn or 和 hé. All three can link two parties in a shared action, but they carry different registers: 和 is the most neutral and most common in writing, 跟 is the most colloquial and northern, and 同 sits in between, slightly formal and common in set phrases like 同…合作 "to cooperate with." When you see 同 standing before a person, test whether "with" fits; it usually does.

From this "togetherness" grows a family of words for joint action: 共同 gòngtóng (in common, jointly), 一同 yītóng and 一起 yīqǐ (together), 同意 tóngyì (to agree, literally "same idea"), and 同时 tóngshí (at the same time). Each takes the shared cover of 同 and applies it to a different thing held in common: a goal, a place, an opinion, a moment.

共同 gòngtóng common; shared; jointly; together
ADJ/ADV 兼类 jiānlèi
共 gòng (together; shared) + 同 tóng (same). Held in common by all parties: 共同的目标 "a shared goal," 共同努力 "to strive together." Stronger and more formal than 一起.
我们有共同的兴趣。
Wǒmen yǒu gòngtóng de xìngqù.
We have common interests.
同时 tóngshí at the same time; meanwhile; moreover
N/ADV 兼类 jiānlèi
同 tóng (same) + 时 shí (time). The same moment: 同时进行 "to proceed simultaneously." As a connector it also means "at the same time, moreover," adding a further point.
他同时学习中文和日文。
Tā tóngshí xuéxí Zhōngwén hé Rìwén.
He studies Chinese and Japanese at the same time.
同意 tóngyì to agree; to consent; to approve
V 动词 dòngcí
同 tóng (same) + 意 yì (idea; intention). To hold the same idea, to give one's assent: 我同意你的看法 "I agree with your view." The opposite is 反对 fǎnduì, to oppose.
大家都同意这个计划。
Dàjiā dōu tóngyì zhège jìhuà.
Everyone agrees with this plan.
同学 tóngxué Shared Roles: Classmate & Colleague
同学 tóngxué classmate; fellow student; (as address) student
N 名词 míngcí
同 tóng (same) + 学 xué (study). One who studies alongside you. A teacher also uses 同学 to address a pupil: 这位同学 "this student here." 老同学 means an old schoolmate.
他是我的大学同学。
Tā shì wǒ de dàxué tóngxué.
He is my university classmate.
同事 tóngshì colleague; co-worker
N 名词 míngcí
同 tóng (same) + 事 shì (work; affair). One who shares the same work, a colleague. Built on the same pattern as 同学: the shared station named by what is held in common.
我和同事一起吃午饭。
Wǒ hé tóngshì yīqǐ chī wǔfàn.
I have lunch with my colleagues.
同志 tóngzhì comrade; (colloquial) a gay person
N 名词 míngcí
同 tóng (same) + 志 zhì (aspiration; will). One of the same aspiration. The standard revolutionary address for "comrade," and in contemporary informal use a self-chosen word for gay people.
王同志，请进。
Wáng tóngzhì, qǐng jìn.
Comrade Wang, please come in.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
同甘共苦 tóng gān gòng kǔ to share the sweet and the bitter together: to stand by one another through good and bad Lit: same-sweet-share-bitter. Said of partners, comrades, or spouses who face both prosperity and hardship side by side rather than parting when times turn hard.
异口同声 yì kǒu tóng shēng different mouths, one voice: everyone saying the same thing in unison Lit: different-mouths-same-sound. When a whole group expresses the same opinion as if with one voice. Often used of unanimous agreement or praise.
同舟共济 tóng zhōu gòng jì in the same boat, crossing together: united by a common difficulty Lit: same-boat-jointly-cross. People who, sharing one boat, must row together to reach the far shore. The classical image for setting aside differences in the face of a shared crisis.
求同存异 qiú tóng cún yì seek common ground while keeping differences: agree where you can, set the rest aside Lit: seek-sameness-preserve-difference. A principle of diplomacy and negotiation: build on what is shared without forcing every disagreement to be resolved first.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a single 口 (mouth) sheltered under one wide cover. That is the whole character: voices brought under one roof, speaking as one. Everything 同 means flows from that single image of shared ground.

When two things share one nature, they are 相同 (the same). When people share one action, they do it 一同 or 共同 (together). When they share one station, they are named by it: fellow study makes a 同学, shared work a 同事, a common cause a 同志. And when 同 leans against a person, it quietly becomes "with." Wherever the cover falls, it gathers separate things into one.
