字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

孩 splits cleanly down the middle: 孩 = 子 + 亥. The left half, 子, is the radical and the meaning: a swaddled infant with arms out, the same glyph that names a "son" or "child" on its own. The right half, 亥, is pure sound. 亥 is the twelfth of the earthly branches and carries no childhood sense; it sits on the right only because in early Chinese it rhymed with the word for a small child. This is the textbook pictophonetic: one side tells you what, one side tells you how it sounds.

The pairing is old. In the seal script the left-hand 子 keeps its rounded, bundled-infant shape, while the right-hand 亥 holds the branched form the earthly-branch cycle adopted. The Shuōwén Jiězì glosses 孩 with the cooing of a baby, the small open-mouthed laugh of an infant who cannot yet form words. That sensory root, a sound before speech, is why the character first attached to the very youngest.

From that infant cry the sense widened outward through age: from the babbling baby, to the toddler, to any child, and finally to "children" as a class of people. Modern Mandarin almost never lets 孩 stand alone; it leans on a partner, most often the noun-softening 子 in 孩子 (háizi). The bare glyph survives mostly inside compounds, the way a brick survives inside a wall.

孩子háiziThe Core Word
孩子háizichild; kid; one's offspring
N 名词 míngcí
孩 (child) plus the neutral-tone suffix 子, which turns many one-syllable nouns into stable everyday words. 孩子 is the default word for a child and also the word a parent uses for a son or daughter of any age.
这个孩子很聪明。
Zhège háizi hěn cōngmíng.
This child is very clever.
他们有两个孩子。
Tāmen yǒu liǎng gè háizi.
They have two children.
辨析 biànxī · 孩子 vs. 儿童孩子 is the warm, conversational word and can mean "my son/daughter." 儿童 (értóng) is the formal, institutional term seen on signs, ticket prices, and laws.
孩童háitóngchild; young children (literary)
N 名词 míngcí
孩 paired with 童 (child, youngster). A bookish, slightly elevated word for children, common in writing and formal speech rather than casual talk.
公园里满是嬉戏的孩童。
Gōngyuán lǐ mǎn shì xīxì de háitóng.
The park was full of children at play.
男女nánnǚBoy & Girl
男孩nánháiboy
N 名词 míngcí
男 (male) plus 孩 (child). Often expanded to 男孩子 (nánháizi) with no change in meaning. Used for boys and, loosely, for young men.
那个男孩在踢足球。
Nàge nánhái zài tī zúqiú.
That boy is playing soccer.
女孩nǚháigirl
N 名词 míngcí
女 (female) plus 孩 (child). Like 男孩, it freely takes the extra 子 as 女孩子. It covers girls and, in everyday speech, young women.
那个女孩唱歌很好听。
Nàge nǚhái chànggē hěn hǎotīng.
That girl sings beautifully.
辨析 biànxī · 女孩 vs. 女子女孩 stresses youth and softness. 女子 (nǚzǐ) is a neutral, formal "female," the word used in sports categories like 女子篮球.
小孩xiǎoháiLittle Ones
小孩xiǎoháilittle kid; small child
N 名词 míngcí
小 (small) plus 孩 (child). The most colloquial word for a young child, often warmed further to 小孩儿 (xiǎoháir) in northern speech. Can gently tease an adult who acts childish.
别像个小孩一样!
Bié xiàng gè xiǎohái yíyàng!
Don't act like a little kid!
这部动画片很受小孩欢迎。
Zhè bù dònghuàpiàn hěn shòu xiǎohái huānyíng.
This cartoon is very popular with kids.
辨析 biànxī · 小孩 vs. 孩子小孩 leans toward the small and young, and works well as a general "kids." 孩子 spans the full range and is the word a parent uses for their own child of any age.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
小孩子家xiǎo hái zi jiājust a kid, only a childColloquial set phrase. The trailing 家 marks a category of person; said dismissively or fondly of someone too young to know better. "你一个小孩子家懂什么。"
小孩子气xiǎo hái zi qìchildish, immature in mannerDescribes adult behavior that is petty or babyish. The 气 (qì) suffix turns a noun into a temperament. Everyday spoken register: "他这样太小孩子气了。"
小孩没娘，说来话长xiǎohái méi niáng, shuō lái huà chángthe child has no mother: it's a long storyA 歇后语 (xiēhòuyǔ) two-part saying. The literal image, a motherless child, sets up the punchline 说来话长, "it's a long story." Spoken as a preface when the backstory is tangled and the speaker hardly knows where to begin.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture the glyph as a nursery split by a curtain. On the left, 子, stands the child itself: a swaddled baby, arms thrown wide, the exact bundled shape you see in the radical. On the right, 亥, hangs a wooden cradle-tag carved with a calendar branch, a marker with no meaning of its own except to ring out the sound hái when you tap it.

So every time you read 孩, you meet the baby on the left and tap the sound-tag on the right: 子 shows you a child, 亥 sings you hái. Hold that division and the compounds fall into place. Hang a 小 above the cradle for 小孩, a little one; stand a 男 or 女 at the door for 男孩 and 女孩; and let the soft 子 settle back in for 孩子, the warm everyday word a parent uses for their own.
