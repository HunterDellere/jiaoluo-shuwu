字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

姐 is a pictophonetic built as ⿰女且: the 女 (nǚ, woman) on the left carries the meaning, and 且 (qiě) on the right carries the sound. The kneeling silhouette of 女 marks this as a word about a woman; 且, a stacked altar-table of offerings in the old graphs, is borrowed purely for its reading and lends nothing of its own ancestral sense to the gloss.

The Shuōwén Jiězì lists 姐 under the 女 section and glosses it as a Shǔ (Sichuan) dialect word for mother. That regional "mother" reading faded; over later centuries the spoken language pulled 姐 toward "elder sister," and that is the meaning a child learns first today in 姐姐 (jiějie).

From the concrete tie of one older girl in a household, the word widened outward. By the late imperial period 姐 had become a polite handle for any young woman, the root of 小姐 (xiǎojiě, miss) and of the warm street address 大姐 (dàjiě) thrown at a woman a little older than oneself. The semantic 女 anchors every one of these to the same idea: a female person, addressed with a touch of respect.

姊妹zǐmèiSisters & Kin
姐姐jiějieelder sister
N 名词 míngcí
The doubled character is the everyday word for one's older sister. The second 姐 takes a neutral tone, giving the warm, soft cadence Chinese kinship terms favor.
我姐姐比我大三岁。
Wǒ jiějie bǐ wǒ dà sān suì.
My older sister is three years older than me.
辨析 biànxī · 姐姐 vs. 妹妹 姐姐 is the elder sister; 妹妹 (mèimei) is the younger one. Chinese names birth order directly, with no single word for "sister" the way English has.
姐妹jiěmèisisters; female friends
N 名词 míngcí
姐 (elder sister) plus 妹 (younger sister) yokes the two into a single word for "sisters" as a pair or group, and by extension a tight circle of close female friends.
她们三姐妹感情很好。
Tāmen sān jiěmèi gǎnqíng hěn hǎo.
The three sisters are very close.
我们是从小一起长大的好姐妹。
Wǒmen shì cóngxiǎo yìqǐ zhǎngdà de hǎo jiěmèi.
We are close sisters who grew up together from childhood.
大姐dàjiěeldest sister; ma'am
N 名词 míngcí
大 (big) plus 姐 marks the eldest of several sisters at home. On the street it doubles as a friendly, respectful way to address a woman somewhat older than the speaker.
大姐，请问火车站怎么走？
Dàjiě, qǐngwèn huǒchēzhàn zěnme zǒu?
Ma'am, may I ask how to get to the train station?
称呼chēnghuForms of Address
用法 yòngfǎ · Usage Note

姐 is one of Chinese's busiest address words. Attach it to a surname and you get 王姐 (Wáng jiě, Sister Wang) for an older female colleague; attach it to a young woman's status and you get 小姐 (xiǎojiě). Online and among friends, women now call themselves 姐 to project confidence, as in 姐不缺钱 (jiě bù quē qián, this lady isn't short on cash).

小姐xiǎojiěmiss; young lady
N 名词 míngcí
小 (small, young) plus 姐 is the classic polite title for a young, unmarried woman, like English "Miss." Register matters: in some mainland contexts a bare 小姐 can sound off, so service settings often prefer 美女 (měinǚ) or a surname plus 姐.
这位是新来的张小姐。
Zhè wèi shì xīn lái de Zhāng xiǎojiě.
This is Miss Zhang, who just joined.
辨析 biànxī · register As a standalone address, 小姐 carries unwanted overtones in parts of the mainland; pairing it with a surname (张小姐) keeps it safely polite.
空姐kōngjiěflight attendant (female)
N 名词 míngcí
空 (sky, air) plus 姐 is the colloquial word for a female flight attendant, literally a "sky sister." It is a clipped, affectionate everyday term beside the formal 空中小姐 (kōngzhōng xiǎojiě).
空姐给乘客送来了热毛巾。
Kōngjiě gěi chéngkè sònglái le rè máojīn.
The flight attendant brought the passengers hot towels.
姐夫jiěfuelder sister's husband; brother-in-law
N 名词 míngcí
姐 (elder sister) plus 夫 (husband) names the man married to one's older sister. Chinese kinship marks the relation precisely, distinct from 妹夫 (mèifu), a younger sister's husband.
我姐夫是一名医生。
Wǒ jiěfu shì yì míng yīshēng.
My brother-in-law (elder sister's husband) is a doctor.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
兄弟姐妹xiōng dì jiě mèibrothers and sisters; siblingsThe full set of siblings named in birth-order pairs: 兄 (elder brother), 弟 (younger brother), 姐 (elder sister), 妹 (younger sister). A staple four-character phrase, and one of the few common ones to carry 姐 directly.
姐妹情深jiě mèi qíng shēndeep sisterly affectionA common modern four-character set phrase for the strong bond between sisters or close female friends. Frequent in titles, toasts, and warm descriptions of female friendship.
大姐大dà jiě dàtop sister: a commanding, dominant womanA colloquial trigram, not a classical idiom, for the female leader of a group or a forceful, authoritative woman. Carries a half-admiring, half-wary tone.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture the kneeling 女 on the left as a young woman seated at the head of the family table. To her right stands 且, the old stacked altar of offering dishes, plate balanced on plate. She is the eldest daughter, the one trusted to set out the ancestral offerings before anyone else may eat.

Hold that scene: woman beside the stacked altar means the senior girl, the 姐姐. From that single image every compound falls into place. Make her the biggest of the daughters and you get 大姐; shrink her to a polite stranger and she becomes 小姐; lift her into the sky beside the drink cart and she is 空姐. The woman radical never moves, and neither does the sense of a female person addressed with quiet respect.
