字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

自 zì is a pictograph of a nose. The oracle-bone and bronze forms draw a nose seen head-on, with the bridge down the middle and the nostrils marked below. The modern shape still holds that outline: read it as a little face with the nose at the center. The link to "self" is a gesture, not a metaphor. To this day a Chinese speaker pointing at the self touches the nose rather than the chest, and that habit is exactly how 自 came to mean me, oneself.

Because the nose sense drifted almost entirely into "self," the language built a new character for the body part, 鼻 bí (nose), which keeps 自 on top as a clue to both meaning and sound. So 自 is the original nose, and 鼻 is the replacement that grew up around it once 自 had gone abstract.

From the pointed-at self three senses branch out:

1. Self, oneself. The reflexive center: 自己 zìjǐ (oneself), 自我 zìwǒ (the self, the ego), 自由 zìyóu (freedom, literally self-determined).
2. From, since. The source a thing starts at: 来自 láizì (to come from), 自从 zìcóng (ever since), 自古 zìgǔ (since ancient times).
3. Naturally, of itself. Acting on its own accord, with no outside push: 自然 zìrán (nature; natural), 自动 zìdòng (automatic), 自发 zìfā (spontaneous).

The thread tying the three together is origin from within. The self is where a person starts; "from" marks where a path starts; and "of itself" describes an action that starts from a thing's own nature rather than from anything outside it.

自己 zìjǐ Self & Ego
自己 zìjǐ oneself; myself, yourself, himself (etc.); one's own
N 名词 míngcí
自 zì (self) + 己 jǐ (oneself). The everyday reflexive word, flexing to whatever person the sentence needs: 我自己 "myself," 你自己 "yourself." It also marks possession, 自己的房间 "one's own room," and stresses doing a thing without help.
这件事你要自己决定。
Zhè jiàn shì nǐ yào zìjǐ juédìng.
You have to decide this yourself.
他有自己的想法。
Tā yǒu zìjǐ de xiǎngfǎ.
He has his own ideas.
自我 zìwǒ the self; the ego; self- (in compounds)
N 名词 míngcí
自 zì (self) + 我 wǒ (I; me). The self as an inner concept, the "ego" of psychology and philosophy. As a prefix it forms self-directed actions: 自我介绍 "self-introduction," 自我批评 "self-criticism."
请做一个自我介绍。
Qǐng zuò yí ge zìwǒ jièshào.
Please give a self-introduction.
辨析 biànxī · 自己 vs. 自我 自己 is the plain pronoun "oneself," used in ordinary speech to point back at whoever the subject is. 自我 is the self as an abstract object of thought, the ego you can examine, and it mostly lives in compounds and formal or psychological language, never as a stand-in pronoun.
自由 zìyóu freedom; liberty; free (to act)
N / ADJ 名词 / 形容词
自 zì (self) + 由 yóu (to follow; from). Literally "following from oneself," to act from one's own will rather than another's: 言论自由 "freedom of speech," 行动自由 "freedom of movement."
每个人都向往自由。
Měi ge rén dōu xiàngwǎng zìyóu.
Everyone longs for freedom.
自信 zìxìn self-confident; confidence in oneself
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
自 zì (self) + 信 xìn (to trust; believe). To trust in oneself: 他很自信 "he is very self-assured." A close cousin is 自尊 zìzūn (self-respect), another of the many self- compounds built on 自.
她对自己很自信。
Tā duì zìjǐ hěn zìxìn.
She is very confident in herself.
来自 láizì From & Since
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The second life of 自 is as a marker of source. Here it means "from," naming the point a thing or a path begins at. With place it answers where something comes from: 来自北京 "comes from Beijing," 自远方 "from afar." With time it sets the start of a stretch: 自古以来 "from ancient times onward," 自小 "from childhood." In this sense 自 leans formal and literary, the written cousin of the everyday 从 cóng.

Watch the two faces of 自 at once and the link is clear. The self is where a person originates; "from" is where a journey originates. One nose, pointed inward, marks the origin of a person; the same character, pointed outward, marks the origin of a path.

来自 láizì to come from; to originate from
V 动词 dòngcí
来 lái (to come) + 自 zì (from). To come from a place, group, or source: 来自世界各地 "from all over the world." Slightly formal, common in writing and introductions.
这些学生来自不同的国家。
Zhèxiē xuéshēng láizì bùtóng de guójiā.
These students come from different countries.
自从 zìcóng ever since; from the time that
ADV 副词 fùcí
自 zì (from) + 从 cóng (from; since). A conjunction marking a starting point in the past, paired with a later clause: 自从他来了以后 "ever since he arrived." Used only of time already begun, never the future.
自从搬家以后，我们很少见面。
Zìcóng bānjiā yǐhòu, wǒmen hěn shǎo jiànmiàn.
Ever since we moved, we rarely meet.
自然 zìrán Of Itself: the Spontaneous 自
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The third sense of 自 is the most philosophical: a thing acting of its own accord, with no outside hand. The clearest case is 自然 zìrán, 自 (self) + 然 (so), literally "so of itself." It names both nature, the world that runs on its own, and the quality of being natural and unforced. In Daoist thought 自然 is a central ideal, the way things unfold when nobody is pushing them.

The same "of itself" underlies a family of modern words: 自动 zìdòng (automatic, moving by itself), 自发 zìfā (spontaneous, arising by itself), 自愿 zìyuàn (voluntary, willed by oneself). In each, 自 says the action comes from the thing's own nature rather than from compulsion, which loops the sense back to the self: what is self-driven is what acts from within.

自然 zìrán nature; natural; naturally; at ease
N / ADJ 名词 / 形容词
自 zì (self) + 然 rán (so; thus). The world that runs of its own accord, hence "nature," and by extension anything unforced and natural: 大自然 "the natural world," 很自然 "quite natural / relaxed."
我喜欢亲近大自然。
Wǒ xǐhuān qīnjìn dà zìrán.
I like being close to nature.
他的表演很自然。
Tā de biǎoyǎn hěn zìrán.
His performance is very natural.
自动 zìdòng automatic; automatically; of one's own accord
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
自 zì (self) + 动 dòng (to move). Moving by itself, without anyone working it: 自动门 "automatic door," 自动售货机 "vending machine." Also of people acting unprompted: 自动报名 "to sign up on one's own."
这扇门是自动的。
Zhè shàn mén shì zìdòng de.
This door is automatic.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
自由自在 zì yóu zì zài free and at ease: unhurried, answerable to no one Lit: self-free-self-present. A doubled 自 that paints a life lived on one's own terms, comfortable and unconstrained, like a bird with the whole sky to itself.
自言自语 zì yán zì yǔ to talk to oneself: to mutter under one's breath Lit: self-speak-self-say. The 自 repeats to stress that both the speaking and the listening are oneself, used of someone murmuring aloud with no one else there.
自相矛盾 zì xiāng máo dùn to contradict oneself: spear against one's own shield Lit: self-mutually-spear-shield. From the fable of a peddler who swore his spear (矛) could pierce anything and his shield (盾) could stop anything, until a bystander asked what would happen if he used the one on the other.
自食其力 zì shí qí lì to support oneself by one's own labor: to earn one's own keep Lit: self-eat-one's-strength. To live on what one's own effort earns rather than leaning on others, high praise for honest self-reliance.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture an ancient speaker raising a finger to the nose and saying "me." That gesture is the whole character: 自 is a drawing of a nose, and a nose pointed at means the self. The original "nose" job later moved to 鼻 bí, leaving 自 to carry only the abstract self.

From that pointed-at nose, fan out the three senses. Point inward and 自 is the self: 自己, 自由, 自信. Point at where a path begins and it is "from": 来自, 自从. Point at a thing that needs no outside push and it is "of itself": 自然, 自动. Every 自 traces back to one origin, the nose at the center of the face, the place where a person starts.
