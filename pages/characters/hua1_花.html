字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

花 huā = 艹 (cǎo, grass) + 化 (huà, to transform). The top element 艹 is the grass radical, the flattened pair of plant shoots that marks words for vegetation. Below it sits 化 huà, "to transform, to change," which here does double duty: it carries the sound (huà → huā) and it hints at the meaning, the plant in its changed, opened-out state. A flower is exactly that, a plant transformed into its blooming form.

This phono-semantic build is relatively late. Early texts wrote the flower with 華 huá (the character now read "splendid, magnificent" and used for China itself, 中華). 花 emerged as the specialized graph for the literal blossom, leaving 華 to carry the figurative "splendor." From the bloom three senses fan out:

1. Flower, blossom. The literal meaning. 花朵 huāduǒ (a flower, a bloom), 鲜花 xiānhuā (fresh-cut flowers), 花园 huāyuán (garden), 开花 kāihuā (to bloom).
2. To spend. A surprising leap into the verb of expenditure, money or time poured out the way a plant pours itself into blossom: 花钱 huā qián (to spend money), 花时间 huā shíjiān (to spend time).
3. Patterned, fancy, blurred. A flower is many-colored and intricate, so 花 came to mean multicolored, ornamented, showy, and by extension visually confused: 花样 huāyàng (pattern, variety), 花白 huābái (grizzled, salt-and-pepper), 眼花 yǎnhuā (eyes blurred, dazzled).

The thread is the blossom's two faces: its beauty and its profusion. The beauty gives "flower" and "fancy, patterned"; the profusion, the lavish outpouring, gives "to spend." Whether you are admiring a bloom, opening your wallet, or rubbing dazzled eyes, you are standing inside the same image of the plant unfolding into color.

鲜花 xiānhuā Flowers & Blossoms
鲜花 xiānhuā fresh flowers; fresh-cut flowers
N 名词 míngcí
鲜 xiān (fresh) + 花 huā (flower). Living, fresh-cut blossoms, the flowers you buy and give: 一束鲜花 "a bouquet of fresh flowers." Distinct from 假花 jiǎhuā, artificial flowers.
他送了女朋友一束鲜花。
Tā sòng le nǚpéngyou yī shù xiānhuā.
He gave his girlfriend a bouquet of fresh flowers.
花园 huāyuán garden
N 名词 míngcí
花 huā (flower) + 园 yuán (garden, plot). A garden, a cultivated plot of flowers and plants. 植物园 zhíwùyuán is a botanical garden; 动物园 dòngwùyuán, a zoo, keeps the same 园.
奶奶在花园里种了很多花。
Nǎinai zài huāyuán lǐ zhòng le hěn duō huā.
Grandma planted many flowers in the garden.
开花 kāihuā to bloom; to blossom; to flower
V 动词 dòngcí
开 kāi (to open) + 花 huā (flower). To come into bloom, said of a plant opening its flowers. Figuratively, of an effort that bears fruit: 遍地开花 "blooming everywhere," spreading and flourishing.
春天到了，桃树开花了。
Chūntiān dào le, táoshù kāihuā le.
Spring has arrived, and the peach trees are in bloom.
花朵 huāduǒ flower; bloom (as a discrete blossom)
N 名词 míngcí
花 huā (flower) + 朵 duǒ (the measure word for blooms). A flower viewed as an individual blossom, slightly more literary than bare 花. Children are often called 祖国的花朵, "the flowers of the motherland."
这些花朵开得真美。
Zhèxiē huāduǒ kāi de zhēn měi.
These blossoms have opened so beautifully.
花钱 huā qián To Spend: Money and Time
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The verb 花 is one of the first surprises a learner meets: the same glyph that means "flower" also means "to spend." The bridge is the image of pouring out. A plant pours itself into blossom; a person pours out money or time the same way, and the resource is consumed in the act. So 花 became the everyday verb for expending what you have.

The pattern is simple and very common: 花 + amount + on what. 我花了一百块 "I spent a hundred kuai." 花 takes both money and time as its object: 花钱 huā qián (spend money), 花时间 huā shíjiān (spend time), even 花力气 huā lìqi (spend effort). Where English uses two verbs, "spend money" and "spend time," Chinese uses the one verb 花 for both. Its near-synonym for money alone is 花费 huāfèi, which works as both verb and noun (an expense).

花钱 huā qián to spend money
V-O 动宾 dòngbīn
花 huā (to spend) + 钱 qián (money). The basic verb-object phrase for spending money. Often split by an amount: 花了不少钱 "spent quite a lot of money," 花很多钱 "spend a lot."
他买车花了很多钱。
Tā mǎi chē huā le hěn duō qián.
He spent a lot of money buying a car.
花时间 huā shíjiān to spend time; to take time
V-O 动宾 dòngbīn
花 huā (to spend) + 时间 shíjiān (time). To spend or take time on something. The same verb 花 covers time as well as money, where English needs a different word for each.
学好中文需要花很多时间。
Xué hǎo Zhōngwén xūyào huā hěn duō shíjiān.
Learning Chinese well takes a lot of time.
辨析 biànxī · 花 the flower vs. 花 the verb The noun 花 huā (flower) and the verb 花 huā (to spend) are the same character with the same sound, told apart only by grammar. As a noun it is counted with 朵 duǒ and modified (鲜花, 一朵花). As a verb it takes an object of money or time and is often followed by 了 le (花了钱, 花时间). Context, not form, marks which 花 you are reading.
花费 huāfèi to spend; expense; cost
V / N 动名 dòngmíng
花 huā (to spend) + 费 fèi (fee, to expend). A more formal word for spending, and also a noun meaning the expense itself: 生活花费 "living expenses." Heavier and more written than bare 花.
这次旅行的花费不小。
Zhè cì lǚxíng de huāfèi bù xiǎo.
The cost of this trip was not small.
花样 huāyàng Patterns, Tricks & the Dazzled Eye
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

A blossom is many-colored and intricate, and that visual richness gives 花 a third life as an adjective: multicolored, patterned, fancy, ornate. 花布 huābù is patterned cloth; 花裙子 huā qúnzi, a floral skirt. From "many-colored" it slides toward "showy" and even "tricky," the sense of dazzle used to deceive, which surfaces in 花样 huāyàng (a trick, a fancy variation) and the chengyu 花言巧语.

The same profusion of color, when it overwhelms the eye, becomes visual confusion. 眼花 yǎnhuā means your eyes are dazzled, swimming, unable to focus: 看得眼花 "looking until your eyes blur." 花白 huābái applies the mixed-color sense to hair, salt-and-pepper, grizzled. So the one adjective runs from the delight of a floral print to the dizziness of a dazzled eye, both rooted in the blossom's overflowing color.

花样 huāyàng pattern; variety; fancy trick
N 名词 míngcí
花 huā (patterned) + 样 yàng (shape, kind). A decorative pattern or a fancy variation, and by extension a trick or gimmick: 玩花样 "to play tricks," 花样滑冰 "figure skating."
他总是玩出新花样。
Tā zǒngshì wán chū xīn huāyàng.
He is always coming up with new tricks.
眼花 yǎnhuā to have blurred vision; to be dazzled
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
眼 yǎn (eye) + 花 huā (blurred). Eyes swimming or dazzled, unable to focus, from too much to look at or from dizziness: 看得我眼花 "it left my eyes swimming." 头晕眼花 is "dizzy and bleary."
看了一天电脑，我眼睛都花了。
Kàn le yì tiān diànnǎo, wǒ yǎnjing dōu huā le.
After staring at the computer all day, my eyes have gone blurry.
花白 huābái grizzled; salt-and-pepper (of hair)
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
花 huā (mixed-color) + 白 bái (white). Of hair or a beard, mixed black and white, grizzled: 花白的头发 "grizzled hair." The "many-colored" sense of 花 applied to the heads of the middle-aged.
他两鬓花白，看起来很有经验。
Tā liǎng bìn huābái, kàn qǐlái hěn yǒu jīngyàn.
His temples are graying, and he looks very experienced.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
花言巧语 huā yán qiǎo yǔ flowery words and clever speech: sweet, deceptive talk Lit: flowery-words-clever-speech. Honeyed, ornamented talk meant to flatter or deceive. Here 花 carries its "showy, dressed-up" sense, words made pretty to mislead rather than to tell the truth.
锦上添花 jǐn shàng tiān huā to add flowers to brocade: to make the good even better Lit: brocade-upon-add-flowers. To improve on something already fine, the perfect made finer still. Its counterpart is 雪中送炭, "to send charcoal in the snow," helping in real need rather than gilding what is already lovely.
花好月圆 huā hǎo yuè yuán flowers in bloom and the moon full: perfect happiness, conjugal bliss Lit: flowers-fine-moon-round. The blossoming flower and the full moon together form the classic emblem of contentment and a happy marriage, a phrase often spoken as a wedding blessing.
走马观花 zǒu mǎ guān huā to view the flowers from a galloping horse: to take a hurried, superficial look Lit: ride-horse-watch-flowers. To glance at things in passing without looking closely, the way scenery blurs by from a fast horse. Used of any quick, surface-level survey that misses the detail.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Read the glyph top to bottom: 艹 grass over 化 transform. A plant changes, and what it changes into is the flower. Hold that single image of the bloom and the three senses fall out of it naturally.

The flower itself is the literal meaning (鲜花, 花园, 开花). Watch the plant pour itself out into blossom, and that outpouring becomes the verb of spending, money or time poured out the same way (花钱, 花时间). Look at the blossom's riot of color, and that richness becomes "patterned, fancy" (花样), then, when the colors overwhelm the eye, "dazzled, blurred" (眼花). One bloom, three lives: the garden, the wallet, and the swimming eye.
