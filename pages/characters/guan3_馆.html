字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

馆 splits cleanly down the middle: 馆 = 饣 + 官. On the left stands 饣, the running-hand abbreviation of 食 shí, the food radical, three quick strokes that once drew a covered vessel of grain. On the right sits 官 guān, an official, a public office, which here does double duty: it carries the sound guān → guǎn and lends the sense of something public, run for others rather than for the household. This is a textbook pictophonetic, the food side telling you what kind of place, the 官 side telling you both how to say it and that the place is a公-facing one.

Read the two halves together and the original picture surfaces: a place where the state feeds its guests. The earliest 馆 were post-houses and official hostels along the imperial roads, where envoys, couriers, and travelling officials were lodged and fed at public expense. Food plus office equals the relay station. The Shuōwén glosses 馆 as the lodging where guests are received, and the food radical is no accident, because hospitality in that world meant a hot meal as much as a roof.

From that relay-house root the meaning spread outward by analogy. Any building set up to serve the public could take the suffix 馆: feed people and it is a 饭馆, shelve books and it is a 图书馆, display relics and it is a 博物馆, house honored guests and it is a 宾馆. The food radical faded from the foreground, but the logic held: 馆 is always a roofed place run for visitors.

Japanese borrowed the character as kan (館), keeping the very same building sense, which is why 図書館, 博物館, and 大使館 read across the two languages with only the script standing between them.

饭馆fànguǎnEating Houses
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · The Food Side First

The food radical earns its place here. Before 馆 named libraries or embassies, it named the spot you went to eat, and that street-level sense is still the first one a beginner meets. 饭馆 is the everyday eatery, smaller and more casual than the grand 饭店.

饭馆fànguǎnrestaurant, eatery
N 名词 míngcí
饭 fàn (cooked rice, a meal) + 馆. A modest, sit-down place to eat, the neighborhood diner rather than a banquet hall. Often said 小饭馆 for the cheap, friendly kind.
我们去街角那家小饭馆吃饭吧。
Wǒmen qù jiējiǎo nà jiā xiǎo fànguǎn chīfàn ba.
Let's go eat at that little restaurant on the corner.
辨析 biànxī · 饭馆 vs. 饭店 饭馆 is a place to eat; 饭店 can mean a restaurant but also, especially in the north, a hotel. When you just want lunch, 饭馆 is unambiguous.
茶馆cháguǎnteahouse
N 名词 míngcí
茶 chá (tea) + 馆. The old social institution where people sat for hours over tea, gossip, and storytelling. Lao She's play 《茶馆》 made the teahouse a mirror of modern Chinese history.
老人们喜欢在茶馆里下棋聊天。
Lǎorénmen xǐhuan zài cháguǎn lǐ xiàqí liáotiān.
The old men like to play chess and chat in the teahouse.
咖啡馆kāfēiguǎncafé
N 名词 míngcí
咖啡 kāfēi (coffee) + 馆. The modern counterpart to the 茶馆, a place to sit with a laptop or a friend. The 馆 suffix quietly marks it as a public establishment.
我们约在地铁站旁边的咖啡馆见面。
Wǒmen yuē zài dìtiě zhàn pángbiān de kāfēiguǎn jiànmiàn.
We arranged to meet at the café next to the subway station.
公馆gōngguǎnCivic Halls

名词 + 馆: a noun naming what the building holds or does, plus 馆, yields its name. 图书 (books) → 图书馆, 博物 (broad things, relics) → 博物馆, 体育 (sport) → 体育馆, 美术 (fine art) → 美术馆. The pattern is wholly productive: name the contents, add 馆, and you have the institution.

图书馆túshūguǎnlibrary
N 名词 míngcí
图书 túshū (books, lit. pictures + writings) + 馆. The building that houses books for public use. One of the first long compounds a learner can fully parse.
图书馆周末也开门吗?
Túshūguǎn zhōumò yě kāimén ma?
Is the library open on weekends too?
博物馆bówùguǎnmuseum
N 名词 míngcí
博 bó (broad, extensive) + 物 wù (things) + 馆. The hall of broad things, where relics and specimens are kept and shown. 故宫博物院 uses 院 instead, but the everyday word is 博物馆.
这座博物馆收藏了很多青铜器。
Zhè zuò bówùguǎn shōucángle hěnduō qīngtóngqì.
This museum has collected many bronze artifacts.
体育馆tǐyùguǎngymnasium, indoor arena
N 名词 míngcí
体育 tǐyù (physical education, sport) + 馆. The roofed sports hall, as opposed to the open-air 体育场 (chǎng, a field/stadium). The 馆 marks the covered, indoor venue.
篮球比赛在学校的体育馆举行。
Lánqiú bǐsài zài xuéxiào de tǐyùguǎn jǔxíng.
The basketball game is held in the school gymnasium.
辨析 biànxī · 馆 vs. 场 馆 is a roofed building (体育馆, indoor); 场 chǎng is an open ground (体育场, an outdoor stadium). The suffix tells you whether there is a ceiling.
宾馆bīnguǎnLodging & State
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · The Guest House Returns

Here 馆 circles back to its origin. The relay-house that fed travelling officials becomes the modern 宾馆 (a hotel for guests) and the 大使馆 (the embassy, literally the great-envoy's house). The 官 inside the character, an official, an arm of the state, surfaces again in these diplomatic and hospitality senses.

宾馆bīnguǎnhotel, guesthouse
N 名词 míngcí
宾 bīn (guest) + 馆. A house for guests, that is, a hotel. The word keeps the old hospitality sense of 馆 right on its sleeve.
我们在火车站附近订了一家宾馆。
Wǒmen zài huǒchēzhàn fùjìn dìngle yì jiā bīnguǎn.
We booked a hotel near the train station.
辨析 biànxī · 宾馆 vs. 酒店 宾馆 is a plain word for hotel, often state-run or mid-range; 酒店 jiǔdiàn leans upscale and is the word the big international chains use.
大使馆dàshǐguǎnembassy
N 名词 míngcí
大使 dàshǐ (ambassador, lit. great envoy) + 馆. The ambassador's official house abroad. A smaller mission led by a consul is a 领事馆 lǐngshìguǎn, a consulate.
办签证要先去大使馆预约。
Bàn qiānzhèng yào xiān qù dàshǐguǎn yùyuē.
To get a visa you first have to make an appointment at the embassy.
展览馆zhǎnlǎnguǎnexhibition hall
N 名词 míngcí
展览 zhǎnlǎn (to exhibit) + 馆. The hall where exhibitions are mounted, cousin to the 博物馆 but temporary in what it shows.
新的科技展览馆下个月开放。
Xīn de kējì zhǎnlǎnguǎn xià gè yuè kāifàng.
The new technology exhibition hall opens next month.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
离宫别馆lí gōng bié guǎndetached palaces and separate lodges: the many residences a ruler kept beyond the main palaceA classical 成语 for the scattered country retreats and travelling-lodges of an emperor or great house. Here 馆 carries its oldest sense, the official lodge where the powerful stayed on the road.
别馆离宫bié guǎn lí gōngseparate lodges and detached palaces: the same scattered residences, with the lodge named firstThe attested inversion of 离宫别馆, listed as its own form in idiom dictionaries. Putting 馆 first foregrounds the lodging over the palace.
茶馆酒肆chá guǎn jiǔ sìteahouses and wine-shops: the everyday public houses of a townA four-character set phrase pairing 茶馆 with 酒肆 (the wine-shop) to stand for the street-level places where ordinary people gathered, drank, and talked.
说明 shuōmíng · A Note on Idioms

馆 is a workhorse noun rather than an idiom character, so set phrases built on it are few, and they cluster around lodging. 离宫别馆 and its inversion 别馆离宫 name the ruler's lodges away from the palace; 茶馆酒肆 names the public houses of the common town. Each frames 馆 the same way: a roofed place where people are received, fed, and put up.

记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a dusty imperial road at dusk. A courier reins in his horse at a low building with a lantern over the door. Inside, an 官, an official in dark robes, ladles hot rice from a covered pot into the traveller's bowl. That single scene holds both halves of the glyph: 饣, the steam rising off the food on the left, and 官, the state official on the right who runs the place. 馆 is a building where the public is fed and housed.

Now fast-forward. The lantern stays, the official fades, but the building keeps serving the public, only the contents change. Pour in books and the relay-house becomes a 图书馆. Fill it with relics and it is a 博物馆. Lay out beds for guests and it is a 宾馆; raise a foreign flag and it is a 大使馆. Whenever you meet 馆, ask what is being served inside, and the word in front of it will tell you.
