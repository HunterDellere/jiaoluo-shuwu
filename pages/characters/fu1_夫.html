字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

夫 fū is a pictograph built on 大 dà, the figure of a standing person seen from the front. Above the outstretched 大 a single horizontal stroke marks a hairpin run through the topknot. In early society a man bound up his hair and pinned it when he came of age, so the added stroke turns the generic person into a grown, capped adult: a man who has reached maturity. The simplified and traditional forms are identical, four strokes that have changed little across the script's long history.

From that core image of the adult man, the senses fan out, and the character also carries a second, literary reading. The everyday reading fū spans three related fields:

1. Husband. The grown man as one half of a married pair: 丈夫 zhàngfu (husband), 夫妻 fūqī (husband and wife), 夫人 fūrén (madam, a man's wife), 夫妇 fūfù (a married couple).
2. An adult man, a laborer. A man defined by his work: 农夫 nóngfū (farmer), 渔夫 yúfū (fisherman), 车夫 chēfū (cart driver). Note that 大夫 read dàifu means doctor, a special colloquial reading.
3. Skill, the effort that mastery takes. 功夫 gōngfu (skill, craft, kung fu) and 工夫 gōngfu (time and effort spent), where the man's labor becomes the work itself.

The second reading is fú, a particle of classical and literary Chinese. At the head of a sentence it works as a soft opener, "now, as for," signalling that a general statement or reflection is about to follow; mid-sentence or at the close it can point like "this, that." It carries no concrete meaning of its own, only a measured, essayistic tone. A learner meets it most in 论语 and 道德经 style prose, almost never in modern speech.

夫妻 fūqī Husband & the Married Couple
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The warmest face of 夫 is the husband, the grown man as one half of a marriage. 丈夫 zhàngfu is the plain everyday word for one's husband. Pair the man with the wife and you get the couple seen from two angles: 夫妻 fūqī, husband and wife as a unit, and 夫妇 fūfù, a more formal pairing of the same idea. 夫人 fūrén, finally, is the polite term for another man's wife, "madam" or "lady," a word of respect rather than of intimacy. Together they map the married man from the inside (my husband) out to the formal (so-and-so's wife).

丈夫 zhàngfu husband
N 名词 míngcí
丈 zhàng (an honorific for an adult man) + 夫 fū (man). The ordinary, neutral word for one's husband, the man one is married to. In older usage 大丈夫 dà zhàngfu also means a true man, a man of character, but on its own 丈夫 simply names the spouse.
她的丈夫是一名医生。
Tā de zhàngfu shì yī míng yīshēng.
Her husband is a doctor.
他们结婚后，他成了一个好丈夫。
Tāmen jiéhūn hòu, tā chéng le yī gè hǎo zhàngfu.
After they married, he became a good husband.
辨析 biànxī · 丈夫 vs. 先生 丈夫 zhàngfu names the marital relationship directly: this man is my husband. 先生 xiānsheng is a polite "Mr." used to address or refer to any adult man, and only by extension a courteous way to mean one's husband. You introduce a stranger as 王先生 (Mr. Wang), but you call the man you married your 丈夫. One states the bond, the other shows respect.
夫妻 fūqī husband and wife; a married couple
N 名词 míngcí
夫 fū (husband) + 妻 qī (wife). The couple named as a pair, the two spouses considered together. Everyday and warm, common in sayings about married life: 夫妻关系 the marital relationship, 老夫妻 an old married couple, 夫妻店 a mom-and-pop shop run by a couple.
他们是一对恩爱的夫妻。
Tāmen shì yī duì ēn'ài de fūqī.
They are a loving married couple.
夫妻之间要互相理解。
Fūqī zhī jiān yào hùxiāng lǐjiě.
Husband and wife should understand each other.
夫人 fūrén madam; lady; another man's wife (polite)
N 名词 míngcí
夫 fū (man) + 人 rén (person). A respectful term for a married woman, especially the wife of a person of standing: 总统夫人 the first lady, 王夫人 Madam Wang. It honors the woman through her husband's status, so it is formal rather than intimate; you would not call your own wife 夫人 in casual speech.
总统和夫人一起出席了晚宴。
Zǒngtǒng hé fūrén yīqǐ chūxí le wǎnyàn.
The president and the first lady attended the banquet together.
夫妇 fūfù a married couple; husband and wife (formal)
N 名词 míngcí
夫 fū (husband) + 妇 fù (married woman). A married couple, close in meaning to 夫妻 but more formal and bookish. Often attached to a surname: 张氏夫妇 the Zhang couple, 新婚夫妇 newlyweds. Where 夫妻 feels everyday and warm, 夫妇 reads as written or ceremonious.
这对新婚夫妇刚搬进新家。
Zhè duì xīnhūn fūfù gāng bān jìn xīn jiā.
This newlywed couple just moved into their new home.
辨析 biànxī · 夫妻 vs. 夫妇 夫妻 fūqī is the everyday, warm word for a couple, common in speech and in talk of married life. 夫妇 fūfù is the same idea in a more formal, written register, favored on invitations and in news: 王先生夫妇 (Mr. Wang and his wife). The meaning is nearly identical; the register is the difference.
农夫 nóngfū Men & Laborers
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Strip away the marriage and 夫 is simply the grown man, and most often the man at work. Set a kind of labor before it and you name the worker: 农夫 nóngfū the farmer who tills the field, 渔夫 yúfū the fisherman who casts the net, 车夫 chēfū the driver who pulls or drives the cart. These read with a slightly old, plain flavor, the laboring man of an earlier age. One compound breaks the pattern in sound: 大夫, when read dàifu, means a doctor, a colloquial word for a physician. The same two characters read 大夫 dàfū once meant a senior official in the old court, so the reading you choose decides the meaning entirely.

农夫 nóngfū farmer; a man of the land
N 名词 míngcí
农 nóng (agriculture, farming) + 夫 fū (man). A man who works the land, the farmer in a plain, somewhat literary or old-fashioned key. It pictures the individual tiller rather than the peasantry as a social class, and turns up often in fables and proverbs about country life.
一位农夫在田里种地。
Yī wèi nóngfū zài tián lǐ zhòng dì.
A farmer is working the soil in the field.
渔夫 yúfū fisherman
N 名词 míngcí
渔 yú (to fish) + 夫 fū (man). A man who makes his living by fishing. Like 农夫 it has a timeless, story-book ring, and it features in the famous proverb about the fisherman who profits while the snipe and the clam are locked in struggle.
老渔夫每天清晨出海。
Lǎo yúfū měitiān qīngchén chūhǎi.
The old fisherman goes out to sea early every morning.
大夫 dàifu doctor; physician (colloquial)
N 名词 míngcí
大 dà + 夫 fū, but in this sense read dàifu with a neutral tone. A common, slightly informal word for a doctor, especially in northern speech: 看大夫 to see a doctor, 张大夫 Dr. Zhang. Read 大夫 dàfū instead and the same characters name a senior official of the old imperial court, a different word entirely.
你最好去看看大夫。
Nǐ zuìhǎo qù kànkan dàifu.
You had better go see a doctor.
读音 dúyīn · 大夫 has two readings Read dàifu, 大夫 means a doctor in everyday speech. Read dàfū, it is a historical rank, a senior court official below the level of a minister. The characters never change; only the reading tells you whether you mean a physician at the clinic or a grandee at the old court.
功夫 gōngfu Skill, Effort & the Particle fú
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

When the man's labor becomes the thing itself, 夫 names skill and the effort that buys it. 功夫 gōngfu is the famous word: the mastery won by long practice, hence kung fu in the martial sense, but also any hard-earned competence. Written 工夫 gōngfu it leans toward the time and trouble that go into a task: 花工夫 to put time and care into something. Apart from these everyday senses sits the wholly separate literary reading fú: a classical particle with no concrete meaning, used to open a measured sentence ("now, as for") or to point quietly like "this, that," the hallmark of essay-style classical prose.

功夫 gōngfu skill; craft; martial arts (kung fu)
N 名词 míngcí
功 gōng (achievement, merit) + 夫 fū (effort). Mastery earned through long practice. In the martial sense it is kung fu, the disciplined skill of the fighter; more broadly it names any hard-won competence: 中文功夫 one's command of Chinese, 真功夫 the real skill behind a craft.
他从小练功夫，身手很好。
Tā cóngxiǎo liàn gōngfu, shēnshǒu hěn hǎo.
He has practiced martial arts since childhood and is very skilled.
这道菜很见功夫。
Zhè dào cài hěn jiàn gōngfu.
This dish really shows the cook's skill.
辨析 biànxī · 功夫 vs. 工夫 功夫 gōngfu stresses the skill that practice produces: kung fu, real mastery. 工夫 gōngfu stresses the time and effort spent: 花了不少工夫 took quite a bit of time and trouble. In modern use the two are often interchanged, but the cleaner split is skill for 功夫 and time-and-effort for 工夫.
工夫 gōngfu time and effort; trouble spent on a task
N 名词 míngcí
工 gōng (work, labor) + 夫 fū (effort). The time and care put into doing something: 花工夫 to spend effort, 没工夫 to have no time. It measures the investment a task demands rather than the skill it yields, the cost side of mastery.
学好一门语言要下工夫。
Xué hǎo yī mén yǔyán yào xià gōngfu.
Learning a language well takes real effort.
我今天没工夫陪你去。
Wǒ jīntiān méi gōngfu péi nǐ qù.
I have no time to go with you today.
夫 fú a literary particle: now, as for; this, that
文言 wényán · classical
Read fú, 夫 is a particle of classical and literary Chinese with no concrete meaning. At the head of a sentence it works as a measured opener, "now, as for," easing the reader into a general statement. Elsewhere it can point softly like "this" or "that." It belongs to written, essay-style prose and almost never appears in modern speech.
夫学须静也。
Fú xué xū jìng yě.
Now, learning requires stillness. (classical style)
读音 dúyīn · fū vs. fú Read fū, 夫 carries real meaning: husband, man, skill. Read fú, it is an empty literary particle that only sets a tone. Modern readers meet 夫 fú almost only inside classical texts and set phrases; in living speech the character is nearly always fū.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
一夫当关 yī fū dāng guān one man guards the pass: a position so strong that a single defender holds off an army From the line about the road to Shu, 一夫当关，万夫莫开, "one man holding the pass, ten thousand cannot force it open." It praises a place of such natural strength that a lone defender can block a whole host, and by extension any well-held strategic point or a person stubbornly guarding a position.
匹夫之勇 pǐ fū zhī yǒng the courage of a common man: reckless bravery without strategy 匹夫 pǐfū is an ordinary fellow of no rank. The phrase names the brute, headlong courage of one who rushes in to fight without thought or plan, contrasted with the cool, calculated valor of a true leader. It is a gentle put-down: brave, but foolishly so.
千夫所指 qiān fū suǒ zhǐ pointed at by a thousand men: condemned by everyone Literally "what a thousand men point their fingers at." It describes a person reviled by the whole public, the target of universal blame and reproach. To be 千夫所指 is to stand utterly condemned in the eyes of the crowd, a heavy figure for public disgrace.
夫唱妇随 fū chàng fù suí the husband sings and the wife follows: a harmonious married couple Literally "the husband leads, the wife accompanies." A traditional image of marital harmony in which the couple act in accord, one taking the lead and the other in step. It reflects an older ideal of the household; today it is often used warmly, and sometimes lightly, for any well-matched, agreeable pair.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Hold one picture at the center of 夫: a grown man standing tall (大) with a hairpin run across his topknot, the cap of adulthood. That single image of the come-of-age man is the seed of every sense.

Marry the man and he is a husband: 丈夫 husband, 夫妻 and 夫妇 the couple, 夫人 the lady of the house. Send him to work and he is the laboring man: 农夫 farmer, 渔夫 fisherman, and, in a special reading, 大夫 dàifu the doctor. Let his labor harden into mastery and he becomes the skill itself: 功夫 the craft won by practice, 工夫 the time it costs. And when the same glyph is read fú, the man steps off the stage entirely, leaving behind an empty literary particle that only opens a measured classical sentence. Whenever you meet 夫, ask first which reading is in play, fū the man or fú the particle, and then which of the man's many roles the compound has in mind.
