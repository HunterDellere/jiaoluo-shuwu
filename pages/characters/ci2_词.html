字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

词 cí = 讠 (yán, speech) + 司 (sī, the phonetic). The left element 讠 is the compressed simplified form of 言 yán, the speech radical that marks words about talking, naming, and language. The right element 司 sī supplies the sound and once carried a sense of taking charge of or managing affairs. As a structure 词 is a 形声字 (xíngshēngzì), a sound-and-meaning compound: the radical tells you it concerns speech, and 司 gives the approximate reading.

The traditional glyph is 詞, with the full 言 on the left; the simplified 词 trims 言 to the three-stroke 讠 but keeps the same 司 on the right. Simplified and traditional name the same character.

From the core idea of a managed piece of speech, three senses branch out:

1. A word or term. The basic unit of vocabulary, larger than a single character: 词语 cíyǔ (words and expressions), 生词 shēngcí (a new vocabulary word), 单词 dāncí (an individual word).
2. Wording, diction. The words a speaker or writer chooses: 台词 táicí (an actor lines), 歌词 gēcí (song lyrics), 致辞 zhìcí (to deliver an address).
3. The classical 词. A lyric-poem form that flourished in the Song period, written to fixed tune-patterns and meant to be sung, set apart from 诗 shī, the regulated verse of the Tang.

The thread through every sense is words as crafted, chosen, fitted to a purpose: the dictionary entry, the line an actor speaks, the syllable laid onto a melody. 词 is always speech that has been shaped into a unit.

生词 shēngcí Words & Vocabulary
生词 shēngcí new word; unfamiliar vocabulary item
N 名词 míngcí
生 shēng (raw; unfamiliar) + 词 cí (word). A word you have not yet learned, the staple of every textbook vocabulary list. Its opposite is 熟词 shúcí, a word already mastered.
这一课有很多生词。
Zhè yī kè yǒu hěn duō shēngcí.
This lesson has a lot of new words.
我把生词记在本子上。
Wǒ bǎ shēngcí jì zài běnzi shàng.
I write the new words down in my notebook.
单词 dāncí a single word; a vocabulary word
N 名词 míngcí
单 dān (single) + 词 cí (word). One word taken on its own, the unit you memorize when studying a language. Common in 背单词 bèi dāncí, to memorize vocabulary.
他每天背五十个单词。
Tā měi tiān bèi wǔshí ge dāncí.
He memorizes fifty words every day.
词语 cíyǔ words and phrases; expressions
N 名词 míngcí
词 cí (word) + 语 yǔ (speech; phrase). A cover term for words and set phrases together, broader than 单词. Used when talking about vocabulary as a body of expressions.
请用这些词语造句。
Qǐng yòng zhèxiē cíyǔ zào jù.
Please make sentences with these words and phrases.
辨析 biànxī · 字 vs. 词 A 字 zì is a single written character, one glyph in the script. A 词 cí is a word, the smallest unit that carries meaning and stands on its own in a sentence; it may be one character (人 rén) or several (朋友 péngyou). 字 counts the symbols; 词 counts the meanings.
词典 cídiǎn dictionary (of words)
N 名词 míngcí
词 cí (word) + 典 diǎn (standard reference). A dictionary organized by words. Its sibling 字典 zìdiǎn is organized by single characters, mirroring the 字 / 词 split exactly.
我查了一下词典。
Wǒ chá le yīxià cídiǎn.
I looked it up in the dictionary.
名词 míngcí Parts of Speech: the Grammar of 词
语法洞见 yǔfǎ dòngjiàn · Grammatical Insight

When grammarians name the categories of words, they stack a descriptive character in front of 词. The 词 supplies "class of word," and the first character says which class. So 名词 míngcí is the "name-word," a noun; 动词 dòngcí is the "moving-word," a verb; 形容词 xíngróngcí is the "form-describing word," an adjective; 量词 liàngcí is the "measuring word," the classifier that goes between a number and a noun.

The same template runs through the whole grammar: 副词 fùcí (adverb), 代词 dàicí (pronoun), 介词 jiècí (preposition), 连词 liáncí (conjunction), 助词 zhùcí (particle). Learn that 词 here means "part of speech" and a long list of grammar terms becomes transparent at a glance. You may notice 词 doing this very job in the gloss boxes throughout this site: every POS tag, from 名词 to 动词, is built on it.

名词 míngcí noun
N 名词 míngcí
名 míng (name) + 词 cí (word). The "name-word," a noun: a word for a person, place, thing, or idea. 桌子, 北京, and 自由 are all 名词.
桌子是一个名词。
Zhuōzi shì yī ge míngcí.
Table is a noun.
动词 dòngcí verb
N 名词 míngcí
动 dòng (move; act) + 词 cí (word). The "moving-word," a verb: a word for an action or state. 吃, 走, and 喜欢 are all 动词.
吃和喝都是动词。
Chī hé hē dōu shì dòngcí.
Eat and drink are both verbs.
量词 liàngcí measure word; classifier
N 名词 míngcí
量 liàng (measure; quantity) + 词 cí (word). The classifier that sits between a number and a noun: 一本书 "one (volume of) book," where 本 běn is the 量词. A signature feature of Chinese grammar.
个是最常用的量词。
Gè shì zuì chángyòng de liàngcí.
Gè is the most commonly used measure word.
歌词 gēcí Wording, Lyrics & the Classical 词
文学洞见 wénxué dòngjiàn · Literary Insight

The third life of 词 is the words people choose and sing. In everyday use it names the wording itself: 歌词 gēcí is the lyrics of a song, 台词 táicí is the lines an actor speaks on stage, 贺词 hècí is a few words of congratulation. In each, 词 is the chosen language fitted to a moment.

This same sense rose to high literature in the classical 词, the lyric form that flourished in the Song. Unlike 诗 shī, the regulated verse perfected in the Tang with its even lines and fixed length, a 词 is written to a 词牌 (cípái), a named tune-pattern that dictates the number of lines, the count of characters in each, and the placement of tones. The poet does not invent the shape; the poet "fills in words," 填词 tiáncí, onto a melody already known by name. The lines run uneven, the music carries them, and the result reads more intimate and song-like than the stately 诗. Song lyric, 宋词, stands beside Tang verse, 唐诗, as one of the twin peaks of classical Chinese poetry.

歌词 gēcí song lyrics; the words of a song
N 名词 míngcí
歌 gē (song) + 词 cí (wording). The words set to music, as opposed to 曲 qǔ, the melody. A songwriter is often credited separately for 作词 zuòcí (writing the lyrics) and 作曲 zuòqǔ (composing the tune).
这首歌的歌词很美。
Zhè shǒu gē de gēcí hěn měi.
The lyrics of this song are beautiful.
台词 táicí an actor lines; stage dialogue; script lines
N 名词 míngcí
台 tái (stage) + 词 cí (words). The lines a performer speaks in a play, film, or show. 背台词 bèi táicí is to learn one lines by heart.
这个演员忘了台词。
Zhège yǎnyuán wàng le táicí.
This actor forgot the lines.
宋词 Sòng cí Song lyric poetry; the classical 词 of the Song
N 名词 míngcí
宋 Sòng (the Song) + 词 cí (lyric). The lyric-poem form at its height, written to set tune-patterns and meant to be sung. Paired with 唐诗 Táng shī, Tang regulated verse, as one of the high points of classical poetry.
他很喜欢读宋词。
Tā hěn xǐhuan dú Sòng cí.
He very much enjoys reading Song lyric poetry.
辨析 biànxī · 诗 vs. 词 诗 shī is regulated verse: even lines of equal length, perfected in the Tang. 词 cí is the lyric written to a named tune-pattern: lines of uneven length, fitted to a melody, at its height in the Song. 诗 is recited; 词 was sung.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
词不达意 cí bù dá yì the words fail to convey the meaning: to express oneself poorly Lit: words-not-reach-meaning. Said when the language a speaker or writer chooses does not match what they meant, so the point comes across muddled or wrong. A common way to apologize for an unclear remark.
振振有词 zhèn zhèn yǒu cí to argue with apparent conviction: to speak plausibly while in the wrong Lit: assured-and-having-words. Describes someone who defends a weak or unjustified position with a confident flow of arguments, sounding righteous though the case does not hold. Mildly critical in tone.
念念有词 niàn niàn yǒu cí to mutter words under one breath: to chant or murmur incantations Lit: muttering-and-having-words. Originally of a priest or sorcerer murmuring a spell, now of anyone muttering steadily to themselves, whether praying, reciting, or grumbling.
义正词严 yì zhèng cí yán righteous in cause and stern in speech: to speak with upright authority Lit: principle-upright-words-stern. Said of someone whose argument rests on a just cause and whose wording is firm and severe, leaving no room for doubt that they hold the moral high ground.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Read 词 as speech, 讠, taken in hand and managed, 司. That is the whole character: a piece of language someone has charge of and has shaped to a purpose. From there the three senses fall out naturally. The unit you look up and learn is a word (生词, 单词). The label grammar hangs on a class of words is the part of speech (名词, 动词). And the words a singer or actor or poet chooses are the diction (歌词, 台词), rising to art in the classical sung lyric.

Keep 字 and 词 apart and you will rarely go wrong: 字 is the symbol on the page, one glyph; 词 is the meaning it helps build, one word. Count glyphs with 字, count words with 词.
