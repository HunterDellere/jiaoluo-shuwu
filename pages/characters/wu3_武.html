字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

武 wǔ is built from 止 zhǐ (a foot, to stop) carrying 戈 gē (a dagger-axe, the classic ancient weapon). The most literal reading of the early graph is a foot striding beneath a weapon: the soldier on the march, arms in hand. The simplified and traditional forms are identical, eight strokes that have changed little across the script's long history.

But the character carries a famous second reading, recorded in the early commentaries and quoted ever after. Because 止 also means to stop, 武 was glossed as 止戈, "to halt the weapons": true martial power is the strength that ends conflict, not the appetite that prolongs it. The two readings, the foot that marches and the foot that halts, sit inside one glyph, and the tension between them runs straight through the Chinese idea of force. Strength is honoured, but the highest strength is the kind that no longer needs to be used.

From this core of armed strength under discipline three semantic lives unfold: 武 as the military and the apparatus of arms (武力, 武器, 武装); 武 as trained fighting skill, the martial arts of the body (武术, 武功, 武打); and 武 as a quality of bearing, valiant, fierce, imposing (威武, 英武, 武勇). Across all three the character stands as the partner and opposite of 文 wén, the civil, the literary, the cultured: 文 the brush and 武 the blade, the two halves of a complete person and a complete state.

武力 wǔlì Military & Arms
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The most concrete face of 武 is organised force: armies, weapons, and the state's capacity to compel. 武力 wǔlì is force in the abstract, the option of using arms; to settle a dispute 用武力 is to settle it by military means. 武器 wǔqì is the weapon itself, the tool that gives that force its edge. 武装 wǔzhuāng turns the idea into an action and a state: to arm, and to be armed, the soldier kitted out for war. Each compound narrows the same root, from the bare possibility of force to the hardware that delivers it to the body that carries it.

武力 wǔlì armed force; military force
N 名词 míngcí
武 wǔ (martial) + 力 lì (force, power). Force exercised through arms, the option of compulsion by military means as opposed to negotiation: 使用武力 "to use force," 武力解决 "to resolve by force." A neutral, often institutional word for the state's capacity to wage war.
他们不愿意使用武力。
Tāmen bù yuànyì shǐyòng wǔlì.
They are unwilling to use force.
这个问题不能靠武力解决。
Zhège wèntí bù néng kào wǔlì jiějué.
This problem cannot be solved by armed force.
辨析 biànxī · 武力 vs. 暴力 武力 wǔlì is organised, often legitimate military force, the army and the means of war. 暴力 bàolì is violence in the raw, brutal physical harm, with no implication of order or sanction: street violence, domestic violence, a violent crime. A state wields 武力; a mob commits 暴力. One names a capacity, the other names a wrong.
武器 wǔqì weapon; arms
N 名词 míngcí
武 wǔ (martial) + 器 qì (implement, tool). The weapon itself, from a single blade to a nation's arsenal: 核武器 "nuclear weapons," 武器装备 "weaponry and equipment." Figuratively, any decisive instrument: 知识是有力的武器 "knowledge is a powerful weapon."
士兵们放下了武器。
Shìbīngmen fàngxià le wǔqì.
The soldiers laid down their weapons.
语言是他最有力的武器。
Yǔyán shì tā zuì yǒulì de wǔqì.
Language is his most powerful weapon.
武装 wǔzhuāng to arm; armed; armed forces
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
武 wǔ (martial) + 装 zhuāng (to equip, to fit out). As a verb, to arm or equip with weapons; as an adjective, armed; as a noun, an armed force: 武装部队 "armed forces," 武装冲突 "armed conflict." Also figurative: 用知识武装头脑 "to arm the mind with knowledge."
他们用现代化的设备武装军队。
Tāmen yòng xiàndàihuà de shèbèi wǔzhuāng jūnduì.
They equip the army with modern equipment.
边境上发生了武装冲突。
Biānjìng shang fāshēng le wǔzhuāng chōngtū.
An armed conflict broke out on the border.
构词 gòucí · The 武 + domain pattern 武 set before an institution names its armed branch: 武警 wǔjǐng (the armed police), 武官 wǔguān (a military attaché). Set before a body part or faculty it turns figurative: 武装头脑 "to arm the mind." The root stays fixed, the second element decides whether the arming is literal or a metaphor.
武术 wǔshù Martial Arts: The Trained Body
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Turn 武 from the army to the individual and it becomes the art of the trained body. 武术 wǔshù is the whole tradition of Chinese fighting arts, from 太极拳 to 少林 boxing, practised as much for discipline and health as for combat. 武功 wǔgōng is the skill itself, the level of attainment a fighter has reached, the word that fills the pages of martial-hero novels. 武打 wǔdǎ is fighting as performance, the choreographed combat of opera and film. Around them grows a whole imaginative world: 武侠 wǔxiá, the genre of wandering swordsmen bound by their own code of honour.

武术 wǔshù martial arts; kung fu (as a discipline)
N 名词 míngcí
武 wǔ (martial) + 术 shù (skill, art, technique). The Chinese martial arts as a whole tradition and discipline: 练武术 "to practise martial arts," 武术学校 "a martial arts school." Often used interchangeably in English with kung fu, though 武术 is the broader, more formal umbrella term.
他从小就开始学习武术。
Tā cóngxiǎo jiù kāishǐ xuéxí wǔshù.
He began learning martial arts from childhood.
武术不只是打斗，也是一种修养。
Wǔshù bù zhǐshì dǎdòu, yě shì yī zhǒng xiūyǎng.
Martial arts are not only combat; they are also a form of self-cultivation.
武功 wǔgōng martial skill; fighting prowess
N 名词 míngcí
武 wǔ (martial) + 功 gōng (skill, achievement, kung). The level of fighting skill a person has attained: 武功高强 "to have formidable martial skill," 一身好武功 "great fighting prowess." The defining measure of a hero in 武侠 fiction, where masters are ranked by how deep their 武功 runs.
这位老人的武功深不可测。
Zhè wèi lǎorén de wǔgōng shēn bù kě cè.
This old man's martial skill is unfathomably deep.
辨析 biànxī · 武术 vs. 武功 武术 wǔshù is the art as a system, the discipline one studies and the named styles one practises. 武功 wǔgōng is one person's mastery of it, the prowess they have built up. You study 武术 in order to acquire 武功. One is the tradition, the other the attainment.
武侠 wǔxiá martial-hero fiction; the chivalrous swordsman
N 名词 míngcí
武 wǔ (martial) + 侠 xiá (a chivalrous knight-errant). The genre of stories about wandering martial heroes bound by honour and loyalty: 武侠小说 "martial-arts fiction," 武侠片 "a martial-arts film." A vast and beloved tradition in Chinese popular culture, peopled by swordsmen who right wrongs outside the reach of the law.
他特别喜欢看武侠小说。
Tā tèbié xǐhuan kàn wǔxiá xiǎoshuō.
He especially loves reading martial-arts novels.
这部武侠片在全国都很受欢迎。
Zhè bù wǔxiá piàn zài quánguó dōu hěn shòu huānyíng.
This martial-arts film is very popular all over the country.
威武 wēiwǔ Valiant & Fierce: The Bearing of Strength
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The third face of 武 is not an institution or a skill but a quality of presence: the look and bearing of strength. 威武 wēiwǔ pairs 威 (awe-inspiring authority) with 武 to describe what is powerful and dignified, the imposing figure of a general or a guard, mighty in a way that commands respect. 英武 yīngwǔ adds 英 (heroic, outstanding) for the valiant, impressive look of a soldier or hero. Here 武 has left the battlefield and become an adjective of character: brave, martial, formidable in oneself, the visible mark of a person built for strength.

威武 wēiwǔ powerful and dignified; imposing; mighty
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
威 wēi (awe, authority) + 武 wǔ (martial). Powerful and commanding in bearing, mighty in a dignified way: 威武的将军 "an imposing general," 威武雄壮 "majestic and powerful." Describes the impressive, formidable presence of soldiers, guards, or anything built on a grand and strong scale.
这些士兵看起来非常威武。
Zhèxiē shìbīng kàn qǐlái fēicháng wēiwǔ.
These soldiers look extremely imposing.
城门前站着两座威武的石狮。
Chéngmén qián zhànzhe liǎng zuò wēiwǔ de shíshī.
Two mighty stone lions stand before the city gate.
英武 yīngwǔ valiant and impressive; heroic in bearing
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
英 yīng (heroic, outstanding) + 武 wǔ (martial). Of a person, valiant and impressive in look and manner, the brave, spirited bearing of a hero or a fine soldier: 英武不凡 "valiant and out of the ordinary." A literary, admiring word, often used of a young officer or commander.
他年轻时是一位英武的军官。
Tā niánqīng shí shì yī wèi yīngwǔ de jūnguān.
In his youth he was a valiant military officer.
武勇 wǔyǒng brave in battle; martial valour
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
武 wǔ (martial) + 勇 yǒng (brave, courageous). Bravery of the martial kind, valour in combat: the courage of a warrior who is both skilled and fearless. A more literary word than the everyday 勇敢, weighting the bravery specifically toward fighting and the battlefield.
史书称赞他的武勇。
Shǐshū chēngzàn tā de wǔyǒng.
The histories praise his martial valour.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
文武双全 wén wǔ shuāng quán accomplished in both letters and arms: master of both pen and sword Literally "civil and martial, both complete." The classic praise for a complete person who excels at both scholarship and fighting, the brush and the blade. The ideal whole human being in the traditional view, equally at home in the study and on the field. Pairs 文 (civil, literary) with 武 (martial) as the two halves of mastery.
武艺高强 wǔ yì gāo qiáng highly skilled in martial arts: a formidable fighter Literally "martial skill high and strong." Describes a person of outstanding fighting ability, a master whose technique is far above the ordinary. 武艺 is the craft of arms, the practised techniques of combat; 高强 marks it as superior. A staple description of heroes in martial fiction and historical romance.
穷兵黩武 qióng bīng dú wǔ to exhaust the troops in endless war: reckless militarism Literally "drain the soldiers, abuse the martial." A sharp condemnation of a ruler who wages war without end, squandering lives and resources in aggression for its own sake. 黩 dú means to defile or abuse, so 黩武 is the abuse of military force. The dark counterpart to the ideal that true 武 is the strength that halts conflict.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Hold 武 wǔ as a foot beneath a dagger-axe: the soldier striding to war, arms in hand. Then remember the old gloss that turned the same picture inside out, 止戈, the foot that halts the weapons. The whole character lives in that double image, the strength that marches and the strength that stops, force honoured but always pointed toward the peace it is meant to secure.

From that root the three senses follow. Force as institution gives you 武力 armed force, 武器 the weapon, 武装 to arm. Force as trained skill gives you 武术 the martial arts, 武功 a master's prowess, and the whole world of 武侠 wandering swordsmen. Force as bearing gives you 威武 powerful and dignified, 英武 valiant. And always 武 stands across from 文, the blade beside the brush: the complete person, says the idiom 文武双全, is master of both.
