字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

阳 yáng pictures a sunlit mound. On the left stands 阝, the mound or hill radical (the so-called "left ear," from 阜 fù, a slope or earthwork); on the right sits 日 rì, the sun. The image is a hill with the sun on it, and the original sense is concrete and geographic: the sunny side of a slope, the south face that catches the light all day while the far side stays in shade. From that bright slope the meaning opened outward into light itself, into brightness, and into the whole cosmic idea of the active, warming, masculine force.

The character carries a simplified-traditional pair worth noticing. The simplified form 阳 is the clean mound-plus-sun you see today; the traditional form 陽 keeps the same mound radical but replaces the simple 日 with a fuller phonetic element 昜 yáng (sun-rays streaming down), which is why the traditional glyph looks so much more elaborate. Both forms read yáng and mean the same thing; the simplification swapped the busy right side for a plain sun.

Three senses branch from the sunlit hill:

1. The sun, sunlight. The light of day made into a thing: 太阳 tàiyáng (the sun), 阳光 yángguāng (sunlight).
2. The yang principle. In cosmology 阳 is the bright, warm, active, masculine half of the universe, forever paired with 阴 yīn, its dark, cool, receptive opposite: 阴阳 yīnyáng.
3. The sunny side, in place names. A town on the sunlit (south) face of a hill or the north bank of a river often takes 阳 in its name: 洛阳 Luòyáng, 沈阳 Shěnyáng. The same sense gives 阳台 yángtái, the balcony that faces the sun.

The thread is the south slope in full light. Sun, brightness, warmth, the active force, the side that faces the day: every sense of 阳 leans toward the light, just as its partner 阴 leans into the shade.

太阳 tàiyáng Sun & Sunlight
太阳 tàiyáng the sun
N 名词 míngcí
太 tài (great; supreme) + 阳 yáng (the bright one). Literally "the great yang," the everyday word for the sun in the sky. The morning sun is 朝阳 zhāoyáng and the setting sun 夕阳 xīyáng, both built on the same 阳.
今天太阳很大。
Jīntiān tàiyáng hěn dà.
The sun is strong today.
太阳从东边升起。
Tàiyáng cóng dōngbiān shēngqǐ.
The sun rises in the east.
阳光 yángguāng sunlight; sunshine; (figuratively) sunny, upbeat
N 名词 míngcí
阳 yáng (sun) + 光 guāng (light). The light of the sun. It also works as an adjective for a bright, cheerful temperament: 阳光的男孩 "a sunny, upbeat boy."
阳光照进房间。
Yángguāng zhào jìn fángjiān.
Sunlight shines into the room.
辨析 biànxī · 太阳 vs. 阳光 太阳 is the sun as a body, the disc in the sky you can point at. 阳光 is the light it gives off, the brightness that falls on things. You sit under the 太阳 but you bask in the 阳光.
朝阳 zhāoyáng the morning sun; the rising sun
N 名词 míngcí
朝 zhāo (morning) + 阳 yáng (sun). The sun newly risen in the east, often used as an emblem of hope and fresh beginnings, as in 朝阳产业 "a sunrise industry," one full of promise.
朝阳照亮了山顶。
Zhāoyáng zhàoliàng le shāndǐng.
The morning sun lit up the mountaintop.
阴阳 yīnyáng Yang & the Pair: the Active Principle
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Beyond the sun in the sky, 阳 names one half of the oldest pair in Chinese thought. 阳 is the bright, warm, active, expanding, masculine force; 阴 yīn is its dark, cool, still, receptive, feminine complement. The geography is right there in the two characters: 阳 is the sunlit south slope, 阴 is the shaded north slope, and the same hill holds both. They are not good and evil but two faces of one whole, each defined by the other and each carrying a seed of its opposite.

Together they form 阴阳 yīnyáng, the framework that underlies traditional cosmology, the Book of Changes, and Chinese medicine alike. In the body, health is read as a balance of 阴 and 阳; a fever or restlessness may be called an excess of 阳气 yángqì, the warm active energy. Note the order in speech: the pair is almost always said 阴阳, dark before light, even though 阳 leads in the place names. To go deeper on the shaded counterpart, the 阴 entry traces the same logic from the other side.

阴阳 yīnyáng yin and yang: the complementary dark and bright forces
N 名词 míngcí
阴 yīn (shade; dark) + 阳 yáng (sun; bright). The paired forces whose interplay is held to drive all change in the cosmos. The two are opposites that complete rather than destroy each other.
中医讲究阴阳平衡。
Zhōngyī jiǎngjiu yīnyáng pínghéng.
Chinese medicine stresses the balance of yin and yang.
阳气 yángqì yang energy: warm, active vital force
N 名词 míngcí
阳 yáng (active; bright) + 气 qì (vital energy). The warm, rising, animating energy of the body in traditional medicine, set against 阴气 yīnqì. A person low on 阳气 is thought to feel cold and sluggish.
早上晒太阳可以补阳气。
Zǎoshang shài tàiyáng kěyǐ bǔ yángqì.
Getting morning sun can build up your yang energy.
阳性 yángxìng positive (medical test); masculine (grammatical gender)
N 名词 míngcí
阳 yáng (the active; the positive pole) + 性 xìng (nature; gender). In medicine, a positive test result; in grammar, the masculine gender. Its pair is 阴性 yīnxìng (negative; feminine).
他的检测结果是阳性。
Tā de jiǎncè jiéguǒ shì yángxìng.
His test result is positive.
向阳 xiàngyáng The Sunny Side & Place Names
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The oldest, most concrete sense of 阳 survives in geography. A spot on the sunny (south) face of a hill, or on the north bank of a river where the water reflects light back, was the 阳, and countless Chinese towns carry that brightness in their names: 洛阳 Luòyáng on the sun-facing bank of the Luo, 沈阳 Shěnyáng, 贵阳 Guìyáng. Read a map of China and you are reading a record of which slopes caught the light.

The same logic gives ordinary words for the sunward side of things. 向阳 xiàngyáng is "facing the sun," said of a south-facing room or a slope that gets full light; 阳台 yángtái is the balcony, the platform built to take in the sun. Wherever 阳 sits in a name, it points at the warm, lit side of the world.

阳台 yángtái balcony; terrace
N 名词 míngcí
阳 yáng (sun) + 台 tái (platform; stage). The sun-platform: the balcony or open terrace of a home, valued precisely because it catches the light and is where laundry is hung to dry.
她在阳台上晒衣服。
Tā zài yángtái shàng shài yīfu.
She is drying clothes on the balcony.
向阳 xiàngyáng facing the sun; sunny (of an aspect)
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
向 xiàng (toward; facing) + 阳 yáng (sun). Oriented toward the sun, said of a room, window, or slope that gets good light. A 向阳的房间 is a sought-after sunny room.
这间屋子向阳，很暖和。
Zhè jiān wūzi xiàngyáng, hěn nuǎnhuo.
This room faces the sun and is very warm.
夕阳 xīyáng the setting sun; evening sun
N 名词 míngcí
夕 xī (dusk; evening) + 阳 yáng (sun). The low sun of late day. By extension 夕阳 marks anything in graceful decline: 夕阳产业 "a sunset industry," the mirror of the 朝阳 sunrise one.
夕阳把天空染红了。
Xīyáng bǎ tiānkōng rǎnhóng le.
The setting sun dyed the sky red.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
阳奉阴违 yáng fèng yīn wéi to comply in the open and defy in secret: outward obedience, hidden resistance Lit: in-the-light obey, in-the-shade disobey. 阳 and 阴 split into "openly" and "covertly," naming the person who nods along to your face but quietly does the opposite. A sharp word for two-faced compliance.
阴差阳错 yīn chā yáng cuò a tangle of yin slips and yang errors: a strange chain of coincidences Lit: yin-mistake, yang-error. A run of small accidents and crossed wires that combine into an unexpected outcome. Used when things go awry, or right, through nobody's plan: 阴差阳错地错过了 "missed it by a strange fluke."
三阳开泰 sān yáng kāi tài three yang lines open into prosperity: an auspicious fresh start, a New Year blessing From the hexagram 泰 (peace) in the Book of Changes, whose lower three lines are 阳, marking yang rising as winter turns to spring. A traditional New Year greeting wishing renewal and good fortune for the year ahead.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Stand the character up as a picture: a hill on the left, 阝, with the sun, 日, resting on its slope. That is 阳, the south face of the mountain in full light. Hold that single image and every sense falls into place.

Point at the disc on the slope and it is the sun, 太阳, and the light it pours down, 阳光. Step back and let the lit slope stand for the warm, active force of the world, and you have the yang of 阴阳, forever paired with the shaded far side, 阴. Read the slope as a place and it becomes the sunny town in a name, 洛阳, or the balcony built to catch the day, 阳台. Wherever you meet 阳, look for the sun on the hill, and ask which way the light is falling.
