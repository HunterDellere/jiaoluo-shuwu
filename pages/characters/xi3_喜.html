字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

喜 xǐ = 壴 (zhù, a standing drum) over 口 (kǒu, mouth). The top element 壴 is the old pictograph of a drum mounted on a stand, the same component at the heart of 鼓 gǔ (drum). Below it sits 口, the mouth. The combined image is a festive drum sounding while a mouth opens, the music of celebration met by the smile and laughter that answer it. From that scene of joyful noise the character settled into the plain meaning of happiness and gladness.

Three senses branch from the drumbeat over the smiling mouth:

1. Joy, happiness, gladness. The core feeling: 喜欢 xǐhuan (to like), 欢喜 huānxǐ (joyful), 喜悦 xǐyuè (delight), 喜爱 xǐ'ài (to be fond of).
2. To like, to be fond of, to suit. The feeling turned toward an object: 喜爱 xǐ'ài (to love, to favor), 喜好 xǐhào (a liking, a preference), and the bound sense of a plant that 喜 (favors) sun or shade.
3. Happy events, above all the wedding. A standing word for an auspicious occasion: 喜事 xǐshì (a happy occasion, a wedding), 喜帖 xǐtiě (a wedding invitation), 报喜 bàoxǐ (to bring glad news). Doubled as 囍, it is the double-happiness emblem pasted up at every Chinese wedding.

The thread running through all three is the bright, outward warmth of the original picture. Whether it is the feeling inside, the fondness reaching toward a thing, or the celebration that gathers a family, 喜 is the drum and the smile sounding together.

喜欢 xǐhuan To Like & Love
喜欢 xǐhuan to like; to be fond of; to enjoy
V 动词 dòngcí
喜 xǐ (to delight in) + 欢 huān (joyful). The everyday word for liking something or someone. It takes both nouns and verbs: 喜欢音乐 "to like music," 喜欢看书 "to like reading." The second syllable is toneless.
我很喜欢喝茶。
Wǒ hěn xǐhuan hē chá.
I really like drinking tea.
你喜欢这个城市吗？
Nǐ xǐhuan zhège chéngshì ma?
Do you like this city?
喜爱 xǐ'ài to love; to be fond of; to favor
V 动词 dòngcí
喜 xǐ (to delight in) + 爱 ài (to love). A warmer, more literary cousin of 喜欢, often used of things admired or cherished: 深受喜爱 "deeply loved (by the public)," 喜爱的作家 "a favorite author."
这位歌手很受年轻人喜爱。
Zhè wèi gēshǒu hěn shòu niánqīngrén xǐ'ài.
This singer is much loved by young people.
辨析 biànxī · 喜欢 vs. 爱 vs. 喜爱 喜欢 is the neutral, all-purpose "to like," fine for tea, films, or people. 爱 ài is stronger and deeper: romantic love, family love, or a consuming passion. 喜爱 sits between them and leans literary, used for things or pursuits one favors rather than for a beloved person. You can 喜欢 a snack, you 爱 your parents, and you 喜爱 a particular kind of music.
喜好 xǐhào a liking; a preference; to be keen on
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
喜 xǐ (to like) + 好 hào (to be fond of, 4th tone). Both a verb and a noun for a settled preference or taste: 个人喜好 "personal preferences," 喜好读书 "fond of reading." Note 好 here is hào, not hǎo.
每个人的喜好都不一样。
Měi ge rén de xǐhào dōu bù yīyàng.
Everyone has different preferences.
喜事 xǐshì Happy Occasions: Weddings & the 囍
文化洞见 wénhuà dòngjiàn · Cultural Insight

In daily speech 喜事 xǐshì means a happy occasion, and by default it means a wedding. To say 你家有喜事 "your family has a happy event" is, without further context, to congratulate someone on a marriage or a coming birth. The same 喜 sits inside 喜酒 xǐjiǔ (the wedding banquet, literally the joy-wine), 喜糖 xǐtáng (the sweets handed out at a wedding), and 喜帖 xǐtiě (the wedding invitation).

The most familiar form of all is the doubled character 囍, the double-happiness emblem. Two 喜 set side by side, it is pasted in red and gold on doors, windows, and gifts at every wedding, and it appears on textiles, porcelain, and paper-cuts as the standing wish for marital joy. The doubling carries its own meaning: not one happiness but a shared one, the two families and the two partners joined. You will also meet the phrase 双喜临门 shuāngxǐ lín mén, "double happiness arrives at the door," said when two good things land at once.

喜事 xǐshì a happy occasion; a joyous event; (esp.) a wedding
N 名词 míngcí
喜 xǐ (joy) + 事 shì (matter, affair). Any auspicious event, and by default a wedding. Its standing opposite is 丧事 sāngshì, a funeral, the unhappy event.
听说你家有喜事，恭喜恭喜！
Tīngshuō nǐ jiā yǒu xǐshì, gōngxǐ gōngxǐ!
I hear your family has a happy occasion, congratulations!
恭喜 gōngxǐ to congratulate; congratulations
V 动词 dòngcí
恭 gōng (respectful) + 喜 xǐ (joy). To respectfully share in someone's happiness. Doubled as 恭喜恭喜 it is the warm spoken "congratulations," and 恭喜发财 gōngxǐ fācái is the standard New Year greeting wishing wealth.
恭喜你们结婚！
Gōngxǐ nǐmen jiéhūn!
Congratulations on your wedding!
惊喜 jīngxǐ a pleasant surprise; to be pleasantly surprised
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
惊 jīng (startled) + 喜 xǐ (joy). The happy jolt of an unexpected good thing: 一个惊喜 "a surprise (gift or event)," 又惊又喜 "both startled and delighted."
这份礼物给了我一个大惊喜。
Zhè fèn lǐwù gěi le wǒ yī ge dà jīngxǐ.
This gift gave me a big surprise.
喜悦 xǐyuè Joy & Delight
喜悦 xǐyuè joy; delight; gladness
N 名词 míngcí
喜 xǐ (joy) + 悦 yuè (pleased). A literary, slightly elevated word for inner gladness, the quiet glow of a good thing: 丰收的喜悦 "the joy of a good harvest," 内心的喜悦 "the delight within."
她的脸上充满了喜悦。
Tā de liǎn shàng chōngmǎn le xǐyuè.
Her face was filled with joy.
欢喜 huānxǐ happy; joyful; delighted
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
欢 huān (merry) + 喜 xǐ (glad). A warm, somewhat colloquial word for being happy and pleased, common in the south and in the phrase 欢欢喜喜 "all merry and glad," and in 皆大欢喜 "everyone happy."
大家欢欢喜喜地过年。
Dàjiā huānhuānxǐxǐ de guònián.
Everyone celebrated the New Year in high spirits.
喜气 xǐqì a festive air; an atmosphere of joy
N 名词 míngcí
喜 xǐ (joy) + 气 qì (air, mood). The bright, celebratory atmosphere of a happy occasion, the feeling in a room hung with red: 满屋喜气 "the whole house full of festive joy."
屋里布置得很有喜气。
Wū lǐ bùzhì de hěn yǒu xǐqì.
The room is decorated with a real festive air.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
喜出望外 xǐ chū wàng wài overjoyed beyond expectation: delighted by something better than hoped Lit: joy exceeds what was looked for. Said when good fortune outstrips anything you dared expect, the happy shock of a result far better than the one you were braced for.
皆大欢喜 jiē dà huān xǐ everyone is happy: an outcome that pleases all sides Lit: all greatly rejoice. Originally a Buddhist sutra formula for an assembly delighted by the teaching, now the everyday phrase for a win-win ending that leaves no party aggrieved.
喜气洋洋 xǐ qì yáng yáng brimming with joy: radiant with a festive, happy spirit Lit: festive air spilling over. 洋洋 paints joy overflowing in every direction. Said of a face, a household, or a whole town lit up with celebration, a stock phrase at weddings and the New Year.
喜闻乐见 xǐ wén lè jiàn glad to hear and happy to see: welcomed and enjoyed by all Lit: gladly heard, happily seen. Said of a form of art or entertainment that ordinary people genuinely enjoy and look forward to, the popular and well-liked.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Hear the drum first. The top of 喜 is 壴, a drum on its stand, and below it is 口, an open mouth. Set them together and you have the sound of celebration and the smile that answers it: the festive drum sounding while a mouth laughs. That is the whole character, joy made loud and visible.

From that one scene every sense follows. Turn the joy inward and it is delight (喜悦, 欢喜). Turn it toward a thing and it is liking (喜欢, 喜爱). Gather a family around the drum and it is a happy occasion, the wedding above all (喜事), and double the smile into 囍 and you have the red-and-gold emblem pasted on the wedding door. Whenever you meet 喜, picture the drum and the smiling mouth, and ask which way the joy is pointing.
