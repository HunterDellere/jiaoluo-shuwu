字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

活 is a pictophonetic compound: 氵 (the water radical) carries the meaning, and 舌 shé (tongue) supplies the sound. On the left runs the three-drop water radical 氵, the standard form of 水 shuǐ when it stands beside another component. On the right sits 舌, the tongue. The water is the heart of the character; the tongue was borrowed for its old pronunciation, close to the sound that became huó.

The original image was a stream in motion. In the Shuōwén the graph is glossed in terms of flowing water, the gurgle and rush of a current that never stands still. A river runs, a spring bubbles, water moves: and moving water is the oldest emblem a writing system could reach for when it wanted to say alive. What flows is living; what sits stagnant is dead. From that single perception the whole character grows.

From flowing water two families of meaning open out. First, to live, to be alive. The thing that breathes and moves is 活 as against 死 sǐ (dead): 活着 huózhe (to be living), 生活 shēnghuó (to live, life), 活下去 huó xiàqù (to go on living). Second, living, lively, animated. Not bare survival but the quality of liveliness, the quickness of something that flows freely: 活泼 huópo (lively), 灵活 línghuó (nimble, flexible), 活力 huólì (vitality).

A third, very concrete sense grew in everyday northern speech: 活 as a piece of manual work, a job, the task in front of your hands. 干活 gànhuó means to do work, and 活儿 huór (with the retroflex ending) is the work itself: the carpenter's 活儿, the day's 活儿. The thread is the same flow of energy, now poured into the hands and the task. Japanese took the character as 活 (katsu) for liveliness and activity, surviving in 活動 (katsudō, activity) and 生活 (seikatsu, daily life).

生活 shēnghuó Living & Alive
生活 shēnghuó life; to live; daily living
V/N 动名词 dòng-míngcí
生 shēng (to be born, to grow) + 活 huó (to live). The pairing of two life-words covers both the verb (to live, to make a life) and the noun (life as it is lived day to day). This is the everyday word for "life," distinct from 命 mìng, the life that can be lost.
他们在北京生活了十年。
Tāmen zài Běijīng shēnghuó le shí nián.
They have lived in Beijing for ten years.
这里的生活很安静。
Zhèlǐ de shēnghuó hěn ānjìng.
Life here is very quiet.
辨析 biànxī · 生活 vs. 生命 生活 is life as you live it: your routines, your circumstances, your daily existence. 生命 shēngmìng is life as the thing itself, the spark that can be saved or lost. You improve your 生活; you risk your 生命.
活着 huózhe to be alive; living
V 动词 dòngcí
活 huó (to live) + 着 zhe (the durative marker, an ongoing state). To be in the state of being alive, as opposed to 死了 (dead). Yú Huá's famous novel 《活着》 takes the word as its whole subject: simply going on living through everything.
只要活着，就有希望。
Zhǐyào huózhe, jiù yǒu xīwàng.
As long as you are alive, there is hope.
复活 fùhuó to revive; to come back to life; resurrection
V 动词 dòngcí
复 fù (again, to return) + 活 huó (to live). To live again: the dead returning to life, or by extension a thing once finished springing back. 复活节 fùhuójié is Easter, the festival of resurrection.
传说他三天后复活了。
Chuánshuō tā sān tiān hòu fùhuó le.
The legend says he came back to life after three days.
救活 jiùhuó to save someone's life; to revive (the dying)
V 动词 dòngcí
救 jiù (to rescue) + 活 huó (alive) as a resultative ending. To rescue with the result of being alive: 活 here marks the outcome, that the rescue succeeded and the person lives. The same pattern builds 养活 yǎnghuó (to support, to keep alive by feeding).
医生终于把病人救活了。
Yīshēng zhōngyú bǎ bìngrén jiùhuó le.
The doctor finally saved the patient.
活泼 huópo Lively & Moving: What Stirs and Flows
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The second family carries the water image forward into the quality of liveliness. A current that runs freely is quick, supple, full of energy, and 活 lends all of this to character and motion. 活泼 huópo describes a lively, animated person, most often a child, the opposite of dull or withdrawn. 灵活 línghuó is the same freedom applied to the body or the mind: nimble, adaptable, able to change course like water finding a new channel.

From there it powers a cluster of vitality words. 活力 huólì is energy and vigor, the vitality of a person or a city; 活跃 huóyuè is to be active, brisk, animated, the verb for a lively atmosphere or a busy market. Even 活动 huódòng, the everyday word for an activity or event, is built from 活 (lively) plus 动 (to move): a stirring, a thing in motion. Wherever Chinese wants to name energy in flow rather than mere existence, 活 is the character that does it.

活泼 huópo lively; vivacious; animated
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
活 huó (lively) + 泼 pō (to splash, itself a water word). A doubled water image, the splash of a living current, describing a bright, spirited, outgoing temperament. Most natural for children and young people: 活泼可爱 (lively and adorable).
这个小女孩很活泼。
Zhège xiǎo nǚhái hěn huópo.
This little girl is very lively.
灵活 línghuó flexible; nimble; agile; adaptable
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
灵 líng (quick, clever, responsive) + 活 huó (lively, free). Able to move or change freely: nimble fingers, a flexible schedule, a supple mind. The opposite is 死板 sǐbǎn (rigid, literally dead-stiff), where the water has stopped flowing.
他的手指很灵活。
Tā de shǒuzhǐ hěn línghuó.
His fingers are very nimble.
我们的安排比较灵活。
Wǒmen de ānpái bǐjiào línghuó.
Our arrangement is fairly flexible.
活动 huódòng activity; event; to move about; to exercise
V/N 动名词 dòng-míngcí
活 huó (lively) + 动 dòng (to move). As a noun, an organized activity or event; as a verb, to move one's body, to stretch and stir after sitting still. The lively-and-moving pair names anything that gets people up and going.
学校周末有很多活动。
Xuéxiào zhōumò yǒu hěn duō huódòng.
The school has many activities on the weekend.
坐久了，出去活动一下。
Zuò jiǔ le, chūqù huódòng yíxià.
You have been sitting a while; go move around a bit.
快活 kuàihuo happy; carefree; merry
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
快 kuài (quick, pleased) + 活 huo (lively). A light, cheerful gladness, the easy happiness of someone living well and without care. Close to 快乐 kuàilè but warmer and more colloquial, often suggesting a carefree ease of life.
他过着快活的日子。
Tā guò zhe kuàihuo de rìzi.
He lives a carefree, happy life.
辨析 biànxī · 快活 vs. 快乐 快乐 is the standard, neutral word for happy, fine in writing and on a birthday card. 快活 is more colloquial and earthier, carrying a sense of carefree, unburdened living rather than just a happy mood.
干活 gànhuó Work & the Job
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The third sense is purely colloquial and very Chinese: 活 as a piece of manual work, the task your hands take on. In everyday northern speech 活儿 huór (with the retroflex 儿 ending) is the work itself, the concrete job: a tailor's 活儿, the farmer's 活儿, a hard day's 活儿. To do work is 干活 gànhuó, where 干 gàn means to do or to labor. This is the language of the workshop and the field, not the office, and it keeps the old energy of the character: living force poured into the hands.

干活 gànhuó to work; to do manual labor
V 动词 dòngcí
干 gàn (to do, to labor) + 活 huó (work, a task). The everyday spoken phrase for doing physical or hands-on work. Colloquial and slightly blue-collar in flavor, the verb you reach for about chores, jobs on a site, or work around the house.
他从早到晚都在干活。
Tā cóng zǎo dào wǎn dōu zài gànhuó.
He works from morning to night.
活儿 huór work; a job; a task; a piece of work
N 名词 míngcí
活 huó (work) + the retroflex 儿 (ér) ending, typical of northern colloquial speech. The concrete job itself: the work to be done, often skilled handwork. A fine piece of craftsmanship is 好活儿 (good work).
这是个细活儿，得慢慢来。
Zhè shì ge xì huór, děi mànman lái.
This is delicate work; it has to be done slowly.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
活灵活现 huó líng huó xiàn vivid and lifelike: so vivid it seems to come alive before you Lit: alive-spirit-alive-appearing, with 活 doubled for emphasis. Describes a story, performance, or portrait so vivid and animated that the thing depicted seems present in the room. High praise for vividness.
活蹦乱跳 huó bèng luàn tiào alive and bouncing about: brimming with health and energy Lit: alive-leaping-wildly-jumping. The image of a fish on the deck or a healthy child who cannot keep still. Used for someone bursting with vitality, often after recovering from illness.
养家活口 yǎng jiā huó kǒu to support the family and feed its mouths: to provide for one's household Lit: support-family-keep-alive-mouths. Here 活 is causative, to keep alive by feeding. The duty of the breadwinner to put food on the table and sustain everyone under the roof.
死去活来 sǐ qù huó lái to die away and come back: to the point of fainting and reviving Lit: die-go-live-come, pairing 死 and 活. Describes extremity of feeling or pain so severe one seems to faint and revive: to cry 哭得死去活来 (to weep oneself into a faint), to be beaten half to death.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a stream running over a tongue. On the left, the three drops of the water radical 氵 are the current itself, splashing downhill. On the right, 舌 the tongue is the rock the water tumbles across, the part that was only ever borrowed for its sound. Hear the gurgle: that rushing, living water is the whole meaning. What flows is alive; what sits still is dead.

Now follow the water. Let it pool into a life and you have 生活, daily living. Let it run quick and bright and it becomes 活泼 (lively) and 灵活 (nimble), water finding any channel. Let it stir a crowd into motion and it is 活动, an activity. And let it pour down into the hands at a workbench and it is 活儿, the day's work, 干活. The drops on the left never stop moving: every sense of 活 is some shape of that flow, from being alive, to being lively, to the labor your hands are alive with.
