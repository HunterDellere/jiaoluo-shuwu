字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

话 huà = 讠 (yán, speech) + 舌 (shé, tongue). The traditional form 話 writes the speech radical in full as 言; the simplified 话 compresses it to 讠. Either way the logic is the same: speech put together with the tongue, the organ that shapes it. Where the bare speech radical 讠 marks anything done through the mouth, the added 舌 narrows it to the living, spoken word, the thing the tongue actually produces.

From spoken words, the meaning spreads along three lines:

1. Words, speech, what one says. The literal sense: 说话 shuōhuà (to speak), 一句话 yí jù huà (a single remark), 听话 tīnghuà (to obey, literally "to heed words").
2. A language or dialect. A whole spoken variety: 普通话 pǔtōnghuà (Mandarin, the "common speech"), 广东话 Guǎngdōnghuà (Cantonese), 中国话 (the Chinese language as spoken).
3. A topic or saying. A unit of talk passed between people: 话题 huàtí (topic), 笑话 xiàohua (joke), 童话 tónghuà (fairy tale).

The thread is the spoken word as a living thing that leaves the tongue and travels: said aloud, carried down a wire, shaped into a dialect, or handed around as a story. 话 is talk in motion, distinct from 字 (the written character) and 语 (language as a system).

说话 shuōhuà To Speak & Talk
说话 shuōhuà to speak; to talk; to say something
V 动词 dòngcí
说 shuō (to say) + 话 huà (words). The everyday verb for speaking, formed as verb-plus-object: literally "to say words." Because 话 is already the object, you do not say 说话一件事; to speak about something you use 说 alone: 说一件事.
请大声说话。
Qǐng dàshēng shuōhuà.
Please speak loudly.
他在跟朋友说话。
Tā zài gēn péngyou shuōhuà.
He is talking with a friend.
听话 tīnghuà to be obedient; to heed what one is told
V 动词 dòngcí
听 tīng (to listen) + 话 huà (words). To listen to the words, hence to do as told. Said above all of children and, gently, of anyone who follows guidance: 这孩子很听话 "this child is very obedient."
这个孩子很听话。
Zhège háizi hěn tīnghuà.
This child is very obedient.
实话 shíhuà the truth; honest words
N 名词 míngcí
实 shí (real; true) + 话 huà (words). Words that match the facts. The set phrase 说实话 shuō shíhuà, "to tell the truth," also works as an aside meaning "honestly speaking."
说实话，我不太喜欢。
Shuō shíhuà, wǒ bú tài xǐhuan.
Honestly, I don't really like it.
辨析 biànxī · 话 vs. 字 vs. 语 话 huà is the spoken word, talk as it leaves the mouth. 字 zì is the written character on the page. 语 yǔ is language as a system (语言 yǔyán, 汉语 Hànyǔ). So 普通话 is the spoken standard you hear, while 汉语 names the Chinese language overall.
电话 diànhuà Phone & Connected Speech
电话 diànhuà telephone; a phone call
N 名词 míngcí
电 diàn (electric) + 话 huà (speech). Speech carried on electricity, the telephone, and by extension the call itself: 打电话 dǎ diànhuà "to make a phone call," 接电话 "to answer the phone."
我给你打电话。
Wǒ gěi nǐ dǎ diànhuà.
I'll give you a call.
普通话 pǔtōnghuà Mandarin; standard spoken Chinese
N 名词 míngcí
普通 pǔtōng (common; ordinary) + 话 huà (speech). The "common speech," the standardized spoken language of mainland China, based on the Beijing dialect. In Taiwan the same standard is called 国语 guóyǔ.
他的普通话说得很好。
Tā de pǔtōnghuà shuō de hěn hǎo.
He speaks Mandarin very well.
对话 duìhuà dialogue; conversation; to hold talks
V/N 动名词
对 duì (facing; mutual) + 话 huà (speech). Speech exchanged face to face. Concrete dialogue in a textbook or film, and figurative dialogue between sides: 和平对话 "peace talks."
请听这段对话。
Qǐng tīng zhè duàn duìhuà.
Please listen to this dialogue.
话题 huàtí Topics & Sayings
话题 huàtí topic; subject of conversation
N 名词 míngcí
话 huà (talk) + 题 tí (subject; theme). What a conversation is about: 换话题 "to change the subject," 热门话题 "a hot topic."
我们换个话题吧。
Wǒmen huàn ge huàtí ba.
Let's change the subject.
笑话 xiàohua a joke; (as a verb) to laugh at, to mock
N/V 名动词
笑 xiào (to laugh) + 话 huà (words). Words that make people laugh, a joke: 讲笑话 "to tell a joke." As a verb it turns sour, to laugh at someone: 别笑话我 "don't make fun of me."
他讲了一个笑话。
Tā jiǎng le yí ge xiàohua.
He told a joke.
童话 tónghuà fairy tale; children's story
N 名词 míngcí
童 tóng (child) + 话 huà (story). A tale told for children. Its kin 神话 shénhuà (god-story) means a myth, and 神话 and 童话 together cover the world of fabulous stories.
孩子们都喜欢听童话。
Háizimen dōu xǐhuan tīng tónghuà.
Children all love to hear fairy tales.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
话里有话 huà lǐ yǒu huà there are words within the words: a remark carries a hidden meaning Lit: inside-the-words-there-are-words. Said when someone speaks with an implied second meaning, hinting at more than the surface remark admits.
二话不说 èr huà bù shuō without a second word: at once, without hesitation or argument Lit: a-second-word-not-said. Said of someone who acts immediately, raising no objection and wasting no breath: 他二话不说就答应了 "he agreed without a moment's hesitation."
说来话长 shuō lái huà cháng to start telling it makes a long story: it is a long story Lit: to-speak-of-it-the-words-grow-long. The standard preface to a complicated explanation, the Chinese equivalent of "well, it's a long story."
话不投机 huà bù tóu jī the talk does not meet: the conversation does not click Lit: words-not-matching-the-moment. Said when two people simply cannot find common ground in conversation. The fuller saying 话不投机半句多 adds that "when talk does not click, even half a sentence is too much."
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

See the speech radical 讠 standing beside a tongue, 舌. That is the whole character: speech shaped by the tongue, the living spoken word. Everything 话 means is talk in motion as it leaves the mouth.

Said plainly to another person, it is speaking (说话) and being heeded (听话). Carried down a wire, it is the telephone (电话). Standardized across a country, it is a spoken language (普通话). Handed around for amusement or wonder, it is a joke or a tale (笑话, 童话). Keep 话 apart from 字, the written character, and from 语, language as a system, and you will always know you are dealing with words that are spoken.
