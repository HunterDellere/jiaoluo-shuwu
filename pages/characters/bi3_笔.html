字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

笔 bǐ is the picture of a writing brush. The simplified form sets bamboo, the radical ⺮ (zhú), over hair, 毛 (máo): the bamboo handle above and the soft animal-hair tip below, the two materials that make a Chinese brush. The character is a compound of meanings rather than a sound-borrowing, and you can read its parts off the page directly: bamboo for the shaft, hair for the tuft that holds the ink.

The traditional form 筆 tells the older story. It keeps bamboo ⺮ on top but sets 聿 (yù) below, an early pictograph of a hand gripping a brush. 聿 by itself meant "writing brush" in the oldest texts; adding the bamboo radical specified the bamboo-handled instrument. The simplified 笔 swapped that hand-and-brush element for the plainer 毛 (hair), making the same object easier to write while keeping the bamboo-over-tuft logic intact. 笔 and 筆 are the standard simplified and traditional pair for the one word.

From the implement three senses unfold:

1. The writing tool. The brush or pen itself: 毛笔 máobǐ (writing brush), 铅笔 qiānbǐ (pencil), 钢笔 gāngbǐ (fountain pen), 笔尖 bǐjiān (the nib or tip).
2. The act and trace of writing. What the brush does and leaves behind: 笔记 bǐjì (notes), 笔画 bǐhuà (a stroke of a character), 笔顺 bǐshùn (stroke order).
3. A measure word. Borrowed to count sums of money, accounts, and deals, things "written down" in a ledger: 一笔钱 yī bǐ qián (a sum of money), 一笔生意 yī bǐ shēngyì (a business deal).

The thread is the loaded brush touching the page. It is at once the object in the hand, the motion it makes, and, by extension, each entry it sets down in a book of accounts.

毛笔 máobǐ Brushes & Pens
毛笔 máobǐ writing brush; ink brush
N 名词 míngcí
毛 máo (hair; fur) + 笔 bǐ. The classic soft-tipped brush of calligraphy and ink painting, named for the animal hair of its tuft. The tool that gives Chinese writing its living, varying line.
他用毛笔写春联。
Tā yòng máobǐ xiě chūnlián.
He writes Spring Festival couplets with a brush.
铅笔 qiānbǐ pencil
N 名词 míngcí
铅 qiān (lead) + 笔 bǐ. The everyday pencil, named for the old idea of a "lead" pencil even though the core is graphite. One of the first writing tools a child learns to name.
请用铅笔写。
Qǐng yòng qiānbǐ xiě.
Please write in pencil.
钢笔 gāngbǐ fountain pen
N 名词 míngcí
钢 gāng (steel) + 笔 bǐ. The fountain pen, named for its steel nib. Its cousins fill out the modern desk: 圆珠笔 yuánzhūbǐ (ballpoint), 毛笔 máobǐ (brush), 铅笔 qiānbǐ (pencil).
这支钢笔是爷爷送的。
Zhè zhī gāngbǐ shì yéye sòng de.
This fountain pen was a gift from grandfather.
笔画 bǐhuà Writing & Strokes
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Once 笔 names the tool, it slides easily into the writing the tool produces. A single touch of the brush to paper is a 笔画 bǐhuà, a stroke, and every Chinese character is built from a fixed set of them. The order in which those strokes are written is the 笔顺 bǐshùn, the stroke order, which a learner drills until the hand moves on its own. The unique trace your particular hand leaves is your 笔迹 bǐjì, your handwriting.

Pushed further, 笔 stands for written expression as a craft. 文笔 wénbǐ is one's writing style or literary skill, the quality of the line a writer draws across a page of prose. A clumsy or spoiled passage is a 败笔 bàibǐ, a "failed stroke," while a brilliant turn of phrase is praised as a 妙笔 miàobǐ. Here 笔 stands in for the act of writing the way "the pen" does in English idioms about authorship.

笔记 bǐjì notes; to take notes
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
笔 bǐ (pen) + 记 jì (to record). What you write down to remember: 记笔记 jì bǐjì "to take notes." It also names the modern 笔记本 bǐjìběn, the notebook and, by extension, the laptop.
上课的时候我喜欢记笔记。
Shàngkè de shíhou wǒ xǐhuan jì bǐjì.
I like to take notes during class.
笔画 bǐhuà stroke (of a Chinese character)
N 名词 míngcí
笔 bǐ (brush) + 画 huà (a line drawn). One movement of the brush, the atomic unit a character is built from. 笔 itself has ten 笔画; 一 has one. Pairs with 笔顺 bǐshùn, the order they are written in.
这个字有几笔画？
Zhège zì yǒu jǐ bǐhuà?
How many strokes does this character have?
文笔 wénbǐ writing style; literary skill
N 名词 míngcí
文 wén (writing; letters) + 笔 bǐ (pen). The quality of someone's prose, the line their pen draws across a page: 文笔流畅 "fluent, flowing style." Praise or critique aimed at how a thing is written, not what it says.
这位作家的文笔很优美。
Zhè wèi zuòjiā de wénbǐ hěn yōuměi.
This writer has a very graceful style.
辨析 biànxī · 笔 vs. 写 笔 is the implement and, by extension, the writing it produces: a noun, the pen and its trace. 写 xiě is the action of writing: a verb, the hand moving the pen. You 写 (write) with a 笔 (pen), and the result can be judged by its 笔画 (strokes) or its 文笔 (style).
一笔 yī bǐ The Measure Word: Sums and Deals
语法洞见 yǔfǎ dòngjiàn · Grammatical Insight

The same 笔 that means "a stroke" works as a measure word, and the image holds: each entry in a ledger is one stroke of the pen. Placed between a number and a noun, 笔 counts sums of money, accounts, and transactions: 一笔钱 yī bǐ qián (a sum of money), 一笔账 yī bǐ zhàng (an account), 一笔生意 yī bǐ shēngyì (a piece of business), 一大笔收入 yī dà bǐ shōurù (a large income).

The choice of measure word frames the thing as a single recorded item: money treated as a lump sum rather than loose coins, a deal treated as one written-down transaction. Compare 一些钱 yīxiē qián "some money," which is vague, with 一笔钱 yī bǐ qián, which feels like a definite, accountable amount. When you hear 笔 in front of 钱, 账, 生意, or 债 zhài (debt), it is this ledger sense, not a literal pen.

一笔钱 yī bǐ qián a sum of money
MW 量词 liàngcí
一 yī (one) + 笔 bǐ (measure word) + 钱 qián (money). A definite, lump amount of money, the kind written down as a single entry: 一大笔钱 "a large sum," 这笔钱 "this sum of money."
他存了一笔钱。
Tā cún le yī bǐ qián.
He saved up a sum of money.
一笔生意 yī bǐ shēngyì a (single) business deal
MW 量词 liàngcí
一 yī (one) + 笔 bǐ (measure word) + 生意 shēngyì (business). One transaction or deal, counted as a single recorded piece of business: 做一笔生意 "to do a deal," 一笔大生意 "a big deal."
他们谈成了一笔大生意。
Tāmen tánchéng le yī bǐ dà shēngyì.
They closed a big deal.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
笔走龙蛇 bǐ zǒu lóng shé the brush moves like dragons and snakes: bold, flowing, vigorous calligraphy Lit: brush-runs-dragon-snake. The line of cursive writing twists and surges with the energy of a dragon and a snake in motion. High praise for a calligrapher whose hand is alive and free.
妙笔生花 miào bǐ shēng huā a marvelous brush brings forth flowers: a gifted, beautiful pen Lit: marvelous-brush-grows-flowers. From the legend that a poet dreamed flowers blossomed on the tip of his brush, after which his writing turned brilliant. Said of writing of rare beauty and grace.
一笔勾销 yī bǐ gōu xiāo to cancel with one stroke of the pen: to write off entirely Lit: one-stroke-cross-out. With a single line drawn through the ledger, a debt or a past grievance is wiped clean. Used of forgiving a debt or letting an old matter be settled and forgotten.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

See the character itself as a brush stood on its tip: bamboo ⺮ at the top is the hollow handle, and 毛 (hair) below is the soft tuft loaded with ink. That is the whole word, the implement drawn in two materials. The traditional 筆 keeps the bamboo but puts a hand on the brush (聿) instead of the hair, the same object from the side of the one who holds it.

From the brush in the hand, follow the ink. Touch it to paper and you get a stroke (笔画); let the hand run and you get writing and style (笔记, 文笔); enter the result in a ledger and each line becomes a count (一笔钱, 一笔生意). Wherever you meet 笔, picture the loaded tip touching the page, and ask whether it is the tool, the writing, or the entry in the book.
