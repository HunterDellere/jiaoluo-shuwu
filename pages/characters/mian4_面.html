字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

面 miàn is a pictograph of a face drawn inside its own outline. The oracle-bone form encloses an eye within a boundary that traces the edge of the head, so the whole character means "the bounded front of the head," the face as the surface you turn toward the world. The single eye in the early form stands for the whole countenance. The modern shape keeps the outer frame and the features packed inside it.

From "the face you present" the meaning opened outward in a straight line. A face is a surface, so 面 came to mean any surface or side; a surface seen one way among many became an aspect or facet; a flat thing measured by its faces gave 面 its life as a measure word. Three senses fall out:

1. Face, facing. The literal countenance and the act of turning toward: 脸面 liǎnmiàn (face), 面对 miànduì (to face), 面前 miànqián (in front of), 面子 miànzi (face in the social sense, prestige).
2. Surface, side, aspect. 表面 biǎomiàn (surface), 方面 fāngmiàn (aspect), and the locational set 上面 shàngmiàn (above), 下面 xiàmiàn (below), 外面 wàimiàn (outside), 里面 lǐmiàn (inside).
3. Measure word for flat objects. 一面镜子 yī miàn jìngzi (a mirror), 一面墙 yī miàn qiáng (a wall), 一面旗 yī miàn qí (a flag).

There is one more sense that is purely a matter of script. In traditional writing, "flour" and "noodles" were a separate character, 麵 (also written 麪), built on the 麦 mài wheat radical. The simplified reform folded 麵 into 面, so today a single 面 carries both the ancient "face" and the unrelated "flour, noodles." Context, never the glyph, tells them apart: 洗面 means wash the face, 吃面 means eat noodles.

构词 gòucí Word-Formation Patterns
构词规律 gòucí guīlǜ · One Glyph, Several Jobs 面 as "face / facing" → 面对 face-toward · 面前 in-front · 面子 social face · 见面 meet (face-see)

脸面 liǎnmiàn Face & Facing, the Literal Countenance
见面 jiànmiàn to meet; to see one another (in person)
V 动词 dòngcí
见 jiàn (to see) + 面 miàn (face). Literally "to see faces," the everyday verb for meeting someone in person. It is a separable verb: 见了一面 "met once," 见过面 "have met before."
我们明天见面吧。
Wǒmen míngtiān jiànmiàn ba.
Let's meet tomorrow.
他们好几年没见面了。
Tāmen hǎojǐ nián méi jiànmiàn le.
They haven't seen each other in years.
面对 miànduì to face; to confront; to be faced with
V 动词 dòngcí
面 miàn (face) + 对 duì (toward; facing). To turn one's face toward something, and so to confront it: a difficulty, a choice, a reality. Used both physically and figuratively.
我们必须面对现实。
Wǒmen bìxū miànduì xiànshí.
We have to face reality.
面前 miànqián in front of; before (someone); in one's presence
N 名词 míngcí
面 miàn (face) + 前 qián (front). The space directly before one's face. Often used figuratively: 在困难面前 "in the face of difficulty," 在事实面前 "before the facts."
困难在我们面前。
Kùnnan zài wǒmen miànqián.
The difficulty lies before us.
面子 miànzi face (social standing); prestige; dignity
N 名词 míngcí
面 miàn (face) + 子 zi (suffix). The face turned outward toward society: one's standing in others' eyes. To 给面子 gěi miànzi is to grant someone respect; to 丢面子 diū miànzi is to lose face. A central concept in Chinese social life.
他很爱面子。
Tā hěn ài miànzi.
He is very concerned with saving face.

表面 biǎomiàn Surface, Side & Aspect
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Once a face is also a surface, 面 becomes the language's standard way of naming sides and aspects. A thing seen from the front shows one 面; turn it and another 面 appears. From this come 方面 fāngmiàn (an aspect of a matter, literally a "direction-face"), 表面 biǎomiàn (the outer surface, often with the sense of mere appearance), 全面 quánmiàn (all-sided, comprehensive), and 片面 piànmiàn (one-sided, partial).

The same logic powers the everyday locational words. 面 attaches to a direction to name the region on that side: 上面 shàngmiàn (the side above), 下面 xiàmiàn (below), 外面 wàimiàn (outside), 里面 lǐmiàn (inside), 后面 hòumiàn (behind), 前面 qiánmiàn (in front). Each is literally a "face" of the space around you.

方面 fāngmiàn aspect; respect; side (of a matter)
N 名词 míngcí
方 fāng (direction) + 面 miàn (face). A direction-face of a question, that is, one aspect of it. The workhorse word for breaking a topic into parts: 一方面…另一方面 "on one hand, on the other hand."
这个问题有很多方面。
Zhège wèntí yǒu hěn duō fāngmiàn.
This question has many aspects.
表面 biǎomiàn surface; outside; outward appearance
N 名词 míngcí
表 biǎo (outer; to show) + 面 miàn (face). The outer surface of a thing, and by extension the surface meaning as opposed to the truth beneath: 表面上 biǎomiàn shàng "on the surface, ostensibly."
表面上他很冷静。
Biǎomiàn shàng tā hěn lěngjìng.
On the surface he seems calm.
上面 shàngmiàn above; on top; the aforementioned
N 名词 míngcí
上 shàng (up) + 面 miàn (side). The region above or on top. In writing it also means "the above," what was said earlier, and colloquially "the higher-ups," the authorities.
桌子上面有一本书。
Zhuōzi shàngmiàn yǒu yī běn shū.
There is a book on top of the table.
一面 yī miàn one side; one surface; (measure) one flat object
Meas 量词 liàngcí
一 yī (one) + 面 miàn (measure word). 面 counts flat, surface-like objects: 一面镜子 (a mirror), 一面墙 (a wall), 一面旗 (a flag). As a noun 一面 also means "one side" of an argument or "one aspect."
墙上挂着一面镜子。
Qiáng shàng guà zhe yī miàn jìngzi.
A mirror hangs on the wall.
面条 miàntiáo Flour & Noodles, the Merged 麵
字形洞见 zìxíng dòngjiàn · Script Insight

This whole family has nothing to do with faces. In traditional script it belonged to a different character, 麵 miàn (also written 麪), built on the 麦 mài wheat radical, meaning ground wheat: flour and the noodles made from it. When characters were simplified, 麵 was merged into the much simpler 面, and the two senses have shared one glyph ever since. On a menu, every 面 is wheat: 面条 miàntiáo (noodles), 面包 miànbāo (bread), 方便面 fāngbiànmiàn (instant noodles).

面条 miàntiáo noodles
N 名词 míngcí
面 miàn (flour, from 麵) + 条 tiáo (strip; long thin thing). Literally "flour-strips." The standard word for noodles, a staple across northern China especially. Often shortened to just 面 in context: 吃面 "eat noodles."
我中午吃了一碗面条。
Wǒ zhōngwǔ chī le yī wǎn miàntiáo.
I had a bowl of noodles at noon.
面包 miànbāo bread
N 名词 míngcí
面 miàn (flour) + 包 bāo (wrapped, bun). Literally "flour-bun." The general word for bread of the Western loaf type, as distinct from steamed 馒头 mántou.
早餐我吃面包喝牛奶。
Zǎocān wǒ chī miànbāo hē niúnǎi.
For breakfast I have bread and milk.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
面面俱到 miàn miàn jù dào every side attended to: thorough, leaving nothing out Lit: side-side-all-reached. To handle every aspect of a matter so that nothing is neglected. Praise for thoroughness, though sometimes a gentle dig at someone who covers all bases without committing to any.
面红耳赤 miàn hóng ěr chì face red and ears scarlet: flushed with anger, shame, or excitement Lit: face-red-ears-scarlet. The visible flush of strong feeling, most often used of two people arguing so heatedly that both go red in the face.
面不改色 miàn bù gǎi sè without a change of expression: perfectly composed in the face of danger Lit: face-not-change-color. To keep an unflinching expression when others would pale, the mark of great calm or great nerve under pressure.
满面春风 mǎn miàn chūn fēng a face full of spring breeze: beaming, radiant with joy Lit: full-face-spring-wind. The whole face glowing like a warm spring day, said of someone visibly pleased, content, or flush with good fortune.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Start with the face drawn inside its outline. That face is the surface you turn toward the world, so 面 is the front you present (面对, 见面) and the standing you hold in others' eyes (面子). Tilt the face flat and it becomes a plain surface (表面), then any side or aspect of a thing (方面), then the region on each side of you (上面, 里面, 外面). Lay it down completely and it counts flat objects: 一面镜子, a mirror.

The noodles are the trick. They were never a face at all, just the old wheat character 麵 that simplified writing dropped onto the same glyph. So when 面 appears at the dinner table, forget the face entirely and think only of flour: 面条, 面包, a bowl of wheat.
