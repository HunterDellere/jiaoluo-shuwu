字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

汉 hàn is a water character: 氵 (the three-dot water radical) + a phonetic. The water element 氵 on the left tells you the original sense is a body of water, and the right side supplies the sound. In the full traditional form 漢 the right side is the phonetic difficult-to-read element that the simplification of the 1950s replaced with the plain stroke cluster 又 (yòu, hand), giving the modern 汉. Both forms share the same reading and the same first meaning.

That first meaning is a place: the Han River, 汉水 Hànshuǐ (also 汉江 Hànjiāng), a major tributary of the Yangtze in central China. The name then climbs from the river to nearly everything Chinese:

1. The river itself. 汉水 Hànshuǐ, the watercourse the character was coined to name. The Milky Way was also called 天汉 tiānhàn (the Sky River) and 银汉 yínhàn (the Silver River) by classical poets.
2. The dynasty. The first ruler founded his realm in the Han River basin and took 汉 as its name. The Han dynasty (206 BCE to 220 CE) became the long golden age against which later China measured itself.
3. The people and their language. Because the dynasty defined the culture, the majority ethnicity came to call itself 汉族 Hànzú (the Han), its speech 汉语 Hànyǔ (Chinese), and its writing 汉字 Hànzì (Chinese characters).
4. A man, a fellow. By a later colloquial extension 汉 came to mean an adult male: 男子汉 nánzǐhàn (a real man), 好汉 hǎohàn (a brave fellow), 大汉 dàhàn (a big strapping man).

The whole chain runs from water outward: a river named a dynasty, the dynasty named a people, the people named their tongue and their script, and along the way 汉 picked up the everyday sense of a sturdy man. To write Chinese in Chinese is, literally, to write 汉字, the characters of the Han.

汉语 hànyǔ Language & Script
汉语 Hànyǔ Chinese (the language); the Han tongue
N 名词 míngcí
汉 Hàn (the Han) + 语 yǔ (language, speech). The standard scholarly name for the Chinese language, framed by ethnicity rather than country. Its sister 中文 zhōngwén leans toward the written language, while 普通话 pǔtōnghuà names the standard Mandarin spoken form.
他的汉语说得很流利。
Tā de Hànyǔ shuō de hěn liúlì.
He speaks Chinese very fluently.
我在大学学汉语。
Wǒ zài dàxué xué Hànyǔ.
I study Chinese at university.
辨析 biànxī · 汉语 vs. 中文 汉语 names the language of the Han people and is the usual term for the spoken tongue and for linguistic study. 中文 literally means "Chinese writing" and tilts toward the written and literary language, though in daily use the two overlap heavily.
汉字 Hànzì Chinese character; Han character
N 名词 míngcí
汉 Hàn (the Han) + 字 zì (character, written word). The logographic characters in which Chinese is written, the very thing this page is made of. The same word is borrowed into Japanese as kanji and Korean as hanja, both writing the characters 漢字.
这个汉字怎么写？
Zhège Hànzì zěnme xiě?
How do you write this character?
汉字有几千年的历史。
Hànzì yǒu jǐ qiān nián de lìshǐ.
Chinese characters have a history of several thousand years.
汉学 hànxué Sinology; the study of Chinese language and civilization
N 名词 míngcí
汉 Hàn (Chinese) + 学 xué (study, learning). The academic study of Chinese language, texts, and culture, especially as pursued by scholars outside China. A 汉学家 hànxuéjiā is a sinologist.
他是一位有名的汉学家。
Tā shì yī wèi yǒumíng de hànxuéjiā.
He is a well-known sinologist.
汉族 hànzú People & Dynasty
历史洞见 lìshǐ dòngjiàn · Historical Insight

The leap from a place name to an ethnic name runs through one dynasty. The founder built his state in the Han River valley and named it 汉, and over its long span (206 BCE to 220 CE) the Han dynasty set the template for what Chinese civilization would be: a unified empire, a Confucian bureaucracy, a shared script, the Silk Road trade. By the time it ended, the people of that empire had begun to call themselves 汉人 Hànrén, the people of Han, and the modern ethnonym 汉族 Hànzú carries the dynasty forward to this day.

This is why 汉 sits at the center of how China names itself from the inside. The country is 中国 Zhōngguó, but the majority people are 汉族, their language 汉语, their writing 汉字. The dynasty is long gone, yet its name became the everyday word for being Chinese in the cultural and ethnic sense, a rare case of a ruling house outliving itself inside the language.

汉族 Hànzú the Han ethnicity; the Han people
N 名词 míngcí
汉 Hàn (of Han) + 族 zú (clan, ethnic group). The majority ethnic group of China, named after the Han dynasty. Set against the 少数民族 shǎoshù mínzú, the country's many minority peoples.
汉族是中国人口最多的民族。
Hànzú shì Zhōngguó rénkǒu zuì duō de mínzú.
The Han are the most populous ethnic group in China.
汉朝 Hàncháo the Han dynasty
N 名词 míngcí
汉 Hàn (the dynastic name) + 朝 cháo (dynasty, court). The dynasty (206 BCE to 220 CE) whose name became the name of a people. A defining golden age of unified empire, remembered as the standard for later eras.
汉朝是中国历史上的一个黄金时代。
Hàncháo shì Zhōngguó lìshǐ shàng de yī gè huángjīn shídài.
The Han dynasty was a golden age in Chinese history.
汉人 Hànrén a Han person; an ethnic Chinese
N 名词 míngcí
汉 Hàn (of Han) + 人 rén (person). An individual of the Han ethnicity, the term often used in historical writing to distinguish the Han majority from neighboring peoples. 汉族 names the group; 汉人 names the person.
那个地区的汉人不多。
Nàge dìqū de Hànrén bù duō.
There are not many Han people in that region.
好汉 hǎohàn The Man: Fellow and Hero
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Alongside the grand senses, 汉 carries a humbler, very colloquial one: a man, a fellow, especially a sturdy or manly one. This is the 汉 of 男子汉 nánzǐhàn, a real man in the sense of someone with grit and backbone, and of 好汉 hǎohàn, a brave fellow or stout-hearted hero. The classic novel 水浒传 (Outlaws of the Marsh) is full of 好汉, the rough but loyal heroes of the marsh.

The flavor is masculine and slightly heroic or earthy. A 大汉 dàhàn is a big strapping man; a 醉汉 zuìhàn is a drunkard; a 单身汉 dānshēnhàn is a bachelor. In every case 汉 frames its man as a physical, plain-spoken type rather than a refined gentleman, which is the work the honorific 子 does in 君子. Two characters for a man, two very different registers.

男子汉 nánzǐhàn a real man; a manly man
N 名词 míngcí
男子 nánzǐ (male, man) + 汉 hàn (fellow). A man in the sense of courage and backbone, the one who keeps his word and bears hardship. 男子汉大丈夫 "a true man and great fellow" is the full heroic flourish.
男子汉说到做到。
Nánzǐhàn shuō dào zuò dào.
A real man does what he says.
好汉 hǎohàn a brave fellow; a stout-hearted hero
N 名词 míngcí
好 hǎo (good) + 汉 hàn (fellow). A bold, admirable man, the heroic outlaw or stalwart of old novels. The saying 好汉不吃眼前亏 "a wise fellow does not take a loss he can avoid" shows its everyday use.
他真是一条好汉。
Tā zhēn shì yī tiáo hǎohàn.
He really is a brave fellow.
单身汉 dānshēnhàn a bachelor; a single man
N 名词 míngcí
单身 dānshēn (single, unmarried) + 汉 hàn (man). An unmarried man living on his own. The 汉 here is purely the colloquial "man," with none of the heroic ring of 好汉.
他到现在还是个单身汉。
Tā dào xiànzài háishì gè dānshēnhàn.
He is still a bachelor to this day.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
彪形大汉 biāo xíng dà hàn a burly, powerfully built man Lit: tiger-bodied big fellow. 彪 (a young tiger) lends its image of muscular bulk, so the phrase paints a tall, broad, imposing man, the bodyguard or strongman of a scene.
不到长城非好汉 bù dào chángchéng fēi hǎohàn one who has not reached the Great Wall is no true hero Lit: not-reach-Great-Wall-not-a-good-fellow. A popular saying about seeing a hard thing through to the end; only by finishing the climb do you earn the name 好汉.
好汉不吃眼前亏 hǎohàn bù chī yǎnqián kuī a wise fellow avoids a loss he can plainly see coming Lit: a good-fellow does not eat the loss before his eyes. Folk wisdom that real courage includes the sense to step back from a fight you cannot win, rather than charging in for pride.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Start at the water radical 氵 on the left and picture a real river, the Han, winding through central China. A king founds his realm on its banks and names the dynasty after the water (汉朝). The dynasty grows so great that its name sticks to the people (汉族), their speech (汉语), and their writing (汉字). Trace the line and you have the whole character: water, dynasty, people, language.

Then remember the side door. In everyday talk 汉 also just means a man, the sturdy plain-spoken kind: the brave 好汉 of the old novels, the bachelor 单身汉, the 男子汉 who keeps his word. When you meet 汉, ask whether it is naming the Chinese world or naming a fellow, and the water on the left will remind you where the word began.
