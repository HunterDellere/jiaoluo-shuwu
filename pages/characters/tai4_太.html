字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

太 tài = 大 (dà, big) + 丶 (a single added dot). It is an indicative character built directly on 大, the picture of a person standing with arms outstretched to mean "big." Add one dot beneath, and 大 is pushed past its own size: bigger than big, the superlative, and then beyond the superlative into "too much." Historically 太 and 大 were closely tied, with 太 the emphatic form that took on "greatest" and "excessive."

Two related senses grow from that one extra dot:

1. Too, excessively (the degree adverb). Big past the proper limit: 太大 tài dà (too big), 太贵 tài guì (too expensive), and the warm 太好了 tài hǎo le (wonderful, literally "too good").
2. Highest, grandest, most senior. Big at the very top: 太阳 tàiyáng (the sun, the "supreme yang"), 太太 tàitai (madam, the lady of the house), 太极 tàijí (the Supreme Ultimate), 太空 tàikōng (outer space, the "grand void").

The thread is excess and supremacy. The extra dot tips 大 over an edge: in everyday speech that edge is "more than is good" (太累 too tired), and in titles and cosmology it is "more than all others" (the sun above the sky, the grandmother above the mother, the Supreme Ultimate above all forms).

太了 tài…le Too & Extremely: the Degree Adverb
语法洞见 yǔfǎ dòngjiàn · Grammatical Insight

The first 太 a learner uses is the degree adverb, placed before an adjective. It pulls in two directions depending on the final particle. With 了 le and a positive adjective it is warm and admiring: 太好了 tài hǎo le "wonderful!", 太漂亮了 "so beautiful!". Without that warmth, or with an unwelcome adjective, it means "too much, more than is good": 太贵了 "too expensive," 太累了 "exhausted."

The negative is the high-frequency softener 不太 bú tài, "not very / not too": 不太好 "not very good," 不太懂 "don't quite understand." It is gentler than a flat 不, the polite way to say no without slamming the door. Between 太…了 for enthusiasm and 不太 for mild denial, 太 is one of the most-used degree words in spoken Chinese.

太…了 tài…le too…; so…; (with praise) wonderfully…
PATTERN 句型 jùxíng
太 tài (excessively) wraps an adjective, with 了 le closing the frame. Warm with a good adjective (太棒了 "awesome"), critical with a bad one (太麻烦了 "too much trouble"). The 了 is what tips it toward exclamation.
这个消息太好了！
Zhège xiāoxi tài hǎo le!
This news is wonderful!
今天太累了，我想休息。
Jīntiān tài lèi le, wǒ xiǎng xiūxi.
I'm too tired today, I want to rest.
不太 bú tài not very; not too; not quite
ADV 副词 fùcí
不 bù (not) + 太 tài (very). The gentle negative, softer than a bare 不: 不太喜欢 "don't really like it," 不太方便 "not very convenient." The standard polite way to decline or downplay.
我不太懂你的意思。
Wǒ bú tài dǒng nǐ de yìsi.
I don't quite understand what you mean.
太阳 tàiyáng The Highest & Grand
太阳 tàiyáng the sun
N 名词 míngcí
太 tài (supreme) + 阳 yáng (the bright, masculine principle). The "supreme yang," the brightest body in the sky. The everyday word for the sun and for sunshine: 晒太阳 "to sit in the sun."
今天太阳很大。
Jīntiān tàiyáng hěn dà.
The sun is strong today.
太太 tàitai Mrs.; madam; wife (polite)
N 名词 míngcí
The doubled 太, the senior lady of the house. Used as a title after a surname (王太太 "Mrs. Wang") and as a polite word for one's own or another's wife. The 太 marks seniority and respect.
这是我太太。
Zhè shì wǒ tàitai.
This is my wife.
太空 tàikōng outer space
N 名词 míngcí
太 tài (grand) + 空 kōng (void; empty). The "grand void," outer space. 太空人 tàikōngrén and 宇航员 yǔhángyuán both mean astronaut.
人类已经能够进入太空。
Rénlèi yǐjīng nénggòu jìnrù tàikōng.
Humans are already able to travel into space.
太极 tàijí the Supreme Ultimate; Taiji (the source of yin and yang)
N 名词 míngcí
太 tài (supreme) + 极 jí (the pole; the ultimate). In the Yijing tradition, the Supreme Ultimate from which 阴 yīn and 阳 yáng divide. The familiar swirl symbol is the 太极图. The slow martial art is 太极拳 tàijíquán.
很多老人早上在公园打太极。
Hěn duō lǎorén zǎoshang zài gōngyuán dǎ tàijí.
Many older people practice taiji in the park in the morning.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
太平盛世 tài píng shèng shì a great age of peace: a flourishing, untroubled era Lit: great-peace-flourishing-age. 太平 (great peace) names a time without war or upheaval; 盛世 is a golden age. Together, the ideal of a prosperous and tranquil reign.
太公钓鱼 tài gōng diào yú Grand Duke Jiang fishes: those who are willing will take the bait From 太公钓鱼，愿者上钩. The sage Jiang Ziya fished with a straight, baitless hook, waiting for King Wen to seek him out. Said of letting the willing come of their own accord rather than forcing them.
天下太平 tiān xià tài píng all under heaven at peace: the realm is calm and orderly Lit: under-heaven-great-peace. The classical statement that the whole world is at rest, sometimes used wryly today to mean "all quiet, nothing to worry about."
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Take 大, the person standing big with arms thrown wide, and drop one extra dot beneath. That dot is the whole difference: it pushes big over its own edge. In daily speech the edge is "more than is good," so 太 becomes "too": 太累 (too tired), 太贵 (too expensive). Add 了 and a kind word and the same excess becomes delight: 太好了 (wonderful).

Aim the dot upward instead and 太 becomes "bigger than all the rest": the sun above the sky (太阳), the most senior lady of the house (太太), the Supreme Ultimate above every form (太极). One dot past 大, and big becomes either too much or the very most.
