字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

成 chéng is built on 戈 gē, the halberd. The dominant element is 戈, the ancient dagger-axe carried on a long shaft, and the early forms add a small marking stroke to it. The reading is one of a task brought to a finish: the weapon set down once the work of building, defending, or settling a thing is done. Where 戈 elsewhere signals cutting and warfare, here it is read as completion, the moment a labor reaches its full form and stands finished. The simplified and traditional shapes are identical, so the same glyph appears in both columns above.

From that core image of a thing brought to completion, several senses fan out:

1. To become, to turn into. One state finished and a new one standing in its place: 成为 chéngwéi (to become), 变成 biànchéng (to turn into).
2. To succeed, to accomplish. A labor carried through to its end: 成功 chénggōng (to succeed), 完成 wánchéng (to complete), 成就 chéngjiù (achievement).
3. Alright, that works. A colloquial yes that settles a matter on the spot: a bare 成 chéng meaning "fine, agreed, it works."
4. A tenth, ten percent. As a fraction word, 成 names one part in ten: 一成 yī chéng (ten percent), 九成 jiǔ chéng (ninety percent).
5. Ready-made, established, set. Already finished and fixed: 成语 chéngyǔ (a set idiom), 成绩 chéngjì (results, grades), 成熟 chéngshú (mature, ripe).

The thread through every sense is a process carried to its full conclusion. Whether the outcome is a new state, a won success, a settled agreement, a fixed fraction, or a finished and ripened form, 成 marks the point where becoming is done and the result stands complete.

成为 chéngwéi To Become
成为 chéngwéi to become; to turn into
V 动词 dòngcí
成 chéng (to become) + 为 wéi (to be). The standard verb for becoming, taking a noun for what one turns into: 成为医生 "to become a doctor," 成为朋友 "to become friends." More formal and outcome-focused than 变成.
她想成为一名老师。
Tā xiǎng chéngwéi yī míng lǎoshī.
She wants to become a teacher.
这座城市成为了旅游中心。
Zhè zuò chéngshì chéngwéi le lǚyóu zhōngxīn.
This city has become a tourism hub.
变成 biànchéng to change into; to turn into
V 动词 dòngcí
变 biàn (to change) + 成 chéng (into, completing the change). The result of a transformation, stressing the shift from one form to another: 水变成冰 "water turns into ice." Here 成 closes the change, marking the new state as fully arrived.
天气一下子变成了冬天。
Tiānqì yīxiàzi biànchéng le dōngtiān.
The weather suddenly turned into winter.
辨析 biànxī · 成 vs. 变 成 chéng is the finish line: a state completed, a result standing whole. 变 biàn is the shifting itself: change in progress, with no promise of where it lands. They pair in 变成, where 变 supplies the movement and 成 supplies the arrival, so the phrase means the change is done and the new form is set.
形成 xíngchéng to form; to take shape
V 动词 dòngcí
形 xíng (form; shape) + 成 chéng (to become complete). For something that gradually comes into being as a coherent whole: a habit, a system, an opinion, a landscape. 形成习惯 "to form a habit."
良好的习惯需要慢慢形成。
Liánghǎo de xíguàn xūyào mànman xíngchéng.
Good habits need to form slowly.
成功 chénggōng Succeed & Complete
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The sense of 成 that learners meet most often is the one of carrying a task all the way through. 成功 chénggōng pairs 成 with 功 gōng (merit, the result of effort) to mean success, the labor brought to its rewarding end. 完成 wánchéng pairs 完 wán (whole, finished) with 成 to mean completion, the job done with nothing left over. And 成就 chéngjiù names the achievement itself, the lasting thing that effort produces.

Notice how often 成 sits in the second slot, where it acts almost like a result-marking ending: 做成 zuòchéng (to get something done), 写成 xiěchéng (to finish writing), 完成 wánchéng (to complete). In these the first character names the action and 成 reports that it reached completion. The same finishing 成 turns up in 达成 dáchéng (to reach, as in reaching an agreement), where what is finished is a meeting of minds.

成功 chénggōng to succeed; success; successful
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
成 chéng (to accomplish) + 功 gōng (merit; result of effort). Both the act of succeeding and success as a thing: 试验成功 "the experiment succeeded," 成功的人 "a successful person." Its opposite is 失败 shībài (to fail).
他终于成功了。
Tā zhōngyú chénggōng le.
He finally succeeded.
失败是成功之母。
Shībài shì chénggōng zhī mǔ.
Failure is the mother of success.
完成 wánchéng to complete; to accomplish; to finish
V 动词 dòngcí
完 wán (whole; to finish) + 成 chéng (to bring to completion). To carry a task all the way to done: 完成任务 "to complete a task," 完成作业 "to finish the homework." The everyday word for getting something fully finished.
我们按时完成了任务。
Wǒmen ànshí wánchéng le rènwù.
We completed the task on time.
成就 chéngjiù achievement; accomplishment
N 名词 míngcí
成 chéng (accomplished) + 就 jiù (to attain). The lasting result of effort, the achievement one can point to: 巨大的成就 "a great achievement," 学术成就 "academic accomplishments." Weightier and more public than a single completed task.
他在科学上取得了很大的成就。
Tā zài kēxué shàng qǔdé le hěn dà de chéngjiù.
He achieved a great deal in science.
成熟 chéngshú Results & Maturity: the Ripened 成
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

A thing that has finished becoming is a thing that is ready, set, fully grown. From this sense 成 reaches into the language of results and ripeness. 成绩 chéngjì names the finished record of how one did, school grades or any measured outcome of effort. 成熟 chéngshú means mature or ripe, said of fruit on the branch, of a plan ready to act on, and of a person grown into steadiness.

The same fixed-and-finished 成 stands at the head of 成语 chéngyǔ, the set idioms of the language. A 成语 is a phrase already cast in a settled four-character form, ready-made and passed down whole, not assembled fresh each time. Here 成 means established rather than newly succeeded: the wording is finished and waits, complete, for use.

成绩 chéngjì grades; results; achievement (in study or work)
N 名词 míngcí
成 chéng (accomplished; finished) + 绩 jì (achievement; spun work). The measured outcome of effort, most often school marks: 考试成绩 "exam results," 成绩单 "a report card." The finished score that shows how the work turned out.
他这次考试成绩很好。
Tā zhè cì kǎoshì chéngjì hěn hǎo.
His grades on this exam are very good.
成熟 chéngshú mature; ripe; well-developed
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
成 chéng (fully formed) + 熟 shú (ripe; cooked). Ripe in the literal sense for fruit and crops, and mature in the figurative sense for people, plans, and technologies: 成熟的苹果 "a ripe apple," 想法还不成熟 "the idea is not yet fully developed."
这个西瓜还没成熟。
Zhège xīguā hái méi chéngshú.
This watermelon is not ripe yet.
成语 chéngyǔ set idiom; fixed four-character phrase
N 名词 míngcí
成 chéng (established; ready-made) + 语 yǔ (words; saying). A phrase cast in a settled form, usually four characters, handed down whole from classical sources. Here 成 means fixed and finished, not freshly succeeded.
中文里有很多成语。
Zhōngwén lǐ yǒu hěn duō chéngyǔ.
There are many set idioms in Chinese.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
一事无成 yī shì wú chéng to accomplish nothing: not one thing brought to completion Lit: one-matter-without-success. Said of a person who, despite time and effort, has nothing finished to show for it. A common note of self-reproach or worry over a life that feels stalled.
成千上万 chéng qiān shàng wàn thousands upon thousands: a vast, countless number Lit: amounting-to-thousands-up-to-ten-thousands. A vivid way to say a great many, used of crowds, stars, raindrops, anything too numerous to count. Here 成 carries the sense of reaching a quantity.
水到渠成 shuǐ dào qú chéng when the water arrives the channel forms: success comes naturally when conditions are ripe Lit: water-arrives-channel-completes. As flowing water cuts its own course, a thing comes about on its own once the groundwork is laid. Counsel to prepare the conditions and let the result follow rather than forcing it.
一成不变 yī chéng bù biàn fixed once and never changing: rigid, unvarying, set in stone Lit: once-formed-not-changing. Said of a rule, method, or habit that stays frozen and refuses to adapt. It plays 成 (settled, formed) against 变 (to change), often as a criticism of inflexibility.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

See the halberd, 戈, set down at the end of a day's labor: the wall is built, the work is defended, the task stands finished. That weapon resting over a completed job is the whole character. Everything 成 means is the moment a process reaches its full conclusion.

On a person, that finish is becoming, one self left behind and a new one standing whole (成为). On a labor, it is success, the effort carried through to its reward (成功). On a job, it is completion, nothing left undone (完成). On fruit or on character, it is ripeness, fully grown and ready (成熟). And on a phrase, it is the set idiom, cast once and handed down unchanged (成语). Whenever you meet 成, ask what has just been finished, and you will find the result standing complete.
