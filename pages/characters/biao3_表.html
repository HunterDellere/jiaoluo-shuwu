字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

表 belongs to the 衣 yī (garment; clothing) family, and the original image is concrete and physical. The Shuōwén glosses 表 as an outer garment of fur, the kind worn with the hairy side facing out. In the oldest forms the 衣 element is split open and a 毛 máo (fur; hair) sits inside it, picturing exactly this: a pelt worn on the outside of the body. From the worn-on-the-outside fur coat comes the whole abstract career of the character. What is on the outside is the surface; the surface is what shows; what shows can be expressed; what is expressed can be laid out in a chart.

This is the thread that ties together five senses that look unrelated in an English dictionary. The outer surface (表面 biǎomiàn) is the fur facing out. The outward appearance (外表 wàibiǎo) is what that surface presents to the world. To express (表示 biǎoshì) is to bring an inner state to the surface where others can read it. A table or form (表格 biǎogé) lays information out on a flat surface for inspection. And to represent (代表 dàibiǎo) is to stand on the outside as the visible face of something larger. The garment worn outside is the seed of all of them.

The watch and the meter belong to a separate strand. In traditional writing, the instrument that displays a reading on its face, a clock, a wristwatch, a gauge, was written 錶 with the 金 jīn (metal) radical added, marking it as a metal device. Simplified Chinese dropped the metal radical and folded 錶 back into plain 表, so today 手表 shǒubiǎo (wristwatch) and 电表 diànbiǎo (electricity meter) are written with the same character as 表面 and 表示. The logic still holds underneath: a watch face or a meter face is, after all, a surface that displays a reading. When you read traditional texts from Taiwan or Hong Kong, expect 錶 for the timepiece and 表 for everything else.

One small family wrinkle sits on top of all this. 表 also marks relatives connected through a sister, a daughter, or a maternal line, the cousins who stand outside the patrilineal surname: 表哥 biǎogē (older male cousin on the mother's or a sister's side) and 表姐 biǎojiě (older female cousin of the same kind). They are the relatives on the outside of the家 jiā surname line, the same outer that runs through every other sense of the character.

构词 gòucí The Five Senses of 表 at a Glance
语义脉络 yǔyì màiluò · One root (the outer surface), five branches 外 (surface) 表面 biǎomiàn, surface; 外表 wàibiǎo, outward appearance; 表皮 biǎopí, the skin or outer layer
达 (express) 表示 biǎoshì, to express; 表达 biǎodá, to convey; 表现 biǎoxiàn, to show / performance; 表演 biǎoyǎn, to perform
格 (chart) 表格 biǎogé, a form or table; 时间表 shíjiānbiǎo, a timetable; 报表 bàobiǎo, a report sheet
錶 (instrument) 手表 shǒubiǎo, wristwatch; 电表 diànbiǎo, electricity meter; 表针 biǎozhēn, a watch hand
代 (represent) 代表 dàibiǎo, to represent / a representative; 表哥 biǎogē, an older cousin on the maternal or sororal side
Every branch reaches back to the same image: the fur worn on the outside. Surface, appearance, expression, chart, watch face, and outer relatives are all versions of what faces out.
表面 biǎomiàn Surface & Appearance
表面 biǎomiàn surface: the outer face; the surface (as opposed to the substance)
N 名词 míngcí
表 biǎo (outer; surface) + 面 miàn (face; side). The literal outer surface of an object, and by extension the superficial layer of a situation as opposed to what lies beneath. The phrase 表面上 biǎomiàn shàng (on the surface) is one of the most useful framing expressions in Mandarin, setting up a contrast with the hidden reality.
湖的表面很平静。
Hú de biǎomiàn hěn píngjìng.
The surface of the lake is very calm.
表面上他很冷静，其实很紧张。
Biǎomiàn shàng tā hěn lěngjìng, qíshí hěn jǐnzhāng.
On the surface he is calm; in fact he is very nervous.
不要只看表面。
Bùyào zhǐ kàn biǎomiàn.
Do not look only at the surface.
语法 yǔfǎ · Grammar 表面上 (biǎomiàn shàng) often pairs with 其实 qíshí (in fact) or 实际上 shíjì shàng (actually) to set up an appearance-versus-reality contrast. The opposite of 表面 in this sense is 实质 shízhì (essence) or 内在 nèizài (the inner).
外表 wàibiǎo outward appearance: the way someone or something looks from outside
N 名词 míngcí
外 wài (outer; outside) + 表 biǎo (surface). The outward appearance of a person or thing, especially how they look to others. Where 表面 leans toward physical surfaces and abstract superficiality, 外表 is most natural for the looks of people and objects, the impression they give at first sight.
她的外表很普通，但很有才华。
Tā de wàibiǎo hěn pǔtōng, dàn hěn yǒu cáihuá.
Her appearance is quite ordinary, but she is very talented.
不能凭外表判断一个人。
Bùnéng píng wàibiǎo pànduàn yí gè rén.
You cannot judge a person by their appearance.
这栋楼的外表很现代。
Zhè dòng lóu de wàibiǎo hěn xiàndài.
The exterior of this building is very modern.
辨析 biànxī · 外表 vs 表面 外表 describes how a person or object looks (their outward appearance). 表面 is the physical outer surface or the superficial layer of an abstract situation. You judge a person by their 外表; you say a problem looks fine 表面上 (on the surface) but is rotten underneath.
表皮 biǎopí outer skin: the epidermis; the outermost layer or rind
N 名词 míngcí
表 biǎo (outer) + 皮 pí (skin; hide; rind). The outermost layer of a living thing: the epidermis of human skin, the rind of a fruit, the bark-like surface of a plant. A precise term used in biology, medicine, and everyday talk about peels and surfaces.
苹果的表皮上有一层蜡。
Píngguǒ de biǎopí shàng yǒu yì céng là.
There is a layer of wax on the apple's skin.
皮肤的表皮可以保护身体。
Pífū de biǎopí kěyǐ bǎohù shēntǐ.
The epidermis of the skin protects the body.
这个伤口只伤到了表皮。
Zhège shāngkǒu zhǐ shāng dào le biǎopí.
This wound only injured the outer skin.
表达 biǎodá To Express & To Perform
表示 biǎoshì to express: to indicate; to show (a feeling, attitude, or position)
V 动词 dòngcíN 名词 míngcí
表 biǎo (to bring to the surface) + 示 shì (to show; to indicate; to make manifest). To express or indicate an attitude, opinion, or feeling, often through a word, gesture, or symbolic act. 表示 frequently introduces what someone states or signals: 表示同意 (express agreement), 表示感谢 (express thanks). It also covers what a sign or symbol stands for.
我想表示感谢。
Wǒ xiǎng biǎoshì gǎnxiè.
I would like to express my thanks.
红灯表示停车。
Hóngdēng biǎoshì tíngchē.
A red light indicates that you should stop.
他点头表示同意。
Tā diǎntóu biǎoshì tóngyì.
He nodded to show his agreement.
辨析 biànxī · 表示 vs 表达 表示 is to indicate or signal an attitude or position, often in a single act (a nod, a statement, a symbol): 表示同意, 表示反对. 表达 is to articulate or convey something complex, especially feelings and ideas, often requiring effort and skill: 表达情感, 表达思想. You 表示 a stance; you 表达 your inner world.
表达 biǎodá to express: to convey; to articulate (thoughts and feelings)
V 动词 dòngcíN 名词 míngcí
表 biǎo (to bring to the surface) + 达 dá (to reach; to arrive at; to get through). To convey thoughts or feelings so that they reach another person clearly. The 达 element adds the sense of successful transmission: not just showing something, but getting it across. Often used for articulation skill itself: 表达能力 (the ability to express oneself).
我不知道怎么表达我的感受。
Wǒ bù zhīdào zěnme biǎodá wǒ de gǎnshòu.
I do not know how to express my feelings.
他的表达能力很强。
Tā de biǎodá nénglì hěn qiáng.
His ability to express himself is very strong.
用中文表达这个意思有点难。
Yòng Zhōngwén biǎodá zhège yìsi yǒudiǎn nán.
Expressing this meaning in Chinese is a little hard.
表演 biǎoyǎn to perform: to act; a performance; a show
V 动词 dòngcíN 名词 míngcí
表 biǎo (to show outwardly) + 演 yǎn (to act; to perform; to develop). To perform before an audience, and the performance itself: music, dance, drama, magic, acrobatics. The 演 element carries the sense of acting out and unfolding, so 表演 is showing something to spectators rather than simply expressing it.
孩子们为客人表演节目。
Háizimen wèi kèrén biǎoyǎn jiémù.
The children performed a program for the guests.
这场表演非常精彩。
Zhè chǎng biǎoyǎn fēicháng jīngcǎi.
This performance was wonderful.
他在台上表演魔术。
Tā zài táishàng biǎoyǎn móshù.
He performed magic on stage.
辨析 biànxī · 表演 vs 表现 表演 is to perform for an audience (a show, an act). 表现 biǎoxiàn is to display a quality through behavior, or how one comes across: 他表现得很勇敢 (he behaved very bravely), or as a noun, 工作表现 (work performance, in the sense of conduct and results, not a stage show).
表格 biǎogé Forms, Watches & Representation
表格 biǎogé form: a table; a chart; a grid laid out for filling in
N 名词 míngcí
表 biǎo (a chart; a surface where data is laid out) + 格 gé (a grid; a square; a cell). A printed or digital form, table, or grid: anything with rows and cells to be read or filled in. Related are 时间表 shíjiānbiǎo (timetable) and 课程表 kèchéngbiǎo (class schedule), where 表 names the laid-out chart of times.
请填写这张表格。
Qǐng tiánxiě zhè zhāng biǎogé.
Please fill in this form.
老师把成绩做成了表格。
Lǎoshī bǎ chéngjì zuò chéng le biǎogé.
The teacher turned the grades into a table.
墙上贴着火车时间表。
Qiáng shàng tiē zhe huǒchē shíjiānbiǎo.
A train timetable is posted on the wall.
手表 shǒubiǎo wristwatch: a watch worn on the wrist (錶 in traditional script)
N 名词 míngcí
手 shǒu (hand; wrist) + 表 biǎo (here the instrument sense, written 錶 in traditional script). A wristwatch. This is the sense that traditional writing marks with the metal radical (錶), since a watch is a metal device whose face displays a reading. Simplified Chinese writes it 表. Compare 电表 diànbiǎo (electricity meter) and 温度表 wēndùbiǎo (thermometer), all instruments with a reading on a face.
这块手表是我爸爸送的。
Zhè kuài shǒubiǎo shì wǒ bàba sòng de.
This watch was a gift from my father.
我的手表慢了五分钟。
Wǒ de shǒubiǎo màn le wǔ fēnzhōng.
My watch is five minutes slow.
请抄一下电表的读数。
Qǐng chāo yíxià diànbiǎo de dúshù.
Please copy down the reading on the electricity meter.
异体 yìtǐ · The 表 / 錶 Merge In traditional Chinese, the timepiece and meter sense is written 錶 (with the 金 metal radical), while every other sense uses 表. Simplified Chinese merged them, so 表 alone now covers the watch too. If you read texts from Taiwan or Hong Kong, expect 手錶 for a wristwatch and 表 for surface, form, and expression.
代表 dàibiǎo to represent: a representative; a delegate; to stand for
V 动词 dòngcíN 名词 míngcí
代 dài (in place of; on behalf of; a generation) + 表 biǎo (to be the outward face of). To represent or stand for, and the person who does so: a delegate, a deputy, a representative. The 表 element keeps its core idea, being the visible outer face, here the face that stands on the outside for a group, an idea, or a thing. Also used for what a symbol stands for.
他代表公司参加会议。
Tā dàibiǎo gōngsī cānjiā huìyì.
He attended the meeting on behalf of the company.
红色代表喜庆。
Hóngsè dàibiǎo xǐqìng.
The color red stands for joy and celebration.
她是我们班的代表。
Tā shì wǒmen bān de dàibiǎo.
She is the representative of our class.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
表里如一 biǎo lǐ rú yī outside and inside are as one: consistent in word and deed; the same within as without 表 biǎo = the outside, the surface. 里 lǐ = the inside, the interior. 如一 rú yī = as if one; identical. A person whose outward conduct matches their inner thoughts, who is honest and undivided, with no gap between the surface they show and the substance underneath. A strong compliment to integrity. 他为人表里如一，从不说一套做一套。(He is consistent inside and out; he never says one thing and does another.) The opposite is 表里不一 biǎo lǐ bù yī (two-faced; the surface does not match the inside).
虚有其表 xū yǒu qí biǎo to have only its surface: impressive on the outside but hollow within; all show and no substance 虚 xū = empty; hollow; in vain. 有其表 yǒu qí biǎo = to have (only) its outer appearance. The phrase describes something or someone that looks fine on the surface but lacks real worth underneath. 这家公司虚有其表，其实早就负债累累。(This company is all show; in reality it has long been drowning in debt.) A pointed criticism that leans directly on 表 as the deceptive outer layer.
为人师表 wéi rén shī biǎo to be a teacher and model for others: to set an example worthy of imitation 为人 wéi rén = to act as, to be for others. 师 shī = teacher. 表 biǎo = model; the standard others measure themselves against. To serve as a moral and intellectual example, especially said of teachers who must embody what they teach. The 表 here is the surface in its best sense: the visible standard, the outward model that others align to. 老师应该为人师表，以身作则。(A teacher should be a model for others, leading by personal example.)
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Hold one image and the whole character organizes itself: a fur coat worn with the hairy side out. That is the literal origin of 表, the garment radical 衣 split open around a tuft of fur, the pelt facing the world. Everything else is what faces out. The outer surface of the lake (表面), the way a person looks at first glance (外表), the feeling you bring out so others can read it (表示, 表达), the act you put on for an audience (表演), the chart laid flat for inspection (表格), the watch face that shows the time (手表), the delegate who stands on the outside for the group (代表). One coat, worn outside, branching into a dozen everyday words.

Keep two practical splits in mind. First, the writing split: in traditional script the timepiece and the meter take an extra metal radical and are written 錶, while simplified Chinese folds them back into plain 表. If you switch between simplified and traditional texts, that is the one place 表 changes its coat. Second, the usage split between 表示 and 表达: you 表示 a stance with a single act, a nod or a stated position, while you 表达 your inner world with effort and skill. The first signals; the second articulates.

When 表 turns up in a new word, ask which branch it is on. Is something facing outward as a surface or appearance? Is an inner state being brought out and shown? Is information being laid out on a chart, or a reading displayed on a face? Is one thing standing on the outside for another? In nearly every case the answer is some version of the same root: the part that faces out. The fur worn outside is two and a half thousand years old, and it still tells you what 表 means.
