字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

错 cuò = 钅 (jīn, metal) + 昔 (xī, the phonetic). A pictophonetic compound of the left-hand metal radical 钅, the compressed form of 金 used down the side of a character, with 昔 on the right carrying the sound (xī, which over time shifted to cuò). The traditional form 錯 writes the radical out in full as 金. The metal radical is no accident: the earliest documented meaning, recorded in the Shuōwén, is metalwork.

That first sense was inlay and plating: 金错刀, the "gold-inlaid blade," names coins and weapons whose surfaces were grooved and filled with threads of gold, the bright metal crossing through the darker base. From inlay the word reached toward the file and the whetstone, the grinding of one hard surface against another. The 昔 on the right, whose own picture is sun over piled, stacked layers, suits a character built from crossing, layered lines of metal.

The leap to the modern senses runs through the idea of crossing. Threads of inlay cross and interlace; from there 错 came to mean interlocking, intersecting, set crosswise (错综 cuòzōng, intricate; 交错 jiāocuò, to interlace). When lines cross where they should run straight, they have gone awry, and that is the everyday sense today: 错 as wrong, mistaken, in error. The thing that does not line up.

错误 cuòwù Error & Mistake
错误 cuòwù mistake, error; wrong, mistaken
N/ADJ 名 · 形
错 cuò (wrong) + 误 wù (to err, mistake). Both a noun, the mistake itself, and an adjective, mistaken or incorrect: 犯了一个错误 "made a mistake," 错误的决定 "a wrong decision." The standard, slightly formal word for an error of fact or judgment.
这是我的错误。
Zhè shì wǒ de cuòwù.
This is my mistake.
他承认了自己的错误。
Tā chéngrèn le zìjǐ de cuòwù.
He admitted his mistake.
辨析 biànxī · 错误 vs. 错 错误 is the noun or formal adjective for a mistake. The bare 错 works as a quick predicate adjective or complement: 你错了 "you're wrong," 写错了 "wrote it wrong." You say 一个错误, never 一个错, for "a mistake."
出错 chūcuò to go wrong; to make an error
V 动词 dòngcí
出 chū (to come out) + 错 cuò (error). For an error to surface, often in a process or system: 系统出错了 "the system threw an error," 计算出错 "a miscalculation came up." Common in technical and procedural contexts.
如果出错了，请重新输入。
Rúguǒ chūcuò le, qǐng chóngxīn shūrù.
If something goes wrong, please enter it again.
错字 cuòzì a wrongly written character; a typo
N 名词 míngcí
错 cuò (wrong) + 字 zì (character). A character written incorrectly, whether mis-formed by hand or mistyped: 这篇作文有很多错字 "this essay has many wrong characters." A 错别字 cuòbiézì folds in 别字, the right-looking but wrong character.
老师圈出了我的错字。
Lǎoshī quān chū le wǒ de cuòzì.
The teacher circled my misspelled characters.
不错 búcuò Right & Not Bad
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The most useful turn 错 takes is negation. 没错 méicuò, "no error," is the warm everyday way to say "that's right, exactly." And 不错 búcuò, literally "not wrong," is the workhorse compliment of spoken Chinese: it means "pretty good, not bad at all," and lands as genuine praise rather than faint approval. 这家饭馆不错 "this restaurant is quite good" is a recommendation, not a hesitation.

The logic is the same one that made 错 mean error in the first place. If error is what crosses where it should run straight, then "no crossing" is the line running true. To be 不错 is to have nothing out of place, and Chinese hears that absence of fault as a quiet, reliable yes.

不错 búcuò pretty good, not bad; correct, right
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
不 bù (not) + 错 cuò (wrong). Two readings from one shape: as praise, "quite good, not bad" (成绩不错 "good grades"); as confirmation, "right, correct" (一点不错 "exactly right"). Note the tone sandhi: 不 shifts to bú before the falling 错.
你的中文说得不错。
Nǐ de Zhōngwén shuō de búcuò.
Your Chinese is quite good.
辨析 biànxī · 不错 vs. 没错 不错 usually praises something as "not bad, pretty good." 没错 confirms a statement as "that's right, correct": 没错，就是他 "right, it's him." 不错 evaluates quality; 没错 verifies truth.
没错 méicuò that's right; exactly; no mistake
PHR 短语 duǎnyǔ
没 méi (not have) + 错 cuò (error). "No error here," used to agree firmly: 没错，我就是这个意思 "exactly, that's just what I mean." Often softened to 没错儿 in northern speech.
没错，会议是明天上午。
Méicuò, huìyì shì míngtiān shàngwǔ.
That's right, the meeting is tomorrow morning.
错过 cuòguò Missing & Crossing
错过 cuòguò to miss (a chance, a train, a person)
V 动词 dòngcí
错 cuò (crosswise) + 过 guò (to pass). To pass by crosswise rather than meet: two things slip past each other and the moment is lost. Used for chances, trains, and people: 错过机会 "to miss a chance," 错过末班车 "to miss the last bus."
我不想错过这次机会。
Wǒ bù xiǎng cuòguò zhè cì jīhuì.
I don't want to miss this opportunity.
他们在车站错过了彼此。
Tāmen zài chēzhàn cuòguò le bǐcǐ.
They missed each other at the station.
辨析 biànxī · 错过 vs. 错误 错过 keeps the older "crossing" sense: to slip past something and lose it. 错误 uses the later "wrong" sense: a mistake of judgment. Missing a train is 错过; a wrong answer is 错误.
差错 chācuò a slip, an error; a mishap going wrong
N 名词 míngcí
差 chā (off, deviating) + 错 cuò (error). A small slip or accidental error, and by extension an unforeseen mishap: 万一出了差错 "in case something goes wrong." Both halves mean "off the mark," doubling the sense of deviation.
账目上有一点差错。
Zhàngmù shàng yǒu yìdiǎn chācuò.
There is a small error in the accounts.
交错 jiāocuò to interlace, crisscross, intersect
V 动词 dòngcí
交 jiāo (to cross) + 错 cuò (crosswise). The original "crossing" sense, with no fault implied: lines, paths, or branches weaving across one another. 纵横交错 describes a network running every which way.
城里的小巷交错相通。
Chéng lǐ de xiǎoxiàng jiāocuò xiāngtōng.
The city's lanes crisscross and connect.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
阴差阳错 yīn chā yáng cuò a yin-yang mix-up: a coincidence of small errors that throws things off Lit: yin-off-yang-wrong. A chain of unforeseen slips and coincidences, each small, that combine to send events down an unexpected path. Said when an outcome turns out the way it did through sheer freakish accident.
将错就错 jiāng cuò jiù cuò to take the mistake and run with it: to leave a wrong thing as it is Lit: take-the-error-go-with-the-error. Rather than undo a mistake already made, one accepts it and proceeds on that footing, often because correcting it would cost more than living with it.
错综复杂 cuò zōng fù zá crisscrossed and tangled: intricate and complicated Lit: interlaced-gathered-doubled-mixed. Keeps the "crossing" sense of 错: relationships or situations so interwoven and layered that they are hard to untangle. 错综复杂的关系 "a web of tangled relationships."
大错特错 dà cuò tè cuò greatly and especially wrong: utterly, completely mistaken Lit: big-error-particularly-error. An emphatic doubling that drives home how badly someone has misjudged: 你这样想就大错特错了 "if you think that, you are gravely mistaken."
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a craftsman cutting fine grooves into a dark bronze blade and pressing threads of gold into them, 钅, the metal radical down the left. The gold lines cross and interlace over the metal, bright over dark. That crossing of metal is the literal heart of 错: inlay, the lines that lay across one another.

Now let one thread slip out of its groove and cross where it should have run straight. The pattern is spoiled; the line has gone wrong. That single crossed line is the everyday 错: a wrong character, a wrong turn, a mistaken answer, the thing that does not line up. To say 错了 is to point at the thread that went astray.

The same image runs the other way too. Two travelers walk crossing paths at a station and slip past without meeting: they 错过 each other. And a thing with not one thread out of place is 不错, "not wrong," which Chinese hears as quiet praise. Hold the gold inlay in mind, and wrong, missed, and not bad all trace back to one set of crossing lines.
