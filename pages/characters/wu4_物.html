字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

物 wù is a pictophonetic compound: 牛 (niú, ox) carries the meaning, 勿 (wù) the sound. The left side is the ox radical, narrowed from its full form, signaling that the word began in the world of cattle. The right side 勿 supplies the reading and survives in the modern pronunciation. The Shuōwén reads 物 as the varicolored ox, the mottled or mixed-color beast, and from that concrete starting point the meaning widened until it covered everything that exists.

How did "spotted cattle" become "the myriad things"? Color and kind were how the ancients sorted their herds, and to name the varieties of a thing is one short step from naming things in general. By the classical period 物 had let go of the ox almost entirely and meant any thing, object, or creature at all.

Three senses branch from that widening:

1. Thing, object, the concrete article. Anything you can point to or hold: 东西 dōngxi (thing), 物品 wùpǐn (goods, articles), 物体 wùtǐ (a physical body), 礼物 lǐwù (gift), 食物 shíwù (food).
2. Matter, substance, the stuff of the world. The technical sense in science: 物质 wùzhì (matter), 生物 shēngwù (living things, biology), 动物 dòngwù (animal), 植物 zhíwù (plant).
3. The wider field of things and beings. Existence taken as a whole, including people seen as figures: 万物 wànwù (all things), 人物 rénwù (a figure, a character), 物价 wùjià (the prices of goods).

The thread is the leap from one ox to all that is. 物 is the most general word the language has for "a thing," the bare noun that stands behind every object, every substance, and every creature in the world.

物品 wùpǐn Things & Objects
物品 wùpǐn article; goods; item
N 名词 míngcí
物 wù (thing) + 品 pǐn (article; kind). The formal word for goods or articles, the kind you see on a shipping label or in a store inventory: 贵重物品 "valuable items," 日用物品 "everyday articles."
请保管好自己的物品。
Qǐng bǎoguǎn hǎo zìjǐ de wùpǐn.
Please look after your own belongings.
辨析 biànxī · 东西 vs. 物品 东西 dōngxi is the everyday, spoken word for a "thing": casual, vague, used constantly in conversation. 物品 wùpǐn is formal and written, the register of signs, rules, and inventories. You buy 东西 at the market but declare 物品 at customs.
礼物 lǐwù gift; present
N 名词 míngcí
礼 lǐ (courtesy; rite) + 物 wù (thing). Literally a "thing of courtesy," the object given to honor an occasion or a person: 生日礼物 "a birthday present," 送礼物 "to give a gift."
这是给你的生日礼物。
Zhè shì gěi nǐ de shēngrì lǐwù.
This is a birthday present for you.
食物 shíwù food
N 名词 míngcí
食 shí (to eat; food) + 物 wù (thing). Food as a category of substance, the general written term: 健康食物 "healthy food." For "a meal" you would say 饭 fàn, and for cooked dishes 菜 cài.
这种食物对身体很好。
Zhè zhǒng shíwù duì shēntǐ hěn hǎo.
This kind of food is very good for you.
物体 wùtǐ object; physical body
N 名词 míngcí
物 wù (thing) + 体 tǐ (body). A physical object regarded as a body occupying space, the term used in physics and plain description alike: 不明飞行物 "an unidentified flying object."
天上有个发光的物体。
Tiānshàng yǒu ge fāguāng de wùtǐ.
There is a glowing object in the sky.
物质 wùzhì Matter & Living Things
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

When Chinese built its modern vocabulary for science, 物 became the natural root for "matter" and for the living kinds. 物质 wùzhì pairs 物 (thing) with 质 zhì (substance, essence) to name matter itself, the stuff of physics and chemistry, and by extension the material side of life as opposed to the spiritual: 物质生活 "material life," 物质文明 "material civilization."

The same 物 sorts the living world into its two great branches. 动物 dòngwù, the "moving things," are the animals; 植物 zhíwù, the "planted things," are the plants; and 生物 shēngwù, the "living things," is the whole field of biology that studies them both. Here 物 has shed every last trace of the ox and works as a pure classifier of kinds, the slot that says "this is a category of thing in the world."

物质 wùzhì matter; substance; material
N 名词 míngcí
物 wù (thing) + 质 zhì (substance). Matter in the scientific sense, and the material as opposed to the spiritual: 化学物质 "a chemical substance," 物质需求 "material needs."
水是一种常见的物质。
Shuǐ shì yī zhǒng chángjiàn de wùzhì.
Water is a common substance.
动物 dòngwù animal
N 名词 míngcí
动 dòng (to move) + 物 wù (thing). The "moving things," the animals, set against 植物 the "planted things," the plants. 动物园 dòngwùyuán is the zoo, the "animal garden."
孩子们最喜欢看动物。
Háizimen zuì xǐhuan kàn dòngwù.
The children love watching the animals most.
生物 shēngwù living thing; organism; biology
N 名词 míngcí
生 shēng (to live) + 物 wù (thing). A living organism, and also the name of the school subject, biology. 微生物 wēishēngwù is a microorganism, a "tiny living thing."
海里有各种各样的生物。
Hǎi lǐ yǒu gèzhǒng-gèyàng de shēngwù.
The sea holds all kinds of living things.
万物 wànwù The Myriad Things & Figures
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Pushed to its limit, 物 names not just one thing but the totality of things. 万物 wànwù, the "ten thousand things," is the classical phrase for all of existence, the whole teeming world of creatures and objects that the Dao gives rise to. It is one of the grandest words 物 enters, the sense in which the bare noun for "thing" becomes a name for everything that is.

At the human end of the scale, 物 also frames a person as a figure. 人物 rénwù, literally "person-thing," means a notable individual or a character in a story, someone who counts as a "somebody." The same widening that turned one ox into all things also lets a person be spoken of as a thing of note, a figure on the stage of events.

万物 wànwù all things; the myriad creatures; everything in existence
N 名词 míngcí
万 wàn (ten thousand) + 物 wù (things). The classical name for the whole of existence: 天地万物 "heaven, earth, and all things," 万物复苏 "all things revive" in spring.
春天来了，万物复苏。
Chūntiān lái le, wànwù fùsū.
Spring has come, and all things revive.
人物 rénwù figure; personage; character (in a story)
N 名词 míngcí
人 rén (person) + 物 wù (thing). A notable person, a "somebody," and also a character in a novel, film, or play: 历史人物 "a historical figure," 主要人物 "the main character."
他是个了不起的人物。
Tā shì ge liǎobuqǐ de rénwù.
He is a remarkable figure.
货物 huòwù goods; cargo; merchandise
N 名词 míngcí
货 huò (goods; commodity) + 物 wù (thing). Goods as freight or stock in trade, the things that move through commerce: 运送货物 "to transport goods," 货物清单 "a manifest of cargo."
这批货物明天到港。
Zhè pī huòwù míngtiān dào gǎng.
This shipment of goods arrives at port tomorrow.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
物美价廉 wù měi jià lián fine goods at a low price: excellent quality and cheap Lit: thing-fine-price-cheap. The shopper's highest praise, a product that is both good and affordable. The everyday compliment paid to a worthwhile bargain.
物以类聚 wù yǐ lèi jù things gather by kind: like attracts like, birds of a feather flock together Lit: things-by-kind-gather. From the idea that creatures of a sort come together; often paired with 人以群分 "people divide into groups," and used, sometimes pointedly, of who keeps company with whom.
地大物博 dì dà wù bó vast land and abundant resources: a great and richly endowed country Lit: land-vast-things-abundant. The stock phrase for a nation blessed with broad territory and plentiful natural wealth, classically applied to China itself.
待人接物 dài rén jiē wù to treat people and handle affairs: the way one conducts oneself with others Lit: treat-people-engage-things. The whole manner of dealing with others and managing matters; said in praise of someone whose conduct is gracious and well judged.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Start with the ox, 牛, on the left of the character: one spotted, varicolored beast standing in a field. That single animal is where 物 began, the mottled ox of the Shuōwén. Now let the herd multiply and the kinds spread until the field is full of every creature and object imaginable. The one ox has become all things.

That widening is the whole word. Point at a single article and it is 物品 or 东西. Name its stuff and it is 物质. Sort it into the living kinds and it is 动物, 植物, 生物. Sweep your arm across the entire world and it is 万物, the myriad things. Whenever you meet 物, remember the one ox that grew into everything.
