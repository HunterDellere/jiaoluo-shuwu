字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

事 shì began as a picture of a hand holding the implements of office. The oracle-bone form shows a hand gripping an upright object, read variously as a hunting standard, a writing brush, or a banner of office. Whatever the exact tool, the image is of someone holding the instrument of a duty. From that single picture three closely related characters fanned out: 事 shì (the affair, the matter handled), 吏 lì (the official who handles it), and 史 shǐ (the scribe who records it). All three trace back to the same hand-and-implement source, and in the earliest texts they were barely distinguished.

The modern shape is opaque, a tangle of strokes ending in the hook 亅 (jué) that gives the character its radical. There is no clean "this part plus that part" breakdown to recover; what survives is the ending hook of the old upright implement. So 事 is best learned not by pulling it apart but by holding onto its idea: the thing one is occupied with.

That idea is wide. 事 covers the whole field of abstract matters that fill a life:

1. A matter, an affair. Anything that happens or must be dealt with: 事情 shìqing (matter), 没事 méishì (it's nothing), 出事 chūshì (something went wrong).
2. Work, an undertaking. A job or a life's project: 事业 shìyè (career, enterprise), 做事 zuòshì (to work, to handle things).
3. An event or incident, often a bad one. 事故 shìgù (accident), 事件 shìjiàn (incident, event).

Crucially, 事 is abstract. It names a matter or happening, never a physical object. The physical "thing" is 东西 dōngxi or 物 wù. You can 有事 (have a matter to attend to) but you cannot 有事 to mean "own an object." That split, affair versus object, is the single most useful thing to fix about 事.

事情 shìqing Matters & Affairs
事情 shìqing matter; affair; thing (to be dealt with)
N 名词 míngcí
事 shì + 情 qíng (situation; circumstance). The everyday noun for a matter or affair, anything happening that you talk about or handle. The most common way to say "thing" when you mean a situation rather than an object.
我有一件事情想跟你说。
Wǒ yǒu yī jiàn shìqing xiǎng gēn nǐ shuō.
There's something I want to tell you.
这件事情很重要。
Zhè jiàn shìqing hěn zhòngyào.
This matter is very important.
辨析 biànxī · 事情 vs. 东西 事情 is an abstract matter or affair: a situation, a task, something that happens. 东西 dōngxi is a physical object you can hold. 这件事情 "this matter"; 这个东西 "this object." Mixing them is the classic beginner slip.
事实 shìshí fact; the actual state of affairs
N 名词 míngcí
事 shì + 实 shí (real; solid). The matter as it really is, a fact. The phrase 事实上 shìshí shàng means "in fact, actually," used to correct an impression or introduce the truth of a case.
这是事实，不是猜测。
Zhè shì shìshí, bù shì cāicè.
This is a fact, not a guess.
事实上，他早就知道了。
Shìshí shàng, tā zǎo jiù zhīdào le.
In fact, he knew all along.
事先 shìxiān beforehand; in advance; prior to the event
ADV 副词 fùcí
事 shì + 先 xiān (before; first). Before the matter happens, in advance. Its mirror is 事后 shìhòu (afterward), so the pair frames an event as before-and-after.
请事先通知我。
Qǐng shìxiān tōngzhī wǒ.
Please let me know in advance.
同事 tóngshì Stories & the People in Them
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Once 事 means "the thing one is occupied with," the words that grow from it sort neatly. A 事 shared with others at work makes a 同事 tóngshì, literally "same-affair person," a colleague. A 事 strung into a telling becomes a 故事 gùshi, an "old matter" retold as a story. And a 事 taken up as a life's purpose becomes 事业 shìyè, a career or enterprise. The character keeps its abstract core throughout: in every case it is a matter, an undertaking, never an object.

同事 tóngshì colleague; coworker
N 名词 míngcí
同 tóng (same) + 事 shì. A "same-affair person," someone you share work with. The standard neutral word for a coworker, regardless of rank.
他是我的同事。
Tā shì wǒ de tóngshì.
He is my colleague.
故事 gùshi story; tale
N 名词 míngcí
故 gù (old; past) + 事 shì. An "old matter," a past happening told as a narrative. The word for any story, from a bedtime tale to a film's plot. Note the neutral tone on the second syllable: gùshi.
妈妈给孩子讲故事。
Māma gěi háizi jiǎng gùshi.
The mother tells the child a story.
事业 shìyè career; undertaking; enterprise; cause
N 名词 míngcí
事 shì + 业 yè (occupation; trade). A matter taken up as a life's work. Larger and more purposeful than a mere job (工作 gōngzuò): a 事业 is something you build, a calling or enterprise.
他把一生都献给了教育事业。
Tā bǎ yīshēng dōu xiàn gěi le jiàoyù shìyè.
He devoted his whole life to the cause of education.
辨析 biànxī · 事业 vs. 工作 工作 gōngzuò is a job: the work you do, day to day. 事业 shìyè is a career or calling: the larger thing you build over a lifetime. You can have a 工作 without a 事业, and a 事业 is praised in a way a 工作 is not.
事故 shìgù accident; mishap
N 名词 míngcí
事 shì + 故 gù (cause; reason; incident). An unwanted matter, an accident. 交通事故 jiāotōng shìgù "traffic accident" is the most common collocation.
路上发生了交通事故。
Lù shàng fāshēng le jiāotōng shìgù.
A traffic accident happened on the road.
没事 méishì No Trouble: the Colloquial 事
口语洞见 kǒuyǔ dòngjiàn · Colloquial Insight

In everyday speech 事 becomes shorthand for "a problem" or "something going on," and the two phrases 没事 méishì and 有事 yǒushì carry an enormous load. 没事, literally "no matter," is the all-purpose Chinese "it's nothing, no problem, never mind, are you okay." 有事, "have a matter," means you are busy, something has come up, or there is a problem to deal with. In the north you will hear them with the 儿 ending: 没事儿 méishìr, 有事儿 yǒushìr.

The same colloquial 事 drives 出事 chūshì, "something went wrong" (an accident, a crisis), and 闹事 nàoshì, "to make trouble." When a Chinese speaker asks 你怎么了，没事吧？ they are asking, with one small character, whether anything is the matter.

没事 méishì it's nothing; no problem; never mind; to be free (not busy)
V/N 动名词
没 méi (not have) + 事 shì. "No matter." The everyday reassurance: it's fine, don't worry, no harm done. Also literally "to have nothing on," to be free.
没事，别担心。
Méishì, bié dānxīn.
It's nothing, don't worry.
我今天没事，可以陪你去。
Wǒ jīntiān méishì, kěyǐ péi nǐ qù.
I'm free today, I can go with you.
有事 yǒushì to be busy; to have something come up; to have a matter to attend to
V/N 动名词
有 yǒu (have) + 事 shì. "Have a matter." Used to beg off (something has come up) or to flag that you need something from someone: 我有事找你 "I have something to discuss with you."
我晚上有事，去不了。
Wǒ wǎnshang yǒushì, qù bù liǎo.
I have something on tonight, I can't go.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
实事求是 shí shì qiú shì to seek truth from facts: to judge by the real state of things, not by wishes Lit: real-matters-seek-truth. From the Book of Han, describing a prince devoted to learning. It became a watchword for pragmatic, evidence-based thinking, weighing matters as they actually are rather than as doctrine prescribes.
事半功倍 shì bàn gōng bèi half the effort, double the result: great gains from the right method Lit: matter-half-merit-double. To accomplish twice as much with half the work. Its opposite is 事倍功半, double the effort for half the result. The phrase praises efficiency and the wisdom of finding the right approach.
平安无事 píng ān wú shì safe and sound, nothing amiss: all is calm and well Lit: peaceful-safe-no-matter. A reassuring summary that nothing has gone wrong: a journey passed without incident, a household at peace. The 无事 here is the formal cousin of colloquial 没事.
多事之秋 duō shì zhī qiū an eventful autumn: a troubled time, full of upheavals Lit: many-matters' autumn. Autumn was the season of harvest reckoning and of war, so a "many-matters autumn" came to mean a period crowded with crises and disturbances, a turbulent, eventful time for a state or a person.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a hand gripping the brush or banner of office, the old image inside 事. That hand is busy: it is occupied with a matter. Hold onto that one idea and every 事-word follows. The matter itself is 事情. The matter retold is a 故事. The matter shared at work makes a 同事. The matter taken up for life is a 事业. The matter gone wrong is a 事故.

And when the hand is empty, 没事: no matter, nothing wrong, all is well. The whole range of the character, from a traffic accident to a gentle "don't worry about it," lives in that one busy or resting hand. Remember too that the hand never holds an object: 事 is always the affair, never the thing. For objects, reach for 东西.
