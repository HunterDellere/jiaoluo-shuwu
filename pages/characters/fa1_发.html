字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

发 is one simplified glyph standing in for two different traditional characters. This is the first thing to fix in your mind, because it is the key to everything else on the page. Where the mainland writes a single 发, traditional script keeps two separate forms with two separate readings:

發 fā, the older and busier word, meaning to shoot, send out, emit, issue, or develop. The traditional 發 is built around 弓 (gōng, bow) and a foot element, the picture of loosing an arrow: to send something flying from oneself.
髮 fà, meaning the hair of the head. The traditional 髮 carries 髟 (biāo), the long-hair radical that sits atop characters about hair and beards.

When the script was simplified both 發 and 髮 collapsed into the same cursive-derived shape, 发, classified under the radical 又 (yòu, a hand). The merge saves strokes but loads one glyph with two unrelated meanings and two tones. So 发 is a true polyphone: read fā (first tone) for the send / emit / develop family, and fà (fourth tone) for hair.

The fā senses fan out from the core image of loosing something outward. To send: 发送 fāsòng (to transmit), 发出 fāchū (to give off). To bring forth or grow: 发展 fāzhǎn (to develop), 发现 fāxiàn (to discover), 发明 fāmíng (to invent). To occur, as if an event were loosed into being: 发生 fāshēng (to happen). To issue formally: 发表 fābiǎo (to publish). The fà sense stays put on a single concrete thing: the hair, 头发 tóufa.

发送 fāsòng Send & Emit (the fā Reading)
发送 fāsòng to send; to transmit; to dispatch
V 动词 dòngcí
发 fā (to send out) + 送 sòng (to deliver). To send a message, signal, or shipment outward to a recipient: 发送邮件 "to send an email." The everyday verb for transmitting in the digital age.
请把文件发送给我。
Qǐng bǎ wénjiàn fāsòng gěi wǒ.
Please send the file to me.
发出 fāchū to give off; to emit; to issue (a sound, light, order)
V 动词 dòngcí
发 fā (to emit) + 出 chū (out). To let something out from a source: a sound, a smell, a light, a command. 发出声音 "to make a sound," 发出命令 "to issue an order."
机器发出奇怪的声音。
Jīqì fāchū qíguài de shēngyīn.
The machine is giving off a strange noise.
发表 fābiǎo to publish; to issue; to express (publicly)
V 动词 dòngcí
发 fā (to issue) + 表 biǎo (to express; surface). To put a piece of writing or an opinion out into the public sphere: 发表文章 "to publish an article," 发表意见 "to voice an opinion."
她在杂志上发表了一篇小说。
Tā zài zázhì shàng fābiǎo le yì piān xiǎoshuō.
She published a short story in a magazine.
发展 fāzhǎn Develop & Happen (the fā Reading)
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The same outward motion that sends an arrow flying also drives the sense of growth. When something 发 develops, it is unfolding from a small start into a fuller state, the way a bud loosens into a flower. 发展 fāzhǎn (to develop) is one of the most common verbs in modern Chinese, anchoring talk of economies, careers, technology, and people. Beside it sit 发现 fāxiàn (to discover, to bring a hidden thing into view), 发明 fāmíng (to invent, to bring a new thing into being), and 发育 fāyù (to mature physically).

A third branch of fā treats an event itself as something loosed into the world. 发生 fāshēng means "to happen, to occur," literally to send-forth-life: an accident, a war, a change all 发生. The English "break out" catches the flavor, an event bursting into being rather than quietly arriving.

发展 fāzhǎn to develop; to grow; development
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
发 fā (to bring forth) + 展 zhǎn (to unfold; spread). To grow and expand from a smaller or earlier state: 经济发展 "economic development," 发展中国家 "a developing country." Works as both verb and noun.
这座城市发展得很快。
Zhè zuò chéngshì fāzhǎn de hěn kuài.
This city is developing very fast.
发现 fāxiàn to discover; to find; to realize
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
发 fā (to bring forth) + 现 xiàn (to appear; manifest). To bring something previously hidden into view, whether a fact, an object, or a realization: 发现问题 "to spot a problem," 科学发现 "a scientific discovery."
我发现自己错了。
Wǒ fāxiàn zìjǐ cuò le.
I realized I was wrong.
发生 fāshēng to happen; to occur; to take place
V 动词 dòngcí
发 fā (to send forth) + 生 shēng (life; to arise). For an event to come into being: 发生事故 "an accident happens," 发生变化 "a change occurs." Used of events, not of people deliberately doing things.
昨天发生了一件大事。
Zuótiān fāshēng le yí jiàn dàshì.
Something big happened yesterday.
头发 tóufa Hair: the fà Reading
辨析 biànxī · The fā vs. fà Split

Here is where the merge bites. The single character 发 also reads fà (fourth tone) and means the hair on a head, a meaning written 髮 in traditional script and entirely unrelated to sending or developing. The reading you choose is fixed by the word:

Say fà in the hair words: 头发 tóufa (hair, though the suffix here is toneless in everyday speech), 理发 lǐfà (to get a haircut), 白发 báifà (white hair), 长发 chángfà (long hair), 短发 duǎnfà (short hair).
Say fā everywhere else: 发送, 发展, 发现, 发生, and the rest of the send / develop family.

A clean test: if the word is about what grows on your head, it is fà; if it is about something leaving, growing, or happening, it is fā. The two never overlap in meaning, only in the simplified glyph that now carries both.

头发 tóufa hair (on the head)
N 名词 míngcí
头 tóu (head) + 发 fà (hair). The hair on a person's head, as a mass. Note that 发 here is the fà / 髮 reading; in casual speech the syllable often softens toward neutral tone. Distinct from 毛 máo, the word for body hair and fur.
她的头发很长。
Tā de tóufa hěn cháng.
Her hair is very long.
理发 lǐfà to get a haircut; to have one's hair done
V 动词 dòngcí
理 lǐ (to tidy; put in order) + 发 fà (hair). To have the hair cut or styled: 理发店 "barbershop, hair salon." A separable verb, so 理了发 "got a haircut."
我想去理发。
Wǒ xiǎng qù lǐfà.
I want to go get a haircut.
辨析 biànxī · 头发 vs. 毛 头发 fà is specifically the hair of the human head. 毛 máo covers body hair, animal fur, feathers, and wool. You wash your 头发, but a dog has 毛, not 头发.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
发扬光大 fā yáng guāng dà to carry forward and make flourish: to develop a good thing to its fullest Lit: send-forth-raise-bright-great. Said of carrying on a tradition, virtue, or fine quality and making it grow even greater. The fā reading: to bring something forth and develop it. 把好传统发扬光大 "to carry a good tradition forward."
一触即发 yī chù jí fā one touch and it goes off: on the very verge of breaking out Lit: one-touch-then-launch. Like a loaded crossbow that fires at the lightest touch. Used of a tense situation, a crisis or conflict, poised to erupt at any moment. The fā reading: to be loosed, to go off.
千钧一发 qiān jūn yī fà a thousand jun hanging by one hair: a moment of extreme peril Lit: thousand-jun-one-hair. A jūn was a heavy ancient weight, so the image is an enormous mass suspended by a single strand of hair, ready to crash down. Here 发 takes the fà (hair) reading. The phrase marks a hair-trigger emergency.
白发苍苍 bái fà cāng cāng hair gone hoary white: aged, in advanced old age Lit: white-hair-grey-grey. A vivid picture of a head of grizzled white hair, used affectionately or respectfully of the elderly. Again the fà reading, since the phrase is literally about hair.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Hold two pictures side by side, because 发 is two characters wearing one face. First picture: an archer looses an arrow, fā. That single act of sending outward is every fā word, the message sent (发送), the sound given off (发出), the bud opening into growth (发展), the discovery brought to light (发现), the event bursting into being (发生).

Second picture: a single strand of hair, fà. That is the other 发 entirely, the one traditional script wrote as 髮, surviving in 头发, 理发, 白发. The tone is your switch: rising-from-rest first tone fā for sending and growing, falling fourth tone fà for the hair on a head. One glyph, two stories, kept apart by a single tone.
