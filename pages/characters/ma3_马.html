字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

马 is a pictograph, the simplified shape of 馬: a single horse drawn in profile and pared down to three strokes. The full traditional form keeps the picture legible. Its top reaches up where the head and flowing mane once stood, the central body carries a row of short strokes that were the four galloping legs, and the long tail sweeps off below. Slow the eye down across 馬 and the animal reassembles itself: ears pricked, neck arched, hooves striking the ground.

The oracle-bone graph is one of the most vivid in the whole script. Scribes of the Shang drew a recognizable horse standing tall, the great eye and tossing mane marked with care, because the horse was a prestige animal, the engine of the war chariot and the hunt. Through bronze and seal forms the legs and mane stiffened into the rungs and hooks you still see in 馬, until the modern simplification collapsed those four leg-dots into a single sweeping bottom stroke and gave us today's 马.

From the live animal the character reached outward by metaphor. A horse is the fastest thing in the old world, so 马上 (mǎshàng), literally "on horseback," came to mean "at once, right away." A road wide enough for horses is a 马路 (mǎlù), the everyday word for a paved street. Anything horse-shaped or horse-sized borrows the word too, from the wooden 木马 (mùmǎ) of the playground to the saddle-shaped knight in Chinese chess.

马 is also one of the commonest Chinese surnames, carried by tens of millions, and it stands as a radical in its own right under which every horse-related character is filed. In Japanese the same graph reads uma or ba; in Korean it is ma. Wherever the script traveled, the horse cantered along with it.

时地shídìOf Time & the Road

The horse's defining trait, speed, drives the two most common compounds. 马上 turns the animal into a clock: to be "on the horse" is to be a moment from arriving. 马路 turns it into geography: the broad road built for hooves and now for traffic.

马上mǎshàngat once, right away
ADV 副词 fùcí
马 (horse) + 上 (on, atop). Literally "on horseback," the fastest way to travel in antiquity, frozen into an adverb meaning "immediately."
我马上就来。
Wǒ mǎshàng jiù lái.
I'll be right there.
火车马上要开了。
Huǒchē mǎshàng yào kāi le.
The train is about to leave.
马路mǎlùroad, street
N 名词 míngcí
马 (horse) + 路 (road). The wide road built for horses, now the ordinary word for a paved city street.
过马路要小心。
Guò mǎlù yào xiǎoxīn.
Be careful crossing the road.
辨析 biànxī · 马路 vs. 路 路 (lù) is "road, route, path" in the broadest sense, including abstract ones like 人生的路. 马路 is concrete: the actual street you cross.
马力mǎlìhorsepower
N 名词 míngcí
马 (horse) + 力 (power, force). A loan-translation of "horsepower," the unit that measures an engine in horses, keeping the animal alive inside the machine that replaced it.
这辆车有三百马力。
Zhè liàng chē yǒu sānbǎi mǎlì.
This car has three hundred horsepower.
物象wùxiàngThings Called Horse
木马mùmǎwooden horse; vaulting horse; trojan
N 名词 míngcí
木 (wood) + 马 (horse). A horse made of wood: the rocking toy, the gymnastics vaulting horse, and the computer 木马 (a trojan), all named for the most famous wooden horse of all, the one at Troy (特洛伊木马).
孩子们在公园里骑木马。
Háizimen zài gōngyuán lǐ qí mùmǎ.
The children ride the wooden horse in the park.
马虎mǎhucareless, sloppy
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
马 (horse) + 虎 (tiger). A folk tale tells of a painter who drew a creature half horse, half tiger and could not say which it was; the slapdash beast gave its name to careless, half-attentive work.
他做事太马虎了。
Tā zuòshì tài mǎhu le.
He's far too careless in how he does things.
辨析 biànxī · 马虎 vs. 认真 马虎 is the opposite of 认真 (rènzhēn), "conscientious." Doubled into 马马虎虎 it softens to "so-so, passable."
马车mǎchēhorse-drawn carriage, cart
N 名词 míngcí
马 (horse) + 车 (cart, vehicle). The literal horse-and-cart, the carriage that ran on the 马路 long before engines.
古时候人们坐马车出门。
Gǔ shíhou rénmen zuò mǎchē chūmén.
In ancient times people traveled by horse-drawn carriage.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
马到成功mǎ dào chéng gōngthe moment the horses arrive, victory comes: swift, immediate successA military image: the cavalry reaches the field and the battle is already won. A warm, common New Year and well-wishing phrase for a new job or venture.
马马虎虎mǎ mǎ hū hūhorse-horse tiger-tiger: so-so, careless, passableThe doubled form of 马虎. Used both for sloppy work and, self-deprecatingly, to answer "how are you?" with a shrugging "just so-so."
一马当先yī mǎ dāng xiānone horse takes the lead: to be out front, to take the initiativeThe lead horse of the charge. Praises someone who steps forward first and sets the pace for others.
老马识途lǎo mǎ shí túan old horse knows the way: experience is the surest guideFrom the Guǎnzǐ: lost in the mountains, the army let an old horse lead and it found the road home. An old hand's seasoned judgment.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a single galloping horse seen from the side at dusk. Its head and mane rise up the left edge of the frame, the long tail streams off the bottom right, and the whole body is caught mid-stride in just three brushstrokes of motion. That blur of speed is 马. The full form 馬 is the same horse drawn slowly, with every leg and hair in place; the simplified 马 is the same horse at full gallop, too fast for detail.

Hold the speed and the compounds fall out on their own. To be 马上 (mǎshàng) is to be already on that horse, a heartbeat from arriving. To do something 马虎 (mǎhu) is to gallop past the work without stopping to look, leaving a sketch that could be horse or tiger. And when the horses finally reach the field, 马到成功, the victory is already won.
