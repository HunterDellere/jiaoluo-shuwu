字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

朝 pictures daybreak. The oracle-bone and bronze forms set the sun among grass or trees on the left, with the moon still hanging beside it on the right. It is the moment the sun has cleared the grass but the moon has not yet faded: the dawn. From that single scene the whole character grows, and the radical here is 月, the moon left in the morning sky.

That dawn moment forks into two readings:

zhāo, the morning itself. The literal daybreak and everything fresh that comes with it: 朝阳 zhāoyáng (the morning sun), 朝气 zhāoqì (youthful vigor, the air of a new day), 朝夕 zhāoxī (morning and evening, every hour), 今朝 jīnzhāo (this morning, today).

cháo, the court and what flows from it. The royal court met at daybreak, when officials came to attend the ruler. So the dawn word also became the word for that gathering: 朝廷 cháotíng (the imperial court), then the reign it served, 朝代 cháodài (a dynasty), 唐朝 Táng cháo (the Tang dynasty). From the act of officials turning to face the throne came a further sense, to face toward, which thins into the everyday preposition 朝 cháo (toward).

The thread runs from a single image. The sun rising past the moon is the morning (zhāo); the court that gathered in that morning, the line of rulers it served, and the act of facing the throne are the cháo senses. One dawn, two readings, four fields of meaning.

朝代 cháodài Dynasty & Court
朝代 cháodài dynasty: a reigning house and its era
N 名词 míngcí
朝 cháo (court, reign) + 代 dài (generation, age). The standard word for a dynasty, the span during which one ruling house held the throne. Chinese history is told as a sequence of 朝代, each with its own name, capital, and character.
中国历史上有很多朝代。
Zhōngguó lìshǐ shàng yǒu hěn duō cháodài.
There have been many dynasties in Chinese history.
唐朝是一个强盛的朝代。
Táng cháo shì yī gè qiángshèng de cháodài.
The Tang was a powerful and prosperous dynasty.
王朝 wángcháo dynasty; royal house: the line of a ruling family
N 名词 míngcí
王 wáng (king) + 朝 cháo (reign). A dynasty seen as a ruling family or royal house, often used of a single line of monarchs and, by extension, of any long-dominant power: 商业王朝 "a business empire," a sports 王朝.
这个王朝统治了三百年。
Zhège wángcháo tǒngzhì le sānbǎi nián.
This dynasty ruled for three hundred years.
辨析 biànxī · 朝代 vs. 王朝 朝代 stresses the period in the historical sequence, one era among many. 王朝 stresses the ruling family or house holding power. The Tang as a slot in the timeline is a 朝代; the Tang as the house of Li is a 王朝.
朝廷 cháotíng the imperial court: the ruler and the central government
N 名词 míngcí
朝 cháo (court) + 廷 tíng (court hall). The ruler together with the ministers and offices around the throne, the seat of central power. To act 代表朝廷 is to act in the name of the throne itself.
大臣们每天早上去朝廷。
Dàchénmen měitiān zǎoshang qù cháotíng.
The ministers went to court every morning.
朝着 cháozhe Toward: the Preposition 朝
语法洞见 yǔfǎ dòngjiàn · Grammatical Insight

Out of the act of facing the throne, 朝 cháo settled into an everyday preposition meaning "toward" or "facing." It sits before a direction or a target and aims the verb at it: 朝南 cháo nán (facing south), 朝前走 cháo qián zǒu (walk forward), 朝我笑 cháo wǒ xiào (to smile toward me). The fuller form 朝着 cháozhe carries the same job with a sense of sustained motion or facing: 朝着目标前进 "advance toward the goal."

Its near-twin is 向 xiàng, which also means "toward." The two are interchangeable for physical facing and gesture: 朝南 and 向南 both mean "facing south," 朝他招手 and 向他招手 both mean "to wave toward him." They part ways with abstract or interpersonal targets: 向 is the one used to direct speech, requests, and learning at a person (向他借钱 "to borrow money from him," 向老师请教 "to consult the teacher"), where 朝 sounds wrong. See the disambiguation note below for the working rule.

朝 cháo toward; facing; in the direction of (preposition)
PREP 介词 jiècí
The bare preposition, placed before a direction or target to aim the verb: 朝南 "facing south," 朝前走 "walk forward." Marks the way an action or a thing is turned. For physical facing it is interchangeable with 向.
这间房子朝南。
Zhè jiān fángzi cháo nán.
This room faces south.
他朝我笑了笑。
Tā cháo wǒ xiào le xiào.
He smiled toward me.
朝前 cháoqián forward; toward the front; facing ahead
ADV 副词 fùcí
朝 cháo (toward) + 前 qián (front). Directed at the space ahead: 朝前走 "walk forward," 朝前看 "look ahead." A close everyday counterpart to 向前.
请朝前走几步。
Qǐng cháo qián zǒu jǐ bù.
Please take a few steps forward.
朝着 cháozhe toward; in the direction of (with sustained motion)
PREP 介词 jiècí
朝 cháo (toward) + 着 zhe (the durative marker). The preposition with a sense of ongoing facing or movement toward something: 朝着大海 "facing the sea," 朝着目标努力 "to work toward a goal." Common in figurative aims as well as physical ones.
我们朝着山顶爬去。
Wǒmen cháozhe shāndǐng pá qù.
We climbed toward the mountaintop.
辨析 biànxī · 朝 vs. 向 For physical facing and gesture the two are interchangeable: 朝南 and 向南 both mean "facing south." The split: 向 is the one used to direct speech, requests, and learning at a person (向他借钱, 向老师请教), where 朝 sounds wrong. 朝 leans toward bodily facing and gesture toward something physical.
朝阳 zhāoyáng Morning & Vigor: the Reading zhāo
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Read as zhāo, 朝 keeps the original dawn. It is the morning itself and, by a natural step, the freshness and energy of a new day. 朝阳 zhāoyáng is the rising sun, light just clearing the horizon; 朝气 zhāoqì is the vigor of morning carried over into people, the lively spirit of the young; 朝夕 zhāoxī is "morning and evening," a way of saying every hour, day in and day out. Where cháo points to thrones and dynasties, zhāo points back to the first reading of the glyph: the sun coming up past the moon.

朝阳 zhāoyáng the morning sun: the rising sun, dawn light
N 名词 míngcí
朝 zhāo (morning) + 阳 yáng (sun). The sun as it rises, and the fresh light it brings. Used figuratively for anything new and full of promise: 朝阳产业 "a sunrise industry," a sector on the rise.
朝阳从东边升起。
Zhāoyáng cóng dōngbiān shēngqǐ.
The morning sun rises in the east.
朝气 zhāoqì youthful spirit: vigor, the lively air of a new day
N 名词 míngcí
朝 zhāo (morning) + 气 qì (air, energy). The energy of morning seen in people, a fresh, lively spirit. Said of the young or of any group full of drive: 充满朝气 "brimming with vigor." Its opposite is 暮气 mùqì, the listlessness of dusk.
这些年轻人充满朝气。
Zhèxiē niánqīng rén chōngmǎn zhāoqì.
These young people are full of vigor.
朝夕 zhāoxī morning and evening: every hour, day after day
N 名词 míngcí
朝 zhāo (morning) + 夕 xī (evening). The two ends of the day standing for all of it: from dawn to dusk, every hour. 朝夕相处 "to be together morning and evening," to live in close daily company; 只争朝夕 "to seize every moment."
他们朝夕相处,感情很好。
Tāmen zhāoxī xiāngchǔ, gǎnqíng hěn hǎo.
They are together day and night and get along very well.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
朝气蓬勃 zhāo qì péng bó full of youthful vigor: brimming with life and energy Lit: morning-vigor, luxuriant-thriving. 朝气 is the spirit of a new day, 蓬勃 the look of growth in full flush. Said of people, teams, or whole societies that are young and full of drive.
朝三暮四 zhāo sān mù sì three in the morning, four at dusk: fickle, forever changing one's mind From a fable in which a keeper offers his monkeys three nuts in the morning and four at night; they protest, so he swaps it to four and three, and they are content though the total is the same. Now it names someone fickle, who keeps switching with no real difference.
改朝换代 gǎi cháo huàn dài to change dynasty and era: a change of regime Lit: change-dynasty, swap-era. 朝 and 代 are the dynasty and its age. The phrase names the fall of one ruling house and the rise of another, and by extension any sweeping change of who holds power.
有朝一日 yǒu zhāo yī rì should there come a day: one day, someday Lit: there-is morning one-day. 朝 here is zhāo, a morning standing for some future day. A set way of saying "one day," usually pointing to a hope or ambition: 有朝一日我会成功 "one day I will succeed."
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture the instant of dawn: the sun has just cleared the grass on one side, while the moon still hangs pale on the other. Hold that single image; it is the whole character.

Read the scene as the hour and you have zhāo, the morning and its freshness (朝阳, 朝气, 朝夕). Now fill that morning with a court of officials come to attend the ruler at daybreak, and you have cháo: the court (朝廷), the reign it served, the dynasty (朝代, 王朝), and the act of facing the throne that thins into the preposition toward (朝南, 朝着). Whenever you meet 朝, first decide whether it is the morning or the court, and the reading follows.
