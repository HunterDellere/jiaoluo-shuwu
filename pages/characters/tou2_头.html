字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

头 is the simplified form of traditional 頭, a pictophonetic compound. In the full form 頭, the element 页 (yè, a stylized human head) carries the meaning, and 豆 (dòu) supplies the sound. The 页 component is the same head that appears in 颜 yán (face), 颈 jǐng (neck), and 顶 dǐng (crown), so the traditional glyph wears its meaning openly: a head, marked by the 页 head-element, pronounced like 豆.

The simplified 头 throws away the phonetic and the head-element alike, replacing them with a compact cursive shape that the dictionaries file under the radical 大 (dà, big). It no longer pictures anything; it is a shorthand glyph standing in for the older 頭. The two are one word, one reading: write 头 on the mainland and in simplified texts, 頭 in traditional Taiwan, Hong Kong, and classical printing.

From the body part, the meaning radiates outward in four directions:

1. The head (body part). The top of the body: 头发 tóufa (hair), 点头 diǎntóu (to nod), 抬头 táitóu (to raise the head).
2. Top, tip, end, beginning. The head of a thing, either extremity of it: 山头 shāntóu (hilltop), 街头 jiētóu (street corner), 开头 kāitóu (the start), 尽头 jìntóu (the very end).
3. Chief, leader, the one at the head. 头目 tóumù (ringleader), 领头 lǐngtóu (to take the lead).
4. The toneless noun-suffix (tou). Bleached of meaning, it just marks a concrete noun: 石头 shítou (stone), 木头 mùtou (wood), 馒头 mántou (steamed bun). It also serves as a measure word for livestock: 一头牛 yī tóu niú (one cow).

The thread is the head as the top, leading end of anything: of a body, of a hill, of a street, of a group, even the leading edge of a noun.

头发 tóufa Head & Body
头发 tóufa hair (on the head)
N 名词 míngcí
头 tóu (head) + 发 fà (hair), with 发 going neutral in speech. The hair on your head specifically; body hair is 毛 máo, a different word. 理头发 means to get a haircut.
她的头发很长。
Tā de tóufa hěn cháng.
Her hair is very long.
点头 diǎntóu to nod (the head)
V 动词 dòngcí
点 diǎn (to touch lightly, to dip) + 头 tóu (head). To dip the head: to nod in agreement or greeting. 点头同意 "to nod in assent." Its opposite gesture is 摇头 yáotóu, to shake the head.
他点了点头表示同意。
Tā diǎn le diǎn tóu biǎoshì tóngyì.
He nodded to show his agreement.
抬头 táitóu to raise the head; to look up
V 动词 dòngcí
抬 tái (to lift) + 头 tóu (head). To lift the head and look up, literally or in the sense of holding one's head high after hardship. Its opposite is 低头 dītóu, to lower the head.
他抬头看了看天。
Tā táitóu kàn le kàn tiān.
He raised his head and looked at the sky.
开头 kāitóu Top, End & Beginning
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Once 头 means "the head of a thing," it can name either extremity of it. The head of a street is the corner where it starts (街头 jiētóu); the head of a hill is its summit (山头 shāntóu). Stretched along a line, 头 marks the leading edge: 开头 kāitóu is the very start, the place where a thing first pokes its head out, while 尽头 jìntóu is the point where it runs out, the far end of the road.

This is why 头 sits naturally with directions and edges. 前头 qiántou (in front), 后头 hòutou (behind), 上头 shàngtou (on top), and 里头 lǐtou (inside) all use the toneless suffix to turn a bare position word into a place-noun. In each, 头 supplies the sense of "the edge, the end-point in that direction."

开头 kāitóu the beginning; the start; at first
N/V 名/动 míng/dòng
开 kāi (to open) + 头 tóu (head, end). The opening end of something: the start of a story, a year, a task. 一开头 "right at the start." Both a noun (the beginning) and a verb (to begin).
这个故事的开头很有意思。
Zhège gùshi de kāitóu hěn yǒu yìsi.
The beginning of this story is very interesting.
辨析 biànxī · 开头 vs. 开始 开头 points at the start as a place or a portion: the opening lines, the first stretch, where the thing begins. 开始 kāishǐ is the act of starting and the moment it happens, used as a verb far more freely: 我们开始吧 "let's begin." You read the 开头 of a book; you 开始 to read it.
尽头 jìntóu the end; the very end; the limit
N 名词 míngcí
尽 jìn (to exhaust, to use up) + 头 tóu (end). The point where a thing runs out: the far end of a road, the limit of a stretch. 路的尽头 "the end of the road."
走到这条路的尽头就到了。
Zǒu dào zhè tiáo lù de jìntóu jiù dào le.
Walk to the end of this road and you are there.
街头 jiētóu street corner; on the streets
N 名词 míngcí
街 jiē (street) + 头 tóu (head, end). The head of a street, hence the corner, and by extension the public space of the streets: 街头艺人 "a street performer," 街头巷尾 "every street and alley."
街头有很多卖小吃的。
Jiētóu yǒu hěn duō mài xiǎochī de.
There are many snack vendors on the streets.
词尾 cíwěi The Noun-Suffix: Toneless 头 (tou)
语法洞见 yǔfǎ dòngjiàn · Grammatical Insight

Like 子 (zi) and 儿 (r), 头 does double duty as one of Mandarin's empty noun-suffixes. Drained of its full second tone, toneless 头 (tou) attaches to a one-syllable root and turns it into a stable, two-syllable noun: 石头 shítou (stone), 木头 mùtou (wood), 骨头 gútou (bone), 舌头 shétou (tongue), 馒头 mántou (steamed bun). The root often cannot stand alone, and 头 fills the second slot to mark "this is a thing."

Listen for the tone, just as with 子. A full rising tóu is the meaningful head: 抬头 táitóu, 开头 kāitóu, 山头 shāntóu. A neutral tou is the bare suffix: 石头 shítou, 木头 mùtou. The reading is your clue to which 头 you are meeting.

Separately, 头 (tóu, full tone) serves as the measure word for large livestock: 一头牛 yī tóu niú (a cow), 三头猪 sān tóu zhū (three pigs), 一头大象 yī tóu dàxiàng (an elephant). The image is counting beasts by their heads, much as English once counted cattle by the head.

石头 shítou stone; rock
N 名词 míngcí
石 shí (stone) + 头 tou (suffix). The everyday word for a stone or rock. The bare 石 survives mostly in compounds and set terms; 石头 is the word you say out loud.
河边有很多石头。
Hé biān yǒu hěn duō shítou.
There are many stones by the river.
木头 mùtou wood; timber; (of a person) wooden, dull
N 名词 míngcí
木 mù (wood, tree) + 头 tou (suffix). Wood as a material. Used figuratively of a person who is slow or unresponsive: 木头人 "a blockhead," literally a wooden person.
这张桌子是木头做的。
Zhè zhāng zhuōzi shì mùtou zuò de.
This table is made of wood.
一头牛 yī tóu niú one cow; a head of cattle
MW 量词 liàngcí
一 yī (one) + 头 tóu (measure word) + 牛 niú (cow). Here 头 keeps its full tone as the classifier for large livestock: cattle, pigs, sheep, elephants. You count beasts by the head.
农场里有十头牛。
Nóngchǎng lǐ yǒu shí tóu niú.
There are ten cows on the farm.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
头头是道 tóu tóu shì dào clear and logical at every point: well-reasoned and articulate Lit: every-head-is-the-way. Said of speech or analysis that is orderly and convincing from start to finish, with each point landing soundly. 说得头头是道 "to argue every point convincingly."
埋头苦干 mái tóu kǔ gàn to bury the head and toil hard: to work diligently without distraction Lit: bury-head-bitter-work. To lower one's head and labor away, absorbed in the task and indifferent to praise or notice. The picture is of someone bent over their work, head down.
头重脚轻 tóu zhòng jiǎo qīng heavy on top and light on the feet: top-heavy and unstable Lit: head-heavy-feet-light. Used of anything badly balanced, with too much weight or emphasis at the top: a wobbling structure, a lopsided plan, or a dizzy, unsteady feeling.
评头论足 píng tóu lùn zú to judge the head and discuss the feet: to nitpick and find fault Lit: appraise-head-discuss-feet. Originally about appraising a person's looks head to toe; now it means to carp over trifles and make petty, unwanted criticisms of others.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Start at the top of the body: the head, 头. Now let "the head of a thing" travel. Put it on a hill and you reach the summit (山头). Put it on a street and you reach the corner (街头). Lay it along a line and it becomes the leading end, the beginning (开头), with the far end, the place it runs out, as its mirror (尽头). Put it over a group and it becomes the chief, the one at the head (头目).

Then watch it empty out entirely. Toneless, 头 (tou) drops all of this and becomes the bare tag that makes a thing a thing: 石头 stone, 木头 wood, 馒头 bun. The tone is your guide back, as with 子: a rising tóu is a meaningful head or end, a neutral tou is only the suffix. And when you count cattle, hear the full tóu return: 一头牛, one beast, counted by its head.
