字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

己 jǐ is a pictograph of a coiled, bending line. The oracle-bone and bronze forms show a thread or cord doubled back on itself in a zigzag, the early picture of something wound and knotted. That original sense survives in the related character 纪 jì (a thread, a record, a discipline), where the silk radical 糸 is added back. The bare shape 己 was borrowed early as a loan-graph for the spoken word meaning "oneself," and the self-meaning so completely took over that the thread sense now lives only in its descendants. 己 is its own radical, the head of a small family that includes 已 and 巳.

Three senses branch from the borrowed self:

1. Oneself, self. The reflexive "self" of a person: 自己 zìjǐ (oneself), 自我 zìwǒ (the ego, the self), 知己 zhījǐ (a bosom friend, one who truly knows you).
2. Personal, one's own, private. What belongs to the self as opposed to others: 己见 jǐjiàn (one's own view), 利己 lìjǐ (self-interest), 克己 kèjǐ (self-restraint).
3. The sixth Heavenly Stem. In the cycle of ten 天干 tiāngān used to count days and years, 己 is the sixth, paired in the calendar with the earth phase.

The thread running through every sense is the turn inward. Whether the self is the grammatical reflexive, the private opinion held against the crowd, or the personal will that must be curbed, 己 is the point a person comes back to: the doubled-back line that always returns to where it started.

自己 zìjǐ Oneself
自己 zìjǐ oneself; one's own; by oneself
N 名词 míngcí
自 zì (self) + 己 jǐ (self). The everyday reflexive pronoun, doubling two self-words for emphasis: 我自己 "I myself," 你自己 "you yourself." Also means "by oneself, on one's own": 自己做 "to do it oneself."
这是我自己做的。
Zhè shì wǒ zìjǐ zuò de.
I made this myself.
你要相信自己。
Nǐ yào xiāngxìn zìjǐ.
You have to believe in yourself.
知己 zhījǐ a bosom friend; one who truly knows you
N 名词 míngcí
知 zhī (to know) + 己 jǐ (self). One who knows your self, the rare friend who understands you completely. The famous line 人生得一知己足矣 holds that to gain a single true friend in life is enough.
他是我多年的知己。
Tā shì wǒ duō nián de zhījǐ.
He has been a close friend of mine for years.
辨析 biànxī · 自己 vs. 自我 自己 is the everyday reflexive: oneself as a person doing or owning something, as in 自己来 "I'll do it myself." 自我 zìwǒ is more abstract, the self as a concept or ego: 自我介绍 "self-introduction," 自我意识 "self-awareness." Use 自己 for the ordinary "myself," 自我 when you mean the inner self that psychology and philosophy talk about.
各自 gèzì each (on their own); respectively
N 名词 míngcí
各 gè (each) + 自 zì (self). Each person doing a thing for their own self, separately: 各自回家 "each went home on their own." A near relative of 己, built on the same idea of the individual self set apart from the group.
大家各自付钱。
Dàjiā gèzì fù qián.
Everyone pays for their own.
克己 kèjǐ Personal & Self-Discipline
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

When 己 points at the self that wants and clings, Chinese ethics treats it as the thing to be governed. Confucius gave the tradition its keyword: 克己 kèjǐ, to master the self. His phrase 克己复礼 ("subdue the self and return to ritual propriety") makes self-restraint the gate to virtue. To curb the personal will so the wider order can hold is, in this view, the whole of moral cultivation.

The same 己 supplies the vocabulary of selfishness and selflessness. 利己 lìjǐ is acting for one's own benefit; its opposite, 舍己 shějǐ, is setting the self aside. Between them sits 己见 jǐjiàn, one's own view, prized when it is independent and warned against when it is stubborn. In every case the self of 己 is a center of gravity that ethics asks a person to hold loosely, neither erased nor indulged.

克己 kèjǐ to restrain oneself; self-disciplined; self-denying
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
克 kè (to overcome) + 己 jǐ (self). To conquer one's own impulses, the Confucian ideal of self-mastery. The root of 克己复礼, "subdue the self and return to ritual," and of 克己奉公, "curb oneself and serve the public good."
他为人克己，从不计较。
Tā wéirén kèjǐ, cóngbù jìjiào.
He is self-disciplined and never haggles over personal gain.
利己 lìjǐ to benefit oneself; self-interested
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
利 lì (benefit) + 己 jǐ (self). Acting for one's own advantage. Often paired in contrast: 利己 vs. 利人 (to benefit others), and condemned in the phrase 损人利己, "harm others to profit oneself."
这种利己的做法不可取。
Zhè zhǒng lìjǐ de zuòfǎ bùkěqǔ.
This kind of self-serving approach is not advisable.
己见 jǐjiàn one's own view; personal opinion
N 名词 míngcí
己 jǐ (one's own) + 见 jiàn (view). The opinion that is distinctly one's own, valued for independence but cautioned against when it hardens into obstinacy. Lives in 固执己见, "to cling stubbornly to one's own view."
他坚持己见，不肯让步。
Tā jiānchí jǐjiàn, bù kěn ràngbù.
He held to his own opinion and would not give ground.
天干 tiāngān The Sixth Heavenly Stem
历法洞见 lìfǎ dòngjiàn · Calendrical Insight

Apart from "self," 己 has a second life as the sixth of the ten 天干 tiāngān, the Heavenly Stems: 甲 乙 丙 丁 戊 己 庚 辛 壬 癸. Cycled against the twelve Earthly Branches, the stems generate the sixty-term count that has numbered days and years across the long sweep of the Chinese calendar. In the five-phase scheme each pair of stems takes a phase, and 己 belongs to earth (土), the yin earth to 戊's yang. A year such as 己亥 pairs the stem 己 with a branch to name one slot in the sixty-year round.

This second reading is the same character and the same sound, jǐ, third tone. Context tells the two apart at once: among pronouns and ethics it is "self," among 甲乙丙丁 it is the calendrical stem.

辨形 biànxíng · 己 jǐ / 已 yǐ / 巳 sì Three near-twins are told apart by one thing: how far the opening stroke rises on the left.
己 jǐ (self) → the stroke stays fully open, not touching the top: 自己 oneself.
已 yǐ (already) → the stroke rises halfway, a small gap left: 已经 already.
巳 sì (6th Earthly Branch) → the stroke closes all the way to the top: 巳时 the snake hour.
The mnemonic taught to schoolchildren: 开口己，半口已，闭口巳 ("open-mouth 己, half-mouth 已, shut-mouth 巳").
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
舍己为人 shě jǐ wèi rén to sacrifice oneself for others: selfless devotion to others' good Lit: give-up-self-for-others. The highest praise for selflessness, setting one's own interests aside for the sake of other people. The 己 here is exactly the self that 舍 (to give up) renounces.
知己知彼 zhī jǐ zhī bǐ know yourself and know the other: understand both sides before acting From Sunzi's Art of War: 知己知彼，百战不殆, "know yourself and know your enemy, and in a hundred battles you will never be in peril." Now used of any contest or negotiation that rewards understanding both sides.
严于律己 yán yú lǜ jǐ strict in disciplining oneself: hold yourself to a high standard Lit: strict-toward-disciplining-self. Often paired with 宽以待人, "lenient toward others," to describe the admired character who is hard on the self and gentle with everyone else.
各抒己见 gè shū jǐ jiàn each airs his own view: everyone speaks their mind freely Lit: each-express-own-view. Said of a discussion where every participant is free to put forward their own opinion, the ideal of open and frank exchange. 抒 shū means to express or pour out.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

See the single coiled thread of 己 bending back on itself and returning to its start. That doubling-back is the self: every meaning of 己 is the point a person comes home to. It is oneself plainly (自己), the friend who knows that self (知己), the private opinion held against the room (己见), and the self that ethics asks you to curb (克己) or even give up (舍己为人).

For the look-alikes, hold the mouth in mind: 开口己 the open-mouthed self, 半口已 the half-open already, 闭口巳 the shut-mouthed branch. The opening stroke on the left rises higher with each one, from fully open in 己, to halfway in 已, to sealed shut in 巳.
