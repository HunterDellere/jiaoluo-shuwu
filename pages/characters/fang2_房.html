字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

房 sits in the half-enclosure frame ⿸: the door radical 户 (hù) leans over the top-left like a single hinged leaf, and the phonetic 方 (fāng) tucks beneath it. The decomposition is 房 = 户 + 方. 户 carries the meaning, a one-leaf door cut into a wall, and 方 carries the sound, lending the rising fáng reading. This makes 房 a textbook pictophonetic: one component points at the thing, the other points at how to say it.

The radical 户 is the key to the original sense. A full gate of two leaves is 門 (mén); a single leaf, the kind hung on an inner room rather than a courtyard entrance, is 户. So 房 named the chamber you reach through a single inner door: the side-room, the wing flanking the main hall, the lesser room off the central space. The Shuōwén Jiězì glosses it as 室在旁也, the room that lies to the side. You can still feel that geometry in a traditional courtyard house, where the 正房 (zhèngfáng) faces south across the yard and the 厢房 (xiāngfáng) line the flanks.

From that side-chamber the sense spread two ways. Outward it grew to mean any built room and then the whole building, the house, until 房子 became the everyday word for a dwelling. Inward and metaphorically, because each branch of a large family took its own wing of the compound, 房 came to mean a branch or line of descent: 大房 the senior branch, 二房 the second branch. The honeycomb logic of partitioned space also lets 房 label a cell or chamber in other systems, as in 心房 (xīnfáng), the atrium of the heart.

居所jūsuǒHouse & Room: the Building Itself
房子fángzihouse, building
N 名词 míngcí
房 (building) plus the neutral-tone noun suffix 子 (zi). The default everyday word for a house or any standalone building you live in. Concrete and countable: you buy one, rent one, or build one.
他们在城里买了一套房子。
Tāmen zài chéng lǐ mǎi le yí tào fángzi.
They bought a house in the city.
辨析 biànxī · 房子 vs. 家 房子 is the physical building; 家 (jiā) is the home as a household and emotional center. You can own three 房子 but have only one 家.
房间fángjiānroom
N 名词 míngcí
房 (room) plus 间 (jiān, a space between two walls, also the measure word for rooms). The standard word for a single room inside a building: a bedroom, a hotel room, an office.
这个房间太小了，放不下大床。
Zhège fángjiān tài xiǎo le, fàng bú xià dà chuáng.
This room is too small to fit a big bed.
楼房lóufángmulti-storey building
N 名词 míngcí
楼 (lóu, a building of multiple floors) plus 房. A building with stories stacked above ground, contrasted with the single-level 平房 (píngfáng). The everyday word for the apartment blocks of a modern Chinese city.
我们家以前住平房，现在搬进了楼房。
Wǒmen jiā yǐqián zhù píngfáng, xiànzài bān jìn le lóufáng.
Our family used to live in a single-storey house; now we have moved into an apartment block.
分间fēnjiānRooms by Function: Named Chambers
X + 房 · the room where X happens 房 attaches to a function word to name a room by what is done in it. 厨 (kitchen) → 厨房, 书 (books) → 书房 (study), 病 (illness) → 病房 (hospital ward), 茶 (tea) → 茶房 (old word for a tea-serving steward or pantry). The pattern reaches back to the courtyard wing: each 房 is a chamber assigned a purpose.
厨房chúfángkitchen
N 名词 míngcí
厨 (chú, cooking, the cook) plus 房. The room where food is prepared. One of the most frequent 房 compounds in daily life, and usually among the first room words a learner needs.
妈妈在厨房里做晚饭。
Māma zài chúfáng lǐ zuò wǎnfàn.
Mum is making dinner in the kitchen.
书房shūfángstudy, library room
N 名词 míngcí
书 (shū, books) plus 房. The room set aside for reading and writing. In the scholar's house it was the heart of the home, lined with 书 and the four treasures of the study (brush, ink, paper, inkstone).
爸爸把书房当成了自己的小天地。
Bàba bǎ shūfáng dàngchéng le zìjǐ de xiǎo tiāndì.
Dad treats the study as his own little world.
卧房wòfángbedroom
N 名词 míngcí
卧 (wò, to lie down) plus 房. The room for sleeping. In everyday speech 卧室 (wòshì) is now more common, but 卧房 survives and shows the same logic: the chamber where one lies down.
这套公寓有两个卧房和一个客厅。
Zhè tào gōngyù yǒu liǎng gè wòfáng hé yí gè kètīng.
This apartment has two bedrooms and a living room.
房产fángchǎnProperty & Tenancy: Who Owns, Who Rents
房东fángdōnglandlord
N 名词 míngcí
房 (house) plus 东 (dōng, east; by old convention the host stands on the east side). The person who owns the dwelling and rents it out. The 东 element marks the host or owner, the same 东 found in 股东 (gǔdōng, shareholder).
房东说下个月要涨房租。
Fángdōng shuō xià gè yuè yào zhǎng fángzū.
The landlord says the rent is going up next month.
辨析 biànxī · 房东 vs. 房客 房东 owns and rents out; 房客 (fángkè, the guest of the house) is the tenant who rents and pays. 东 is the host, 客 the guest.
房租fángzūrent
N 名词 míngcí
房 (house) plus 租 (zū, to rent, rent money). The money paid to occupy a dwelling. Pair it with 交 (jiāo, to hand over): 交房租 means to pay the rent.
这里的房租比市中心便宜多了。
Zhèlǐ de fángzū bǐ shì zhōngxīn piányi duō le.
The rent here is much cheaper than downtown.
房价fángjiàhousing price
N 名词 míngcí
房 (house) plus 价 (jià, price). The purchase price of property. A constant topic in contemporary Chinese life, where 房价 in big cities is a daily-news obsession.
这几年大城市的房价涨得很快。
Zhè jǐ nián dà chéngshì de fángjià zhǎng de hěn kuài.
In recent years housing prices in big cities have risen fast.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
房谋杜断fáng móu dù duànFáng plans, Dù decides: a perfect pairing of complementary talentsFrom the Tang statesmen Fáng Xuánlíng, famed for strategy, and Dù Rúhuì, famed for decisiveness. Here 房 is the surname Fáng; the idiom praises two people whose strengths complete each other.
洞房花烛dòng fáng huā zhúbridal chamber and wedding candles: the wedding night洞房 is the bridal chamber, the deep inner 房 of the house; 花烛 are the red wedding candles. The set phrase stands for a marriage celebration, often paired as 洞房花烛夜, the night of the wedding.
文房四宝wén fáng sì bǎothe four treasures of the study: brush, ink, paper, inkstone文房 is the scholar's study, the writing 房; 四宝 are its four treasures: 笔 (brush), 墨 (ink), 纸 (paper), 砚 (inkstone). The phrase names the classical toolkit of Chinese calligraphy and painting.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a courtyard house at dusk. The great two-leaf gate 門 of the compound stands shut for the night, but along the inner walls a row of single doors 户 hangs open, each one a single hinged leaf swinging into a small lit chamber. Step through one of those single doors and you are inside a 房: a side-room with a lamp, a bed, the smell of cooking drifting from the next 房 down the row.

The glyph holds that scene in its frame. The 户 leans across the top-left like the single open door you just pushed through, and 方 sits squarely beneath it, giving you the sound fáng to say as you cross the threshold. Hear it: the rising tone lifts like the door swinging up and open.

Once the image is set, the compounds slot in as rooms along the corridor: cooking smells make 厨房 (the kitchen), books and lamplight make 书房 (the study), a bed makes 卧房 (the bedroom). Step out of the building entirely and the same word zooms out to mean the whole house, 房子, with a 房东 (landlord) holding the key and 房租 (rent) due at month's end.
