字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

坏 splits cleanly down the middle: 坏 = 土 + 不. On the left stands 土 (tǔ), the mound of earth that marks this character as belonging to the soil, ground, and the things that crumble back into it. On the right sits 不 (bù), which carries no meaning of its own here; it lends the sound. This is a picto-phonetic of the most common type, semantic earth plus phonetic 不, and the radical 土 tells you where to file it: among walls, dust, ruins, and rot.

The simplified glyph you read today, with 不 on the right, is a modern standardization. The traditional 壞 carried a far busier phonetic, 褱 (huái), stacked above the same 土 base, and Shuōwén Jiězì defines that older form plainly as 敗也, "to be ruined, to fall apart." The throughline survives the simplification untouched: earth that gives way. A wall that slumps, a dyke that breaches, a pot fired wrong that cracks in the kiln.

From collapsing earth the sense spreads outward in two directions. Physically it becomes anything broken or spoiled: a machine that quits, fruit that softens to mush, milk that turns. Morally it becomes anything corrupt or rotten: a bad person, a wicked plan, a child who has gone astray. Mandarin keeps both alive in one syllable, so 坏 can describe a dead refrigerator and a villain in the same breath.

The fourth tone falls hard, huài, the voice dropping like the wall it names. Japanese borrowed the traditional 壞 as kai (the verb 壊れる, kowareru, "to break"), and Korean reads it goe. Across all three the image holds: something that once stood whole has come apart at the ground.

坏人huàirénThe Morally Rotten
坏人huàirénbad person, villain
N 名词 míngcí
坏 (bad) + 人 (person). The default word a child learns for the villain in a story or the stranger to avoid. Broad and moral, covering everyone from the cartoon antagonist to a genuine criminal.
他不是坏人，只是一时糊涂。
Tā bù shì huàirén, zhǐshì yìshí hútu.
He isn't a bad person, he just had a moment of bad judgment.
故事里的坏人最后被抓住了。
Gùshi lǐ de huàirén zuìhòu bèi zhuāzhù le.
The villain in the story was finally caught.
坏蛋huàidànscoundrel, bad egg
N 名词 míngcí
坏 (bad) + 蛋 (egg). A literal rotten egg, used figuratively for a scoundrel. Colloquial and elastic: a genuine insult when angry, a flirtatious tease between a couple when said with a smile.
你这个坏蛋，又骗我！
Nǐ zhège huàidàn, yòu piàn wǒ!
You scoundrel, you tricked me again!
辨析 biànxī · 坏蛋 vs. 坏人 坏人 is neutral and descriptive; 坏蛋 is colloquial and emotionally charged, swinging between real abuse and playful teasing depending on tone.
使坏shǐhuàito play a dirty trick
V 动词 dòngcí
使 (to make, to set going) + 坏 (bad). To deliberately make trouble for someone behind their back, to pull a malicious prank or sabotage on the sly.
是谁在背后使坏，害我丢了工作？
Shì shéi zài bèihòu shǐhuài, hài wǒ diūle gōngzuò?
Who was scheming behind my back and made me lose my job?
破坏pòhuàiRuin & Damage
破坏pòhuàito destroy, to undermine
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
破 (to break, smash) + 坏 (to ruin). Two near-synonyms doubled for force. Destroys physical things (buildings, environment) and abstract ones alike (trust, an atmosphere, a plan, the rules).
乱砍树木会破坏生态环境。
Luàn kǎn shùmù huì pòhuài shēngtài huánjìng.
Reckless logging damages the ecological environment.
他的一句话破坏了整个晚上的气氛。
Tā de yí jù huà pòhuàile zhěnggè wǎnshang de qìfēn.
One remark from him ruined the whole evening's mood.
坏了huài lebroken; out of order
V 动词 dòngcí
坏 (to break down) + 了 (change-of-state particle). The everyday way to say a device or appliance has stopped working, or that food has gone off. Also a spoken exclamation: 坏了！ "Uh-oh, this is bad!"
空调坏了，房间里很热。
Kōngtiáo huài le, fángjiān lǐ hěn rè.
The air conditioner is broken, the room is very hot.
坏了，我把钥匙锁在车里了！
Huài le, wǒ bǎ yàoshi suǒ zài chē lǐ le!
Oh no, I locked the keys inside the car!
辨析 biànxī · 坏了 vs. 破坏 坏了 is intransitive and usually accidental, something simply stopped working; 破坏 is transitive and deliberate, an agent actively wrecks something.
学坏xuéhuàiGoing Wrong
学坏xuéhuàito pick up bad habits, to be led astray
V 动词 dòngcí
学 (to learn) + 坏 (bad). To learn the wrong things, the classic parental worry that good company makes good children and bad company corrupts them. 坏 here works as a result complement: the learning turns out badly.
父母担心孩子跟那些人学坏。
Fùmǔ dānxīn háizi gēn nàxiē rén xuéhuài.
The parents worry their child will be led astray by those people.
变坏biànhuàito go bad, to deteriorate
V 动词 dòngcí
变 (to change) + 坏 (bad). To turn for the worse, whether food spoiling in the heat, weather souring, or a person's character degrading over time.
天气热，剩菜很快就变坏了。
Tiānqì rè, shèngcài hěn kuài jiù biànhuài le.
It's hot, so the leftovers spoiled quickly.
坏处huàichuharm, drawback, downside
N 名词 míngcí
坏 (bad) + 处 (point, aspect). The disadvantage or harm of something, the standard counterpart to 好处 (hǎochu, benefit). Weighed up when deciding whether something is worth doing.
熬夜对身体只有坏处，没有好处。
Áoyè duì shēntǐ zhǐ yǒu huàichu, méiyǒu hǎochu.
Staying up late only harms your health, it has no benefits.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
气急败坏qì jí bài huàibreath urgent, composure ruined: flustered and furious, beside oneself with ragePictures someone so panicked or enraged that their composure has fallen apart. 败坏 doubles "defeated" and "ruined." Common in narration for a person who has lost their cool and is lashing out.
坏人坏事huài rén huài shìbad people and bad deeds: wrongdoers and their misdeedsA set four-character phrase pairing 坏人 with 坏事, often in the formula 与坏人坏事作斗争, "to struggle against wrongdoers and wrongdoing." Carries a formal, almost official register.
败坏门风bài huài mén fēngto ruin the family's good name: to disgrace one's household败坏 (to corrupt, ruin) acting on 门风, a family's reputation and moral tradition. Said of conduct shameful enough to stain the whole family, a phrase out of old domestic and clan morality.
使坏心眼shǐ huài xīn yǎnto harbor a wicked scheme: to act with malicious intentA colloquial set phrase, 使坏 (to scheme) plus 心眼 (mind, intention). Describes someone secretly plotting harm rather than acting openly. Everyday spoken register, not classical.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a mud-brick wall after a week of rain. The 土 on the left is the wall itself, a packed mound of earth still standing. Then read the 不 on the right as the verdict the wall is about to deliver: not anymore. The clay sags, a seam opens, and the whole face slumps into a heap of crumbling soil. 坏 is the sound that collapse makes, the falling fourth tone dropping with the bricks: huài.

Hold that image and the two halves of the meaning come for free. The physical wall coming apart is 坏了, your air conditioner that quit, the leftovers gone sour. The moral version is the same collapse moved indoors: a person whose character has slumped like wet clay is a 坏人, and a child kept in bad company will 学坏, learn to fall apart the same way. Whatever crumbles, in matter or in morals, is 坏.
