字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

公 gōng is a meaning-built compound: 八 (bā, to divide) above 厶 (sī, private, self). The lower element 厶 is the old graph for the private and the enclosing, a closed-off self; the upper 八 is a pair of strokes splaying apart, the picture of splitting or dividing. Read together, the early gloss is famous: to divide away what is private is to arrive at the public. The Shuōwén reads the character exactly this way, with 八 set against 厶 to mean the opposite of the private.

From that one image, dividing the private into the shared, four senses fan out:

1. Public, common, shared. What belongs to everyone rather than to one: 公共 gōnggòng (public), 公园 gōngyuán (park), 公司 gōngsī (company), 办公室 bàngōngshì (office).
2. Fair, impartial, just. Holding to no private side: 公平 gōngpíng (fair), 公正 gōngzhèng (just), where the moral 公 lines up with the upright 正.
3. Official, metric, standard. The publicly agreed measure: 公里 gōnglǐ (kilometer), 公斤 gōngjīn (kilogram), 公历 gōnglì (the common calendar).
4. A duke, an elder male. A title of rank and a term of respect for older men: 公公 gōnggong (grandfather, father-in-law), 老公 lǎogōng (husband, colloquial), and the male of animals, 公鸡 gōngjī (rooster).

The thread through every sense is the move from private to public. The duke held office on behalf of all; the kilometer is a measure all agree on; fairness is judgment with the private self divided away. 公 is the line drawn between what is mine and what is ours, always falling on the side of ours.

公共 gōnggòng Public & Common
公共 gōnggòng public; communal; shared by all
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
公 gōng (public) + 共 gòng (common; together). Belonging to or used by the community at large: 公共汽车 "public bus," 公共场所 "public place." The plain modifier for anything held in common.
请爱护公共设施。
Qǐng àihù gōnggòng shèshī.
Please take care of public facilities.
我每天坐公共汽车上班。
Wǒ měitiān zuò gōnggòng qìchē shàngbān.
I take the public bus to work every day.
公园 gōngyuán park; public garden
N 名词 míngcí
公 gōng (public) + 园 yuán (garden). A garden held in common, open to all, as opposed to a private 花园 huāyuán. One of the first 公 words a beginner meets.
周末我们去公园散步。
Zhōumò wǒmen qù gōngyuán sànbù.
On weekends we go for a walk in the park.
公司 gōngsī company; firm; corporation
N 名词 míngcí
公 gōng (public; common) + 司 sī (to manage; an office). A jointly run enterprise, a body managed in common: 一家公司 "a company." Note the contrast with the homophone 私 sī (private).
他在一家大公司工作。
Tā zài yī jiā dà gōngsī gōngzuò.
He works at a big company.
辨析 biànxī · 公 vs. 私 The whole field of 公 stands against 私 sī (private, personal, selfish), which is in fact the very element 厶 buried inside 公. 公 is what is shared and impartial; 私 is what is one's own and self-serving. The pair runs through ethics, law, and everyday speech: 公事 "public/official business" against 私事 "private matters," 公车 "official car" against 私车 "private car."
公平 gōngpíng Fair & Impartial
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The moral sense of 公 is older and deeper than the modern "public transit" one. To act 公 is to set the private self aside and judge by the common standard, which is why 公 pairs so naturally with 正 zhèng (upright). 公正 gōngzhèng, just and impartial, fuses the two: the public standard (公) and the straight line everyone is measured against (正). The classical ideal of the ruler or the judge is exactly this, to hold to the line without bending it toward kin, friend, or personal gain.

Its opposite here is again 私, the private and the partial. Where 私 means favoring one's own, 公 means favoring no one, which is the same as treating all alike. This is the thread that ties the fairness of a referee, the impartiality of a court, and the selflessness praised in the idiom 大公无私 into one idea: the private divided away leaves only the common good.

公平 gōngpíng fair; even-handed; equitable
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
公 gōng (impartial) + 平 píng (level; even). Level treatment that tilts to no side: 公平竞争 "fair competition," 不公平 "unfair." The everyday word for fairness.
这样对她不公平。
Zhèyàng duì tā bù gōngpíng.
This isn't fair to her.
公正 gōngzhèng just; impartial; fair-minded
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
公 gōng (impartial) + 正 zhèng (upright). The public standard joined to the straight line: justice as judgment held to the same mark for all. Common of judges, verdicts, and arbiters: 公正的判决 "a fair verdict."
我们需要一个公正的裁判。
Wǒmen xūyào yī ge gōngzhèng de cáipàn.
We need an impartial referee.
公开 gōngkāi open; public; to make public
V/ADJ 动词·形容词 dòngcí xíngróngcí
公 gōng (public) + 开 kāi (to open). Out in the open rather than hidden: as an adjective, 公开信 "an open letter"; as a verb, 公开秘密 "to make a secret public." The opposite of 秘密 mìmì (secret).
公司公开了财务报告。
Gōngsī gōngkāi le cáiwù bàogào.
The company made its financial report public.
公里 gōnglǐ Metric Measures & the Elder Male
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Two further senses of 公 sit a little off the public-fairness road. The first is the metric one. When China adopted the international system of measures, 公 became the prefix marking the standard, agreed-upon unit, the public measure as against the old local ones: 公里 gōnglǐ (kilometer), 公斤 gōngjīn (kilogram), 公尺 gōngchǐ (meter). The logic still runs through 公: a unit everyone shares.

The second is the oldest of all, 公 as a noble title and a term of respect for older men. As a rank it named a duke, the highest of the classical noble grades. From that respect it softened into kinship and address: 公公 gōnggong (a grandfather or a husband's father), 老公 lǎogōng (the colloquial word for husband), 外公 wàigōng (maternal grandfather). The same character even marks the male of animals: 公鸡 gōngjī (rooster), 公牛 gōngniú (bull).

公里 gōnglǐ kilometer
N 名词 míngcí
公 gōng (metric; standard) + 里 lǐ (a traditional unit of distance). The standard kilometer, distinguished from the old 里 by the 公 prefix that marks the international measure.
从这里到机场有三十公里。
Cóng zhèlǐ dào jīchǎng yǒu sānshí gōnglǐ.
It's thirty kilometers from here to the airport.
公斤 gōngjīn kilogram
N 名词 míngcí
公 gōng (metric) + 斤 jīn (a traditional unit of weight, about half a kilo). The standard kilogram. The 公 again marks the international unit against the older 斤.
这个西瓜有五公斤重。
Zhège xīguā yǒu wǔ gōngjīn zhòng.
This watermelon weighs five kilograms.
老公 lǎogōng husband (colloquial)
N 名词 míngcí
老 lǎo (old; familiar prefix) + 公 gōng (elder male). The warm, everyday word a woman uses for her husband, paired with 老婆 lǎopó (wife). More casual than 丈夫 zhàngfu.
我老公在做饭呢。
Wǒ lǎogōng zài zuò fàn ne.
My husband is cooking right now.
辨析 biànxī · the male 公 The "elder male" 公 spreads across kinship and the barnyard alike. 公公 gōnggong is a grandfather or a husband's father; 外公 wàigōng is a maternal grandfather; 公鸡 gōngjī is a rooster and 公牛 gōngniú a bull. Its mate in the animal sense is 母 mǔ (female): 母鸡 mǔjī is a hen.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
大公无私 dà gōng wú sī utterly fair and selfless: putting the common good above all private interest Lit: great-public-without-private. The highest praise for an official or leader who holds to the public good and lets no personal interest sway the judgment. 公 and 私 stand at the two poles of the phrase.
公事公办 gōng shì gōng bàn to handle official business by the book: no favors, no shortcuts Lit: public-matter-public-handle. To deal with official affairs strictly on their merits, without bending the rules for friendship or private gain. Often said with a touch of formality or coolness.
公而忘私 gōng ér wàng sī so devoted to the common good as to forget oneself Lit: public-and-forget-private. Praise for one who throws themselves into public duty to the point of neglecting personal interest. A close cousin of 大公无私.
愚公移山 yú gōng yí shān the foolish old man moves the mountains: dogged perseverance moves any obstacle Lit: foolish-old-man-move-mountain. From the fable of the old man who set out to dig away two mountains blocking his door, vowing his sons and grandsons would finish the work. Here 公 is the respectful "old man," and the phrase celebrates relentless resolve.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

See the two strokes of 八 at the top splaying apart, a pair of hands prying something open, and beneath them 厶, the closed-off private self. The whole character is that act: dividing the private away to leave the common. Hold that one picture and the senses follow.

Split the private from a garden and it becomes a park (公园); from a business, a company (公司); from a judgment, fairness (公平, 公正); from a measure, the shared unit everyone agrees on (公里, 公斤). Even the duke and the grandfather are the elder who stands for the family or the realm rather than himself. Whenever you meet 公, look for the line between mine and ours, and you will find it falling on the side of ours.
