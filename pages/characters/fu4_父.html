字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

父 is an ideograph: a hand gripping a stone axe or staff, the working tool of the one who heads the household. The oracle-bone form draws a raised hand closed around a vertical implement, the slanting strokes above it the blade or the haft. The father is identified by what he holds, the tool that clears the field, splits the wood, and enforces the household order.

The Shuōwén Jiězì reads 父 as 矩也，家长率教者，从又举杖: a measure or rule, the head of the family who leads and instructs, drawn from 又 (a hand) raising a staff. The raised staff carries both senses at once, the labor of the provider and the authority of the disciplinarian. By the seal script the gripping hand had flattened into the four crossing strokes of the modern glyph, but the closed-fist shape still reads in the two inner strokes.

父 became its own radical and sits as the top element in the everyday words for father: 爸 bà (the colloquial 父 over the phonetic 巴), 爷 yé (grandfather), and 爹 diē (dad, dialectal and warm). Each one stacks the axe-holding figure above a sound component, so the father stands literally at the head of his own characters.

The character splits a tone in older usage. Read fù it means father and male elder; read fǔ it was an honorific suffix for a respected man, as in 渔父 yúfǔ, the old fisherman of the Chuci. Modern Mandarin keeps fù as the living reading.

亲属称谓 qīnshǔ chēngwèi Father & the Core Family
父亲 fùqīn father (formal)
N 名词 míngcí
父 fù (father) + 亲 qīn (kin, close relative). The formal, written word for father, the one used on documents, in introductions, and in respectful speech. In daily conversation people say 爸爸 bàba instead; 父亲 carries weight and distance.
我的父亲是一名医生。
Wǒ de fùqīn shì yī míng yīshēng.
My father is a doctor.
辨析 biànxī · 父亲 vs. 爸爸 父亲 is formal and used in writing or polite report; 爸爸 is what you actually call him at home.
父母 fùmǔ parents (father and mother)
N 名词 míngcí
父 fù (father) + 母 mǔ (mother). The standard word for parents as a pair. Father comes first by convention, the same ordering that runs through the whole kinship vocabulary.
他每个周末都去看父母。
Tā měi gè zhōumò dōu qù kàn fùmǔ.
He visits his parents every weekend.
父子 fùzǐ father and son
N 名词 míngcí
父 fù (father) + 子 zǐ (son). The father-son pair, one of the five cardinal relationships (五伦 wǔlún) of Confucian ethics. The bond is described by the phrase 父慈子孝, the father kind, the son filial.
他们父子俩长得很像。
Tāmen fùzǐ liǎ zhǎng de hěn xiàng.
The father and son look very much alike.
长辈与尊称 zhǎngbèi yǔ zūnchēng The Senior Line & Respectful Titles
文化洞见 wénhuà dòngjiàn · Cultural Insight

父 reaches past the household to mark the whole parent-and-above generation. 祖父 zǔfù is the paternal grandfather, 父辈 fùbèi the fathers' generation as a class, 师父 shīfu the master who teaches a craft as a father teaches a son. In each the axe-holder stands for seniority and the duty of instruction, the same 家长率教者 that the Shuōwén named.

The word also climbs to the figurative. 国父 guófù, father of the nation, is the title given to a founding statesman; 父老 fùlǎo, the fathers and elders, addresses the senior men of a place as a body. Calling a man 父 is the strongest claim of respect the kinship system can make outside one's own blood.

祖父 zǔfù paternal grandfather (formal)
N 名词 míngcí
祖 zǔ (ancestor, forebear) + 父 fù (father). The father's father, in formal register. Spoken Mandarin uses 爷爷 yéye for the same man, the way 爸爸 stands in for 父亲.
我的祖父年轻时是一位老师。
Wǒ de zǔfù niánqīng shí shì yī wèi lǎoshī.
My grandfather was a teacher when he was young.
辨析 biànxī · 祖父 vs. 外祖父 祖父 is the paternal grandfather; the maternal grandfather is 外祖父 wàizǔfù, with 外 (outside) marking the mother's line.
师父 shīfu master; teacher of a craft
N 名词 míngcí
师 shī (teacher) + 父 fù (father). A master who trains an apprentice in a trade or martial art, addressed with the warmth and deference owed a father. The near-homophone 师傅 shīfu is the everyday term for a skilled worker or driver; 师父 keeps the closer master-disciple bond.
他跟着师父学了十年武术。
Tā gēnzhe shīfu xué le shí nián wǔshù.
He studied martial arts under his master for ten years.
父辈 fùbèi the fathers' generation
N 名词 míngcí
父 fù (father) + 辈 bèi (generation, cohort). The fathers' generation taken as a class, everyone of the parents' age and above. Set against 子辈 zǐbèi, the children's generation, when speaking of how a family changes across the line.
这些老照片记录了父辈的青春。
Zhèxiē lǎo zhàopiàn jìlù le fùbèi de qīngchūn.
These old photographs record the youth of our parents' generation.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
父慈子孝 fù cí zǐ xiào the father kind, the son filial: the reciprocal duty between generations A Confucian formula for the father-son relationship, paired in the classics with 兄友弟恭 (elder brother caring, younger respectful). Authority and obedience are framed as mutual obligations, not one-way command.
父债子还 fù zhài zǐ huán the father's debt, the son repays: a debt or wrong passes down the line Once a literal expectation in family law, now used figuratively for any burden inherited from a previous generation. Carries a tone of grim inevitability.
认贼作父 rèn zéi zuò fù to take a thief for a father: to serve one's enemy as a benefactor A harsh condemnation, aimed at a traitor or collaborator who submits to the very people who wronged him. The image inverts the sacred father-son bond into betrayal.
子承父业 zǐ chéng fù yè the son carries on the father's trade: inheriting the family calling Said when a child takes up the parent's profession or business, from a craftsman's shop to a family firm. Neutral to approving, depending on whether the inheritance is seen as continuity or constraint.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

See a weathered hand closing around the worn haft of a stone axe, knuckles raised, the blade catching the light. That fist is 父. The father is the one who holds the tool, the hand that splits the firewood and rules the household by the same grip.

The four strokes carry it: the two slanting marks above are the lifted blade, the crossed strokes below the fist that holds it. Once you see the hand and the axe, the modern shape stops being abstract and becomes a gesture frozen mid-swing.

Now stack the figure upward. Put it over 巴 and you get 爸 bà, dad. Put it over the grandfather's word and you get 爷 yé. Put it beside the master's 师 and you get 师父. The axe-holder rises to the top of every word for the men who lead and teach, exactly where the Shuōwén placed him: 家长率教者, the head of the family who leads and instructs.
