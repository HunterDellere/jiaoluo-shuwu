字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

系 xì is a hand holding silk threads. The oracle-bone and bronze forms show a hand grasping one or more strands of silk, the bundled threads hanging below the fingers. The modern shape preserves both halves: the short stroke at the top is the remnant of the gripping hand (爪, a clawing hand from above), and below it sits 糸 (mì), the radical that pictures a skein of twisted silk. As a structure the character reads 系 = 丿 (the hand) over 糸 (silk), one hand keeping many threads gathered as one.

To hold threads together is to connect them, and from that single image the character fans out into the connecting vocabulary of modern Chinese:

1. Connection, relation between things. The tie itself: 关系 guānxi (relationship; connection), 联系 liánxì (to link; to contact), 系列 xìliè (a series, things strung in a row).
2. System, an ordered whole. Many parts bound into one structure: 系统 xìtǒng (system), 体系 tǐxì (a structured system; framework), 太阳系 tàiyángxì (the solar system).
3. Department, a faculty of a university. A branch of study held together as a unit: 中文系 zhōngwénxì (the Chinese department), 系主任 xìzhǔrèn (department head).
4. To tie, to fasten (read jì). In its everyday verb sense the character is read jì: 系鞋带 jì xiédài (to tie one's shoelaces), 系扣子 jì kòuzi (to do up a button), 系安全带 jì ānquándài (to fasten a seatbelt).

The thread that runs through every sense is the original thread itself: the hand that holds strands together. Bound abstractly, it is a relationship or a system; bound by a branch of learning, it is a department; bound literally with a cord, it is the knot you tie. The two readings split the labor: xì carries the abstract senses of connecting and system, while jì carries the concrete, physical act of tying.

关系 guānxi Relationship & Connection
关系 guānxi relationship; connection; bearing on something
N 名词 míngcí
关 guān (to close; a barrier) + 系 xì (tie). The tie between two parties, whether people, things, or ideas. In social life 关系 also names the web of personal connections that smooths the way through favors and trust, a force of real weight in Chinese society.
他们的关系很好。
Tāmen de guānxi hěn hǎo.
They have a very good relationship.
这件事和我没有关系。
Zhè jiàn shì hé wǒ méiyǒu guānxi.
This matter has nothing to do with me.
联系 liánxì to contact; to get in touch; to link; connection
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
联 lián (to join; to unite) + 系 xì (tie). Both the act of making contact and the link itself. As a verb: 联系我 "get in touch with me." As a noun: 保持联系 "keep in contact."
有问题请联系我。
Yǒu wèntí qǐng liánxì wǒ.
If you have any questions, please contact me.
辨析 biànxī · 关系 vs. 联系 关系 is the standing tie that already exists between parties, a noun you describe as good, close, or tense. 联系 is the active link you make or keep, and it works as a verb (to contact someone) as well as a noun (the contact itself). You have a 关系; you make or maintain a 联系.
系列 xìliè series; set (of related things)
N 名词 míngcí
系 xì (string together) + 列 liè (row; to arrange). A run of related items strung in a row: 系列产品 "a product series," 一系列问题 "a whole series of problems." The connecting 系 makes the separate items read as one ordered set.
这是一系列的电影。
Zhè shì yī xìliè de diànyǐng.
This is a series of films.
系统 xìtǒng System & Department
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

When 系 names not a single tie but a whole structure of ties, it becomes the word for a system. 系统 xìtǒng (系 connection + 统 tǒng, to unify or govern) is the modern catch-all for any ordered whole whose parts work together: a computer 系统, a transit 系统, the digestive 系统, an economic 系统. The same 系 gives 体系 tǐxì (a structured framework, as in 教育体系, the education system) and the astronomer's 太阳系 tàiyángxì, the solar system, the sun and its planets bound by gravity into one family.

From "a bound whole" the character also took on the meaning of a university department. A 系 is the unit that holds a single field of study together: 中文系 zhōngwénxì (the Chinese department), 历史系 lìshǐxì (the history department), 物理系 wùlǐxì (the physics department). The person who heads it is the 系主任 xìzhǔrèn. Here 系 stands alone as a noun, the smallest academic home where everyone shares one subject.

系统 xìtǒng system; systematic
N 名词 míngcí
系 xì (connection) + 统 tǒng (to unify). An ordered whole of interworking parts: 操作系统 "operating system," 神经系统 "nervous system." As an adjective it means systematic: 系统地学习 "to study systematically."
这个系统很稳定。
Zhège xìtǒng hěn wěndìng.
This system is very stable.
体系 tǐxì framework; structured system (of ideas or institutions)
N 名词 míngcí
体 tǐ (body; structure) + 系 xì (connection). An organized framework, usually of something abstract: 理论体系 "a theoretical framework," 教育体系 "the education system." More architectural and conceptual than the everyday 系统.
他建立了完整的理论体系。
Tā jiànlì le wánzhěng de lǐlùn tǐxì.
He built a complete theoretical framework.
中文系 zhōngwénxì department of Chinese (at a university)
N 名词 míngcí
中文 zhōngwén (Chinese language) + 系 xì (department). The Chinese department of a university. The bare 系 is the academic department itself, so any field plugs in front of it: 数学系 math department, 法律系 law department.
她在北京大学中文系学习。
Tā zài Běijīng Dàxué zhōngwénxì xuéxí.
She studies in the Chinese department at Peking University.
联系 liánxì To Link & To Tie: the Verb 系 and the jì Reading
读音洞见 dúyīn dòngjiàn · Pronunciation Insight

系 is a polyphone, and the two readings divide cleanly along the abstract-versus-physical line. Read xì, it carries every connecting and system sense covered above: 关系, 系统, 中文系, and the bookish verb "to link" in 联系. Read jì, it shrinks to one concrete job: to tie or fasten something with a cord, strap, or knot. 系鞋带 jì xiédài is to tie your shoelaces, 系扣子 jì kòuzi is to do up a button, 系安全带 jì ānquándài is to buckle a seatbelt.

A useful rule of thumb: if you could mime the action with your hands, knotting, buckling, fastening, it is jì. If the connection is invisible, a relationship, a system, a department, it is xì. The single glyph keeps the silk-holding hand for both, but only the jì reading still ties an actual knot.

系 jì to tie; to fasten; to do up
V 动词 dòngcí
The physical verb sense, read jì rather than xì. To bind something with a cord or strap: 系鞋带 "tie shoelaces," 系安全带 "fasten the seatbelt." Same glyph, different reading, fully concrete.
请系好安全带。
Qǐng jì hǎo ānquándài.
Please fasten your seatbelt.
他蹲下来系鞋带。
Tā dūn xiàlái jì xiédài.
He crouched down to tie his shoelaces.
维系 wéixì to hold together; to maintain (a bond)
V 动词 dòngcí
维 wéi (to tie; to hold) + 系 xì (bind). To keep a relationship or order from coming apart: 维系感情 "to keep a bond alive," 维系和平 "to maintain peace." Two binding words doubled, the threads kept firmly in hand.
是共同的爱好维系着他们的友谊。
Shì gòngtóng de àihào wéixì zhe tāmen de yǒuyì.
It is their shared hobbies that hold their friendship together.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
一脉相承 yī mài xiāng chéng descended from one bloodline: an unbroken line of inheritance Lit: one-vein-mutually-inherit. Said of ideas, schools, or traditions that pass down through a single connected lineage with nothing broken in between. The image is of a single connecting thread, the same 系 logic of strands held together across generations.
休戚相关 xiū qī xiāng guān sharing joys and sorrows: bound together by a common fate Lit: joy-sorrow-mutually-connected. Two parties so closely tied that whatever befalls one befalls the other, the deep 关系 in which fortunes rise and fall as one.
千丝万缕 qiān sī wàn lǚ a thousand threads and ten thousand strands: countless intricate connections Lit: thousand-silk-ten-thousand-threads. Used of relationships or matters knit together by innumerable fine ties: 千丝万缕的联系 "connections beyond counting." The literal silk threads behind every abstract 系.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

See a hand at the top of 系 gathering a hank of silk, 糸, into its fingers: one hand, many threads, all held as one. That gesture of holding strands together is the whole character. Every meaning is something bound.

Bind people and you get a relationship (关系). Bind parts into an ordered whole and you get a system (系统). Bind a field of study into one academic home and you get a department (中文系). And bind a cord into an actual knot, and the reading shifts to jì: you 系鞋带, tie your shoelaces. When you meet 系, ask what is being held together, and then ask whether you could tie it with your hands. If yes, it is jì; if the tie is invisible, it is xì.
