字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure: Pattern Strokes
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

形 is a pictophonetic compound: the right-hand element 彡 shān carries the meaning, and the left-hand element 开 supplies the sound. The radical 彡 is the cluster of three drawn-in strokes that runs through characters about visible markings and texture: 彩 cǎi (color), 彩饰 (ornament), 影 yǐng (shadow), 须 xū (whiskers). Those three slanting strokes stand for lines, sheen, hair, anything that gives a surface its visible pattern. So the meaning side of 形 says, in effect: this is a character about what something looks like.

The Shuōwén Jiězì 说文解字 glosses 形 as 象形也 in the broad sense of an outward image, the look that a thing presents to the eye. From that root the meaning fans out cleanly. First the literal sense: the shape or outline of a thing, what you would trace if you drew its edge. Then the bodily sense: 形 as the physical body, the figure, the material form a person or thing has. Then a verbal turn: to take shape, to appear, to become visible, and from there to describe, to put a thing's look into words. Finally the abstract sense: the form or configuration of a situation, the shape that circumstances have taken.

One image holds all of these together. 彡 is the visible pattern; 形 is the moment a thing presents that pattern to the eye, whether the thing is a triangle, a human body, a forming cloud, or the lay of a battlefield. When you meet 形 in a new compound, ask what is being made visible, and the sense usually falls out.

辨析 biànxī Form vs the Lay of Things: 形式 vs 形势
辨析 biànxī · One syllable apart 形式 xíngshì (form 式 = pattern, format): the outward form or format of something, as opposed to its content. 内容 nèiróng (content) is its natural opposite. 形式主义 = formalism, going through the motions.
形势 xíngshì (势 = momentum, power): the situation, the configuration of forces, the way circumstances are shaped at a given moment. 国际形势 = the international situation; 形势严峻 = the situation is grim.
Same pinyin, same tones, one character apart. 形式 is about the shape of a thing (form vs. content); 形势 is about the shape of events (the lay of the land). Hear 势 "momentum" and you are in the world of unfolding circumstances.
速记 sùjì · Quick test Is it form against content? → 形式 xíngshì. (改变形式, change the format; 形式上 = nominally, in form only.)
Is it how the situation stands? → 形势 xíngshì. (形势大好 = the situation is excellent; 看清形势 = read the situation clearly.)
形状 xíngzhuàng Shape, Figure & Format
形状 xíngzhuàng shape: the outward form of a thing
N 名词 míngcí
形 xíng + 状 zhuàng (state; appearance). The everyday word for the shape of a physical object: what outline it has, what it looks like. 奇怪的形状 = a strange shape; 各种形状 = all kinds of shapes. This is the first 形-word a learner meets.
这块石头的形状很特别。
Zhè kuài shítou de xíngzhuàng hěn tèbié.
The shape of this stone is very unusual.
图形 túxíng figure; graphic; geometric shape
N 名词 míngcí
图 tú (drawing; diagram) + 形 xíng. A drawn figure or graphic: shapes on a page, icons on a screen, the figures of geometry. 几何图形 = geometric figures; 图形界面 = a graphical interface. Where 形状 is a thing's shape, 图形 is a shape that has been drawn as such.
屏幕上出现了一个红色的图形。
Píngmù shàng chūxiàn le yí ge hóngsè de túxíng.
A red figure appeared on the screen.
三角形 sānjiǎoxíng triangle
N 名词 míngcí
三 sān (three) + 角 jiǎo (corner; angle) + 形 xíng. The three-corner shape. 形 is the standard suffix that turns a description into the name of a geometric shape: 正方形 zhèngfāngxíng (square), 圆形 yuánxíng (circle), 长方形 chángfāngxíng (rectangle). Learn the pattern once and the whole vocabulary of shapes opens up.
三角形有三条边。
Sānjiǎoxíng yǒu sān tiáo biān.
A triangle has three sides.
形式 xíngshì form; format: the outward shape, as opposed to content
N 名词 míngcí
形 xíng + 式 shì (pattern; format). The form or format of something, set against its 内容 nèiróng (content). 各种形式 = various forms; 形式上 = in form only, nominally. 形式主义 = formalism, prizing appearance over substance. Do not confuse with 形势 (the situation): see the 辨析 section above.
内容比形式更重要。
Nèiróng bǐ xíngshì gèng zhòngyào.
Content matters more than form.
形体 xíngtǐ physical form; body; bodily figure
N 名词 míngcí
形 xíng + 体 tǐ (body). The body as form: the physical figure of a person, often in contexts of posture, dance, and bearing. 形体训练 = physical/figure training; 形体优美 = a graceful figure. This is the bodily branch of 形, where shape means the shape of a living body.
舞蹈演员的形体非常优美。
Wǔdǎo yǎnyuán de xíngtǐ fēicháng yōuměi.
The dancer's figure is extremely graceful.
形成 xíngchéng To Take Shape & To Describe
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

When 形 turns verbal, two senses dominate. The first is to take shape: 形成 xíngchéng, the standard verb for something coming into existence by a gradual process, a habit forming, a system forming, clouds forming. The second is to describe: 形容 xíngróng, literally to give a thing a face and a figure in words. To describe something is, in this metaphor, to draw its form so that a listener can see it. The two senses share one logic: 形 is the moment a shape becomes visible, whether to the eye (it takes shape) or to the imagination (you describe it into view).

形成 xíngchéng to form; to take shape; to come into being
V 动词 dòngcí
形 xíng + 成 chéng (to become; to complete). To shape-into-being. The all-purpose verb for something forming through a process: 形成习惯 form a habit; 形成共识 reach (form) a consensus; 逐渐形成 gradually take shape. Used constantly in writing about how systems, opinions, and conditions arise.
这个习惯是慢慢形成的。
Zhège xíguàn shì mànman xíngchéng de.
This habit formed gradually.
形容 xíngróng to describe; to characterize in words
V 动词 dòngcí
形 xíng + 容 róng (appearance; countenance). To give a thing its look in words. The verb for describing, especially feelings and impressions that resist plain statement. 难以形容 = hard to describe; 无法形容的美 = indescribable beauty. The grammar term 形容词 xíngróngcí (adjective) is literally a "describing word."
那种感觉很难用语言形容。
Nà zhǒng gǎnjué hěn nán yòng yǔyán xíngróng.
That feeling is hard to describe in words.
原形 yuánxíng original form; true shape (often the one revealed)
N 名词 míngcí
原 yuán (original) + 形 xíng. The original form a thing had before it was disguised or altered. The vivid idiom 原形毕露 yuánxíng bìlù means "the true shape is fully exposed," said when a deception finally collapses and someone is seen for what they really are.
谎言被拆穿后，他露出了原形。
Huǎngyán bèi chāichuān hòu, tā lòuchū le yuánxíng.
Once the lie was exposed, his true colors showed.
情形 qíngxíng Situation, Circumstance & Terrain
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The most abstract branch of 形 treats circumstances as something with a shape. A situation has a 形: a configuration you can read, like the outline of a landscape. 情形 qíngxíng is the everyday "situation" or "state of affairs." 形势 xíngshì raises the stakes: the lay of forces, the momentum of events, the strategic picture. And 地形 dìxíng brings the metaphor back to literal ground: the shape of the land, terrain, the contour map a general or a hiker reads. All three say the same thing in different registers: to understand a situation is to see its form.

情形 qíngxíng situation; circumstances; state of affairs
N 名词 míngcí
情 qíng (circumstance; feeling) + 形 xíng. The plain, everyday word for a situation or set of circumstances. 实际情形 = the actual situation; 这种情形 = this kind of case. Close to 情况 qíngkuàng and often interchangeable, with 情形 leaning slightly more descriptive.
当时的情形我记得很清楚。
Dāngshí de qíngxíng wǒ jìde hěn qīngchu.
I remember the situation at the time very clearly.
形势 xíngshì the situation; the lay of forces; circumstances
N 名词 míngcí
形 xíng + 势 shì (momentum; force). The configuration of forces at a given moment, weightier than 情形. Used for politics, war, economy, and any contest of momentum: 国际形势 the international situation; 形势严峻 the situation is grim; 看清形势 read the situation clearly. Do not confuse with 形式 (form vs. content).
目前的经济形势比较复杂。
Mùqián de jīngjì xíngshì bǐjiào fùzá.
The current economic situation is rather complicated.
地形 dìxíng terrain; topography; the shape of the land
N 名词 míngcí
地 dì (ground; land) + 形 xíng. The shape of the land: terrain, topography, the rise and fall of the ground. 地形复杂 = the terrain is complex; 地形图 = a topographic map. The most literal of the situation-words, where the shape being read is the physical landscape itself.
这一带的地形很复杂。
Zhè yídài de dìxíng hěn fùzá.
The terrain in this area is very complex.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
形影不离 xíng yǐng bù lí form and shadow never part: inseparable Lit: form and shadow do not separate. A shadow follows its form everywhere; two people who are 形影不离 are constant companions, always together. Warm and affectionate, used for close friends, devoted couples, or a child who will not leave a parent's side.
形形色色 xíng xíng sè sè all shapes and colors: of every kind, all sorts Lit: shape-shape, color-color. The reduplication piles up variety: people, goods, schemes, opinions of every imaginable kind. 形形色色的人 = all sorts of people. Often carries a faint edge of "a mixed bag," not all of it admirable.
形单影只 xíng dān yǐng zhī a lone form, a single shadow: solitary and without companions Lit: the form is alone, the shadow is single. The mirror image of 形影不离: here there is only one form and its one shadow, no one alongside. A literary, somewhat melancholy phrase for a person who is utterly on their own.
喜形于色 xǐ xíng yú sè joy takes shape in the face: visibly delighted Lit: joy 喜 takes form 形 upon 于 the complexion 色. Happiness that cannot be hidden and shows plainly on the face. Here 形 is verbal, "to take shape, to appear," a clear window onto the verb sense of the character.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Read the three strokes on the right, 彡, as the visible markings on a surface: lines, sheen, the strokes of a brush. 形 is the moment a thing shows those markings, the moment a shape becomes visible to the eye. Hold that one image and the whole vocabulary unfolds: a thing's outline (形状, 图形, 三角形), a body's figure (形体), the act of taking shape or being put into words (形成, 形容), and the shape that circumstances have taken (情形, 形势, 地形).

The single trap to lock in: 形式 xíngshì is form against content, while 形势 xíngshì is the lay of the situation. Same sound, one character apart. When you hear 势 "momentum," you are reading events, not formats.
