字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

望 wàng began as a vivid picture of looking far. The early graph shows a person standing tall on a mound, with a large staring eye lifted to take in the distance; later forms added 月 (the moon), the very thing one strains upward to see across the night sky. The phonetic element 亡 (wáng) lends the sound. In the modern eleven-stroke form the 月 (moon) sits at the lower left and 王 (king) at the lower right, with 亡 riding on top, the components plainly visible. The simplified and traditional forms are identical here; only one glyph exists.

From that one image of a raised, far-reaching gaze, three senses grow, moving from the eye outward to the heart and finally to the eyes of others:

1. To gaze, to look into the distance. The literal act of looking far: 眺望 tiàowàng (to gaze into the distance), 张望 zhāngwàng (to look around), 望远镜 wàngyuǎnjìng (telescope, literally a glass for looking far).
2. To hope, to expect. The inner gaze toward what has not yet come: 希望 xīwàng (to hope), 期望 qīwàng (to expect), 盼望 pànwàng (to look forward to), 渴望 kěwàng (to crave), 失望 shīwàng (to be disappointed, literally to lose the hope).
3. Reputation, prestige. Being looked up to by others: 名望 míngwàng (fame, standing), 威望 wēiwàng (prestige), 声望 shēngwàng (reputation).

A fourth, grammatical use survives as a coverb meaning toward, much like 朝 cháo or 向 xiàng: 望前看 wàng qián kàn (to look toward the front, to look ahead). The thread through all of them is the lifted, directed gaze: the eye looks toward the horizon, the heart looks toward what it longs for, and the world looks toward the person it esteems.

眺望 tiàowàng To Gaze & Look Afar
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The most concrete face of 望 is the literal act of looking into the distance, the gaze that strains beyond what is near at hand. 眺望 tiàowàng is the elevated, deliberate version, gazing out from a high place over a wide view. 张望 zhāngwàng is restless and searching, the eyes darting this way and that to find something. And 望远镜 wàngyuǎnjìng, the telescope, freezes the idea in an object: a tool whose whole purpose is to make the far near. All three keep the original picture of a person on a mound, eye lifted toward the horizon.

眺望 tiàowàng to gaze into the distance; to look out over
V 动词 dòngcí
眺 tiào (to look afar) + 望 wàng (to gaze). To stand at a height and let the eye travel over a wide, distant view: 登高眺望 "to climb high and gaze out." More elevated and literary than ordinary 看, it implies an open vista and a lifted vantage point.
他站在山顶眺望远方。
Tā zhàn zài shāndǐng tiàowàng yuǎnfāng.
He stood on the mountaintop and gazed into the distance.
从这座楼可以眺望整个城市。
Cóng zhè zuò lóu kěyǐ tiàowàng zhěnggè chéngshì.
From this building you can look out over the whole city.
张望 zhāngwàng to look around; to peer about
V 动词 dòngcí
张 zhāng (to open, to spread) + 望 wàng (to look). To look around searchingly, the eyes opened wide and turning in every direction: 四处张望 "to look all around." It carries a note of uncertainty or expectation, as of someone scanning a crowd for a face.
他在门口四处张望，好像在等人。
Tā zài ménkǒu sìchù zhāngwàng, hǎoxiàng zài děng rén.
He looked around at the doorway, as if waiting for someone.
小孩好奇地向窗外张望。
Xiǎohái hàoqí de xiàng chuāngwài zhāngwàng.
The child peered out the window with curiosity.
望远镜 wàngyuǎnjìng telescope; binoculars
N 名词 míngcí
望 wàng (to look far) + 远 yuǎn (far) + 镜 jìng (lens, mirror). Literally "a lens for looking far": the instrument that brings the distant near. 天文望远镜 is an astronomical telescope; 双筒望远镜 is a pair of binoculars. A neat transparent compound that reads itself.
他用望远镜观察天上的星星。
Tā yòng wàngyuǎnjìng guānchá tiānshàng de xīngxing.
He used a telescope to observe the stars in the sky.
辨析 biànxī · 一望无际 The literal gaze also lives in the set phrase 一望无际 yī wàng wú jì, "a single glance, no boundary," used of a view that stretches unbroken to the horizon: 一望无际的草原, a grassland that runs as far as the eye can see. The whole image rests on 望 as the far-reaching look.
希望 xīwàng To Hope & Expect
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Turn the gaze inward and 望 becomes the look of the heart toward what has not yet come. 希望 xīwàng is the broad, hopeful version, both the verb to hope and the noun hope itself. 期望 qīwàng is firmer, an expectation tied to a set time or a definite outcome, often what one person expects of another. 盼望 pànwàng is warm and longing, a yearning fixed on something one waits eagerly for. 渴望 kěwàng is hotter still, a thirsting craving. And when the awaited thing fails to arrive, the gaze collapses into 失望 shīwàng, literally to lose the hope: disappointment.

希望 xīwàng to hope; hope (the most general word)
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
希 xī (to wish for) + 望 wàng (to look toward). The everyday word for hoping, as a verb and as a noun: 我希望你早点回来 "I hope you come back soon," 充满希望 "full of hope." Broad and neutral, the default way to express a wish for some future good.
我希望明天天气好。
Wǒ xīwàng míngtiān tiānqì hǎo.
I hope the weather is good tomorrow.
孩子是一个家庭的希望。
Háizi shì yí ge jiātíng de xīwàng.
Children are the hope of a family.
辨析 biànxī · 希望 vs. 期望 希望 xīwàng is a plain wish for some future good, light and very common: I hope it does not rain. 期望 qīwàng is heavier and more demanding, an expectation placed on a person or outcome, often with a standard attached: parents 期望 a great deal of their children. You can 希望 for almost anything, but you 期望 when there is a real claim or anticipation behind the wish.
期望 qīwàng to expect; expectation; to set hopes on
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
期 qī (a set time, to anticipate) + 望 wàng (to look toward). To expect something of someone or some outcome, with a definite hope and often a standard behind it: 不要辜负大家的期望 "do not let down everyone's expectations." Weightier and more formal than 希望.
父母对他的期望很高。
Fùmǔ duì tā de qīwàng hěn gāo.
His parents have very high expectations of him.
盼望 pànwàng to look forward to; to long for eagerly
V 动词 dòngcí
盼 pàn (to long for, to expect) + 望 wàng (to look toward). To wait eagerly and warmly for something one cares about: 盼望已久 "long looked forward to." More emotional and yearning than 希望, it suggests an active, heartfelt waiting.
孩子们盼望着过年。
Háizimen pànwàng zhe guònián.
The children are looking forward to the New Year.
失望 shīwàng to be disappointed; disappointment
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
失 shī (to lose) + 望 wàng (hope). Literally "to lose the hope": the feeling when what one looked forward to does not come. 对他很失望 "to be very disappointed in him," 大失所望 "greatly let down." The exact emotional shadow of 希望.
考试没考好，他很失望。
Kǎoshì méi kǎo hǎo, tā hěn shīwàng.
He did poorly on the exam and was very disappointed.
请不要让我失望。
Qǐng bú yào ràng wǒ shīwàng.
Please do not let me down.
名望 míngwàng Reputation & Prestige
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The third sense turns the gaze around: now it is others who look toward the person, with respect. 望 here means the standing one holds in the eyes of the world. 名望 míngwàng pairs it with 名 (name) for fame and good repute. 威望 wēiwàng pairs it with 威 (authority, awe) for the prestige that commands deference, the standing of a leader or an elder. 声望 shēngwàng pairs it with 声 (voice, reputation) for general public esteem. All three share the picture of many eyes lifted toward one esteemed figure, the original gaze inverted.

名望 míngwàng fame; good standing; reputation
N 名词 míngcí
名 míng (name) + 望 wàng (esteem). The fame and good repute a person enjoys: 很有名望 "to be of high standing." It blends being widely known with being respected, the regard attached to a name.
他在医学界很有名望。
Tā zài yīxuéjiè hěn yǒu míngwàng.
He has a great reputation in the medical field.
威望 wēiwàng prestige; authority and esteem
N 名词 míngcí
威 wēi (authority, awe) + 望 wàng (esteem). The prestige that commands respect and deference, the standing of someone in a position of leadership: 树立威望 "to establish one's prestige." Stronger than plain reputation, it carries a sense of earned authority.
这位老教授在学生中很有威望。
Zhè wèi lǎo jiàoshòu zài xuéshēng zhōng hěn yǒu wēiwàng.
This old professor enjoys great prestige among the students.
辨析 biànxī · 名望 vs. 威望 名望 míngwàng is fame and good repute, being widely known and well thought of. 威望 wēiwàng adds authority: it is the prestige that makes people defer and obey, the standing of a leader or an elder. A celebrated artist has 名望; a respected commander has 威望.
声望 shēngwàng reputation; public esteem; prestige
N 名词 míngcí
声 shēng (voice, repute) + 望 wàng (esteem). The general public regard a person or institution enjoys: 声望很高 "to be held in high esteem." Close to 名望, it leans toward the public, reputational dimension, what people say and think of someone.
这所大学在国际上声望很高。
Zhè suǒ dàxué zài guójì shàng shēngwàng hěn gāo.
This university enjoys a high reputation internationally.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
望子成龙 wàng zǐ chéng lóng to hope ones son becomes a dragon: to long for ones child to succeed greatly Literally "to hope the son becomes a dragon," the dragon being the emblem of supreme success and distinction. The classic expression of parental ambition: the deep hope that ones child will rise high in life. A close partner phrase, 望女成凤, hopes the daughter becomes a phoenix.
大失所望 dà shī suǒ wàng to be greatly let down: bitterly disappointed in what one had hoped for Literally "greatly to lose what one hoped for." Used when reality falls far short of expectation and the disappointment is severe, as when an eagerly awaited result turns out badly. The intensified form of plain 失望.
望尘莫及 wàng chén mò jí to see only the dust and never catch up: to be hopelessly outdistanced Literally "to gaze at the dust and be unable to reach." One can see only the dust kicked up by someone far ahead, with no hope of catching them. Used to admit that another is so far superior or so far in front that one cannot hope to match them.
望梅止渴 wàng méi zhǐ kě to quench thirst by thinking of plums: to comfort oneself with illusions Literally "to look toward plums to stop ones thirst." From a tale of the warlord Cao Cao, who spurred on his parched troops by telling them a plum grove lay just ahead, so the thought of the sour fruit set their mouths watering. Now it means consoling oneself, or others, with hopes that cannot really be fulfilled.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Hold one picture at the center of 望: a figure standing on a mound, eye lifted, gazing toward the moon and the far horizon. That single raised gaze is the seed of every sense.

Point the gaze at the distance and you get the literal look: 眺望 to gaze afar, 张望 to look around, 望远镜 the telescope that makes the far near, 一望无际 a view that runs to the edge of sight. Turn the gaze inward, toward what the heart longs for, and you get hope: 希望 to hope, 期望 to expect, 盼望 to long for, 渴望 to crave, and its shadow 失望, to lose the hope. Turn the gaze around so that others look toward you with respect, and you get reputation: 名望 standing, 威望 prestige, 声望 esteem. Even the coverb 望前看, to look toward the front, keeps the same arrow of a directed gaze. Whenever you meet 望, ask which way the eyes are pointed: out to the horizon, in toward a longing, or back toward a person the world holds high.
