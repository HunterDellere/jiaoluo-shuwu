字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

个 gè traces back to a single stalk of bamboo. The traditional form 個 is a phonetic-semantic compound: 亻 (rén, person) supplies the human sense of a single individual, and 固 (gù) supplies the sound. But the simplified 个 reaches further back, to an old graph that pictured one half of 竹 (zhú, bamboo). 竹 is two bamboo leaves side by side; split it down the middle and you are left with the shape of 个, a single drooping leaf. Counting stalks one by one is one of the oldest uses of a tally word, which is exactly the job 个 still does.

From "one stalk, one piece" the character settles into two everyday roles:

1. The universal measure word. Chinese counts nouns with a classifier between the number and the noun, and 个 is the default one, reached for whenever no more specific classifier fits: 一个人 yí ge rén (one person), 一个问题 yí ge wèntí (one question), 三个苹果 sān ge píngguǒ (three apples). In speech it is usually neutral-toned (ge) and is by far the most common classifier in the language.
2. Individual, single. The same "one piece" sense, applied to a single member of a group: 个人 gèrén (the individual; personal), 个性 gèxìng (personality, what is particular to one person), 个别 gèbié (individual; a few).

The thread is singleness. Whether 个 is tallying apples or naming the lone self set apart from the crowd, it picks out one unit from many. To count a thing is to treat it as a discrete piece, and that same act of picking out one is what makes 个 the root of "the individual."

一个 yí gè The Universal Measure Word
语法洞见 yǔfǎ dòngjiàn · Grammatical Insight

Chinese does not say "three apples" directly. It says "three [classifier] apples," 三个苹果, with a measure word naming the kind of thing being counted. Many nouns take a specific classifier: 本 běn for bound books, 张 zhāng for flat sheets, 只 zhī for many animals, 条 tiáo for long thin things. 个 gè is the general-purpose default, the one you reach for when no special classifier comes to mind, and the one that covers people, abstract ideas, and most ordinary objects.

Because it fits almost anywhere, 个 is the first classifier a learner meets and the safest fallback in conversation: 一个人 (a person), 一个想法 (an idea), 一个星期 (a week), 那个东西 (that thing). It is also the workhorse behind 这个 zhège (this one) and 那个 nàge (that one). When 个 is doing this counting job, it usually drops to a neutral tone (ge) in fluent speech, even though its citation tone is the falling fourth (gè).

一个 yí ge one (of something); a; an
MW 量词 liàngcí
一 yī (one) + 个 ge (measure word). The most basic count phrase in the language, sitting before a noun: 一个人 "one person," 一个苹果 "one apple." Note the sandhi: before the fourth-tone 个, the 一 shifts to a rising second tone (yí).
我想买一个苹果。
Wǒ xiǎng mǎi yí ge píngguǒ.
I would like to buy an apple.
这是一个很好的问题。
Zhè shì yí ge hěn hǎo de wèntí.
This is a very good question.
这个 zhège this; this one
PRON 代词 dàicí
这 zhè (this) + 个 ge. The demonstrative "this one," with 个 supplying the classifier slot. Its pair is 那个 nàge (that one). Often heard as a filler too, like an English "um."
这个多少钱？
Zhège duōshao qián?
How much is this one?
几个 jǐ ge a few; several; how many
MW 量词 liàngcí
几 jǐ (how many; a few) + 个 ge. Either a question ("how many?") or a vague small number ("a few"), depending on context: 几个人 "how many people?" or "a few people."
你有几个问题？
Nǐ yǒu jǐ ge wèntí?
How many questions do you have?
辨析 biànxī · 个 vs. 本 / 张 / 只 个 is the catch-all classifier; the others are specific. Use 本 běn for bound books (一本书), 张 zhāng for flat sheets like paper or tables (一张纸), and 只 zhī for many animals and one of a pair (一只猫). When a specific classifier exists, it is preferred; 个 is the safe default when none comes to mind.
个人 gèrén Individual & Personal
个人 gèrén individual; personal; in one's own view
N 名词 míngcí
个 gè (single) + 人 rén (person). The single person set against the group, and by extension "personal, private": 个人观点 "a personal opinion," 个人信息 "personal information." Its foil is 集体 jítǐ (the collective).
这只是我个人的看法。
Zhè zhǐshì wǒ gèrén de kànfǎ.
This is just my personal view.
个性 gèxìng personality; individual character; distinctiveness
N 名词 míngcí
个 gè (individual) + 性 xìng (nature; quality). The particular character that sets one person apart: 很有个性 "has a strong personality, very distinctive." More about what makes someone unique than 性格 xìnggé, which is temperament in general.
她是一个很有个性的人。
Tā shì yí ge hěn yǒu gèxìng de rén.
She is a person with a strong personality.
个体 gètǐ an individual (entity); a single member
N 名词 míngcí
个 gè (single) + 体 tǐ (body; entity). A single unit within a larger whole, used in biology and economics alike: 个体差异 "individual differences," 个体户 "a self-employed business owner."
每个个体都不一样。
Měi ge gètǐ dōu bù yíyàng.
Every individual is different.
个别 gèbié Build & the Singled-Out
个子 gèzi height; stature; build (of a person)
N 名词 míngcí
个 gè + 子 zi (noun-suffix). A person's height or physical build: 高个子 "tall build," 矮个子 "short build," 个子很高 "very tall." Used of the whole frame of a person, not a measured number.
他个子很高。
Tā gèzi hěn gāo.
He is very tall.
个别 gèbié individual; a small number of; exceptional
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
个 gè (single) + 别 bié (separate; other). Either "one by one, individually" (个别谈话 "to talk to people one at a time") or "only a few, exceptional" (个别情况 "a few isolated cases").
只有个别学生迟到了。
Zhǐyǒu gèbié xuésheng chídào le.
Only a few students were late.
整个 zhěnggè the whole; entire
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
整 zhěng (whole) + 个 gè. "The whole of" something taken as one piece: 整个城市 "the whole city," 整个晚上 "the entire evening." Here 个 keeps its sense of a single complete unit.
整个城市都安静下来了。
Zhěnggè chéngshì dōu ānjìng xiàlái le.
The whole city quieted down.
惯用 guànyòng Set Phrases & Reduplication
个个 gègè each and every one; all of them Reduplicating the measure word makes it mean "every single one without exception." 他们个个都很努力 "every one of them works hard." A vivid, emphatic way to say "all."
一个个 yí ge gè one by one; one after another The triple form stresses that things happen or are dealt with individually, in sequence. 学生一个个走进教室 "the students filed into the classroom one by one."
个人主义 gèrén zhǔyì individualism 个人 (the individual) + 主义 (-ism). The modern term for individualism, the doctrine that puts the single person ahead of the collective. Its standing contrast is 集体主义 jítǐ zhǔyì (collectivism).
个中滋味 gè zhōng zī wèi the flavor within: the inner experience only the one who lived it can know Lit: the-taste-within-this. 个中 means "within this matter," and 滋味 is flavor. The phrase points to feelings or hardships that an outsider cannot fully grasp, known only to the person who went through them.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

See one stalk of bamboo standing alone, a single leaf drooping from it. That lone stalk is 个: one piece, picked out from the grove. To count is to pull out one stalk at a time, which is why 个 is the measure word you reach for when nothing more specific fits: 一个人, 一个问题, 一个想法.

Now let the stalk become a person. The same act of singling out one unit gives you 个人 (the individual), 个性 (what makes that one person distinct), 个子 (the build of that single frame). Wherever 个 appears, look for the one piece set apart from the many: counted as a unit, or standing on its own as an individual.
