字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

海 hǎi is a pictophonetic compound: the water radical 氵 carries the meaning, and 每 (měi) supplies the sound. On the left stands 氵, the three-dot form of 水 shuǐ (water), which marks the great family of liquid and waterway characters: 河 hé (river), 湖 hú (lake), 洋 yáng (ocean). On the right is 每 měi, here doing phonetic duty rather than contributing its everyday sense of "each." The Shuōwén glosses 海 as the great body of water into which the hundred rivers pour: water, plus a phonetic, naming the place where all water gathers.

From the literal sea, three senses fan out:

1. Sea, ocean. The body of salt water itself: 大海 dàhǎi (the sea), 海洋 hǎiyáng (the ocean), 海边 hǎibiān (the seaside), 海岛 hǎidǎo (an island).
2. By the sea, in place and institution names. A city or thing tied to the coast: 上海 Shànghǎi (the city "upon the sea"), 海关 hǎiguān (customs), 海军 hǎijūn (the navy).
3. A vast quantity. Anything as boundless as the ocean: 人海 rénhǎi (a sea of people), 火海 huǒhǎi (a sea of fire), 海量 hǎiliàng (a great capacity, especially for drink).

The thread is vastness that gathers. The sea is where every river ends, the widest thing the early Chinese world knew, so 海 came to stamp the idea of the immense and the all-collecting onto whatever it touched, whether a crowd, a blaze, or a person's tolerance for wine.

大海 dàhǎi Sea & Ocean
大海 dàhǎi the sea; the open sea
N 名词 míngcí
大 dà (big) + 海 hǎi (sea). The everyday word for the sea as a whole, the vast expanse of salt water. 大海捞针 "to fish a needle from the sea" is the Chinese "needle in a haystack."
我喜欢在大海里游泳。
Wǒ xǐhuan zài dàhǎi lǐ yóuyǒng.
I like to swim in the sea.
河流最终都流入大海。
Héliú zuìzhōng dōu liúrù dàhǎi.
Rivers all flow into the sea in the end.
海洋 hǎiyáng ocean; the seas (collectively)
N 名词 míngcí
海 hǎi (sea) + 洋 yáng (the open ocean). The full body of the world's salt water, the scientific and grand register: 海洋生物 "marine life," 太平洋是最大的海洋 "the Pacific is the largest ocean."
海洋覆盖了地球的大部分。
Hǎiyáng fùgài le dìqiú de dà bùfen.
The ocean covers most of the Earth.
辨析 biànxī · 海 vs. 洋 海 is the sea closer to land, the bordering and bounded water (the East China Sea, 东海). 洋 is the open ocean far from shore, the largest divisions of world water (the Pacific, 太平洋). Joined as 海洋 they cover all of it, but apart, 海 hugs the coast while 洋 reaches the horizon.
海边 hǎibiān seaside; the coast; the shore
N 名词 míngcí
海 hǎi (sea) + 边 biān (edge; side). The edge where land meets sea, the place you go to be beside the water: 去海边度假 "to vacation at the seaside."
夏天我们常去海边。
Xiàtiān wǒmen cháng qù hǎibiān.
In summer we often go to the seaside.
上海 Shànghǎi Coastal Places & Institutions
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Because the sea was the edge of the known world and the door to trade, 海 became the natural marker for places and institutions that face the water. 上海 Shànghǎi, China's largest city, is literally "upon the sea," a name it earned as a fishing and shipping town at the mouth of the Yangtze. The same logic builds the apparatus of a maritime state: 海关 hǎiguān (the customs office, the "sea barrier" through which goods pass), 海军 hǎijūn (the navy, the "sea army"), 海港 hǎigǎng (a seaport), 海运 hǎiyùn (sea freight).

The sea also reaches inland through metaphor. 海外 hǎiwài, "beyond the seas," is the standard word for abroad or overseas, a reminder that for most of Chinese history the wider world was reached by water. To this day 海外华人 means the overseas Chinese, the communities scattered past the coast.

上海 Shànghǎi Shanghai (city); literally upon the sea
N 名词 míngcí
上 shàng (upon; on) + 海 hǎi (sea). China's largest city and busiest port, at the mouth of the Yangtze. The name reads "on the sea," fitting for a town that grew from fishing village to global harbor.
我下个月要去上海出差。
Wǒ xià ge yuè yào qù Shànghǎi chūchāi.
I'm going to Shanghai on business next month.
海关 hǎiguān customs (the office or checkpoint)
N 名词 míngcí
海 hǎi (sea) + 关 guān (pass; barrier). The customs authority that inspects goods crossing a border, named for the sea-passage through which trade once arrived: 过海关 "to clear customs."
他们在海关检查了我的行李。
Tāmen zài hǎiguān jiǎnchá le wǒ de xíngli.
They checked my luggage at customs.
海军 hǎijūn navy
N 名词 míngcí
海 hǎi (sea) + 军 jūn (army; armed force). The naval branch of the military, the "sea army." Its siblings are 陆军 lùjūn (the army, "land force") and 空军 kōngjūn (the air force).
他年轻时在海军服役。
Tā niánqīng shí zài hǎijūn fúyì.
He served in the navy when he was young.
人海 rénhǎi A Vast Quantity: the Sea as Multitude
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The sea is the largest thing the eye can take in, so 海 became a unit of the immeasurable. Append it to a noun and that noun turns boundless. 人海 rénhǎi is a "sea of people," a crowd so dense it has no edge; 火海 huǒhǎi is a "sea of fire," a blaze that has swallowed everything. The pair 火海 and 刀山 (a "mountain of knives") combine in the vow 上刀山下火海, to climb the knife-mountain and plunge into the fire-sea for someone, meaning to face any danger for them.

The same vastness can measure an inner capacity. 海量 hǎiliàng is "an ocean's measure," a tremendous capacity, used both for a person who can drink an enormous amount without falling and, in the modern world, for huge volumes of data (海量信息, "a flood of information"). To call someone 海量 at a banquet is half compliment, half dare.

人海 rénhǎi a sea of people; a vast crowd
N 名词 míngcí
人 rén (person) + 海 hǎi (sea). A crowd so large it reads as an ocean of heads. Famous in the phrase 茫茫人海 "the boundless sea of humanity," the throng in which two people might never meet.
在茫茫人海中找到你不容易。
Zài mángmáng rénhǎi zhōng zhǎodào nǐ bù róngyì.
Finding you in the vast sea of people was not easy.
海量 hǎiliàng a great capacity; a vast amount
N 名词 míngcí
海 hǎi (sea) + 量 liàng (measure; capacity). An ocean's worth of room. Traditionally a polite word for someone who can hold their drink; in the digital age, also a huge quantity of data: 海量数据 "massive data."
您真是海量，再喝一杯吧。
Nín zhēn shì hǎiliàng, zài hē yì bēi ba.
You can really hold your drink; have another cup.
海岛 hǎidǎo island (in the sea)
N 名词 míngcí
海 hǎi (sea) + 岛 dǎo (island). A piece of land surrounded by sea. Where 岛 alone is any island, 海岛 stresses the open-sea setting, far from the mainland.
他们在一座小海岛上住了一周。
Tāmen zài yí zuò xiǎo hǎidǎo shàng zhù le yì zhōu.
They stayed on a small island for a week.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
人山人海 rén shān rén hǎi people-mountains and people-seas: an enormous crowd Lit: person-mountain person-sea. The crowd is so dense it piles up like mountains and spreads out like seas. The standard description of a packed holiday street, station, or scenic spot.
海枯石烂 hǎi kū shí làn till the seas run dry and the rocks crumble: forever, unchanging Lit: sea-dry stone-rot. A vow of eternal constancy, used in oaths of love and loyalty: even when the impossible happens and the ocean dries up, the feeling will not change.
海阔天空 hǎi kuò tiān kōng the sea is wide and the sky is open: boundless; (of talk) freewheeling and unconstrained Lit: sea-wide sky-empty. It paints limitless space, hence both a feeling of total freedom and a way to describe rambling, anything-goes conversation: 海阔天空地聊 "to chat about everything under the sun."
翻江倒海 fān jiāng dǎo hǎi to overturn rivers and upend seas: overwhelming, world-shaking force Lit: overturn-river topple-sea. Said of a power great enough to churn the waters of the world, and by extension of turbulent emotion or upheaval: 心里翻江倒海 "the heart in utter turmoil."
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

See the three water dots of 氵 on the left, and let them pour rightward into 每 until the whole character brims with water. That is 海: the place where every river ends, the widest thing in view. Hold the image of water gathering and you hold the meaning.

Now let the sea stand for whatever is too big to count. A crowd packed shoulder to shoulder becomes a 人海, a sea of people. A city that opened to the world by water is 上海, the town upon the sea. A person who can drink without limit has 海量, an ocean's capacity. Wherever 海 appears, ask what is being called boundless, and you will find the sea standing in for it.
