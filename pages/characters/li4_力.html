字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

力 lì is a pictograph of exertion. The oracle-bone and bronze forms show a single bent shape that most scholars read as a plough pushed through soil, while others see a flexed arm with the muscle and sinew standing out. Either way the image is the same: the body straining against a load. The modern glyph keeps just two strokes, a long downward sweep hooked at the foot and a short stroke pressing across it, the whole thing leaning forward like a limb under strain. 力 is its own radical, the strength radical, and it lends that sense of effort to a wide family of characters such as 加 (to add), 动 (to move), and 努 (to strive).

Three senses branch from the straining body:

1. Physical strength, force. The muscle sense, raw bodily power: 力气 lìqi (physical strength), 体力 tǐlì (stamina), 用力 yònglì (to exert force).
2. Power, capacity, ability. Strength abstracted into what a person or thing can do: 能力 nénglì (ability), 动力 dònglì (driving force), 压力 yālì (pressure).
3. Effort, by force of trying. Strength deliberately poured into a task: 努力 nǔlì (to strive), 尽力 jìnlì (to do one's utmost), 暴力 bàolì (violence, force used wrongly).

The thread running through every word is push: the foot on the plough, the muscle under load, the will set against a task. Wherever 力 appears, something is being moved by force, whether the force is a body lifting a weight, a capacity meeting a demand, or a person bending their whole effort toward an end.

力气 lìqi Strength & Force
力气 lìqi physical strength; bodily effort
N 名词 míngcí
力 lì (force) + 气 qì (breath; vital energy). The bodily strength you can feel in your muscles, the kind used to lift, carry, or push: 没力气 "have no strength," 花力气 "to spend effort." Concrete and physical, never abstract.
他力气很大。
Tā lìqi hěn dà.
He is very strong.
我今天一点力气都没有。
Wǒ jīntiān yìdiǎn lìqi dōu méiyǒu.
I have no energy at all today.
力量 lìliàng strength; power; force (often abstract)
N 名词 míngcí
力 lì (force) + 量 liàng (quantity; measure). Strength considered as a force or magnitude, ranging from physical might to moral and social power: 团结的力量 "the power of unity."
知识就是力量。
Zhīshi jiùshì lìliàng.
Knowledge is power.
辨析 biànxī · 力量 vs. 力气 力气 is concrete bodily strength, what your arms can do; you spend it carrying boxes. 力量 is strength as a force or magnitude, and reaches into the abstract: the power of an idea, an army, or a feeling. You can say 知识就是力量 "knowledge is power," but not 知识就是力气.
用力 yònglì to exert force; to use one's strength; hard (of physical effort)
V 动词 dòngcí
用 yòng (to use) + 力 lì (force). To put physical force into an action: 用力推 "push hard," 用力拉 "pull with all your strength." Describes how an action is done, with muscle behind it.
用力把门推开。
Yònglì bǎ mén tuī kāi.
Push the door open with force.
体力 tǐlì physical stamina; bodily strength; endurance
N 名词 míngcí
体 tǐ (body) + 力 lì (strength). The body's stamina, the strength that sustains physical effort over time. Often paired against 脑力 nǎolì (mental effort): 体力劳动 "physical labor."
跑完马拉松很消耗体力。
Pǎo wán mǎlāsōng hěn xiāohào tǐlì.
Running a marathon drains a lot of stamina.
能力 nénglì Power & Ability
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Past the muscle, 力 becomes the measure of what a person or thing can do. 能力 nénglì pairs 能 (be able) with 力 (force) to name ability, the capacity to carry something off. 动力 dònglì joins 动 (to move) with 力 to name the force that drives motion, whether the literal power of an engine or the inner motivation that keeps a person going. And 压力 yālì joins 压 (to press down) with 力 to name pressure, the force bearing down from outside, used both for physical load and for the everyday weight of stress.

Notice how 力 turns a verb into a noun of capacity. 想象 means to imagine, and 想象力 is the power of imagination; 记忆 means to remember, and 记忆力 is the faculty of memory; 影响 means to influence, and 影响力 is influence as a measurable force. In each, the 力 suffix says "the strength to do this," converting an action into the standing ability behind it.

能力 nénglì ability; capability; capacity
N 名词 míngcí
能 néng (be able) + 力 lì (strength). The general word for ability, what a person is capable of doing: 工作能力 "work ability," 有能力 "to be capable."
她有很强的学习能力。
Tā yǒu hěn qiáng de xuéxí nénglì.
She has a strong ability to learn.
动力 dònglì driving force; motive power; motivation
N 名词 míngcí
动 dòng (to move) + 力 lì (force). The force that drives motion. Literal in machines, where it names motive power, and figurative in people, where it names the motivation that keeps one moving toward a goal.
梦想是前进的动力。
Mèngxiǎng shì qiánjìn de dònglì.
Dreams are the driving force of moving forward.
压力 yālì pressure; stress (physical or mental)
N 名词 míngcí
压 yā (to press down) + 力 lì (force). The force pressing from outside. In physics it is pressure on a surface; in daily life it is the weight of stress: 工作压力 "work stress," 压力很大 "under a lot of pressure."
他最近工作压力很大。
Tā zuìjìn gōngzuò yālì hěn dà.
He has been under a lot of work pressure lately.
努力 nǔlì Effort & Striving
努力 nǔlì to make an effort; to strive; hardworking
V/N 动词/名词 dòngcí/míngcí
努 nǔ (to put forth) + 力 lì (effort). To bend one's strength toward a goal, the everyday word for trying hard: 努力工作 "to work hard," 努力学习 "to study diligently." Works as both verb and noun.
只要努力，就会成功。
Zhǐyào nǔlì, jiù huì chénggōng.
As long as you make an effort, you will succeed.
尽力 jìnlì to do one's utmost; to try one's best
V 动词 dòngcí
尽 jìn (to exhaust; to the limit) + 力 lì (effort). To use up all the effort one has, to spare nothing: 我会尽力 "I will do my best," 尽力而为 "do all one can."
我会尽力帮你。
Wǒ huì jìnlì bāng nǐ.
I will do my best to help you.
暴力 bàolì violence; brute force
N 名词 míngcí
暴 bào (violent; cruel) + 力 lì (force). Force used to harm, the dark side of 力: 家庭暴力 "domestic violence," 暴力行为 "violent behavior." Here strength has turned destructive.
我们反对一切暴力。
Wǒmen fǎnduì yíqiè bàolì.
We oppose all violence.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
全力以赴 quán lì yǐ fù to go all out: to throw one's whole strength into something Lit: with-full-strength-to-rush-in. To commit every bit of effort to a task, holding nothing back. The standard way to say someone gave it everything they had.
力不从心 lì bù cóng xīn strength falls short of the heart: the will is there but the ability is not Lit: strength-not-follow-heart. Said when one wants to do something but lacks the strength or means to carry it through, the gap between intention and capacity.
身体力行 shēn tǐ lì xíng to practice what one preaches: to put a principle into action oneself Lit: with-body-and-strength-to-act. To live out what one believes through one's own conduct rather than mere words. Here 力行 means to act with full effort.
鼎力相助 dǐng lì xiāng zhù to render great help: a respectful thanks for another's strong assistance Lit: with-tripod-strength-to-help. 鼎 is the massive bronze ritual tripod, so 鼎力 is great strength. A polite phrase used to acknowledge or request another person's powerful support.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

See the two strokes as a flexed arm: the long hooked sweep is the limb, the short stroke pressing across it is the swollen muscle straining under a load. That arm under strain is the whole character. Everything 力 means is the body, or something like a body, pushing against weight.

Keep the muscle and you get raw strength (力气, 体力). Abstract the muscle into capacity and you get ability and the forces around it (能力, 动力, 压力). Aim the muscle at a task and you get effort (努力, 尽力). Whenever you meet 力, look for the load it is pushing against, and the sense will follow.
