字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

朋 sets two 月 side by side: 朋 = 月 + 月. The modern glyph reads as a doubled moon component, but the picture underneath is older and more concrete. In the oracle-bone and bronze forms 朋 draws two strings of cowrie shells hung together, the strands dangling in parallel. Cowries were money before coins, and a 朋 was a fixed unit of them, a pair of strings counted as one bundle.

Two strands of equal length, hung as a matched pair, gave the character its lasting idea: things of the same kind kept close together. From shells of equal value the sense slid to people of equal standing, companions who keep company on a level footing. By the time of the classical texts 朋 already means a friend, a fellow of one's own group.

The opening line of the Analects fixes the word in every learner's ear: 有朋自远方来，不亦乐乎 "to have friends come from afar, is that not a joy?" Here 朋 is the companion who shares your path. In later prose 朋 rarely stands alone; it pairs into 朋友 péngyou for the everyday friend, and into bound compounds like 亲朋 and 良朋 for the wider circle.

Read the glyph as two matched strands and the whole field opens: equal value, equal standing, company kept side by side. The radical 月 here is a shape inherited from the shell-strings, not the moon in the sky, but it is what you count strokes by today.

朋友 péngyou Friends: the Everyday Word
用法洞见 yòngfǎ dòngjiàn · Usage Insight

In modern Mandarin 朋 almost never walks alone; it leans on a second syllable. The workhorse is 朋友 péngyou, the plain word for a friend, light enough to cover a schoolmate, a colleague you like, or a lifelong companion. It stretches into set phrases too: 男朋友 nán péngyou is a boyfriend, 女朋友 nǚ péngyou a girlfriend, and 交朋友 jiāo péngyou is the verb-object "to make friends." The single 朋 keeps its old weight only inside compounds and idioms.

朋友 péngyou friend: a companion of one's own choosing
N 名词 míngcí
朋 péng (companion) + 友 yǒu (friend). The everyday word for a friend, the person you keep company with by choice rather than by blood. The two halves are near-synonyms doubled for the standard disyllable; neither half carries the meaning alone in speech.
他是我最好的朋友。
Tā shì wǒ zuì hǎo de péngyou.
He is my best friend.
我们做了很多年的朋友。
Wǒmen zuò le hěn duō nián de péngyou.
We have been friends for many years.
交朋友 jiāo péngyou to make friends: to form friendships
VO 动宾 dòngbīn
交 jiāo (to associate, to exchange) + 朋友 péngyou (friend). A verb-object phrase: to make friends, to build a circle. 交 can take an object between it and 朋友: 交个朋友 "make a friend," 交了不少朋友 "made quite a few friends."
他很喜欢交朋友。
Tā hěn xǐhuan jiāo péngyou.
He really likes making friends.
男朋友 nán péngyou boyfriend: a male romantic partner
N 名词 míngcí
男 nán (male) + 朋友 péngyou (friend). The standard word for a boyfriend; 女朋友 nǚ péngyou is the matching girlfriend. The 朋友 base shows how the modern romantic term grew out of the plain word for companion.
她的男朋友是医生。
Tā de nán péngyou shì yīshēng.
Her boyfriend is a doctor.
辨析 biànxī · 朋友 vs. 男朋友 朋友 is a plain friend, no romance implied. 男朋友 and 女朋友 are romantic partners. Context and the 男 / 女 prefix do the work; bare 朋友 stays neutral.
亲朋 qīnpéng Kin & Company: the Wider Circle
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Outside 朋友, the bare 朋 lives on in bound compounds with a more literary ring. 亲朋 qīnpéng folds kin and friends into one phrase, the whole circle of people close to you; it is the heart of 亲朋好友 "relatives and friends," the set greeting for any gathering. 良朋 liángpéng is a good friend in the older, weightier sense, a worthy companion. These keep the older standing of 朋, the equal whose company is worth keeping, that the casual 朋友 has softened.

亲朋 qīnpéng kin and friends: one's close circle
N 名词 míngcí
亲 qīn (kin, relatives) + 朋 péng (friends). Relatives and friends taken together, the whole circle of those close to you. Most often heard in the four-character 亲朋好友, the standard phrase for everyone you would invite to a wedding or a feast.
婚礼上来了很多亲朋好友。
Hūnlǐ shàng lái le hěn duō qīnpéng hǎoyǒu.
Many relatives and friends came to the wedding.
良朋 liángpéng a good friend: a worthy companion
N 名词 míngcí
良 liáng (good, fine) + 朋 péng (friend). A good and worthy friend, the term carrying the older weight of 朋. Literary in tone; often paired as 良朋益友 "good friends who do you good." You would write it, not say it over coffee.
人生难得一良朋。
Rénshēng nándé yī liángpéng.
A truly good friend is rare in life.
辨析 biànxī · 朋友 vs. 良朋 朋友 is the everyday spoken word, neutral and wide. 良朋 is literary and weighted, a worthy companion you would name in writing. Use 朋友 when you talk, 良朋 when you reach for a higher register.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
高朋满座 gāo péng mǎn zuò distinguished friends fill the seats: a gathering of honored guests Lit: high-friends fill the seats. 高朋 are eminent guests, the 高 marking rank or worth. The phrase from Wang Bo's preface to the Tengwang Pavilion praises a host whose hall is full of distinguished company, a warm word for a well-attended feast.
朋比为奸 péng bǐ wéi jiān to band together for evil: to collude in wrongdoing Lit: friends-allying to-do evil. 朋比 is to band together as a clique, 为奸 to commit villainy. A sharply negative idiom for cronies who join forces for corrupt or treacherous ends, the dark side of company kept on a level footing.
狐朋狗友 hú péng gǒu yǒu fox-friends and dog-companions: a bad crowd Lit: fox-friends, dog-friends. 狐 fox and 狗 dog stand for the sly and the low. The phrase scorns worthless drinking companions and hangers-on, the kind of friends who lead you nowhere good. A parent's word of warning.
宾朋满座 bīn péng mǎn zuò guests and friends fill the seats: a house full of company Lit: guests-friends fill the seats. 宾朋 pairs guest with friend; the image is a hall packed with visitors. Close kin to 高朋满座, used of a lively, well-attended occasion where every seat is taken.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture two strings of cowrie shells hung side by side, each strand the same length, the polished shells clicking together as they sway. They are matched: equal value, equal weight, counted as one bundle. That paired bundle is 朋, two 月 components standing shoulder to shoulder on the page.

Now let the shells become people. Two friends of equal standing, walking side by side, neither above the other, that is the friend (朋友) at the heart of the character. Widen the strands and the circle grows: kin and friends together (亲朋), a worthy companion worth keeping (良朋), a hall where honored guests fill every seat (高朋满座). Each time you see 朋, hear the two strands click together: company on a level footing.
