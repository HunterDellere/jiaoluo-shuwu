字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

相 = 木 (mù, tree) + 目 (mù, eye). An eye set beside a tree: the picture is of looking, of inspecting a thing closely. The Shuōwén reads it as 省视, to examine and view. The radical is the eye 目 on the right, and the tree 木 on the left supplies the scene being looked at. The glyph is identical in simplified and traditional script, so the two hero panels show the same form.

From that single act of looking, two readings split apart, and the split carries the meaning:

Level tone, xiāng: mutual, each other. When two parties look at one another, the looking becomes reciprocal. This reading marks an action done back and forth between sides: 互相 hùxiāng (mutually), 相同 xiāngtóng (the same), 相信 xiāngxìn (to trust), 相反 xiāngfǎn (opposite).
Falling tone, xiàng: appearance, to examine, minister. When the looking settles on a fixed object, 相 becomes what is looked at and the one who looks. It names the appearance or photograph (照相 zhàoxiàng, 相貌 xiàngmào), the act of inspecting a person (相亲 xiāngqīn, the matchmaking look), and the high official who oversees the realm (宰相 zǎixiàng, 首相 shǒuxiàng).

The thread is the gaze. Turned outward toward another, it is reciprocity; turned onto a single object, it is the face that is seen, the act of sizing up, and the minister whose whole office is to look over the affairs of state. One eye on one tree, branching into mutual and into mien.

互相 hùxiāng Mutual: the Reciprocal xiāng
互相 hùxiāng mutually; each other; one another
ADV 副词 fùcí
互 hù (mutual) + 相 xiāng (each other). Placed before a verb, it says the action runs in both directions between two or more parties: 互相帮助 "to help each other," 互相理解 "to understand one another."
同学们应该互相帮助。
Tóngxuémen yīnggāi hùxiāng bāngzhù.
Classmates should help one another.
相信 xiāngxìn to believe; to trust
V 动词 dòngcí
相 xiāng (mutual) + 信 xìn (trust). To place trust in someone or to believe a thing is so: 我相信你 "I trust you," 相信科学 "to believe in science." One of the most common verbs built on the mutual reading.
我相信你说的话。
Wǒ xiāngxìn nǐ shuō de huà.
I believe what you said.
相同 xiāngtóng identical; the same; alike
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
相 xiāng (mutually) + 同 tóng (same). Said of two things that match each other: 相同的意见 "the same opinion." Its standing opposite is 相反 xiāngfǎn, mutually opposed.
我们的看法相同。
Wǒmen de kànfǎ xiāngtóng.
Our views are the same.
相处 xiāngchǔ to get along; to spend time together
V 动词 dòngcí
相 xiāng (each other) + 处 chǔ (to dwell; to be with). To live alongside or get along with others: 容易相处 "easy to get along with," 相处了很多年 "spent many years together."
他们相处得很好。
Tāmen xiāngchǔ de hěn hǎo.
They get along very well.
照相 zhàoxiàng Appearance & Photos: the Falling-Tone xiàng
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Read in the falling tone, xiàng turns from an action between people into the thing that is seen. It is the appearance, the look, the face: 相貌 xiàngmào (a person's looks), 长相 zhǎngxiàng (facial features), 真相 zhēnxiàng (the true face of a matter, i.e. the truth behind it).

The same reading names the captured image. 照相 zhàoxiàng is "to take a photograph," literally to shine a light and fix the appearance; the picture itself is 相片 xiàngpiàn or 照片 zhàopiàn, and the device is the 相机 xiàngjī (camera) or 照相机 zhàoxiàngjī. Listen for the tone: the mutual xiāng of 互相 rises level, while the appearance xiàng of 照相 falls. The shift in tone is the clearest signal of which 相 you have met.

照相 zhàoxiàng to take a photograph
V 动词 dòngcí
照 zhào (to shine; to reflect) + 相 xiàng (appearance). To fix a likeness in a picture: 给我照相 "take a photo of me." A separable verb: 照了一张相 "took one photo."
我们在长城前照相。
Wǒmen zài Chángchéng qián zhàoxiàng.
We took photos in front of the Great Wall.
相片 xiàngpiàn photograph; photo
N 名词 míngcí
相 xiàng (appearance) + 片 piàn (a flat slice; sheet). The printed photograph as an object. Its everyday synonym 照片 zhàopiàn is equally common; both name the picture you hold or hang.
墙上挂着一张老相片。
Qiáng shàng guà zhe yī zhāng lǎo xiàngpiàn.
An old photograph hangs on the wall.
真相 zhēnxiàng the truth; the real state of affairs
N 名词 míngcí
真 zhēn (true) + 相 xiàng (appearance; face). The true face of a matter once the surface is stripped away: 事情的真相 "the truth of the matter," 揭露真相 "to expose the truth."
我们终于知道了真相。
Wǒmen zhōngyú zhīdào le zhēnxiàng.
We finally learned the truth.
宰相 zǎixiàng To Examine & the Minister Who Looks Over
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The Shuōwén gloss, 省视 (to inspect, to look over), survives most plainly in two falling-tone uses. The first is the verb of sizing-up: 相亲 xiāngqīn is the formal meeting where a prospective couple look each other over with marriage in view, and 看相 kàn xiàng is to read a face for character or fortune. To 相 someone is to examine them.

The second is the office. The 宰相 zǎixiàng was the highest minister of the imperial court, the one whose duty was to look over the affairs of the realm on the ruler's behalf. The word carries into modern usage as the title for a foreign head of government: 首相 shǒuxiàng (prime minister) and 丞相 chéngxiàng (chancellor) belong to the same family. Across all of these, the falling-tone 相 is the gaze that inspects, weighs, and oversees.

宰相 zǎixiàng prime minister (of imperial times); chief minister
N 名词 míngcí
宰 zǎi (to govern; to manage) + 相 xiàng (minister). The highest official under the emperor, charged with overseeing the government. A staple figure of historical drama and idiom.
他是当时最有权力的宰相。
Tā shì dāngshí zuì yǒu quánlì de zǎixiàng.
He was the most powerful chief minister of his day.
首相 shǒuxiàng prime minister (of a modern foreign government)
N 名词 míngcí
首 shǒu (head; chief) + 相 xiàng (minister). The modern title for the head of government in countries such as Britain and Japan: 英国首相 "the British Prime Minister."
日本首相发表了讲话。
Rìběn shǒuxiàng fābiǎo le jiǎnghuà.
The Japanese Prime Minister gave a speech.
相亲 xiāngqīn to meet a prospective marriage partner; a matchmaking meeting
V 动词 dòngcí
相 xiàng (to examine) + 亲 qīn (marriage; relative). The arranged meeting where two people look each other over with marriage in mind, often set up by family or a matchmaker. Note: this 相 keeps a level tone for many speakers, but the sense is the inspecting one.
妈妈安排他去相亲。
Māma ānpái tā qù xiāngqīn.
His mother arranged a matchmaking meeting for him.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
相辅相成 xiāng fǔ xiāng chéng to complement each other: two things that reinforce and complete one another Lit: mutually-assist mutually-complete. Said of two factors that work together so that each makes the other stronger, like theory and practice or form and content.
自相矛盾 zì xiāng máo dùn to contradict oneself: spear against shield, an inner contradiction Lit: self mutually spear-shield. From the tale of a man hawking a spear that pierces any shield and a shield no spear can pierce. Used of any claim that defeats itself.
一脉相承 yī mài xiāng chéng cut from the same cloth: descended in one unbroken line Lit: one-vein mutually-inherited. Said of ideas, styles, or traditions that pass down through a single continuous lineage, one generation handing them to the next.
萍水相逢 píng shuǐ xiāng féng to meet by chance: like duckweed drifting together on water Lit: duckweed-water mutually-meet. Floating duckweed has no fixed place, so the phrase describes strangers who meet briefly by accident and then drift apart again.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture an eye, 目, standing beside a tree, 木, and looking hard at it. That gaze is the whole character. Now let the gaze move two ways.

Turn it toward another person who looks back, and the looking becomes mutual: you trust each other (相信), help each other (互相), share the same view (相同). Read it level, xiāng. Fix it instead on one object, and 相 becomes what the eye rests on: the appearance and the photograph (照相, 相片), the face that is the truth of a thing (真相), the act of sizing someone up (相亲), and the minister whose office is to look over the realm (宰相). Read it falling, xiàng. One eye on one tree, opening into mutual on the one hand and into mien on the other; the tone tells you which way the gaze has turned.
