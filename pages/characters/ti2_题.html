字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

题 is a pictophonetic compound: the radical 页 (yè, head; leaf) carries the meaning, and 是 (shì) supplies the sound. The 页 element is the ancient pictograph of a head, and it lends its sense of "top, front, the leading part" to the whole glyph. The original meaning of 题 was the forehead, the topmost surface of the face. From "forehead" the character moved naturally to "the front of a thing, the part placed at the top": the heading written above a piece, then the question set at the head of an exam.

The simplified form 题 and the traditional form 題 differ only in the left phonetic: simplified uses 是 directly, while the fuller traditional shape preserves the same component beside 页. The 页 head-radical is unchanged in both, which is why every sense of 题 still circles back to the idea of the heading, the top line, the thing put forward to be read.

Three senses branch from that heading at the top:

1. A question or problem. The item set out to be solved: 问题 wèntí (question; problem), 题目 tímù (exam question; title), 难题 nántí (hard problem), 习题 xítí (practice exercises).
2. A topic, theme, or title. The subject a piece of writing or talk is organized around: 话题 huàtí (topic of conversation), 主题 zhǔtí (theme), 标题 biāotí (headline; title).
3. To inscribe. To brush words onto a surface, the act of writing the heading itself: 题字 tízì (to inscribe), 题诗 tíshī (to inscribe a poem).

The thread through all three is the heading placed where the eye lands first: the question at the top of the page, the theme that names the whole, the inscription brushed across the front of a scroll or wall.

问题 wèntí Questions & Problems
问题 wèntí question; problem; issue
N 名词 míngcí
问 wèn (to ask) + 题 tí (question). The everyday word that covers both a question you ask and a problem you face: 问一个问题 "to ask a question," 解决问题 "to solve a problem." One of the most common nouns in the language.
我有一个问题。
Wǒ yǒu yí ge wèntí.
I have a question.
这是一个很难的问题。
Zhè shì yí ge hěn nán de wèntí.
This is a difficult problem.
题目 tímù exam question; title; subject (of an essay)
N 名词 míngcí
题 tí (heading) + 目 mù (item; entry). The item set at the head of a test, or the title given to a composition: 考试题目 "exam questions," 作文题目 "the essay prompt." It points at the specific thing to be answered or written about.
这次考试的题目很多。
Zhè cì kǎoshì de tímù hěn duō.
There are a lot of questions on this exam.
辨析 biànxī · 问题 vs. 题目 问题 is the broad word for a question or a problem in life, work, or thought: anything that needs answering or fixing. 题目 is narrower and schoolroom-flavored: the specific exam item or the title of a piece. You solve a 问题 in the real world; you answer a 题目 on a test.
难题 nántí hard problem; knotty issue; difficult question
N 名词 míngcí
难 nán (difficult) + 题 tí (problem). A problem that resists easy solution, whether on paper or in life: 出了一道难题 "posed a tough problem." A close cousin is 习题 xítí, the practice exercises a student works through.
这道难题没人能解开。
Zhè dào nántí méi rén néng jiěkāi.
No one can crack this hard problem.
话题 huàtí Topics & Titles
话题 huàtí topic; subject of conversation
N 名词 míngcí
话 huà (speech; talk) + 题 tí (topic). What a conversation is about: 换个话题 "change the subject," 热门话题 "a hot topic." It frames the heading of talk rather than writing.
我们换个话题吧。
Wǒmen huàn ge huàtí ba.
Let us change the subject.
主题 zhǔtí theme; central subject; motif
N 名词 míngcí
主 zhǔ (main; principal) + 题 tí (theme). The central idea that organizes a work, an event, or a piece of music: 电影的主题 "the theme of the film," 主题公园 "a theme park." It is the heading that everything else serves.
这部小说的主题是爱情。
Zhè bù xiǎoshuō de zhǔtí shì àiqíng.
The theme of this novel is love.
标题 biāotí headline; title; caption
N 名词 míngcí
标 biāo (to mark; label) + 题 tí (heading). The title line printed at the top of an article, book, or section: 新闻标题 "a news headline," 大标题 "a big banner headline." Literally the marked heading.
这篇文章的标题很吸引人。
Zhè piān wénzhāng de biāotí hěn xīyǐn rén.
The headline of this article is very eye-catching.
题字 tízì To Inscribe: the Verb 题
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Beyond its life as a noun, 题 is also a verb: to brush words onto a surface. The sense reaches straight back to the original meaning of the heading written at the top. When a calligrapher 题字 tízì, they inscribe characters onto a painting, a fan, a plaque, or a wall, and when a poet 题诗 tíshī, they write a poem directly onto a scroll or a temple beam. A related word, 题词 tící, is a written dedication, the few brushed lines an honored guest leaves on a visitors' book or a gift.

This verbal 题 carries a flavor of ceremony. To be invited to 题 something is to be asked to add your hand and name to it, and the inscription becomes part of the object's worth. Many famous paintings carry layers of later 题字 from collectors and emperors, each addition a heading laid over the work by a new reader.

题字 tízì to inscribe characters; an inscription
V/N 动名 dòngmíng
题 tí (to inscribe) + 字 zì (characters). To brush words onto a painting, plaque, or wall, or the inscription itself. Often done by a respected figure as a mark of honor.
这块匾上的题字出自名家。
Zhè kuài biǎn shang de tízì chūzì míngjiā.
The inscription on this plaque is by a master.
题诗 tíshī to inscribe a poem (on a painting, wall, or scroll)
V 动词 dòngcí
题 tí (to inscribe) + 诗 shī (poem). To write a poem directly onto a surface, a classic gesture in Chinese painting where verse and image share one scroll.
他在画上题诗一首。
Tā zài huà shang tí shī yì shǒu.
He inscribed a poem on the painting.
答题 dátí to answer questions (on a test)
V 动词 dòngcí
答 dá (to answer) + 题 tí (question). To work through the items on an exam or quiz: 认真答题 "answer the questions carefully." Its mirror is 出题 chūtí, to set the questions.
考试时要仔细答题。
Kǎoshì shí yào zǐxì dátí.
During the exam you should answer the questions carefully.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
文不对题 wén bù duì tí the writing does not match the topic: off the point, irrelevant Lit: writing-not-match-topic. Said of an essay or answer that wanders away from what was asked, or of a reply that misses the actual question. The classic teacher's note on a stray composition.
小题大做 xiǎo tí dà zuò to make a small topic into a big production: to overreact, to make a fuss over nothing Lit: small-topic-big-do. Originally to treat a minor exam topic with a grand essay; now to blow a trivial matter out of all proportion. 别小题大做 "don't make such a fuss over nothing."
离题万里 lí tí wàn lǐ to stray ten thousand li from the topic: to wander hopelessly off subject Lit: leave-topic-ten-thousand-li. The 万里 (ten thousand li) is hyperbole for an enormous distance, so the phrase describes talk or writing that has drifted so far it has lost the thread entirely.
答非所问 dá fēi suǒ wèn the answer is not what was asked: to give a reply beside the question Lit: answer-not-what-asked. A reply that fails to address the actual question, whether from evasion or misunderstanding. A near neighbor of 文不对题, but about spoken answers rather than written work.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Anchor 题 on its head-radical 页, the forehead at the top of a page. Everything 题 means lives up there at the heading. The question stands at the top of the exam sheet (问题, 题目). The theme is the heading that names a whole work (话题, 主题, 标题). And the inscription is the heading a brush lays across the front of a scroll (题字, 题诗).

The tone is steady: 题 is always second tone, tí, a rising note like a question lifting at the end. When you see the 页 head on the right, picture the line written at the top of the page, and you will land on the right sense: the thing put forward to be read, asked, or answered.
