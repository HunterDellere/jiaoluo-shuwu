字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

河 = 氵 + 可. The three-drop water radical 氵 on the left carries the meaning, water; 可 (kě) on the right supplies the sound. This is the standard pictophonetic pattern of the water family, where the splash of 氵 fixes the domain and a borrowed syllable does the rest. In archaic readings the 可 element sat much closer to the word's old pronunciation than the modern hé suggests.

The first thing 河 named was not rivers in general but one river. In oracle-bone and bronze inscriptions 河 is the proper name of the great northern waterway the Shang and Zhou kings sacrificed to, the muddy artery we now call 黄河 (huánghé), the Yellow River. To early scribes 河 meant that river the way a capital city needs no qualifier. The Shuōwén Jiězì defines it precisely as a watercourse rising in the northwest and flowing east to the sea.

Over the Han and after, the name slid loose from its single owner. 河 broadened from the Yellow River to any river of the north, then to rivers as a category, while its southern counterpart 江 (jiāng) traveled the same road out from the Yangtze. The split still echoes in usage: northern waterways skew toward 河, southern ones toward 江, a fossil of two rivers that each once owned a word.

Japanese keeps the character as 河 (ka / kawa) for larger rivers and reads the everyday word 川 (kawa) more often; Korean carries it as 하 (ha), surfacing in place names and surnames. Across the script's reach 河 still trails its origin, a word that started as one river's name and never fully left the water it was born in.

水系shuǐxìRivers & Canals
河流héliúriver; watercourse
N 名词 míngcí
河 (river) + 流 (liú, to flow / a current). The generic technical term for a river as a flowing body of water, the word a geography textbook or a news report reaches for.
这条河流一直流到大海。
Zhè tiáo héliú yìzhí liú dào dàhǎi.
This river flows all the way to the sea.
黄河huánghéthe Yellow River
N 名词 míngcí
黄 (huáng, yellow) + 河 (river). The silt-yellow river of north China, cradle of early Chinese civilization. Here 河 returns to its oldest job, naming this exact river, with 黄 added once 河 stopped meaning it by default.
黄河是中华文明的摇篮。
Huánghé shì Zhōnghuá wénmíng de yáolán.
The Yellow River is the cradle of Chinese civilization.
辨析 biànxī · 河 vs. 江 The Yangtze is 长江 (chángjiāng) with 江; the Yellow River is 黄河 with 河. The two great rivers split the two river-words between them.
运河yùnhécanal
N 名词 míngcí
运 (yùn, to transport / haul) + 河 (river). A dug waterway built to carry boats and freight, a river made by labor rather than rain. The Grand Canal is 大运河 (dà yùnhé).
京杭大运河连接了北京和杭州。
Jīng-Háng dà yùnhé liánjiē le Běijīng hé Hángzhōu.
The Beijing-Hangzhou Grand Canal connects Beijing and Hangzhou.
银河yínhéthe Milky Way
N 名词 míngcí
银 (yín, silver) + 河 (river). A silver river across the night sky. Chinese named the galaxy as a river of stars long before it was a galaxy, and the metaphor stuck.
夏夜的银河特别清楚。
Xià yè de yínhé tèbié qīngchu.
The Milky Way is especially clear on summer nights.
河边hébiānThe River's Edge
河边hébiānriverside; riverbank
N 名词 míngcí
河 (river) + 边 (biān, edge / side). The strip of ground along a river, where people walk, fish, and wash. One of the first place-words a beginner builds with 河.
我们晚饭后常去河边散步。
Wǒmen wǎnfàn hòu cháng qù hébiān sànbù.
We often take a walk along the river after dinner.
过河guò héto cross a river
VO 动宾 dòngbīn
过 (guò, to cross) + 河 (river). A verb-object pair, to get to the far bank. It anchors several proverbs, since crossing water is the classic image of a one-way commitment.
过河以后就没有退路了。
Guò hé yǐhòu jiù méiyǒu tuìlù le.
Once you cross the river there is no way back.
辨析 biànxī · the saying 过河拆桥 (guò hé chāi qiáo), tear down the bridge after crossing, means to discard a helper the moment they are no longer useful.
河水héshuǐriver water
N 名词 míngcí
河 (river) + 水 (shuǐ, water). The water of a river as a substance, distinct from sea water 海水 (hǎishuǐ) or rain. Common in the proverb about minding your own affairs.
井水不犯河水。
Jǐngshuǐ bù fàn héshuǐ.
Well water does not intrude on river water (each keeps to its own sphere).
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
河东狮吼hé dōng shī hǒuthe lioness of Hedong roars: a fierce, jealous wifeFrom a Song poem by Su Shi teasing his friend Chen Jichang (Chen Zao), whose wife's temper made him quail. 河东 (Hedong) names her clan's home east of the Yellow River. Still used, half teasing, for a domineering spouse.
过河拆桥guò hé chāi qiáocross the river, then tear down the bridgeA vivid picture of ingratitude: discarding the very means or person that got you across once you are safely over. A sharp accusation in modern speech.
河清海晏hé qīng hǎi yànthe river runs clear and the sea is calm: peace under good ruleSince the Yellow River is famously muddy, its running clear is a near-impossible omen of a sage age. Paired with a calm sea, it stands for an era of order and prosperity. Formal, literary register.
气壮山河qì zhuàng shān héspirit grand as the mountains and riversSaid of heroic resolve or stirring deeds whose grandeur matches the land itself. 山河 (mountains and rivers) is a standing phrase for the territory of a nation. Used to praise courage and noble bearing.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Stand on the loess plain and watch the 黄河 go by: water so thick with yellow silt it pours like liquid clay. That is 河 in its birthplace. On the left of the glyph, the three drops of 氵 are the silt-heavy current itself; on the right, 可 (kě) is just the sound, a borrowed syllable riding the water the way a raft rides the flood.

Hold the order in mind: first 河 was a name, this one muddy river, and only later a word for all rivers. So when you read 黄河, hear the original swallowing its later self, yellow added back to a name that once needed nothing. Every other 河 word is that first river loaning out its character: 运河 a river people dug, 银河 a river of stars, 河边 the bank you walk along at dusk.
