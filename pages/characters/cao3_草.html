字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

草 cǎo = 艹 (cǎo, the grass radical) + 早 (zǎo, early). The top element 艹 is the grass radical, the two short tufts that mark a word as belonging to plants. Below it sits 早 zǎo, which here supplies the sound, the dipping cǎo echoing the rising-then-clipped zǎo. This is a pictophonetic build: 艹 carries the meaning of growing things, 早 carries the pronunciation.

The grass radical itself began as a doubled 屮 (chè), the sprout, a single shoot pushing two leaves out of the ground. Stack two sprouts and you get a clump; flatten the clump into the conventional 艹 strip and you have the marker that crowns hundreds of plant characters, from 茶 (chá, tea) to 菜 (cài, vegetable). The original word for grass was in fact written 艸, the bare doubled sprout, before 早 was added underneath to fix the sound and 草 became standard.

From the plant in the field the character spreads into three families of meaning. First the literal: grass, herbs, weeds, the green that covers a meadow (草地 cǎodì, 草原 cǎoyuán). Second the harvested: grass cut and dried into straw and thatch (稻草 dàocǎo, rice straw). Third, and most surprising, the figurative: because wild grass grows fast and tangled, 草 came to mean hasty and rough, hence a first draft (草稿 cǎogǎo) and the flying, untidy "grass script" of calligraphy (草书 cǎoshū).

The same character carries into Japanese as kusa and 草書 (sōsho), the running cursive hand, and into Korean as 초 (cho) in words like 초안, a draft. Everywhere the thread is the meadow: dense, quick-growing, never quite tidy.

草地 cǎodì Grass & Meadow
草地 cǎodì lawn; grassland; a grassy patch
N 名词 míngcí
草 cǎo (grass) + 地 dì (ground). Any stretch of ground covered in grass, from a park lawn to an open meadow. 在草地上 "on the grass."
孩子们在草地上踢球。
Háizimen zài cǎodì shàng tī qiú.
The children are playing ball on the grass.
草原 cǎoyuán grassland; steppe; prairie
N 名词 míngcí
草 cǎo (grass) + 原 yuán (plain). A vast open plain of grass, the wind-rippled steppe of Inner Mongolia or the prairies of the world. 内蒙古大草原 "the great grasslands of Inner Mongolia."
蒙古的草原一望无际。
Měnggǔ de cǎoyuán yī wàng wú jì.
The Mongolian grasslands stretch beyond the horizon.
辨析 biànxī · 草地 vs. 草原 草地 cǎodì is a grassy patch on a human scale, the lawn you walk on or picnic on. 草原 cǎoyuán is a vast natural grassland, the steppe stretching to the horizon. One you cross in a few steps, the other in days on horseback.
杂草 zácǎo weeds
N 名词 míngcí
杂 zá (mixed; miscellaneous) + 草 cǎo (grass). The unwanted, untended grass that crowds a garden or field, the plants you pull out rather than plant. 拔杂草 "to pull weeds."
院子里长满了杂草。
Yuànzi lǐ zhǎng mǎn le zácǎo.
The yard is overgrown with weeds.
稻草 dàocǎo Straw & Herbs
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Cut the grass and dry it, and 草 names the harvest. 稻草 dàocǎo is the straw left after rice (稻 dào) is threshed, the golden stalks bundled for fuel, fodder, thatch, and the stuffing of a scarecrow (稻草人). 干草 gāncǎo is hay, grass dried to feed livestock through the winter. The same plant that grew green in the meadow now lies cut and useful.

A second branch leads to medicine. 草药 cǎoyào is herbal medicine, the leaves, roots, and stems of plants gathered and brewed in the tradition of 中草药 zhōng cǎoyào, Chinese herbal pharmacy. Here 草 carries its oldest, widest sense: not just grass underfoot but every useful green growing thing, the herb as well as the blade.

稻草 dàocǎo rice straw
N 名词 míngcí
稻 dào (rice plant) + 草 cǎo (straw). The dried stalks left after rice is harvested, used for thatch, rope, and scarecrows. 最后一根稻草 "the last straw," the final small thing that breaks a back.
农民用稻草盖屋顶。
Nóngmín yòng dàocǎo gài wūdǐng.
The farmers thatch their roofs with rice straw.
草药 cǎoyào herbal medicine; medicinal herbs
N 名词 míngcí
草 cǎo (herb) + 药 yào (medicine). Medicine made from plants, the leaves and roots brewed in traditional Chinese pharmacy. Often expanded to 中草药 zhōng cǎoyào, Chinese herbal medicine.
奶奶懂得用草药治病。
Nǎinai dǒngdé yòng cǎoyào zhì bìng.
Grandma knows how to treat illness with herbs.
草帽 cǎomào straw hat
N 名词 míngcí
草 cǎo (straw) + 帽 mào (hat). A hat woven from dried straw, the broad-brimmed shade of farmers and summer beaches. 戴草帽 "to wear a straw hat."
他戴着草帽在田里干活。
Tā dài zhe cǎomào zài tián lǐ gàn huó.
He works in the field wearing a straw hat.
起草 qǐcǎo Rough & Draft
引申洞见 yǐnshēn dòngjiàn · Extended-Sense Insight

Wild grass grows fast, dense, and untidy, and from that picture 草 leaps into the abstract. A 草稿 cǎogǎo is a rough draft, the first tangled version of a text before it is tidied; to 起草 qǐcǎo is to draft a document, to throw down the first growth of words. The adjective 草率 cǎoshuài means careless and hasty, doing a thing as roughly as grass grows, and 潦草 liáocǎo describes sloppy, scrawled handwriting.

The most beautiful turn is in calligraphy. 草书 cǎoshū, "grass script," is the flying cursive hand in which strokes run together and characters dissolve into gesture, as wild and quick on the page as grass across a field. What began as a weed in the meadow becomes the freest, most expressive script a brush can make.

草稿 cǎogǎo rough draft; manuscript draft
N 名词 míngcí
草 cǎo (rough) + 稿 gǎo (manuscript). The first, unpolished version of a piece of writing or a plan. 打草稿 "to make a draft," to sketch something out before committing.
这只是个草稿，还要修改。
Zhè zhǐshì ge cǎogǎo, hái yào xiūgǎi.
This is only a draft; it still needs revising.
起草 qǐcǎo to draft (a document); to draw up
V 动词 dòngcí
起 qǐ (to begin; to raise) + 草 cǎo (draft). To compose the first version of a formal text, a contract, law, speech, or plan. 起草合同 "to draft a contract."
委员会负责起草新规定。
Wěiyuánhuì fùzé qǐcǎo xīn guīdìng.
The committee is responsible for drafting the new rules.
草率 cǎoshuài careless; hasty; rash
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
草 cǎo (rough; hasty) + 率 shuài (rate; rashly). Done quickly and without care, like grass thrown up overnight. 做事草率 "to do things carelessly."
这么重要的决定不能太草率。
Zhème zhòngyào de juédìng bùnéng tài cǎoshuài.
Such an important decision can't be made too rashly.
辨析 biànxī · 草 the plant vs. 草 the rough When 草 names a growing or cut plant it is concrete: grass, straw, herb (草地, 稻草, 草药). When it modifies an action or text it is figurative, borrowing the wildness and haste of grass: rough, careless, draft (草稿, 草率, 草书). Context decides whether you are in the meadow or at the writing desk.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
草木皆兵 cǎo mù jiē bīng grass and trees all look like soldiers: to be so jumpy that every shadow seems a threat From the Battle of Fei River (淝水之战, 383 CE) in the Jin Shu: the routed Former Qin emperor Fú Jiān, in panic, mistook the grass and trees on Mount Bagong for enemy troops. Now describes nervous, paranoid fear that sees danger everywhere.
斩草除根 zhǎn cǎo chú gēn cut the grass and pull the root: to eliminate a problem at its source Lit: cut-grass-remove-root. Weeds cut at the surface grow back; only by tearing out the roots are they truly gone. The idiom urges finishing a job completely so trouble cannot return, often with a ruthless edge.
草草了事 cǎo cǎo liǎo shì to finish a matter hastily and carelessly The doubled 草草 means hurriedly and roughly, borrowing the haste of fast-growing grass. To 草草了事 is to wrap something up sloppily just to be rid of it, the opposite of thorough work.
草菅人命 cǎo jiān rén mìng to treat human life as cheap as grass: to kill or harm people without regard From the Hanshu account of Jia Yi. 菅 jiān is a coarse wild grass; to regard a person's life as no more than a weed is to be utterly callous about killing. Used of tyrants and reckless rulers.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a meadow at first light: the grass radical 艹 is the tufted green spreading across the top, and beneath it the early sun 早 zǎo rises over the field, lending its sound to the whole. That is 草, grass under the morning, the plant that covers every open ground.

From that one field the senses branch. Left growing, it is the meadow you cross (草地, 草原) and the weeds you pull (杂草). Cut and dried, it is straw for the roof, hay for the ox, the herb in the medicine pot (稻草, 草帽, 草药). And because grass grows so fast and tangled, the word leaps to the desk: a hasty rough draft (草稿), a careless hand (草率), the flying grass-script of the brush (草书). Whenever you meet 草, see the meadow first, then ask whether you stand in it, harvest it, or borrow its wild speed for the page.
