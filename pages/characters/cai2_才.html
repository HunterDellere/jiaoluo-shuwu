字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

才 cái is a pictograph of a sprout breaking through the ground. The oracle-bone and bronze forms show a young shoot pushing up past a line that marks the surface of the soil: the horizontal stroke is the ground, and the vertical stroke with its small turn is the first growth pressing through. The image is the very beginning of growth, the moment a thing first shows what it can become. Although 才 stands in the dictionary under the hand radical 扌 (shǒu), the glyph itself is a single integrated picture of the sprout rather than a hand plus a phonetic.

From this one image of nascent growth the character carries two intertwined threads:

1. Talent, ability, the gifted person. The promise in the sprout read as human potential: 人才 réncái (a talented person), 天才 tiāncái (genius), 才能 cáinéng (ability), 口才 kǒucái (eloquence). This is the noun 才, the raw capacity a person is born with or has cultivated.
2. The adverb: only then, not until, merely. Borrowed for sound, 才 became one of the busiest adverbs in Mandarin. It marks a fact as later, harder, or smaller than the listener might have assumed: 我现在才知道 "only now do I know," 八点才来 "did not come until eight," 才五块钱 "only five yuan."

The thread running through both senses is the bare minimum just emerging. The sprout is the least of what the plant will be, and the adverb 才 always points at a threshold barely crossed: the knowledge that arrives only just now, the guest who shows up only at last, the price that is only this small. Talent is the seed of much; the adverb is the just-so of a single moment.

构词 gòucí Word-Formation Patterns
构词规律 gòucí guīlǜ · One Sprout, Two Threads 才 as talent / the gifted → 人才 talent · 天才 genius · 才能 ability · 口才 eloquence
才 as talent in compounds → 才华 brilliance · 才干 capability · 英才 outstanding talent · 庸才 mediocrity
才 as the adverb only-then / not-until → 才来 only just came · 才知道 only now know · 才明白 only now understand

人才 réncái Talent & Ability
人才 réncái a talented person; talent; capable people
N 名词 míngcí
人 rén (person) + 才 cái (talent). A person of real ability, and by extension the pool of skilled people an organization or country can draw on: 培养人才 "to cultivate talent," 人才市场 "the job market for skilled workers."
公司需要更多人才。
Gōngsī xūyào gèng duō réncái.
The company needs more talented people.
他是个难得的人才。
Tā shì ge nándé de réncái.
He is a rare talent.
天才 tiāncái genius; a person of innate gifts
N 名词 míngcí
天 tiān (Heaven) + 才 cái (talent). Talent granted by Heaven, the inborn brilliance that goes beyond training: 数学天才 "a math genius," 音乐天才 "a musical prodigy." It names both the gift and the person who has it.
她从小就是个天才。
Tā cóngxiǎo jiùshì ge tiāncái.
She has been a genius since childhood.
才能 cáinéng ability; talent; capability
N 名词 míngcí
才 cái (talent) + 能 néng (capacity). The abilities a person actually possesses and can put to use: 发挥才能 "to give full play to one's talents." Note the toned reading cáinéng (the noun); the adverb 才 plus 能 (cái néng, "only then can") is a separate construction.
这份工作能发挥他的才能。
Zhè fèn gōngzuò néng fāhuī tā de cáinéng.
This job lets him put his talents to use.
口才 kǒucái eloquence; a gift for speaking
N 名词 míngcí
口 kǒu (mouth) + 才 cái (talent). Literally "mouth-talent," the ability to speak well and persuasively: 他很有口才 "he is very eloquent." One of a family of 才 compounds naming a specific gift, alongside 文才 wéncái (literary talent).
这位律师口才很好。
Zhè wèi lǜshī kǒucái hěn hǎo.
This lawyer is very eloquent.
才 cái Only Then & Not Until: the Adverb 才
语法洞见 yǔfǎ dòngjiàn · Grammatical Insight

The adverb 才 cái sits right before the verb and says that something happened later, more slowly, or only after some condition than the listener might expect. 他八点才来 means "he didn't come until eight" (later than wanted); 我现在才知道 means "only now do I know" (later than I should have). The same 才 marks a result that comes only once a condition is met: 你努力才能成功 "only by working hard can you succeed," 吃了药才好 "got better only after taking the medicine."

Two habits go with this 才. First, it usually takes no aspect marker 了 after the verb, because the focus is on the lateness or the condition, not on completion: 他昨天才到 "he only got here yesterday," not 才到了. Second, it pairs naturally with a leading clause of cause or condition, with the 才 in the second clause: 因为下雨，我们才取消了 "it was only because it rained that we canceled."

The sharpest way to feel the adverb is to set it against its mirror image, 就 jiù. See the comparison note below.

才 cái only then; not until; only after
ADV 副词 fùcí
The adverb placed before the verb, marking the event as later or more conditional than expected: 他很晚才睡 "he didn't go to sleep until very late," 解释了半天他才明白 "only after a long explanation did he understand."
我等了一个小时,他才来。
Wǒ děng le yí ge xiǎoshí, tā cái lái.
I waited an hour before he finally came.
努力学习才能进步。
Nǔlì xuéxí cái néng jìnbù.
Only by studying hard can you make progress.
辨析 biànxī · 才 vs. 就 才 cái and 就 jiù are a matched pair. 才 says the event is later, slower, or harder than expected: 九点才到 "didn't arrive until nine." 就 says it is earlier, faster, or easier than expected: 七点就到了 "arrived as early as seven." Notice too that 就 takes 了 freely while 才 usually does not, because 才 stresses the threshold barely reached rather than a finished result.
才 cái Only & Merely: the Quantity 才
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The same adverb 才 has a second, closely related job: before a number or amount it means "only, merely, no more than." Here the threshold is one of size rather than time, and 才 signals that the quantity is smaller than the listener might assume. 这本书才十块钱 "this book is only ten yuan," 他才十岁 "he's only ten," 我们才走了一半 "we've only walked half the way."

Both jobs of 才 share the same flavor: the speaker is pointing at a bare minimum and often nudging the listener's expectation. With time it is "no sooner than"; with quantity it is "no more than." In speech a light stress on 才 makes the smallness or the lateness vivid, and the sentence carries a faint note of surprise, complaint, or reassurance depending on context.

才 cái only; merely; no more than
ADV 副词 fùcí
The adverb before a quantity, marking it as smaller than expected: 才五块钱 "only five yuan," 才三个人 "only three people." Often colored by surprise that the amount is so low.
这件衣服才二十块钱。
Zhè jiàn yīfu cái èrshí kuài qián.
This piece of clothing is only twenty yuan.
她女儿才五岁。
Tā nǚ'ér cái wǔ suì.
Her daughter is only five years old.
刚才 gāngcái just now; a moment ago
N 名词 míngcí
刚 gāng (just; barely) + 才 cái. A time word for the very recent past, the moment that has only just passed: 刚才他还在这儿 "he was still here just now." Here 才 keeps its sense of a threshold only just crossed.
刚才谁给我打电话?
Gāngcái shéi gěi wǒ dǎ diànhuà?
Who just called me a moment ago?
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
多才多艺 duō cái duō yì of many talents and many skills: versatile, multitalented Lit: much-talent-much-skill. High praise for a person gifted in several arts at once, the painter who also writes and plays music. 艺 yì is craft or art, the paired half of 才.
才华横溢 cái huá héng yì talent brimming over: overflowing with brilliance Lit: talent-brilliance-spilling-overflowing. 才华 is creative or literary brilliance, and 横溢 paints it as water flooding past its banks. Said of someone whose gifts are too abundant to contain.
德才兼备 dé cái jiān bèi virtue and talent both complete: equally upright and able Lit: virtue-talent-both-possessed. The classic Confucian standard for choosing officials and leaders: ability alone is not enough, 德 (moral character) must come with it. The ideal candidate has both in full.
怀才不遇 huái cái bù yù to harbor talent yet meet no chance: gifted but unrecognized Lit: cradle-talent-not-encounter. The lament of the able person who never finds a ruler, patron, or era that will use their gifts. A recurring theme in classical poetry, where the unemployed scholar mourns his wasted ability.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a single sprout pushing up through the soil, the smallest first sign that something can grow. That sprout is the whole character. As a noun it is talent: the raw promise in a person waiting to unfold (人才, 天才, 才能). As an adverb it is the bare minimum just reached: the moment barely arrived, the amount barely there.

When 才 sits before a verb, hear "not until, only then": the thing came late or only after a struggle (八点才来, 努力才能成功). When it sits before a number, hear "only, merely": the amount is smaller than you thought (才五块, 才十岁). And remember its twin 就: where 才 says slow, late, hard, 就 says quick, early, easy. The little sprout always points at the least of a thing, just beginning to show.
