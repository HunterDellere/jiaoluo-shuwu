字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

The structure stacks two layers: 热 = 执 + 灬. The four dots along the bottom, 灬, are the squashed-flat form of 火 (huǒ, fire), the semantic radical that places this character squarely in the domain of burning and warmth. Above it sits 执 (zhí, to grasp), which carries the sound rather than the meaning, marking 热 as a classic pictophonetic compound.

The seal-script ancestor was busier than the modern glyph. It paired the fire element with a figure holding something over the flames, the older graph 埶 that fed both 执 and 势. Read the picture literally: a hand tending a fire, and the warmth that rises off it. As the script regularized through the clerical reform, the upper element simplified to today's 执 and the fire collapsed into its four-dot variant.

From the fire pit the meaning fanned outward. First the plain physical sense, hot to the touch, the heat of summer, water near boiling. Then the figurative reach: a fever burning in the body (发热 fārè), a crowd's energy boiling over (热闹 rènao), a person's ardor running high (热情 rèqíng). Modern Mandarin pushes it one step further to mean popular or trending, the way English calls a song or a stock hot. The same fire heats a kettle, a fever, and a fashion.

Japanese keeps the character as 熱 (netsu, atsui), preserving the fuller fire base in its standard form, and Korean reads it 열 (yeol) in compounds like 열정 (yeoljeong), the direct cognate of 热情.

温度wēndùHeat & Water
热水rèshuǐhot water
N 名词 míngcí
热 (hot) plus 水 (shuǐ, water). The everyday word for hot water, the staple drink offered to a guest and the cure a Chinese parent prescribes for almost anything.
多喝热水。
Duō hē rèshuǐ.
Drink more hot water.
家里没有热水了。
Jiā lǐ méiyǒu rèshuǐ le.
There's no more hot water at home.
热rèto be hot
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
On its own 热 is the bare adjective for hot weather or a hot object. It needs no 是; the character itself is the predicate, often softened or intensified by 很 (hěn), 太 (tài), or 真 (zhēn).
今天太热了。
Jīntiān tài rè le.
It's too hot today.
汤还很热，小心点。
Tāng hái hěn rè, xiǎoxīn diǎn.
The soup is still very hot, be careful.
辨析 biànxī · 热 vs. 暖 热 is high heat that can scald or oppress; 暖 (nuǎn) is gentle warmth that comforts. A radiator makes a room 暖; the desert sun makes it 热.
加热jiārèto heat up
V 动词 dòngcí
加 (jiā, to add) plus 热 (heat): to add heat, to warm something up. The verb on the microwave button and the recipe step.
把剩菜加热一下。
Bǎ shèngcài jiārè yíxià.
Heat up the leftovers.
人情rénqíngWarmth & Bustle
语义延伸 yǔyì yánshēn · Sense Extension

Carry the fire into human company and it becomes warmth of feeling and warmth of a crowd. 热情 is the heat of personal ardor and hospitality; 热闹 is the heat of a packed, lively, noisy scene. Both turn raw temperature into social energy, the difference between a cold formal room and a wedding banquet roaring with toasts.

热情rèqíngwarm, enthusiastic; warmth
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcíN 名词 míngcí
热 (hot) plus 情 (qíng, feeling): hot feeling. As an adjective it praises someone warm and welcoming; as a noun it names the enthusiasm itself.
这里的人都很热情。
Zhèlǐ de rén dōu hěn rèqíng.
The people here are all very warm.
他对工作充满热情。
Tā duì gōngzuò chōngmǎn rèqíng.
He is full of enthusiasm for his work.
辨析 biànxī · 热情 vs. 热心 热情 is warmth of manner and feeling; 热心 (rèxīn) is warmth shown through action, a willingness to help. A 热情 host greets you warmly; a 热心 neighbor carries your bags upstairs.
热闹rènaolively, bustling
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
热 (hot) plus 闹 (nào, noisy, to make a din): the heat of a busy, crowded, festive scene. Note the neutral tone on 闹 in this word. A market, a holiday street, a full restaurant are all 热闹.
春节的时候街上很热闹。
Chūnjié de shíhou jiē shàng hěn rènao.
During Spring Festival the streets are very lively.
我们去看看热闹。
Wǒmen qù kànkan rènao.
Let's go and see the excitement.
热门rèménpopular, in demand
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
热 (hot) plus 门 (mén, gate, field): a hot gateway, hence a popular field, trending topic, or in-demand option. The opposite is 冷门 (lěngmén), a cold and obscure one.
人工智能是现在的热门专业。
Réngōng zhìnéng shì xiànzài de rèmén zhuānyè.
Artificial intelligence is a popular major right now.
身体shēntǐFever & Body
发热fārèto run a fever; to give off heat
V 动词 dòngcí
发 (fā, to emit) plus 热 (heat): to give off heat. Of a body it means to run a fever; of a machine it means to overheat. The clinical register on a thermometer reading or a hospital chart.
孩子昨晚开始发热。
Háizi zuówǎn kāishǐ fārè.
The child started running a fever last night.
手机充电时有点发热。
Shǒujī chōngdiàn shí yǒudiǎn fārè.
The phone gets a bit hot while charging.
辨析 biànxī · 发热 vs. 发烧 发热 is the broader, more clinical term for raised temperature; 发烧 (fāshāo) is the colloquial word for running a fever, the one you say at home. 发热 also applies to overheating objects, 发烧 does not.
退热tuìrèto bring down a fever
V 动词 dòngcí
退 (tuì, to recede) plus 热 (heat, fever): to make the fever fall. The word on a fever-reducing medicine box and what a worried parent waits for through the night.
吃了药以后慢慢退热了。
Chī le yào yǐhòu mànman tuìrè le.
After taking the medicine the fever slowly came down.
热敷rèfūhot compress
VN 动名词 dòngmíngcí
热 (hot) plus 敷 (fū, to apply to the skin): to apply heat, a hot compress. The home remedy for a stiff neck or a sore back, the warm counterpart to 冷敷 (lěngfū), a cold compress.
肩膀酸，你热敷一下吧。
Jiānbǎng suān, nǐ rèfū yíxià ba.
Your shoulder aches; put a hot compress on it.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
热火朝天rè huǒ cháo tiānheat and fire toward the sky: a buzzing, full-tilt sceneThe image is a fire roaring up at the heavens. Used for a worksite, campaign, or rehearsal humming with energy. Common in news and everyday speech.
满腔热血mǎn qiāng rè xuèa chest full of hot blood: brimming with fervor满腔 a full chest, 热血 hot blood. Describes youthful idealism and the will to throw oneself into a cause. Warm and slightly literary in tone.
热泪盈眶rè lèi yíng kuànghot tears filling the eye sockets: moved to tears热泪 hot tears, 盈眶 brimming the eye rims. For the moment emotion wells up and the eyes flood. Used for reunions, awards, and stirring speeches.
趁热打铁chèn rè dǎ tiěstrike the iron while it's hot: seize the moment趁 while, 热 hot, 打铁 to forge iron. The blacksmith's wisdom, identical to the English proverb. Used to urge acting on momentum before it cools.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a blacksmith's hand, 执, gripping tongs over a forge, and under it four glowing embers, 灬, the four dots of flame. Heat shimmers off the iron. That single image holds the whole character: hand-on-fire, the upper 执 doing the gripping and lending the sound, the lower four dots burning.

Now let the forge throw its heat in three directions. Toward objects it is 热水 and 加热, water and food brought up to temperature. Toward people it is 热情 and 热闹, the warmth of a welcome and the buzz of a crowd. Toward the body it is 发热, the fever that makes a forehead burn. One fire, three kinds of heat.

When you reach for the word, hear the falling fourth tone drop like a hammer onto hot iron: rè, the clang of 趁热打铁, striking while it's hot.
