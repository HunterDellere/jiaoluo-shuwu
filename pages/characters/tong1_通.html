字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure: A Path With No Obstruction
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

通 tōng is a pictophonetic compound: the walk-movement radical 辶 (chuò, a foot moving along a road, abbreviated from 辵 which shows a foot and a crossroads) carries the meaning, while 甬 yǒng supplies the sound. The 辶 places 通 squarely in the family of motion characters, the same radical that drives 道 (the Way), 过 (to pass through), 近 (near), and 远 (far). Where those characters measure distance or name the path, 通 names a different quality of the path: that it is open, that movement along it meets no obstacle.

The phonetic 甬 is itself worth noticing. In its own right it named a covered walkway or a lane, a passage with walls on either side that channels movement straight through. So even the sound-bearing half of 通 carries an echo of corridors and through-routes. The combined character means to go through, to pass without being stopped, to get from one side to the other.

The Shuōwén Jiězì glosses 通 as 達也 (dá yě), to reach through, to arrive by passing through. From this root sense of unobstructed passage every later meaning radiates. A road that is 通 is open to traffic. A message that 通 has gotten through to its recipient. A person who is 通 in a subject has passed all the way through it and understands it thoroughly. And something that is 通 across many cases is general, common, the ordinary state that holds throughout. Passage, communication, mastery, and generality are one image seen from four sides: nothing blocks the way.

通 carries the same range across the Sinosphere. Japanese 通 (tsū / tsūjiru) gives 交通 kōtsū (traffic), 通信 tsūshin (communication), and 通 used alone for a connoisseur, someone who has passed all the way into a field. Korean 통 (tong) appears in 교통 (gyotong, traffic) and 통신 (tongsin, communication). Vietnamese thông survives in giao thông (traffic) and thông tin (information), direct borrowings of the Chinese compounds.

构词 gòucí Word-building Pattern: 通 as a Semantic Base
构词基础 gòucí jīchǔ · 通 as semantic base 通 (to go through) + 过 guò (to cross; to pass) = 通过 (to pass through; by means of)
交 jiāo (to exchange; to cross) + 通 = 交通 (traffic; transport)
通 + 知 zhī (to make known) = 通知 (to notify; a notice)
普 pǔ (widespread; universal) + 通 = 普通 (ordinary; common)
精 jīng (refined; thorough) + 通 = 精通 (to be expert in; to master)
The pattern: 通 contributes the idea of getting all the way through, whether through a road, a message, a subject, or every case alike.
通行 tōngxíng Going Through & Connecting: Passage and Transport
通过 tōngguò to pass through; to get past; by means of; via
V 动词
通 (to go through) + 过 guò (to cross; to pass). The everyday verb for getting through something: passing through a gate, passing an exam, a motion passing a vote. It is equally a function word meaning "by means of" or "via," introducing the channel through which an action happens: 通过努力 (through effort), 通过电话 (by phone). One of the highest-frequency 通 compounds, useful in both physical and abstract senses.
他终于通过了驾驶考试。
Tā zhōngyú tōngguò le jiàshǐ kǎoshì.
He finally passed the driving test.
我们通过努力解决了问题。
Wǒmen tōngguò nǔlì jiějué le wèntí.
We solved the problem through hard work.
交通 jiāotōng traffic; transport; transportation
N 名词
交 jiāo (to cross; to exchange; mutual) + 通 (to go through; to connect). The crossing-and-passing of people and vehicles: the whole system of movement through a place. 交通 covers traffic in the everyday sense (交通堵塞, a traffic jam) and transport as a category (交通工具, means of transport). The two halves reinforce each other: roads that cross and roads that run through together make up the network.
这个城市的交通很方便。
Zhège chéngshì de jiāotōng hěn fāngbiàn.
Transport in this city is very convenient.
早上的交通特别拥挤。
Zǎoshang de jiāotōng tèbié yōngjǐ.
The morning traffic is especially crowded.
通车 tōngchē to be open to traffic; to begin service (of a road or line)
VO 动宾
通 (to open; to be through) + 车 chē (vehicle). To be through to vehicles: the moment a new road, bridge, railway, or subway line opens and traffic can run along it. A staple of infrastructure news, where the opening of a line is itself an event: 正式通车 (officially opened to traffic). The verb captures the instant an obstructed route becomes an open one.
这条高速公路明年就能通车。
Zhè tiáo gāosù gōnglù míngnián jiù néng tōngchē.
This expressway will be open to traffic next year.
沟通 gōutōng to communicate; to bridge; to reach an understanding
V 动词
沟 gōu (a ditch; a channel; a conduit) + 通 (to connect; to make pass through). Originally to link two waterways by a channel; now overwhelmingly used for communication between people: to open a channel of understanding so meaning can flow back and forth. 沟通 stresses the two-way connection, the mutual reaching: 加强沟通 (strengthen communication), 缺乏沟通 (a lack of communication). A central word in workplaces, families, and counseling.
我们需要多沟通，才能理解彼此。
Wǒmen xūyào duō gōutōng, cái néng lǐjiě bǐcǐ.
We need to communicate more in order to understand each other.
良好的沟通是合作的基础。
Liánghǎo de gōutōng shì hézuò de jīchǔ.
Good communication is the foundation of cooperation.
通信 tōngxìn Notifying & Communicating: Getting a Message Across
通知 tōngzhī to notify; to inform; a notice; a notification
V/N 动词/名词
通 (to get through; to convey) + 知 zhī (to know; to make known). To make something known by getting word through: the standard word for an official notification and the act of giving it. Works as a verb (请通知大家, please notify everyone) and a noun (一份通知, a notice). The 通 stresses that the information has actually reached its recipient, not merely been sent.
学校通知我们明天放假。
Xuéxiào tōngzhī wǒmen míngtiān fàngjià.
The school notified us that tomorrow is a holiday.
我收到了面试通知。
Wǒ shōudào le miànshì tōngzhī.
I received the interview notice.
通信 tōngxìn to correspond; to communicate; telecommunications
V/N 动词/名词
通 (to connect; to convey) + 信 xìn (a letter; a message; trust). To get messages through to one another. Historically it meant to exchange letters: 我们通了多年信 (we corresponded for many years). In the modern era it has become the technical term for telecommunications: 通信技术 (communications technology), 通信卫星 (a communications satellite). The same word spans handwritten letters and fiber-optic networks.
他们靠书信通信了十年。
Tāmen kào shūxìn tōngxìn le shí nián.
They corresponded by letter for ten years.
现代通信技术发展得非常快。
Xiàndài tōngxìn jìshù fāzhǎn de fēicháng kuài.
Modern communications technology is developing very fast.
打通 dǎtōng to get through (a call); to open up (a passage); to clear a blockage
VC 动补
打 dǎ (to make; to strike; to do) + 通 (to get through). A resultative verb: to do something until it is through. Most often heard about phone calls (电话打通了, the call got through), but also used for clearing any obstructed passage, physical or figurative: 打通关节 (to clear the way through the right connections). The 通 is the result, the moment the obstruction gives way and the connection completes.
我打了好几次，电话终于打通了。
Wǒ dǎ le hǎojǐ cì, diànhuà zhōngyú dǎtōng le.
I called several times, and the call finally got through.
辨析 biànxī · the measure word reading tòng In the second tone-one reading 通 tòng serves as a measure word for letters, calls, and stretches of talking or scolding: 一通电话 (a phone call), 打了一通 (gave them an earful). This is a separate reading from the verb 打通 (dǎtōng) above, where 通 keeps its first-tone sense of getting through.
普通 pǔtōng General & Thorough: Ordinary, Usual, Expert
普通 pǔtōng ordinary; common; general; average
Adj 形容词
普 pǔ (widespread; universal) + 通 (general; running throughout). Widespread and holding everywhere: the quality of being ordinary, the common case rather than the special one. 普通人 (an ordinary person), 普通话 (the common spoken standard, Mandarin). 普通 is neutral, neither praise nor complaint; it simply locates something in the broad middle where most things sit.
他只是一个普通的上班族。
Tā zhǐ shì yīgè pǔtōng de shàngbānzú.
He is just an ordinary office worker.
请用普通话交流。
Qǐng yòng pǔtōnghuà jiāoliú.
Please communicate in Mandarin.
辨析 biànxī · 普通 vs 一般 Both mean "ordinary," but they lean differently. 普通 describes a thing as belonging to the common, widespread type: 普通人 (an ordinary person), 普通房间 (a standard room). 一般 (yībān) leans toward "average" or "so-so" as a judgment of degree: 他的成绩一般 (his grades are mediocre). You can call a meal 很普通 (quite ordinary) without criticism, but 很一般 nudges toward "nothing special."
通常 tōngcháng usually; ordinarily; generally; as a rule
Adj/Adv 形容词/副词
通 (general; holding throughout) + 常 cháng (constant; usual; frequent). The case that holds across most instances: usually, as the general rule. 通常 sets up the ordinary expectation against which exceptions are measured: 我通常七点起床 (I usually get up at seven). It reads as slightly more formal than 一般 and is common in writing and careful speech.
我通常周末在家看书。
Wǒ tōngcháng zhōumò zài jiā kàn shū.
I usually read at home on weekends.
通常情况下，会议在上午举行。
Tōngcháng qíngkuàng xià, huìyì zài shàngwǔ jǔxíng.
Under normal circumstances, the meeting is held in the morning.
精通 jīngtōng to be proficient in; to master; to have a thorough command of
V 动词
精 jīng (refined; essence; finely worked) + 通 (to be all the way through). To have passed thoroughly through a field down to its fine grain: full mastery, not mere acquaintance. 精通 is reserved for genuine expertise: 精通三门外语 (fluent in three foreign languages), 精通法律 (thoroughly versed in law). The 通 says you have reached the far side of the subject; the 精 says you did so with precision.
她精通中文和日文。
Tā jīngtōng Zhōngwén hé Rìwén.
She is fluent in Chinese and Japanese.
这位医生精通中医和西医。
Zhè wèi yīshēng jīngtōng zhōngyī hé xīyī.
This doctor has a thorough command of both Chinese and Western medicine.
通用 tōngyòng general-purpose; in common use; interchangeable
Adj 形容词
通 (general; running throughout) + 用 yòng (to use). Usable throughout, across cases: not specialized to one situation but serving generally. 通用 describes something accepted everywhere or applicable across the board: 全国通用 (valid nationwide), 通用规则 (a general rule that applies in all cases). It marks the opposite of the bespoke or the local.
这种货币在很多国家都通用。
Zhè zhǒng huòbì zài hěn duō guójiā dōu tōngyòng.
This currency is in common use in many countries.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
四通八达 sì tōng bā dá "open on four sides, reaching to eight": extending in all directions; well connected 四 sì (four) + 通 (open through) + 八 bā (eight) + 达 dá (to reach). Roads that are open through the four cardinal directions and reach out to the eight points of the compass. The phrase describes a place so well connected that one can travel from it anywhere: a transport hub, a crossroads city, a network with no dead ends. Used in praise of a city's location or an organization's reach. The four-and-eight pairing is a stock way of saying "every direction without exception."
通情达理 tōng qíng dá lǐ "through to feeling, reaching to reason": sensible, reasonable, understanding 通 (to be through to) + 情 qíng (human feeling; the way people are) + 达 dá (to reach) + 理 lǐ (reason; principle). A person who is 通情达理 understands both how people feel and what reason requires, and weighs the two fairly. It is high praise for someone you can argue with productively: not rigid, not merely emotional, but able to see a situation from all sides and respond justly. Often used of an elder, a boss, or a mediator who can be trusted to be fair.
融会贯通 róng huì guàn tōng "melt together, thread through": to master a subject by integrating all its parts 融 róng (to melt; to merge) + 会 huì (to come together) + 贯 guàn (to thread through; to run a string through) + 通 (to penetrate fully). To melt separate pieces of knowledge into one whole and thread a single understanding through all of them, so that the parts connect and the subject opens up completely. The phrase, associated with the learning method discussed by the Neo-Confucian philosopher Zhu Xi, describes the moment study stops being a heap of facts and becomes an integrated grasp. The ideal of deep, connected learning rather than rote accumulation.
水泄不通 shuǐ xiè bù tōng "not even water can leak through": packed solid; completely blocked or surrounded 水 shuǐ (water) + 泄 xiè (to leak; to drain out) + 不 bù (not) + 通 (to pass through). So densely packed that not even a drop of water could trickle out. The phrase describes a crowd, a traffic jam, or a siege so tight that nothing can get through: 挤得水泄不通 (jammed so tight not even water could leak through). It is the vivid negative of 通, the image of total obstruction set against the character's core sense of free passage.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

A foot setting off down a covered corridor that runs straight from one end to the other, with no door bolted across it and no wall built halfway. That is the picture inside 通: the walk-movement radical 辶 is the foot on the move, and the phonetic 甬 was itself a name for a walled passage that channels you straight through. Whatever the corridor leads to, the point is that you reach it. Nothing stops the way.

Hold that one image and every meaning falls out of it. A road that lets vehicles run from end to end is 通车, open to traffic; the whole crossing-and-running network is 交通. A phone call that finds its way to the person you dialed has 打通; a message that reaches its recipient is a 通知; people who open a channel of understanding between them 沟通. A person who has walked all the way through a subject down to its fine grain is 精通, a master of it. And what holds throughout, the common case running across all instances, is 普通, ordinary, or 通用, good everywhere.

The negative is just as sharp. When the corridor is jammed so tight that not even water could leak through, you have 水泄不通, the exact opposite of 通. Keep both ends in mind: the open corridor that lets everything pass, and the blockage that lets nothing. 通 is always the version where the way is clear.
