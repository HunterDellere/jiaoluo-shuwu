字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

改 = 己 + 攵. On the left, 己 (jǐ) sits as a small kneeling figure; on the right, 攵 is the old picture of a hand gripping a stick, the same striking element that drives 教 (to teach) and 政 (to govern). The character is a scene, not a sound-spelling: a raised rod comes down over a crouching child, and the child is made to be different than it was. The 攵 side is semantic, the action of forcing; the 己 side is semantic too, the small body that the action lands on.

The seal form keeps both parts clear, the bent kneeling shape on the left and the hand-and-rod on the right. From this disciplinary image the Shuōwén Jiězì reads 改 as 更也, to make over, to renew. The path from concrete to abstract is short here. A blow that reshapes a child becomes any deliberate act that reshapes a thing: a draft, a habit, a law, a route.

That origin still tilts the modern word. 改 is rarely passive drift. It carries an agent and an intention, often the sense of fixing what was wrong rather than merely swapping one state for another. To 改 a sentence is to correct it; to 改 a habit is to break and remake it; to 改 a name is to choose a new one on purpose. The stick is gone from daily speech, but the force of correction it implied is still inside the glyph.

Japanese carries 改 as かい / あらた-める (kai / aratameru), as in 改正 (kaisei, revision) and 改札 (kaisatsu, the ticket gate where a stub is altered). Korean reads it 개 (gae), in 개혁 (gaehyeok, reform) and 개정 (gaejeong, amendment). Across all three the word leans the same way, toward correcting and reforming rather than idle change.

变易biànyìTo Change, To Alter
改变gǎibiànto change, to transform
V/N 动名 dòngmíng
改 (alter) + 变 (change). The broadest everyday word for change: to make something different, or the difference itself. Works on situations, minds, fates, and plans.
这件事改变了我的一生。
Zhè jiàn shì gǎibiàn le wǒ de yìshēng.
This event changed the whole course of my life.
他不想改变主意。
Tā bù xiǎng gǎibiàn zhǔyì.
He doesn't want to change his mind.
辨析 biànxī · 改变 vs. 变化改变 needs an agent who acts on something; 变化 (biànhuà) is change that simply happens, observed from outside.
改善gǎishànto improve, to better
V/N 动名 dòngmíng
改 (alter) + 善 (good). To change toward the good: to better conditions, relations, or quality. Used for situations and systems more than for a person's skill.
政府努力改善居民的生活。
Zhèngfǔ nǔlì gǎishàn jūmín de shēnghuó.
The government is working hard to improve residents' lives.
辨析 biànxī · 改善 vs. 改进改善 betters a condition (relations, environment, living standards); 改进 (gǎijìn) betters a method or product through refinement.
改天gǎitiānanother day, some other time
Adv 副词 fùcí
改 (change) + 天 (day). To shift a plan to a different day. A soft, friendly way to postpone or to propose meeting later, very common in spoken Mandarin.
今天太忙了，咱们改天再聊吧。
Jīntiān tài máng le, zánmen gǎitiān zài liáo ba.
I'm too busy today; let's talk another time.
改正gǎizhèngTo Correct, To Revise
修改xiūgǎito revise, to amend, to edit
V 动词 dòngcí
修 (mend, trim) + 改 (alter). To go back over a text, plan, or law and revise it. The standard word for editing a document or amending a draft.
老师让我修改这篇作文。
Lǎoshī ràng wǒ xiūgǎi zhè piān zuòwén.
The teacher asked me to revise this essay.
这份合同还需要修改几个地方。
Zhè fèn hétóng hái xūyào xiūgǎi jǐ ge dìfang.
This contract still needs a few places revised.
辨析 biànxī · 修改 vs. 改正修改 reworks and polishes a whole text; 改正 targets a specific mistake and sets it right.
改正gǎizhèngto correct, to set right
V 动词 dòngcí
改 (alter) + 正 (correct, upright). To change a wrong thing into a right one: a mistake, an error, a fault. The 正 spells out the destination, correctness itself.
请改正你作业里的错误。
Qǐng gǎizhèng nǐ zuòyè lǐ de cuòwù.
Please correct the mistakes in your homework.
改成gǎichéngto change into, to turn into
V 动词 dòngcí
改 (alter) + 成 (become). A result complement: to alter A so that it becomes B. Names the new state explicitly after the verb.
把会议改成下午三点吧。
Bǎ huìyì gǎichéng xiàwǔ sān diǎn ba.
Let's move the meeting to three in the afternoon.
改革gǎigéTo Reform, To Remake
改革gǎigéreform; to reform
V/N 动名 dòngmíng
改 (alter) + 革 (hide, to change utterly). 革 is raw hide scraped and remade into leather, so 改革 is structural change: overhauling a system, an institution, or a policy. The headline word of 改革开放 (gǎigé kāifàng), the Reform and Opening era from 1978.
教育制度需要进一步改革。
Jiàoyù zhìdù xūyào jìnyíbù gǎigé.
The education system needs further reform.
辨析 biànxī · 改革 vs. 改良改革 overhauls the structure; 改良 (gǎiliáng) improves within it, refining a crop strain or a method without tearing it down.
改造gǎizàoto transform, to remold, to renovate
V 动词 dòngcí
改 (alter) + 造 (build, make). To rebuild something into a new form: renovating a building, retrofitting a machine, or, in older political language, reforming a person's thinking.
他们把老厂房改造成了书店。
Tāmen bǎ lǎo chǎngfáng gǎizào chéng le shūdiàn.
They renovated the old factory building into a bookstore.
改进gǎijìnto improve, to refine
V/N 动名 dòngmíng
改 (alter) + 进 (advance). To better a method, product, or process by moving it forward. The everyday word in engineering and management for incremental improvement.
我们不断改进产品的质量。
Wǒmen búduàn gǎijìn chǎnpǐn de zhìliàng.
We keep improving the quality of the product.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
改邪归正gǎi xié guī zhèngto change from evil and return to the right: to turn over a new leafA wrongdoer abandons crooked ways (邪) and comes back to the upright path (正). Common in novels and modern speech for a reformed offender.
知错就改zhī cuò jiù gǎirecognize a fault and at once correct itPraise for the rare person who, the moment they see their error, fixes it without excuse. Echoes the Confucian ideal of 过则勿惮改, do not fear to mend a fault.
改头换面gǎi tóu huàn miànchange the head and swap the face: a cosmetic makeoverUsually critical: the outside is altered while the rotten substance stays the same. Said of repackaged scams or superficial rebrands.
朝令夕改zhāo lìng xī gǎiissue an order at dawn and change it by duskFrom debates on Han fiscal policy. A sharp criticism of fickle, unstable rule whose constant reversals leave people unable to plan.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a small child, 己, kneeling on a packed-earth floor, head bowed over a writing slate. Behind the child stands a teacher whose right hand grips a thin bamboo rod, 攵, raised and ready. The rod taps once on the slate, the child rubs out the crooked stroke and draws it straight. That single corrective tap is 改: not the child changing on a whim, but a hand reaching in to set the line right.

Hold the two halves apart and the glyph reads itself. The 己 on the left is the thing being worked on, the draft, the habit, the mistake. The 攵 on the right is the hand that comes down to fix it. Wherever 改 appears, look for that hand and that intention. 改正 sets a wrong stroke straight, 修改 goes over the whole essay rod in hand, 改革 brings the rod down on an entire institution and remakes it like hide tanned into leather.

Keep the rod always pointed at a flaw. That is why 改天 feels gentle but 改革 feels heavy: both are the same correcting hand, one resetting a coffee date, the other resetting a country.
