字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

香 = 禾 + 日. The top is grain 禾, a stalk of millet or rice bent under the weight of its own ripe ear. Beneath it sits what is now written 日 (sun) but was originally 甘 (gān), sweetness: a mouth with a stroke marking something held on the tongue. Put the two together and you have the literal scene the character draws, the sweet smell rising off cooked or ripening grain.

The seal form keeps the picture honest. Grain above, the sweet mouth below, with no sun in it at all. The 甘 component flattened and squared over the centuries until scribes wrote it as the simpler 日, which is why the modern glyph looks like grain over a sun. The original logic was about taste, not daylight.

From that one cooking-pot image the meaning fanned out the way an aroma does. First fragrant, the smell of anything pleasant: flowers, perfume, fresh bread. Then delicious, since a good smell promises a good taste, so 吃得很香 means eating with real appetite. Then the burnt offering, an incense stick whose smoke carries scent upward to the ancestors. And finally, by metaphor, popular or well-liked, a person or thing everyone wants, as in 吃香 (chīxiāng), to be in demand.

香 is its own radical, heading the small family of scent characters such as 馨 (xīn, lingering fragrance). In Japanese it reads ka or kō and names both perfume and the incense ceremony 香道 (kōdō); in Korean it is hyang, the scent burned at the family rite.

气味qìwèiFragrance & Taste
用法 yòngfǎ · Usage

香 sits at the head of words for everything that smells or tastes good. As a standalone adjective it covers both senses at once: 这个菜很香 can mean the dish smells wonderful and that it is delicious to eat. Pair it with 甜 for the warm, mellow note of ripe fruit and cooked rice.

香味xiāngwèiaroma, fragrance, pleasant smell
N 名词 míngcí
香 (fragrant) + 味 (smell, flavor). The scent something gives off, especially of food or flowers. The everyday word for a good aroma.
厨房里飘出阵阵香味。
Chúfáng lǐ piāo chū zhènzhèn xiāngwèi.
Waves of delicious aroma drifted out of the kitchen.
香甜xiāngtiánsweet-smelling and tasty; sound (of sleep)
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
香 (fragrant) + 甜 (sweet). Of food, fragrant and sweet at once. By extension, of sleep, deep and contented, the way you sleep after a good meal.
这种苹果又香甜又多汁。
Zhè zhǒng píngguǒ yòu xiāngtián yòu duōzhī.
This kind of apple is fragrant, sweet, and juicy.
孩子睡得很香甜。
Háizi shuì de hěn xiāngtián.
The child is sleeping soundly and sweetly.
辨析 biànxī · 香甜 vs. 甜 甜 (tián) is plain sweetness on the tongue. 香甜 adds the smell and the warmth, and it alone can describe sleep.
香xiāngto smell good; to taste good; with appetite
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
On its own 香 carries the whole sense. As a complement after a verb of eating or sleeping it means doing it with relish: 吃得香 (chī de xiāng) is to eat heartily.
饿的时候，什么都吃得香。
È de shíhou, shénme dōu chī de xiāng.
When you are hungry, everything tastes good.
焚香fénxiāngIncense & Perfume

The same character that smells of ripe grain names the stick of incense burned at a temple or before the family altar. 香 here is a count noun for the joss stick itself, and the verb 烧 (shāo, to burn) or 上 (shàng, to offer) goes in front: 烧香拜佛 (shāo xiāng bài fó), to burn incense and worship the Buddha. The smoke is the scent made visible, carrying the offering upward.

烧香shāo xiāngto burn incense, to offer joss sticks
VO 动宾 dòngbīn
烧 (to burn) + 香 (incense). The ritual act of lighting incense at a temple, shrine, or grave. A separable verb-object: 烧了一炷香 (shāo le yí zhù xiāng), burned one stick of incense.
奶奶每月初一都去庙里烧香。
Nǎinai měi yuè chūyī dōu qù miào lǐ shāo xiāng.
Grandma goes to the temple to burn incense on the first of every month.
辨析 biànxī · separable verb 香 can be counted and modified inside the phrase: 烧三炷香 (shāo sān zhù xiāng), to burn three sticks of incense.
香水xiāngshuǐperfume, cologne
N 名词 míngcí
香 (fragrant) + 水 (water). Literally fragrant water, the modern word for bottled perfume. Measured with 瓶 (píng, bottle) or 滴 (dī, drop).
她身上的香水很好闻。
Tā shēnshang de xiāngshuǐ hěn hǎowén.
The perfume she is wearing smells lovely.
香烟xiāngyāncigarette
N 名词 míngcí
香 (fragrant) + 烟 (smoke). The everyday word for a cigarette, named for the scented smoke it gives off. Counted with 支 (zhī, stick) or 包 (bāo, pack).
这里不能抽香烟。
Zhèlǐ bù néng chōu xiāngyān.
You cannot smoke cigarettes here.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
鸟语花香niǎo yǔ huā xiāngbirds singing, flowers fragrant: a beautiful spring sceneA stock phrase for an idyllic countryside in spring, the birds talking and the blossoms in scent. Warm and common in description.
书香门第shū xiāng mén dìbook-fragrance household: a scholarly family书香 (the fragrance of books) once meant the herb used to keep insects out of libraries; the phrase praises a family of generations of scholars. Respectful, slightly formal.
怜香惜玉lián xiāng xī yùpity the fragrance, cherish the jade: to be tender toward women香 (fragrance) and 玉 (jade) both stand for a beautiful woman. To treat women with gentleness and care. Literary, sometimes ironic.
古色古香gǔ sè gǔ xiāngancient color, ancient fragrance: full of old-world charmOf architecture, furniture, or art that carries the flavor of antiquity. A compliment for anything with classical elegance.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture the moment that built the character. A stalk of ripe grain 禾, heavy and golden, is laid across the top of a steaming clay pot. Below it a mouth holds a single grain of rice and finds it 甘, sweet. The two stacked together are the whole word: the warm, sweet smell rising off cooked rice. Read 香 top to bottom and you are reading that scene, grain over sweetness.

Hold that rising smoke in mind and every sense follows it. The same fragrant column is the incense stick at the altar in 烧香, the bottled scent in 香水, the curl off a cigarette in 香烟. Let the steam carry your appetite and you reach 香甜, the food so good you sleep soundly after it.

One last turn. When a smell is this good, everyone leans toward it. That is 吃香, to be wanted, the aroma a crowd cannot resist. Grain over a sweet mouth, scent rising, a room full of people who all want a taste.
