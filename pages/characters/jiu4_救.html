字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

救 splits cleanly down the middle: 救 = 求 + 攵. On the left sits 求 (qiú), the phonetic; on the right sits 攵, a hand gripping a stick, the action radical that drives so many verbs of forceful doing (改 to alter, 攻 to attack, 放 to release). The right side carries the meaning, an arm in motion; the left side carries the sound and, by happy accident, a fitting echo.

That echo runs deep. 求 in its oldest forms pictures a fur garment, and very early it was borrowed to write the verb "to seek, to beg, to plead." So the two halves of 救 read almost like a small drama: a plea on the left, a striking hand on the right. Someone cries out; a hand reaches in and acts. The Shuōwén Jiězì glosses 救 as 止也, "to stop, to halt," the act of arresting a disaster before it finishes its work.

From that physical core, to halt a calamity by force of hand, the sense fanned outward. First the literal: pull a drowning child from the river, beat out a fire. Then the social: relieve a famine, deliver a kingdom, salvage a sinking reputation. The hand never disappears from the word; even in abstract uses like 救国 (jiùguó, to save the nation), the verb still implies decisive intervention, not mere wishing.

Japanese keeps the character as 救 (kyū / suku-u), the second reading meaning "to rescue, to save," as in 救う. Korean reads it 구 (gu), surfacing in 구조 (救助, rescue). Across all three the image holds steady: an arm thrust into danger to drag a life back out.

救命jiùmìngSaving Life · The Cry for Help
救命jiùmìnghelp! / to save a life
VO 动宾 dòngbīn
救 (rescue) + 命 (mìng, life). Literally "save a life." As a shout it is the standard Chinese cry of distress, the equivalent of "Help!" As a verb-object phrase it describes the act of saving someone from death.
救命！有人落水了！
Jiùmìng! Yǒu rén luòshuǐ le!
Help! Someone has fallen into the water!
是这位医生救了我的命。
Shì zhè wèi yīshēng jiùle wǒ de mìng.
It was this doctor who saved my life.
救人jiùrénto save / rescue someone
VO 动宾 dòngbīn
救 (rescue) + 人 (rén, person). The general act of pulling a person out of danger, whether from water, fire, illness, or ruin.
他跳进河里救人，自己却没能上岸。
Tā tiào jìn hé lǐ jiùrén, zìjǐ què méi néng shàng'àn.
He jumped into the river to save someone, but could not get back to shore himself.
辨析 biànxī · 救人 vs. 帮人 帮人 (bāng rén) is everyday help with any task; 救人 implies the person is in real danger and the stakes are life or ruin.
救火jiùhuǒto fight a fire
VO 动宾 dòngbīn
救 (rescue) + 火 (huǒ, fire). Counterintuitively this means "to put out a fire," not to save the fire; here 救 keeps its Shuōwén sense of 止, halting the disaster. The same logic gives 救灾 (jiùzāi, disaster relief).
消防员连夜赶去救火。
Xiāofángyuán liányè gǎn qù jiùhuǒ.
The firefighters rushed through the night to fight the blaze.
急救jíjiùEmergency Aid · Rescue in Crisis

救 anchors the vocabulary of urgent intervention. Pair it with 急 (jí, urgent) and you get first aid; pair it with 援 (yuán, to assist) and you get organized rescue; pair it with 护 (hù, to protect) and 车 (chē, vehicle) and you get the ambulance, 救护车.

急救jíjiùfirst aid; emergency treatment
N 名词 míngcíV 动词 dòngcí
急 (urgent) + 救 (rescue). Immediate medical care given on the spot before a patient reaches the hospital, and by extension any urgent stopgap measure.
每个人都应该学一点急救知识。
Měi gè rén dōu yīnggāi xué yìdiǎn jíjiù zhīshi.
Everyone should learn a little first-aid knowledge.
救援jiùyuánto rescue; rescue operation
V/N 动名 dòngmíng
救 (rescue) + 援 (yuán, to aid, to reach a hand). A coordinated, often large-scale rescue effort: teams, equipment, logistics. The default word for earthquake and mountain rescue.
地震发生后，救援队第一时间赶到现场。
Dìzhèn fāshēng hòu, jiùyuán duì dì-yī shíjiān gǎndào xiànchǎng.
After the earthquake struck, the rescue team reached the scene at the first possible moment.
辨析 biànxī · 救援 vs. 救助 救援 stresses the active operation of reaching the endangered; 救助 (jiùzhù) leans toward sustained relief and material support for the vulnerable.
救护车jiùhùchēambulance
N 名词 míngcí
救 (rescue) + 护 (hù, protect) + 车 (chē, vehicle). The "rescue-and-protect vehicle." Hear a siren in a Chinese city and this is the word.
快打120叫救护车！
Kuài dǎ yāo-èr-líng jiào jiùhùchē!
Quick, call 120 for an ambulance!
拯救zhěngjiùTo Deliver · Lift Out of Ruin
拯救zhěngjiùto save, to deliver
V 动词 dòngcí
拯 (zhěng, to lift up out of) + 救 (rescue). A weightier, more literary "save," used for deliverance on a grand or moral scale: saving a soul, a people, a planet.
这部电影讲的是一个普通人拯救世界的故事。
Zhè bù diànyǐng jiǎng de shì yí gè pǔtōng rén zhěngjiù shìjiè de gùshi.
This film tells the story of an ordinary person who saves the world.
辨析 biànxī · 拯救 vs. 救 Bare 救 is everyday and physical; 拯救 is elevated and sweeping, reserved for deliverance from large or abstract peril.
挽救wǎnjiùto save, to retrieve
V 动词 dòngcí
挽 (wǎn, to pull back) + 救 (rescue). To salvage something already sliding toward loss: a failing marriage, a patient's life, a tottering situation. The emphasis is on reversing a decline.
医生尽了全力，却没能挽救他的生命。
Yīshēng jìnle quánlì, què méi néng wǎnjiù tā de shēngmìng.
The doctors did everything they could, yet could not save his life.
救济jiùjìto relieve, give relief
V/N 动名 dòngmíng
救 (rescue) + 济 (jì, to aid, to cross over). Material relief for the poor or disaster-struck: food, money, shelter. This is 救 in its social-welfare register.
政府向灾区发放了救济物资。
Zhèngfǔ xiàng zāiqū fāfàngle jiùjì wùzī.
The government distributed relief supplies to the disaster area.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
见死不救jiàn sǐ bù jiùto see someone dying and not save themThe harshest social indictment in the 救 family: standing by while another perishes. Used today to condemn indifference, whether a bystander ignoring an accident or an institution refusing aid.
救死扶伤jiù sǐ fú shāngsave the dying, aid the wounded救 (save) the 死 (dying), 扶 (support) the 伤 (wounded). The classic motto of medicine and the Red Cross spirit, naming the doctor's and rescuer's duty in four characters.
救苦救难jiù kǔ jiù nànto relieve suffering and rescue from perilA Buddhist phrase, the standing epithet of the bodhisattva 观音 (Guānyīn), 救苦救难 (one who relieves the suffering and saves from calamity). Now also figurative for any savior figure.
远水救不了近火yuǎn shuǐ jiù bù liǎo jìn huǒdistant water cannot put out a nearby fireFrom the Hán Fēizǐ. Help too far away is useless against an urgent crisis; a homely proverb on the gap between resources and immediate need.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a person waist-deep in a flooding river, one arm flung up out of the water, mouth open in a plea. That raised, pleading arm is 求 on the left of the character, the cry for help frozen into the phonetic. On the bank stands a rescuer, and in their hand a long pole reaching out across the current. That is 攵 on the right, the striking hand, the action that does the saving.

Hold those two halves together and the whole word becomes a single gesture: plea meets reaching hand. 救 = 求 + 攵. The Shuōwén gloss 止, "to halt," is exactly what the pole does, it stops the drowning before the water finishes its work.

Run the rescuer's pole through every compound and the field opens up. Reach it toward a sinking 命 (life) and you get 救命; toward a burning 火 (fire) and you get 救火; toward a wounded stranger in 急救; toward a whole nation in 拯救. Wherever the pole reaches, 救 follows: a hand thrust into danger to drag a life back to the bank.
