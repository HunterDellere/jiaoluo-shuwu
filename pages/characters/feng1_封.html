字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

封 sets 圭 (guī) beside 寸 (cùn): ⿰圭寸. The 寸 on the right is a hand with a wrist-pulse marked, the standard graph for a deliberate manual act. The left side traces back through doubled 土 (tǔ, earth): the seal and bronze forms show a hand patting soil into a raised mound. The character records a single physical gesture, heaping earth by hand to mark and close a boundary.

The oldest sense is territorial. To 封 a piece of land was to raise a mound of packed earth along its edge, often planting a tree on top, so that the line of ownership stood visible on the ground. From that mound came the political sense: a ruler who granted territory was said to 封 a lord, fixing the borders of a fief and the rank that came with it. 分封 (fēnfēng), the carving of the Zhōu realm into hereditary domains, is built on exactly this image.

The everyday modern sense, to seal shut, is the same gesture turned inward. Where the feudal lord closed off a border with earth, an ordinary writer closes off a letter with a flap of paper. 封 covers both the act (to seal, to block) and the object that holds the sealing (信封, envelope; one 封 of mail). The measure-word use, 一封信 (yì fēng xìn, one letter), preserves the idea that each sealed packet is a single closed unit.

Japanese keeps the graph as 封 (fū / hō), used in 封筒 (envelope) and 封建 (feudal). Korean reads it 봉 (bong) in 봉투 (envelope) and 밀봉 (hermetic seal). Across all three, the thread holds: a hand closing something off so it stays shut.

信件xìnjiànEnvelope & Letter
信封xìnfēngenvelope
N 名词 míngcí
信 (xìn, letter) plus 封 (the sealing/wrapper): the paper sleeve that closes around a letter. The most common everyday word built on 封.
请把地址写在信封上。
Qǐng bǎ dìzhǐ xiě zài xìnfēng shàng.
Please write the address on the envelope.
封面fēngmiàncover (of a book or magazine)
N 名词 míngcí
封 (the closing layer) plus 面 (miàn, face / surface): the outer face that wraps and closes a book. By extension, the front cover of any publication.
这本杂志的封面很吸引人。
Zhè běn zázhì de fēngmiàn hěn xīyǐn rén.
This magazine's cover is very eye-catching.
辨析 biànxī · 封面 vs. 表面 封面 is specifically a book or magazine cover; 表面 (biǎomiàn) is the surface of anything, often figurative ("on the surface").
一封信yì fēng xìnone letter
MW 量词 liàngcí
封 also works as the measure word for letters and other sealed correspondence: each sealed packet counts as one 封. 一封信 is one letter; 一封电子邮件 (yì fēng diànzǐ yóujiàn), one email.
我收到了一封来自老朋友的信。
Wǒ shōudào le yì fēng láizì lǎo péngyou de xìn.
I received a letter from an old friend.
封闭fēngbìSealing Shut
封闭fēngbìto seal off; closed, sealed
V/Adj 动 / 形
封 (to seal) plus 闭 (bì, to shut): to close something off completely. Used for sealing a road, a building, a wound, or, figuratively, a closed-off mindset.
这条路因为施工被封闭了。
Zhè tiáo lù yīnwèi shīgōng bèi fēngbì le.
This road has been sealed off because of construction.
他的思想比较封闭。
Tā de sīxiǎng bǐjiào fēngbì.
His thinking is rather closed-off.
密封mìfēngto seal hermetically; airtight
V/Adj 动 / 形
密 (mì, tight / dense) plus 封 (to seal): to seal tightly so nothing leaks in or out. The word for airtight packaging, vacuum jars, and sealed envelopes.
食物要密封保存才不会变质。
Shíwù yào mìfēng bǎocún cái bú huì biànzhì.
Food must be kept airtight so it won't spoil.
封锁fēngsuǒto blockade; to seal off
V/N 动 / 名
封 (to seal) plus 锁 (suǒ, to lock): to seal and lock down, blocking passage or flow. Used for military blockades, sealed-off areas, and information blackouts.
军队封锁了整个城市。
Jūnduì fēngsuǒ le zhěnggè chéngshì.
The army blockaded the entire city.
辨析 biànxī · 封锁 vs. 封闭 封锁 stresses active enforcement against passage (a blockade, a news blackout); 封闭 is the plainer "closed off / sealed," with no sense of standing guard.
册封cèfēngConferring Rank
语境 yǔjìng · Historical Sense

The feudal sense is alive in formal and historical Chinese. To 封 someone is to grant them a title, a fief, or a rank from above. 分封制 (fēnfēng zhì) names the Zhōu system of parcelling out land to kinsmen and allies; 册封 (cèfēng) is the formal investiture of a noble or consort by imperial decree. The grammar still carries the old direction: rank flows downward from the one who 封s to the one who is 封ed.

分封fēnfēngto enfeoff; to grant fiefs
V 动词 dòngcí
分 (fēn, to divide) plus 封 (to confer land): to carve a realm into fiefs and grant them to vassals. The defining act of the early Zhōu political order.
周王把土地分封给诸侯。
Zhōu wáng bǎ tǔdì fēnfēng gěi zhūhóu.
The Zhōu king granted land as fiefs to the feudal lords.
封建fēngjiànfeudal; feudalistic
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
封 (to grant fiefs) plus 建 (jiàn, to establish): the granting and establishing of domains, hence "feudal." In modern speech it also means old-fashioned or socially conservative.
他的想法太封建了。
Tā de xiǎngfǎ tài fēngjiàn le.
His ideas are far too old-fashioned.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
故步自封gù bù zì fēngto confine oneself to old ways: to be complacent and refuse to progressLiterally "wall yourself in with your old footsteps." 封 here is the self-imposed boundary. A common criticism of stagnant institutions or people who resist new ideas.
封官许愿fēng guān xǔ yuànto dole out titles and make promises: to buy support with offers of office封官 is to confer official posts; 许愿 is to promise rewards. Used critically of someone who secures loyalty through promised positions rather than merit.
封妻荫子fēng qī yìn zǐtitles for the wife, privilege for the sons: rank and honor extended to one's familyThe reward of high office under the empire: a man's success earned a title for his wife and protected status for his sons. Evokes the prestige of the old examination-and-rank system.
原封不动yuán fēng bù dòngthe original seal unmoved: left exactly as it was, untouched原封 is the original seal on a package. To leave something 原封不动 is to return or keep it intact, never opened or altered. Everyday modern usage.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

See a lord kneeling at the edge of his new land at dusk. He scoops up a double handful of damp earth, 圭 of soil, and pats it into a low mound along the border with his own hand, 寸. The cool grit packs under his palm; he presses until the line of ownership stands raised and unmistakable. That heap of earth, closed by hand, is 封.

Now shrink the gesture. The same hand that mounded a border later licks a flap and presses it down over a letter: 信封, the envelope. The same closing motion shuts a road (封闭), locks down a city (封锁), and squeezes a jar airtight (密封). Whether the thing sealed is a kingdom's edge or a single sheet of paper, 封 is always one hand pressing a boundary shut.

Hold the two extremes together and the character locks in: a fief granted from above and an envelope licked at the kitchen table are the same verb. To 封 is to close something off so it stays closed, then hand it on.
