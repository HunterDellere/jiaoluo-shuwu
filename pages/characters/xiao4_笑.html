字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

笑 stacks two parts: ⺮ (bamboo) sits on top of 夭 yāo (a person with the head tilted, the body bent at the waist). 笑 = ⺮ + 夭. The shape is a figure bowed over beneath a crown of bamboo leaves, and that double picture is exactly why the character has provoked argument for a thousand years.

The Shuōwén Jiězì does not give 笑 a clean entry, and the gap opened a debate that the Song scholar Xú Xuàn made famous: does the bamboo bend because a person doubles over laughing, or does a person bend the way bamboo bends when wind rakes through a grove? The Tang annotator Lǐ Yángbīng argued for the rustling reeds; later commentators preferred the body convulsed with mirth. Neither reading was ever settled, which is rare for so common a glyph.

What is firm is the lived image. To laugh hard is to lose the upright spine: the shoulders drop, the head pitches forward, the whole frame folds the way a green bamboo stalk folds and springs back. 夭 already carries the sense of a supple, bending body, and the bamboo above lends both the sound and the swaying. The sense never had to travel far from concrete to abstract, because the posture of laughing and the posture of a wind-bent reed are the same posture.

Japanese keeps the character as 笑 (read warau, "to laugh") and Korean reads it so (소), both holding the bamboo-over-bending-person shape unchanged across the writing systems.

笑声xiàoshēngKinds of Laughter: Warm vs. Mocking
语气梯度 yǔqì tīdù · From a Quiet Smile to a Sneer 微笑 wēixiào → a faint, gentle smile; no sound, lips only
笑 xiào → to laugh or smile, the neutral base word
大笑 dàxiào → to laugh loudly, the whole body in it
嘲笑 cháoxiào → to laugh at someone; mockery, contempt
微笑wēixiàoto smile; a smile
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
微 wēi (faint; slight; minute) + 笑 xiào (laugh/smile). A small, soundless laugh: the smile. The word that names the warm, closed-lipped expression, distinct from the open-mouthed 大笑.
她对我微微一笑。
Tā duì wǒ wēiwēi yí xiào.
She gave me a faint little smile.
他脸上总带着微笑。
Tā liǎn shàng zǒng dàizhe wēixiào.
He always wears a smile on his face.
可笑kěxiàolaughable; ridiculous; ludicrous
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
可 kě (worthy of; -able) + 笑 xiào (laugh). Worthy of being laughed at: ridiculous, absurd. Built on the same 可 + verb pattern as 可爱 (worthy of love → cute) and 可怕 (worthy of fear → frightening). Often carries a sting.
他的借口很可笑。
Tā de jièkǒu hěn kěxiào.
His excuse is laughable.
嘲笑cháoxiàoto mock; to ridicule; to jeer at
V 动词 dòngcí
嘲 cháo (to taunt; to deride) + 笑 xiào (laugh). To laugh at someone with scorn. This is the cruel edge of 笑: the same sound aimed downward at a target. Takes a direct object, the person mocked.
同学们嘲笑他的口音。
Tóngxuémen cháoxiào tā de kǒuyīn.
His classmates mocked his accent.
辨析 biànxī · 嘲笑 vs. 微笑 微笑 is warmth turned outward; 嘲笑 is contempt turned downward. The shared 笑 is why a smile and a sneer can look so alike, and why "他笑了" alone leaves you guessing which one it was.
玩笑wánxiàoJokes & Teasing: Play With Words
笑话xiàohuaa joke; to laugh at (someone)
N/V 名动词 míngdòngcí
笑 xiào (laugh) + 话 huà (speech; words). Laugh-words: a joke, a funny story. As a verb it flips to "to laugh at, to make a fool of." 讲笑话 = to tell a joke; 闹笑话 = to make an embarrassing blunder that others laugh at.
他讲了一个笑话，大家都笑了。
Tā jiǎngle yí ge xiàohua, dàjiā dōu xiào le.
He told a joke and everyone laughed.
别笑话我，我是认真的。
Bié xiàohua wǒ, wǒ shì rènzhēn de.
Don't laugh at me, I'm serious.
玩笑wánxiàoa joke; a prank; banter
N 名词 míngcí
玩 wán (to play) + 笑 xiào (laugh). Play-laughter: a joke made between people who are at ease together. 开玩笑 kāi wánxiào (to crack a joke, to be kidding) is one of the highest-frequency phrases in spoken Mandarin.
我只是开个玩笑，别生气。
Wǒ zhǐshì kāi ge wánxiào, bié shēngqì.
I was just joking, don't be angry.
辨析 biànxī · 玩笑 vs. 笑话 玩笑 is the shared, friendly kind, banter you make with someone; 笑话 is a packaged joke you tell, or, as a verb, laughter aimed at someone. Open the first, but try not to become the second.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
哈哈大笑hā hā dà xiàoto roar with laughter: a full belly laugh哈哈 is the sound of laughter itself, 大笑 a big laugh. The everyday, vivid way to say someone burst out laughing. Common in narration and casual speech alike.
谈笑风生tán xiào fēng shēngto chat and laugh with verve: lively, easy conversationLit: talk-laugh wind-rises, as if a fresh breeze sprang up from the talk. Describes a person who holds a room with relaxed, witty conversation. A warm compliment to a host or a speaker.
见笑jiàn xiàoto be laughed at: forgive my poor effortLit: be-seen laughed-at. A set humble phrase: 让您见笑了 ("I've let you laugh at me") deflects praise for one's own work or skill. Polite, slightly formal register.
笑里藏刀xiào lǐ cáng dāoa dagger hidden in a smile: friendly on the surface, treacherous beneathLit: laugh-inside hide-knife. Traced to the Tang minister Lǐ Yìfǔ, whose mild smile masked a ruthless heart. Names a person whose warmth is a weapon, the dark twin of 微笑.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a stand of green bamboo on a hillside. A gust comes through and the whole grove bows in one motion, every slim stalk folding at the waist and whipping back up, leaves hissing. Now picture a person who has just heard something irresistibly funny: the head drops forward, the shoulders shake, the spine gives way at exactly the same hinge. That is the glyph. ⺮ (bamboo) rides on top, 夭 (the bent body) underneath. The whole character is one body folding over.

Hang the compounds off that bend. A 微 (faint) fold is a 微笑, a smile barely creasing the lips. A 大 (big) fold is 大笑, doubled over and loud. Add 话 (words) and you get 笑话, the joke that triggers the fold; add 玩 (play) and you get 玩笑, the friendly banter that makes a whole table fold together. Once you see the wind moving through the bamboo, every word in the family is just a different strength of gust.
