字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

哥 = 可 + 可, the same element written twice and stacked vertically. The lower 可 sits beneath the upper one, so the glyph reads like an echo: one breath of sound returned a second time. Each 可 already contains a 口 (mouth), which is why the official radical of the whole character is 口.

可 began as a phonetic loan. In the seal script it depicts a curved breath rising past a mouth, and it was borrowed first to write the sound now heard in 歌 (gē, to sing). Stack two of them and you get the older written form of that song-sound. For a long stretch 哥 was simply a way of writing what we now spell 歌, the act of chanting or singing, before 歌 took on its own 欠 (gaping mouth) signific and split off.

The kinship sense arrived later and won. By the Tang dynasty 哥 had been pulled into spoken address for an older male, and the doubling fit the use: a sound you call out, repeated, the way a child calls 哥哥 (gēge) across a courtyard. The character that once stored a song now stores the warmest title in the household.

Japanese keeps the older trail visible. 哥 survives there chiefly inside 哥薩克 and a handful of literary spellings, while everyday "elder brother" went to 兄. Mandarin took the opposite road and let the doubled 可 own the family.

兄弟xiōngdìThe Sibling Words
哥 ranks siblings by age. 哥哥 is any elder brother; prefix a number and you fix his exact place in the line. 大哥 is the eldest, 二哥 the second, 三哥 the third, counting down toward 弟弟 (dìdi), the younger brother.
哥哥gēgeelder brother
N 名词 míngcí
The base word: 哥 doubled, the second syllable toned down to neutral. Standard everyday term for one's own older brother, and a child can also use it for an unrelated older boy.
我哥哥比我大三岁。
Wǒ gēge bǐ wǒ dà sān suì.
My elder brother is three years older than me.
哥哥，等等我！
Gēge, děngdeng wǒ!
Brother, wait for me!
大哥dàgēeldest brother; big bro
N 名词 míngcí
大 (big) + 哥. Literally the eldest brother in the family. By extension it addresses the leader of a group, the boss of a gang, or any older man you want to flatter into helping you.
大哥，这个多少钱？
Dàgē, zhège duōshao qián?
Hey big bro, how much is this?
辨析 biànxī · 大哥 vs. 老大大哥 is respectful and friendly; 老大 (lǎodà) leans toward the underworld "boss" and is rougher in tone.
二哥èrgēsecond eldest brother
N 名词 míngcí
二 (two) + 哥. The second of the brothers, ranked after 大哥. The numbering system is literal: each older brother is addressed by his birth order, never by name within the family.
二哥在北京工作。
Èrgē zài Běijīng gōngzuò.
My second-eldest brother works in Beijing.
哥们儿gēmenrbuddy, bro, mate
N 名词 míngcí
哥 + 们 (plural) + the Beijing 儿 ending. Close male friends, sworn-brother style. Singular or plural, it marks loyalty between guys; northern and casual, common in Beijing speech.
咱们是哥们儿，别客气。
Zánmen shì gēmenr, bié kèqi.
We're buddies, no need to stand on ceremony.
称呼chēnghuSocial Titles
用法 yòngfǎ · How It Works

哥 reaches far outside blood kinship. Chinese address strangers as relatives constantly, and a young man slightly older than you is a 哥 by courtesy. Attach it to a surname, 王哥 (Wáng gē), and you have a warm, deferential way to call a coworker. Attach it to a quality, 帅哥 (shuàigē), and you have a compliment you can shout across a street.

帅哥shuàigēhandsome guy; hey, handsome
N 名词 míngcí
帅 (handsome) + 哥. A good-looking young man, and also a friendly way to flag down any young man whose name you don't know, the male counterpart of 美女 (měinǚ).
帅哥，可以帮我拍张照吗？
Shuàigē, kěyǐ bāng wǒ pāi zhāng zhào ma?
Hey handsome, could you take a photo for me?
辨析 biànxī · 帅哥 vs. 美女帅哥 hails a man, 美女 (měinǚ) hails a woman; both are light, friendly, and used freely with strangers in service settings.
大哥大dàgēdàbrick phone; top boss
N 名词 míngcí
大哥 (big brother) + 大 again. First a nickname for the 1980s brick mobile phone, the status symbol of bosses; by extension the kingpin or undisputed number one in any field.
他是这个行业的大哥大。
Tā shì zhège hángyè de dàgēdà.
He's the kingpin of this industry.
表哥biǎogēolder male cousin
N 名词 míngcí
表 (maternal/external) + 哥. An older male cousin on the mother's side, or through a paternal aunt. The 表 prefix marks cousins outside the father's patriline, distinct from 堂哥 (tánggē).
这是我表哥，他在上海读书。
Zhè shì wǒ biǎogē, tā zài Shànghǎi dúshū.
This is my cousin; he studies in Shanghai.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
哥 is a colloquial Tang-era word, so it stays out of the classical four-character chengyu, where "brother" is always 兄 (称兄道弟, 长兄如父). The fixed expressions that do carry 哥 are spoken set phrases, the kind a friendship runs on.
哥俩好gē liǎ hǎothe two of us are tight: a toast and a drinking-game callA finger-guessing drinking call and a stock phrase for two close male friends. 俩 (liǎ) is "the two of us," so the whole thing means "we two brothers are solid."
铁哥们儿tiě gēmenriron buddies: rock-solid male friends铁 (iron) intensifies 哥们儿. The metaphor is metal: a friendship that won't bend or rust. Reserved for the few friends you would drop everything for.
大哥别说二哥dàgē bié shuō èrgēthe eldest shouldn't mock the second: pot calling the kettle blackA proverb on shared flaws: 大哥 and 二哥 are equally at fault, so neither has standing to criticize the other. The Mandarin take on glass houses.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a younger child standing at the bottom of a stairwell, calling up: 哥! 哥! The cry goes out once, and a second time, an echo. That doubled call is the glyph: two 可 stacked one above the other, the lower one answering the upper, the small 口 mouth on each one open and sounding.

Now let the echo widen. The same call climbs out of the stairwell and into the street, where any older boy answers to it: the handsome stranger becomes 帅哥, the man behind the counter becomes 王哥, the leader of the pack becomes 大哥. Every one of these is the same doubled cry aimed at a wider circle. 哥 is the sound you send upward to someone older, repeated until it lands.
