字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

如 rú joins 女 (nǚ, woman) and 口 (kǒu, mouth). The two parts sit side by side: 女 on the left, 口 on the right. The traditional and simplified forms are identical, so the same glyph stands in both script slots above. The Shuōwén reads the early sense as "to follow, to comply with a command," the picture of someone hearing words from the mouth and acting in accordance with them.

From "to comply with, to accord with" the meaning generalized outward. To accord with a thing is to match it, to be like it; and to suppose a thing accords is to set a condition. So one verb of obedience spread into the three jobs 如 does today:

1. Like, as (comparison). One thing accords with another, resembles it: 如同 rútóng (just like), 犹如 yóurú (just as), 例如 lìrú (for example), 如此 rúcǐ (like this, so).
2. If, supposing (condition). Suppose the case accords: 如果 rúguǒ (if), 假如 jiǎrú (supposing), 如何 rúhé (how, in what way).
3. To be as good as, according to (measure). To come up to a standard or follow a wish: 不如 bùrú (not as good as), 如意 rúyì (as one wishes).

The thread through every sense is accord. Whether the page says two things are alike, sets up a supposed case, or measures one thing against another, 如 is the word for one thing lining up with another. It is one of the highest-frequency function words in the language, the workhorse of comparison and the opening of countless conditional sentences.

如同 rútóng Like & As: Comparison
如同 rútóng just like; the same as
V 动词 dòngcí
如 rú (like) + 同 tóng (same). A slightly literary way to say one thing is just like another, common in description and writing: 如同白昼 "as bright as day." In speech 好像 hǎoxiàng is the everyday equivalent.
这里的夜晚亮如同白昼。
Zhèlǐ de yèwǎn liàng rútóng báizhòu.
The night here is as bright as day.
他待我如同家人。
Tā dài wǒ rútóng jiārén.
He treats me just like family.
例如 lìrú for example; for instance; such as
V 动词 dòngcí
例 lì (example; case) + 如 rú (like). The standard phrase for introducing an instance: 例如这本书 "for example, this book." Its close cousin is 比如 bǐrú, which is more colloquial.
很多水果都很甜，例如芒果和荔枝。
Hěnduō shuǐguǒ dōu hěn tián, lìrú mángguǒ hé lìzhī.
Many fruits are sweet, for example mango and lychee.
如此 rúcǐ like this; so; in this way
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
如 rú (like) + 此 cǐ (this). A literary "like this, so," used both to point back at what was said and to intensify: 如此美丽 "so beautiful." The set phrase 原来如此 means "ah, so that's how it is."
原来如此，我明白了。
Yuánlái rúcǐ, wǒ míngbai le.
So that is how it is, now I understand.
辨析 biànxī · 如同 vs. 好像 如同 is literary and states a likeness as a fact, common in writing. 好像 hǎoxiàng is the everyday spoken word for "seems like, as if," and can also signal a guess: 好像要下雨 "it seems like it might rain." Reach for 好像 in conversation, 如同 on the page.
如果 rúguǒ If & How: Condition
语法洞见 yǔfǎ dòngjiàn · Grammatical Insight

The most common job 如 does for a learner is open a conditional sentence. 如果 rúguǒ is the everyday "if," placed at the front of the supposed clause and usually paired with 就 jiù in the result: 如果下雨，我们就不去 "if it rains, then we won't go." The 如 sets up the supposed case, and 就 delivers the consequence.

Its near-twin is 假如 jiǎrú (jiǎ "supposing" + rú). Both mean "if," and in most sentences they swap freely. The difference is register and hypotheticality: 如果 is neutral and by far the more frequent in speech, while 假如 leans a touch more formal and is favored when the case is more clearly imagined or unreal, as in "supposing you were the boss." The third member of the family, 如何 rúhé (rú + hé "what"), is the literary "how, in what way," the written-register cousin of spoken 怎么 zěnme.

如果 rúguǒ if; in the event that
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
如 rú (suppose) + 果 guǒ (outcome). The default "if," opening the condition clause and usually answered by 就 jiù in the result: 如果你来，我就很高兴 "if you come, I'll be glad."
如果明天天气好，我们就去爬山。
Rúguǒ míngtiān tiānqì hǎo, wǒmen jiù qù páshān.
If the weather is good tomorrow, we will go hiking.
假如 jiǎrú supposing; if; in case
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
假 jiǎ (false; supposed) + 如 rú (suppose). A slightly more formal "if," at home with imagined or hypothetical cases: 假如我是你 "supposing I were you." Interchangeable with 如果 in most sentences.
假如我是你，我会先问问他。
Jiǎrú wǒ shì nǐ, wǒ huì xiān wènwen tā.
If I were you, I would ask him first.
如何 rúhé how; in what way; what about
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
如 rú (according to) + 何 hé (what). The literary "how," asking by what means or in what manner: 如何解决 "how to solve it." The spoken everyday word is 怎么 zěnme; 如何 belongs to writing and formal speech.
我们应该如何解决这个问题？
Wǒmen yīnggāi rúhé jiějué zhège wèntí?
How should we solve this problem?
不如 bùrú To Match or Fall Short
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

When 如 means "to be as good as," its negative 不如 bùrú becomes a quiet comparison tool. 不如 says "A is not as good as B," ranking the second thing higher: 我的中文不如他 "my Chinese is not as good as his." It also has a second, suggestive life: placed before a proposal, 不如 means "it would be better to," nudging toward a different choice: 不如我们明天去 "we'd be better off going tomorrow." Both readings turn on the same idea, that one option does not measure up to another.

On the positive side, 如意 rúyì means "according to one's wishes," a thing turning out the way the heart hoped. It is woven through New Year greetings (万事如意, "may all your affairs go as you wish") and gives its name to the 如意 scepter, the S-curved ornament that symbolizes wishes fulfilled.

不如 bùrú not as good as; would be better to
V 动词 dòngcí
不 bù (not) + 如 rú (as good as). Either ranks one thing below another (A 不如 B, "A is not as good as B") or proposes a better course ("we'd be better off..."). Context tells the two apart.
今天太累了，不如我们明天再去。
Jīntiān tài lèi le, bùrú wǒmen míngtiān zài qù.
We are too tired today, we would be better off going tomorrow.
如意 rúyì as one wishes; satisfactory
ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí
如 rú (according to) + 意 yì (wish; intention). Turning out the way one hoped: 称心如意 "perfectly content." A staple of festive blessings, where 万事如意 wishes that every affair go as desired.
祝你新年快乐，万事如意。
Zhù nǐ xīnnián kuàilè, wànshì rúyì.
Wishing you a happy new year, and may all things go as you wish.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
如鱼得水 rú yú dé shuǐ like a fish that has found water: perfectly in one's element Lit: like-fish-getting-water. Said of someone who has landed in just the right place or company, where everything supports them and they thrive. Often used of a good job or a good partnership.
如释重负 rú shì zhòng fù as if a heavy load were set down: deeply relieved Lit: as-if-releasing-a-heavy-burden. The feeling of relief once a worry or duty is finally over, the body lightening as the weight comes off. 长舒一口气 "to let out a long breath" is its companion image.
如火如荼 rú huǒ rú tú like fire and like white reeds: in full, blazing swing Lit: like-fire-like-white-flowers. 荼 is the white flower of a reed. From an account of an army arrayed in red and white, the phrase now describes a movement or campaign surging ahead at full intensity.
称心如意 chèn xīn rú yì to suit the heart and match the wish: perfectly satisfactory Lit: fitting-the-heart-as-the-wish. Everything turning out exactly as one hoped, in feeling and in fact alike. A warm phrase for contentment, and a common blessing for someone's plans or new beginnings.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture someone hearing a word from a 口, a mouth, and falling into step with it, doing exactly as told. That is the root sense of 如: to accord, to fall in line. Hold that picture and the three modern jobs follow on their own.

To accord with a thing is to be like it, so 如 means "like, as" (如同, 例如). To suppose a case accords is to set a condition, so 如 means "if" (如果, 假如). And to ask whether a thing measures up to a standard is 不如, "not as good as," while 如意 is the happy outcome that accords with the heart's wish. Whenever you meet 如, ask what is lining up with what, and the sense falls into place.
