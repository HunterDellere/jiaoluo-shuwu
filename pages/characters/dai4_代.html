字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

代 dài is a pictophonetic compound: the person radical 亻 carries the meaning, while 弋 (yì) supplies the sound. The 亻 on the left marks the character as something to do with people, and the core idea is one person standing in the place of another. The phonetic 弋, an old word for a stake or a tethered arrow, lends only its pronunciation, not its meaning. The simplified and traditional forms are identical; only one glyph exists.

From the image of one figure replacing another, the senses unfold along two paths, the personal and the temporal:

1. To replace, to substitute. One thing or person put in the place of another: 代替 dàitì (to replace), 取代 qǔdài (to supersede), 代理 dàilǐ (to act as agent).
2. On behalf of. To act in another's stead: 代表 dàibiǎo (to represent), 代办 dàibàn (to handle on someone's behalf), 代购 dàigòu (to buy for another).
3. Generation. One human cohort succeeding the one before: 一代 yī dài (a generation), 后代 hòudài (descendants), 上一代 shàng yī dài (the previous generation).
4. Era, period, dynasty. A stretch of time defined by what fills it: 时代 shídài (era), 古代 gǔdài (ancient times), 现代 xiàndài (modern), 年代 niándài (decade), 朝代 cháodài (dynastic period).

The thread is succession: one standing where another stood. A person who steps into your role replaces you; a person who acts in your name speaks for you; the children who follow you are the next generation; and the long sweep of generations stacked one on another is the procession of eras.

代替 dàitì To Substitute & Act For
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The most concrete face of 代 is the act of putting one thing in the place of another. 代替 dàitì is the everyday verb for replacing: you stand in for an absent colleague, one material replaces another. 取代 qǔdài is sharper and more final, a supersession in which the new thing pushes the old one out altogether. 代理 dàilǐ narrows the idea to authority: to act in an official capacity for someone who cannot, an agent or a stand-in manager. All three share the single image of a substitution, differing only in how complete and how formal that substitution is.

代替 dàitì to replace; to substitute for; in place of
V 动词 dòngcí
代 dài (to stand in for) + 替 tì (to replace). The general verb for one thing or person taking another's place: 用机器代替人工 "to replace manual labor with machines." Often follows the pattern A 代替 B, "A in place of B."
今天我代替老师上课。
Jīntiān wǒ dàitì lǎoshī shàngkè.
Today I am teaching the class in place of the teacher.
没有人能代替你。
Méiyǒu rén néng dàitì nǐ.
No one can replace you.
取代 qǔdài to supersede; to replace; to displace
V 动词 dòngcí
取 qǔ (to take) + 代 dài (the place of). To take over another's place and push it out entirely: 新技术取代了旧技术 "the new technology superseded the old." Stronger and more decisive than 代替, with a sense that the old thing is now gone.
电子邮件逐渐取代了纸质信件。
Diànzǐ yóujiàn zhújiàn qǔdài le zhǐzhì xìnjiàn.
Email gradually superseded paper letters.
辨析 biànxī · 代替 vs. 取代 代替 is a neutral, often temporary substitution: I stand in for you today. 取代 is a permanent displacement: the new thing takes over and the old one is gone. You can 代替 a colleague for an afternoon, but a machine that 取代 a worker has replaced the job for good.
代理 dàilǐ to act as agent; acting (in a post); agency
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
代 dài (on behalf of) + 理 lǐ (to manage). To handle affairs in an official capacity for someone else: 代理人 "an agent," 代理商 "a distributor," 代理校长 "acting principal." As a modifier it marks a temporary or proxy holder of a post.
他是这家公司的代理人。
Tā shì zhè jiā gōngsī de dàilǐrén.
He is the agent for this company.
代表 dàibiǎo On Behalf Of
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Closely tied to substitution is the gentler idea of acting in someone's name. Here 代 does not push the other out but speaks or acts for them while they remain. 代表 dàibiǎo is the central word: to represent, and the representative who does so, from a class monitor to a member of a national congress. 代办 dàibàn is to handle a piece of business on another's behalf, the everyday errand done in someone's stead. In both, the doer is a stand-in by consent, carrying the absent party's interest forward.

代表 dàibiǎo to represent; a representative; to stand for
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
代 dài (on behalf of) + 表 biǎo (to express; outward). As a verb, to represent or stand for: 红色代表喜庆 "red stands for celebration." As a noun, the person who represents others: 班代表 "class representative," 人民代表 "people's deputy."
我代表全班感谢老师。
Wǒ dàibiǎo quán bān gǎnxiè lǎoshī.
On behalf of the whole class, I thank the teacher.
他是我们公司的代表。
Tā shì wǒmen gōngsī de dàibiǎo.
He is the representative of our company.
代办 dàibàn to handle on behalf of; to do for another
V 动词 dòngcí
代 dài (on behalf of) + 办 bàn (to handle; to manage). To take care of a task or piece of business in someone else's stead: 代办手续 "to handle the formalities for someone," 代办签证 "to arrange a visa on another's behalf." The errand done in your name.
这件事可以请人代办。
Zhè jiàn shì kěyǐ qǐng rén dàibàn.
You can ask someone to handle this matter on your behalf.
代购 dàigòu to buy on behalf of; a purchasing proxy
V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí
代 dài (on behalf of) + 购 gòu (to purchase). To buy something for another person, often goods from abroad: 海外代购 "overseas purchasing proxy." A familiar modern word from the rise of cross-border shopping, where one traveler buys for many at home.
她在日本帮朋友代购化妆品。
Tā zài Rìběn bāng péngyou dàigòu huàzhuāngpǐn.
She buys cosmetics in Japan on behalf of her friends.
时代 shídài Generation & Era
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

Stack one substitution on the next, generation after generation, and 代 becomes a unit of time. At the human scale it counts cohorts: 一代 yī dài (a generation), 后代 hòudài (the descendants who come after), 上一代 (the elders before us). Widen the lens and the same syllable names the great stretches of history: 时代 shídài (an era or age), 古代 gǔdài (antiquity), 现代 xiàndài (the modern age), 年代 niándài (a decade), 朝代 cháodài (a dynastic period). Time, in this character, is measured by who fills it, one set of people handing the world on to the next.

时代 shídài era; age; epoch; the times
N 名词 míngcí
时 shí (time) + 代 dài (period). A historical age defined by its character: 信息时代 "the information age," 时代精神 "the spirit of the age." Also one's own lifetime as a phase: 学生时代 "one's student days." A broad, qualitative stretch of time.
我们生活在一个变化很快的时代。
Wǒmen shēnghuó zài yí ge biànhuà hěn kuài de shídài.
We live in an era of rapid change.
辨析 biànxī · 时代 vs. 年代 时代 is a qualitative age defined by its character (the information age, the Tang era), often spanning long stretches. 年代 is more measured and numerical: a specific decade (九十年代, "the nineties") or a rough dating of when something happened. One asks "what kind of age," the other "which span of years."
古代 gǔdài ancient times; antiquity
N 名词 míngcí
古 gǔ (ancient) + 代 dài (era). The distant past, the age of the old dynasties and classics: 古代文学 "classical literature," 古代史 "ancient history." Stands in contrast to 现代 (modern) and 近代 (the recent, early-modern past).
古代的人没有手机。
Gǔdài de rén méiyǒu shǒujī.
People in ancient times did not have cell phones.
现代 xiàndài modern; the modern age; contemporary
N/ADJ 名词 míngcí
现 xiàn (present) + 代 dài (era). The present age and what belongs to it: 现代化 "modernization," 现代社会 "modern society," 现代汉语 "modern Chinese." The default opposite of 古代, marking the world as it now is.
这座城市非常现代。
Zhè zuò chéngshì fēicháng xiàndài.
This city is very modern.
后代 hòudài descendants; later generations; offspring
N 名词 míngcí
后 hòu (after; later) + 代 dài (generation). The generations that come after: 子孙后代 "children and grandchildren, all later generations." Used both for one's own line of descendants and, more broadly, for posterity as a whole.
我们要为后代保护环境。
Wǒmen yào wèi hòudài bǎohù huánjìng.
We must protect the environment for future generations.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
世世代代 shì shì dài dài generation after generation: for all the generations to come A doubling of 世 (age) and 代 (generation), each repeated for emphasis. Describes something passed down or continuing without break across every generation, as in a craft, a duty, or a family living on the same land.
取而代之 qǔ ér dài zhī to take over and replace it: to supplant another and assume their place Lit: take it, and replace it. From the Records of the Grand Historian, where the young Xiang Yu, seeing the First Emperor's procession, vows that he could take the emperor's place. Now used for any decisive supplanting of one thing by another.
改朝换代 gǎi cháo huàn dài to change the dynasty and replace the era: a sweeping change of regime Lit: change the court, swap the era. Describes the fall of one dynasty and the rise of another, the wholesale turnover of power. Often extended to any fundamental change of leadership or order.
新陈代谢 xīn chén dài xiè the new replaces the old: metabolism; constant renewal Lit: new and old, replacing and discharging. The scientific term for metabolism, the body taking in the new and expelling the spent. Figuratively, the natural cycle by which fresh things supersede worn-out ones in any system.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Hold one picture at the center of 代: a person (亻) stepping into the place another has left. That single act of standing in for someone is the seed of every sense.

Have the newcomer take over the job and you get substitution: 代替 to replace, 取代 to supersede, 代理 to act as agent. Have them act in the other's name while the other still stands behind them and you get the gentler "on behalf of": 代表 to represent, 代办 to handle for, 代购 to buy for. Now let the substitutions repeat down a line of people, parent to child to grandchild, and you get the generation: 一代, 后代, 上一代. Stack those generations into the long march of history and you get the era: 时代, 古代, 现代, 朝代. Whenever you meet 代, ask who is standing in whose place, and over how long a stretch of time.
