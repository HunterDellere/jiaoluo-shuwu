字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

困 = 囗 + 木: a tree 木 sealed inside the four-walled enclosure 囗 (wéi), the same square that frames 国, 园, and 围. The whole glyph is a compound ideograph, ⿴囗木, with the wall wrapped fully around the wood. A tree needs open ground and sky to spread its roots and crown. Walled in on every side, it cannot grow. That single image carries the entire word.

The Shuōwén Jiězì reads the seal form along these lines and glosses 困 as the dead, exhausted timber of an old house, wood that has reached the end of its use. From there the sense widens to anything pressed in and unable to move: a person hemmed by walls, an army ringed by the enemy, a household squeezed by want. The wall does not strike the tree. It simply holds it, and the holding is the hardship.

Concrete senses came first. 困 meant to besiege and to trap, to pin a thing in place. The abstract reading followed: hard-pressed, stuck in difficulty, worn down. The modern colloquial sense of sleepy is the latest layer. A tired body sags and stalls the way a fenced tree stalls, so 困 took on the heaviness of the eyelids that will not stay open.

Japanese keeps the character as 困る (komaru), to be troubled or at a loss, and 困難 (konnan), difficulty, both holding the original sense of being boxed into a corner with no easy way out.

困境kùnjìngHardship & Being Hemmed In
困 in compounds marks the bind itself: 困难 the difficulty, 困境 the trap, 贫困 the squeeze of poverty. In each, the walls press from outside while the subject cannot push back.
困难kùnnandifficulty; hardship
N 名词 míngcíAdj 形容词 xíngróngcí
困 (hemmed in) + 难 (hard). The everyday word for a problem that resists you, or for the strained circumstances a person lives through. Works as both a noun (a difficulty) and an adjective (difficult, hard-up).
我们遇到了很大的困难。
Wǒmen yùdào le hěn dà de kùnnan.
We have run into a serious difficulty.
他家生活很困难。
Tā jiā shēnghuó hěn kùnnan.
His family lives in hard circumstances.
困境kùnjìngpredicament; tight spot
N 名词 míngcí
困 (trapped) + 境 (situation, circumstance). A predicament you are stuck inside, with no obvious exit. Stronger and more structural than 困难: a 困境 is the whole walled situation, not a single problem.
公司陷入了财务困境。
Gōngsī xiànrù le cáiwù kùnjìng.
The company has fallen into financial straits.
辨析 biànxī · 困难 vs. 困境 困难 is a difficulty you face; 困境 is the trap you are inside. You overcome 困难; you escape 困境.
贫困pínkùnpoverty; impoverished
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcíN 名词 míngcí
贫 (poor) + 困 (hard-pressed). Poverty seen as a wall that pens people in: not merely low income, but the trapped condition want creates. Formal register, common in policy and reporting.
这个山区曾经非常贫困。
Zhège shānqū céngjīng fēicháng pínkùn.
This mountain district was once deeply impoverished.
围困wéikùnto besiege; to hem in
V 动词 dòngcí
围 (surround) + 困 (trap). The concrete, original sense: to ring something so it cannot break out. Used for armies besieging a city and, by extension, for floodwaters or crowds that cut people off.
敌军把小城围困了三个月。
Díjūn bǎ xiǎo chéng wéikùn le sān gè yuè.
The enemy army besieged the small city for three months.
犯困fànkùnTiredness & Sleepiness
用法 yòngfǎ · Usage Note

In spoken Mandarin 困 stands alone as an adjective meaning sleepy: 我困了 (wǒ kùn le), I am getting drowsy. This is the most common everyday use of the bare character. The sense is the eyelid weight of a body that wants to stall, distinct from 累 (lèi), which is physical tiredness or exhaustion. You can be 累 without being 困, and 困 without being 累.

犯困fànkùnto get drowsy; to feel sleepy
V 动词 dòngcí
犯 (to come on, to have a bout of) + 困 (sleepy). A spell of drowsiness creeping over you, often at the wrong time. Colloquial and very common: the after-lunch slump, the late-night struggle to stay awake.
下午开会的时候我老犯困。
Xiàwǔ kāihuì de shíhou wǒ lǎo fànkùn.
I always get drowsy during afternoon meetings.
困倦kùnjuànweary and drowsy
Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí
困 (sleepy) + 倦 (weary, jaded). The settled, heavy tiredness that pulls a person toward sleep. More literary than 犯困, fitting written description of a long day's fatigue.
连日加班，他感到十分困倦。
Liánrì jiābān, tā gǎndào shífēn kùnjuàn.
After days of overtime, he felt thoroughly weary and drowsy.
困意kùnyìdrowsiness; the urge to sleep
N 名词 míngcí
困 (sleepy) + 意 (feeling, inclination). The sensation of sleepiness itself, the rising pull you can either fight off or give in to.
一杯咖啡赶走了她的困意。
Yì bēi kāfēi gǎnzǒu le tā de kùnyì.
A cup of coffee chased away her drowsiness.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
内忧外困nèi yōu wài kùntroubles within, pressures without: beset on every sideA state or organization squeezed simultaneously by internal worry and external pressure. Common in political and business commentary; a near twin of 内忧外患.
困兽犹斗kùn shòu yóu dòua cornered beast still fights: desperate last-ditch resistanceFrom the Zuǒ Zhuàn. A trapped animal will fight on even with no hope, so the phrase warns that a cornered opponent is dangerous. Used of enemies making a final stand.
困知勉行kùn zhī miǎn xíngto learn through hardship and act through effortFrom the Zhōngyōng (Doctrine of the Mean). Knowledge won the hard way, by struggle, and conduct sustained by force of will. Praises perseverance over native talent.
人困马乏rén kùn mǎ fámen worn out and horses spent: an army exhaustedThe classic picture of a column at the end of a long march, fighters and mounts alike drained. Frequent in historical novels such as the Sānguó Yǎnyì.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a young tree planted in a courtyard, then the courtyard walled up on all four sides with no gate. That is the literal glyph: 木 inside 囗. The roots want to spread but hit brick; the branches reach up but find only a square of sky. The tree is alive and yet it cannot move. Hold that one image, a green thing pinned in a stone box, and you have the root of every meaning.

Walk it through the compounds. The wall as a problem you cannot get past is 困难. The wall as a whole trap you live inside is 困境. The wall as the squeeze of poverty is 贫困. The wall as an army's ring around a city is 围困. The character never changes shape; only what does the pressing.

The sleepy sense fits the same picture. When you are 困, your eyelids are the walls, sliding down and boxing in your sight until everything stalls. 我困了 (wǒ kùn le): the tree of you, hemmed by heavy lids, can no longer reach toward the day.
