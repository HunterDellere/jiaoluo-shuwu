工程

gōngchéng

From the carpenter's square inside 工 to the high-speed rail map, the vocabulary of a civilization that has always thought in projects.

~5 min read
工程 gōngchéng The Word
量好的活 liáng hǎo de huó · Measured Work

工程 (gōngchéng) joins 工 (gōng, work, craft) with 程 (chéng, a measured stage, a schedule; the same 程 as in 过程 guòchéng, "process," and 日程 rìchéng, "itinerary"). The compound therefore means work that has been measured and staged: a project. Chinese uses it far more broadly than English uses "engineering." A construction site is a 工程; a government initiative is a 工程 (the 希望工程 Hope Project built rural schools); a semester's coursework can be jokingly called a big 工程. The professional is a 工程师 (gōngchéngshī), with 师 (shī), the master suffix of 老师 (teacher) and 律师 (lawyer).

工 is one of the oldest characters in the script, a pictograph of a work tool visible on oracle bones, most commonly read as a carpenter's square or a tamping tool. It anchors an entire family of making-words: 工人 (gōngrén, worker), 工厂 (gōngchǎng, factory), 工具 (gōngjù, tool), 工作 (gōngzuò, work), 工资 (gōngzī, wages), and, as a phonetic-semantic component, it sits inside 功 (gōng, achievement), 巧 (qiǎo, skillful), and 左 (zuǒ, left, originally a hand holding the tool).

土木 tǔmù · Earth and Wood

The standout term is 土木 (tǔmù, "earth and wood") for civil engineering. It preserves, inside a modern discipline name, the two materials of traditional Chinese building: rammed-earth (夯土 hāngtǔ) walls and platforms, and the timber post-and-beam frames that let structures flex through earthquakes. 大兴土木 (dà xīng tǔmù, "greatly raise earth and wood") has been the stock phrase for launching massive construction since imperial histories used it, usually with a disapproving eye on the treasury. A civil engineering student today majors in 土木, and the abbreviation 土木人 ("earth-and-wood people") is how they refer to themselves online, often wryly, since the major is famous for hard fieldwork.

The formula X + 工程 generates the rest of the disciplines transparently, and 学院 (xuéyuàn, college) or 系 (xì, department) attaches to name the institution. Worth separating: 工程 is the activity and the project, while 工业 (gōngyè) is industry as an economic sector, and 技术 (jìshù) is technique or technology as know-how. 科技 (kējì), the compound in this page's category eyebrow, compresses 科学技术, "science and technology."

古代工程 gǔdài gōngchéng Ancient Works
都江堰 Dūjiāngyàn · The Project That Still Runs

The masterpiece of ancient Chinese engineering is not the Great Wall but a dam that is not a dam. Around 256 BCE, the Qin administrator Li Bing (李冰) and his son directed the cutting of the Dujiangyan (都江堰) waterworks on the Min River in Sichuan: an artificial island splits the river, a spillway sheds flood water and silt, and a channel cut through a mountain shoulder feeds the Chengdu plain. No barrier blocks the river; the system works by geometry and hydraulics alone. It has irrigated the plain continuously for some 2,300 years, still waters millions of mu of farmland, and is the reason Sichuan earned the name 天府之国 (tiānfǔ zhī guó, "the land of heaven's storehouse"). UNESCO listed it in 2000.

The other two canonical mega-projects frame it. The Grand Canal (大运河 Dà Yùnhé), pushed to completion under the Sui dynasty (581–618 CE), linked the Yellow and Yangtze river systems into a 1,700-kilometer grain-transport spine; its Yuan-era summit section crossed hills using pound locks, a Chinese invention of the Song dynasty. And the Great Wall (长城 Chángchéng) is less a single wall than the accumulated fortification programs of many dynasties, the Ming brick-and-stone sections near Beijing being the ones photographed. The engineering vocabulary these projects fixed, 坝 (bà, dam), 渠 (qú, channel), 闸 (zhá, sluice gate), 堤 (dī, dike), is still the working lexicon of Chinese hydraulic engineering.

基础建设 jīchǔ jiànshè Modern Infrastructure
基建狂魔 jījiàn kuángmó · The Infrastructure Maniac

Contemporary China talks about itself, half proudly and half ironically, as the 基建狂魔 (jījiàn kuángmó, "infrastructure maniac"). 基建 abbreviates 基础建设 (jīchǔ jiànshè, "foundation construction"), and the nickname earned itself: the high-speed rail network (高铁 gāotiě) went from zero dedicated lines in 2007 to well over 40,000 kilometers, more than the rest of the world combined; the Three Gorges Dam (三峡大坝 Sānxiá Dàbà) on the Yangtze is the largest power station on earth by installed capacity; and the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau bridge system spans 55 kilometers of open sea. Every one of these is referred to in the news as a 工程, and the biggest carry the honorific 超级工程 (chāojí gōngchéng, "super project"), the title of a popular CCTV documentary series.

The everyday vocabulary of all this construction surrounds anyone living in a Chinese city: 工地 (gōngdì, construction site), 施工 (shīgōng, construction in progress, the word on every barrier fence), 装修 (zhuāngxiū, renovation, the neighbor's drill at 8 a.m.), and 桥 (qiáo, bridge), 隧道 (suìdào, tunnel), 地铁 (dìtiě, metro). The sign 前方施工 ("construction ahead") is among the first written phrases a learner decodes from a taxi window.

词汇 cíhuì Key Vocabulary
n 工程师 gōngchéngshī

Engineer: project + master suffix 师. A prestige profession title; 总工程师 (zǒng gōngchéngshī) is the chief engineer of a firm or project, often shortened to 总工.

n 建筑 jiànzhù

Architecture, building. 建筑师 (jiànzhùshī) is an architect, distinct from the 工程师 who makes the building stand up. As a verb, 建 alone means to build: 建一座桥, build a bridge.

v/n 设计 shèjì

To design; a design. 设计师 (shèjìshī) covers designers from fashion to industrial. In engineering documents, 设计院 (design institute) is the state-descended firm that produces the drawings.

v 施工 shīgōng

To carry out construction: "execute the work." The word on every fence and detour sign. 施工中 means "under construction," on streets and, borrowed, on websites.

n 桥梁 qiáoliáng

Bridge, in the formal engineering register (spoken Chinese just says 桥). Used figuratively exactly as in English: a person or institution can be a 桥梁 between two sides.

n 高铁 gāotiě

High-speed rail: "high iron," short for 高速铁路. The system's ticketing, station, and seat vocabulary (一等座 first-class seat, 二等座 second-class) is core travel Chinese.

常见问题chángjiàn wèntíFrequently Asked Questions
What does 工程 (gōngchéng) literally mean?
工 is work or craft (the character began as a picture of a work tool, likely a carpenter's square) and 程 is a measured stage or schedule. Together they mean a planned, staged work: a project or engineering. 工程师 (gōngchéngshī) adds the master suffix 师 to make 'engineer.'
Why is civil engineering called 土木工程 (tǔmù gōngchéng) in Chinese?
土木 is 'earth and wood,' the two materials of traditional Chinese construction: rammed-earth walls and timber frames. The phrase 大兴土木 ('greatly raise earth and wood') has meant large-scale construction since imperial times, so the old material pair became the modern name of the discipline.
What is 基建狂魔 (jījiàn kuángmó)?
A self-mocking internet nickname for China meaning 'infrastructure maniac,' coined as high-speed rail, bridges, and metro systems expanded at unprecedented speed. 基建 is short for 基础建设, infrastructure ('foundation construction').
