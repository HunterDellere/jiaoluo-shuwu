    <span class="section-anchor" id="table"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">座位</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zuòwèi</span>
      <span class="sh-en">At the Table</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="局">
      <div class="scholar-label">饭局 fànjú · A Meal That Is a Meeting</div>
      <p>饭局 is a meal with a purpose; 局 is the word for a game or a situation in play. After a factory visit the boss will usually host, and the dinner is where the relationship, more than the price, gets settled. This page is the short version for the day itself. The longer background is on the toasting (敬酒), hosting (请客), gift (送礼), and face (面子) pages. The host sits in the 主位 (zhǔwèi), usually facing the door; the most important guest, probably you, sits at the host's right. Wait to be seated, let the host order, and do not try to pay: the host has already paid, and insisting costs them face. Reciprocate by hosting on their next visit or at a later dinner.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">请坐</span>
          <span class="card-py">qǐng zuò</span>
          <span class="card-en">please sit</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">You will be guided to your seat. A modest protest, 您先坐 (nín xiān zuò, "after you"), is polite; then sit where you are put.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">王总您先请，我坐这儿就好。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wáng zǒng nín xiān qǐng, wǒ zuò zhèr jiù hǎo.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">After you, Mr Wang; here is fine for me.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">招待</span>
          <span class="card-py">zhāodài</span>
          <span class="card-en">to host, hospitality</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">The thank-you at the end names the hospitality: 谢谢款待 or 谢谢您的热情招待.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">谢谢王总的热情招待，今天吃得很开心。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Xièxie Wáng zǒng de rèqíng zhāodài, jīntiān chī de hěn kāixīn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Thank you, Mr Wang, for the warm hospitality; I really enjoyed tonight.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">喝酒</span>
      <span class="sh-py">hē jiǔ</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Drinking and Declining</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">敬酒的规矩 jìngjiǔ de guīju · The Toast in Brief</span>
      <strong>干杯 gānbēi</strong> → "dry the cup": drink it all<br>
      <strong>随意 suíyì</strong> → "as you like": sip what you want; say it to make drinking optional for both<br>
      <strong>我先干为敬 wǒ xiān gān wéi jìng</strong> → "I'll drain mine first as a mark of respect"<br>
      <strong>杯子放低 bēizi fàng dī</strong> → clink with your glass rim below a senior person's<br>
      <strong>以茶代酒 yǐ chá dài jiǔ</strong> → "tea in place of wine": the standard way to toast without alcohol
    </div>

    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">不太会喝酒</span>
          <span class="card-py">bú tài huì hē jiǔ</span>
          <span class="card-en">not much of a drinker</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">Decide before the dinner whether you drink, and say it early with a reason that leaves no one at fault: health, medication, an early meeting, or driving. Once you have drunk one toast, refusing the next is harder.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">不好意思，我最近在吃药，不能喝酒，我以茶代酒敬大家。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Bù hǎoyìsi, wǒ zuìjìn zài chī yào, bù néng hē jiǔ, wǒ yǐ chá dài jiǔ jìng dàjiā.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Sorry, I'm on medication and can't drink; let me toast everyone with tea.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我酒量不好，我随意，您也随意。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ jiǔliàng bù hǎo, wǒ suíyì, nín yě suíyì.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">I can't hold much; I'll sip, and you drink as you like too.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">敬</span>
          <span class="card-py">jìng</span>
          <span class="card-en">to toast respectfully</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">Return the host's toast once the host has toasted you, standing and holding the glass with both hands. A short toast in Chinese is worth more than a long one in English.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我敬王总一杯，感谢今天的安排，希望我们合作愉快！</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ jìng Wáng zǒng yì bēi, gǎnxiè jīntiān de ānpái, xīwàng wǒmen hézuò yúkuài!</div>
            <div class="ex-en">A toast to Mr Wang: thank you for today's arrangements, and here's to a good partnership!</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">为我们的长期合作，干杯！</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wèi wǒmen de chángqī hézuò, gānbēi!</div>
            <div class="ex-en">To our long-term partnership, cheers!</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>

          <span class="card-py">bù tán shēngyi</span>
          <span class="card-en">no business talk</span>

        <div class="card-def">Price is rarely negotiated at the table, but the boss may ask a soft question about volume or the future. Answer warmly and without numbers; numbers belong in the meeting room the next day.</div>
