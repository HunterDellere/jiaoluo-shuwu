      <h1 class="topic-hero-title">会谈</h1>
      <span class="topic-hero-title-py">huìtán</span>
      <p class="topic-hero-desc">The phrases that keep a foreign buyer in control of a factory meeting: titles, openings, clarifying, pinning down commitments, and decoding a polite no.</p>
    </header>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="frame"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">会谈</span>
      <span class="sh-py">huìtán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">The Frame</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="谈">
      <div class="scholar-label">会议室里 huìyìshì lǐ · Who Is in the Room</div>
      <p>会谈 (huìtán) is "meet and talk," the formal word for a business meeting between two parties; inside the company the same event is just 开会 (kāihuì). At a factory you will usually face four roles. The <strong>boss</strong> (老板 lǎobǎn, addressed as 某总) decides price and terms. The <strong>sales or account person</strong> (业务 yèwù, 业务员 yèwùyuán) speaks the most and often some English. The <strong>merchandiser</strong> (跟单 gēndān, literally "follow the order") tracks your order day to day and is the person you will message on WeChat for the next year. The <strong>engineer</strong> (工程师 gōngchéngshī, addressed as 某工) answers the technical questions and is the one most likely to tell you plainly what cannot be done.</p>
      <p>The boss talks in principles and the engineer talks in millimetres. A commitment from the salesperson is worth only what the engineer and the boss confirm afterwards, so the phrases on this page aim at one thing: getting each promise said by the person who can keep it, in a form specific enough to write down. Read the soft-no section before the first meeting; it changes how you hear everything else.</p>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="titles"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">称呼</span>
      <span class="sh-py">chēnghu</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Titles and Address</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">姓 + 头衔 xìng + tóuxián · Surname + Title</span>
      <strong>王总 Wáng zǒng</strong> → Mr./Ms. Wang, the head; short for 总经理 or 总裁. The default for any boss or senior executive<br>
      <strong>李经理 Lǐ jīnglǐ</strong> → Manager Li; sales, purchasing, or department managers<br>
      <strong>张工 Zhāng gōng</strong> → Engineer Zhang; short for 张工程师, used for any technical staff<br>
      <strong>刘厂长 Liú chǎngzhǎng</strong> → Factory Director Liu; runs the plant floor, not always the owner<br>
      <strong>陈主管 Chén zhǔguǎn</strong> → Supervisor Chen; QC or line supervisors<br>
      <strong>小周 Xiǎo Zhōu</strong> → "little Zhou"; for a younger staffer, only after they introduce themselves this way
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="总">
      <div class="scholar-label">往高里叫 wǎng gāo lǐ jiào · Round the Title Up</div>
      <p>Chinese business address is surname plus title, never given name alone, and never 先生 (xiānsheng, Mr.) for a counterpart who has a title. 王总 is safe for anyone who might be in charge; erring upward flatters, erring downward stings. Deputies are often addressed without the 副 (fù, deputy): 副总经理 Wang is still 王总 to his face. Read the business card for the title, then use it in every sentence that addresses that person.</p>
      <p>For yourself, give the company first and your name second, the same order as the card: 我是[公司]的 Hunter, 负责产品和供应链 (I'm Hunter from [company], responsible for product and supply chain). Your counterparts will look for your rank in that sentence. If you own the company, say so plainly: 我是公司的创始人 (chuàngshǐrén, founder) or 我是老板. It tells the room that what you agree to is final, which speeds everything up.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">开场</span>
      <span class="sh-py">kāichǎng</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Opening the Meeting</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">幸会</span>
          <span class="card-py">xìnghuì</span>
          <span class="card-en">a pleasure to meet you</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">Formal "fortunate meeting," said with the handshake on first contact. Pairs naturally with 久仰 (jiǔyǎng, "I've long admired you") when you know the person by reputation. Plain 很高兴认识您 is never wrong.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">王总，您好！幸会幸会。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wáng zǒng, nín hǎo! Xìnghuì xìnghuì.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Mr. Wang, hello! A real pleasure.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">很高兴终于见到您，我们在微信上聊了很久了。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Hěn gāoxìng zhōngyú jiàndào nín, wǒmen zài Wēixìn shang liáole hěn jiǔ le.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Great to finally meet you; we've been talking on WeChat for a long time.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">名片</span>
          <span class="card-py">míngpiàn</span>
          <span class="card-en">business card</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">Offered and received with both hands, Chinese side up and facing the reader. Read it, say the person's title aloud, and leave it on the table in front of you during the meeting. Many people now skip cards and scan WeChat instead; bring cards anyway, printed bilingual.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这是我的名片，请多指教。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè shì wǒ de míngpiàn, qǐng duō zhǐjiào.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Here's my card; I look forward to your guidance.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我们加个微信吧，我扫您。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒmen jiā ge Wēixìn ba, wǒ sǎo nín.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Let's add each other on WeChat; I'll scan your code.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">请多关照</span>
          <span class="card-py">qǐng duō guānzhào</span>
          <span class="card-en">please look after us; I look forward to working with you</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">The standard closing line of a first introduction, asking the other side's goodwill for the relationship ahead. 初次见面，请多关照 ("meeting for the first time, please look after me") is the full formula. 请多指教 asks for guidance and is slightly humbler.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">初次见面，以后还请多多关照。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Chūcì jiànmiàn, yǐhòu hái qǐng duōduō guānzhào.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This is our first meeting; I hope we'll work well together from here on.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">辛苦了</span>
          <span class="card-py">xīnkǔ le</span>
          <span class="card-en">thank you for your trouble</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">Acknowledges effort someone made for you: picking you up at the station, preparing samples, staying late. Factory staff hear it constantly from managers and appreciate it from a buyer. Answer with 不辛苦 or 应该的 (yīnggāi de, "it's what I should do").</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">张工，样品准备得这么快，辛苦了！</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhāng gōng, yàngpǐn zhǔnbèi de zhème kuài, xīnkǔ le!</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Engineer Zhang, you got the samples ready so fast; thank you for the effort!</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">让您专门来高铁站接我，太麻烦您了。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Ràng nín zhuānmén lái gāotiězhàn jiē wǒ, tài máfan nín le.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">You came all the way to the high-speed rail station to pick me up; that's too much trouble.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">今天主要想谈</span>
          <span class="card-py">jīntiān zhǔyào xiǎng tán</span>
          <span class="card-en">today I mainly want to discuss</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">Sets the agenda. Saying it early, with numbered points, is the strongest move a visitor has: it decides what gets talked about and in what order. Follow with 第一 (dì-yī), 第二, 第三, and close each topic with 这个先到这里 ("let's leave this one here for now").</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">今天主要想谈三件事：第一是样品，第二是价格，第三是交期。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Jīntiān zhǔyào xiǎng tán sān jiàn shì: dì-yī shì yàngpǐn, dì-èr shì jiàgé, dì-sān shì jiāoqī.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Today I want to cover three things: first the samples, second the price, third the delivery time.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">样品的问题先到这里，我们看下一个。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Yàngpǐn de wèntí xiān dào zhèlǐ, wǒmen kàn xià yí ge.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Let's leave the samples there for now and move to the next item.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">贵公司</span>
          <span class="card-py">guì gōngsī</span>
          <span class="card-en">your company (honorific)</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">The polite way to refer to the other side's company; 贵厂 (guì chǎng) for a factory. Your own side is plain 我们公司 or 我们. 贵 is the same character as "expensive," here meaning "honoured."</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我们对贵厂的不锈钢工艺很有信心。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒmen duì guì chǎng de bùxiùgāng gōngyì hěn yǒu xìnxīn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">We have a lot of confidence in your factory's stainless steel workmanship.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="clarify"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">听懂</span>
      <span class="sh-py">tīngdǒng</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Staying on the Thread</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="懂">
      <div class="scholar-label">别装懂 bié zhuāng dǒng · Never Nod Along</div>
      <p>The costliest habit for a learner in a negotiation is nodding at a sentence you only half caught. In a factory meeting the half you missed is often the number. Interrupting to check is normal and reads as seriousness; Chinese buyers do it to each other. Three tools cover nearly every case: ask for speed (说慢一点), ask for the hard word in writing (写下来), and restate what you heard so the other side can correct it (我理解的是……对吗). Expect regional accents: in Zhejiang and Guangdong many managers speak Mandarin with a southern accent where zh/z and sh/s merge, so 十 (shí, ten) and 四 (sì, four) can sound alike. Confirm every number by writing it or holding up fingers.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">说慢一点</span>
          <span class="card-py">shuō màn yìdiǎn</span>
          <span class="card-en">speak a little slower</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">Lead with 不好意思 (bù hǎoyìsi, "excuse me") to soften it. Most people slow down for one sentence and then speed back up; asking twice is fine.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">不好意思，您能说慢一点吗？我的中文还不太好。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Bù hǎoyìsi, nín néng shuō màn yìdiǎn ma? Wǒ de Zhōngwén hái bú tài hǎo.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Sorry, could you speak a little slower? My Chinese isn't very good yet.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">请再说一遍，最后那个数字我没听清楚。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Qǐng zài shuō yí biàn, zuìhòu nàge shùzì wǒ méi tīng qīngchu.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Please say that again; I didn't catch the last number clearly.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">写下来</span>
          <span class="card-py">xiě xiàlái</span>
          <span class="card-en">write it down</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">For technical terms and every figure. Hand over your notebook or phone. Pinyin input is fine; most people will type the characters for you.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这个词我不认识，您能帮我写下来吗？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhège cí wǒ bú rènshi, nín néng bāng wǒ xiě xiàlái ma?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">I don't know this word; could you write it down for me?</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">价格和数量能写在纸上吗？我怕听错。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Jiàgé hé shùliàng néng xiě zài zhǐ shang ma? Wǒ pà tīngcuò.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Could you write the price and quantity on paper? I'm worried I'll mishear.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">您的意思是</span>
          <span class="card-py">nín de yìsi shì</span>
          <span class="card-en">what you mean is</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">Restating what you heard is the most powerful check you have, because it forces a yes or no on your version. 我理解的是……对吗 ("my understanding is ..., right?") does the same job. 也就是说 (yě jiù shì shuō, "in other words") works mid-conversation.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">您的意思是，模具费我们付，但是模具归你们，对吗？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nín de yìsi shì, mújùfèi wǒmen fù, dànshì mújù guī nǐmen, duì ma?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">You mean we pay the tooling fee, but the mold belongs to you, is that right?</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我理解的是，三千个起订，四十五天交货。对吗？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ lǐjiě de shì, sānqiān ge qǐdìng, sìshíwǔ tiān jiāohuò. Duì ma?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">My understanding is a minimum order of 3,000 and delivery in 45 days. Correct?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">什么意思</span>
          <span class="card-py">shénme yìsi</span>
          <span class="card-en">what does it mean</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">Ask about a word, not a person: "这个词是什么意思" is neutral, while a bare 你什么意思？ means "what are you implying?" and sounds hostile. Keep 这个词 in front.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">"封样"是什么意思？是确认过的样品吗？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">"Fēngyàng" shì shénme yìsi? Shì quèrèn guo de yàngpǐn ma?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">What does "fēngyàng" mean? Is it the approved sample?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">帮我翻译一下</span>
          <span class="card-py">bāng wǒ fānyì yíxià</span>
          <span class="card-en">please translate this for me</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">For your interpreter, or for the English-speaking salesperson when the engineer goes too fast. Using an interpreter for one hard passage and Chinese for the rest is common and costs no face.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这一段比较专业，麻烦帮我翻译一下。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè yí duàn bǐjiào zhuānyè, máfan bāng wǒ fānyì yíxià.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This part is fairly technical; could you translate it for me, please?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="confirm"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">确认</span>
      <span class="sh-py">quèrèn</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Confirming and Pinning Down</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">把话落实 bǎ huà luòshí · Turn Talk Into Commitments</span>
      <strong>什么时候？shénme shíhou</strong> → 能给个具体日期吗？Can you give a specific date?<br>
      <strong>多少？duōshao</strong> → 具体是多少？What is the exact figure?<br>
      <strong>谁？shéi</strong> → 这个由谁负责？Who is responsible for this?<br>
      <strong>怎么确认？zěnme quèrèn</strong> → 我们书面确认一下。Let's confirm it in writing.<br>
      <strong>记下来 jì xiàlái</strong> → 请写进会议纪要。Please put it in the meeting minutes.
    </div>

    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">确认一下</span>
          <span class="card-py">quèrèn yíxià</span>
          <span class="card-en">let me confirm</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">确认 (quèrèn) is the verb of the whole relationship: samples are confirmed, drawings are confirmed, orders are confirmed. 我确认一下 before restating a point; 您确认一下 when handing the other side something to check.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我确认一下：单价不含税，含包装，对吧？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ quèrèn yíxià: dānjià bù hán shuì, hán bāozhuāng, duì ba?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Let me confirm: the unit price excludes tax and includes packaging, right?</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">图纸我发您了，您确认一下尺寸。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Túzhǐ wǒ fā nín le, nín quèrèn yíxià chǐcùn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">I've sent you the drawing; please check the dimensions.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">具体</span>
          <span class="card-py">jùtǐ</span>
          <span class="card-en">specific, concrete</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">The antidote to 尽快 (jǐnkuài, "as soon as possible") and 差不多 ("roughly"). Any answer without a date or number gets a follow-up with 具体.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">"尽快"是多快？能给个具体时间吗？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">"Jǐnkuài" shì duō kuài? Néng gěi ge jùtǐ shíjiān ma?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">How fast is "as soon as possible"? Can you give me a specific time?</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">下周三之前能给我们一个具体的报价吗？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Xià zhōusān zhīqián néng gěi wǒmen yí ge jùtǐ de bàojià ma?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Can you give us a firm quotation before next Wednesday?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">负责</span>
          <span class="card-py">fùzé</span>
          <span class="card-en">to be responsible for</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">Names the owner of an action. Ask it about every open item before the meeting ends. 对……负责 also means "to be accountable for" a result, which makes it the word of quality disputes.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">改模的事由谁负责跟进？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Gǎimú de shì yóu shéi fùzé gēnjìn?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Who is responsible for following up on the mold modification?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">会议纪要</span>
          <span class="card-py">huìyì jìyào</span>
          <span class="card-en">meeting minutes</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">A short written summary of decisions, owners, and dates, sent the same day. Offering to write it yourself means your version becomes the record. 白纸黑字 (báizhǐ hēizì, "black ink on white paper") is the idiom for having it in writing.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">今天的内容我整理一个会议纪要，晚上发到群里。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Jīntiān de nèiróng wǒ zhěnglǐ yí ge huìyì jìyào, wǎnshang fā dào qún li.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">I'll write up minutes of today's discussion and post them in the group chat tonight.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">口头说的不算，我们还是白纸黑字写清楚。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Kǒutóu shuō de bú suàn, wǒmen háishi báizhǐ hēizì xiě qīngchu.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">What's said out loud doesn't count; let's put it clearly in writing.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="decode"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">潜台词</span>
      <span class="sh-py">qiántáicí</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Decoding the Soft No</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="难">
      <div class="scholar-label">不说"不" bù shuō "bù" · The No That Is Never Said</div>
      <p>潜台词 (qiántáicí) is a theatre word, "the lines beneath the lines." In a negotiation it is most of the content. A flat 不行 (bù xíng, "no way") to a client's face costs both sides face, so refusals arrive dressed as difficulty, effort, or delay. A learner who translates word by word hears a yes and flies home with a promise nobody made. The rule of thumb: anything other than a specific yes with a number or a date attached is a no, a maybe, or a price increase waiting to be named.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">字面 vs 实际 zìmiàn vs shíjì · What Is Said and What It Means</span>
      <strong>没问题 méi wèntí</strong> → "no problem": a real yes only when followed by details; on its own, often a reflex<br>
      <strong>问题不大 wèntí bú dà</strong> → "not a big problem": there is a problem<br>
      <strong>应该可以 yīnggāi kěyǐ</strong> → "should be possible": not confirmed; ask what it depends on<br>
      <strong>我们尽量 wǒmen jǐnliàng</strong> → "we'll do our best": no commitment<br>
      <strong>差不多 chàbuduō</strong> → "about the same": it is different; ask by how much<br>
      <strong>有点难 / 比较难 yǒudiǎn nán / bǐjiào nán</strong> → "a bit difficult": usually no, or yes at a higher price<br>
      <strong>我们研究研究 wǒmen yánjiū yánjiū</strong> → "we'll study it": often a polite no<br>
      <strong>再看看吧 zài kànkan ba</strong> → "let's see": shelving it<br>
      <strong>这个要问老板 zhège yào wèn lǎobǎn</strong> → "I have to ask the boss": true, and also a way to buy time
    </div>

    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">取决于</span>
          <span class="card-py">qǔjué yú</span>
          <span class="card-en">to depend on</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">The follow-up question to every 应该 and 尽量: find the condition behind the hedge. Once the condition is named, it can be negotiated.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">您说"应该可以"，那取决于什么？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nín shuō "yīnggāi kěyǐ", nà qǔjué yú shénme?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">You said "it should be possible." What does it depend on?</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">如果有困难，请直接告诉我，我们一起想办法。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Rúguǒ yǒu kùnnan, qǐng zhíjiē gàosu wǒ, wǒmen yìqǐ xiǎng bànfǎ.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">If there's a difficulty, please tell me directly, and we'll find a solution together.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">实话实说</span>
          <span class="card-py">shíhuà shíshuō</span>
          <span class="card-en">to speak frankly</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">Inviting candour removes the face cost of a no. Say early that you prefer bad news now to surprises later; engineers in particular will take you up on it.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">张工，您实话实说，这个公差你们做得到吗？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhāng gōng, nín shíhuà shíshuō, zhège gōngchā nǐmen zuò de dào ma?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Engineer Zhang, tell me frankly: can you hold this tolerance?</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">现在说"做不到"没关系，到时候出问题才麻烦。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Xiànzài shuō "zuò bú dào" méi guānxi, dào shíhou chū wèntí cái máfan.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Saying "we can't" now is fine; it's problems showing up later that cause trouble.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="position"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">表态</span>
      <span class="sh-py">biǎotài</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Stating Your Position</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="态">
      <div class="scholar-label">软话硬说 ruǎnhuà yìng shuō · Firm Content, Soft Wrapping</div>
      <p>表态 (biǎotài) is "to show one's attitude," to state where you stand. The same face logic that softens the factory's refusals applies to yours. A visitor who says 不行 loudly wins the sentence and loses the relationship; one who says 这个我们接受不了 (this we can't accept) with a reason and a counter-proposal keeps both. The pattern is acknowledgement first, then 不过 (búguò, "however"), then the position, then a way forward. You also have the visitor's best tool, deferral: 我回去跟团队商量一下 ("I'll discuss it with my team when I get back") ends any pressure at the table without conceding anything.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">原则上同意</span>
          <span class="card-py">yuánzé shang tóngyì</span>
          <span class="card-en">agree in principle</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">Signals agreement on direction while keeping the details open. Useful when the boss pushes for a handshake before the numbers are settled.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这个方案我们原则上同意，细节还要再谈。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhège fāng'àn wǒmen yuánzé shang tóngyì, xìjié hái yào zài tán.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">We agree with this plan in principle; the details still need discussion.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">商量一下</span>
          <span class="card-py">shāngliang yíxià</span>
          <span class="card-en">to talk it over</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">The deferral. Buys time without saying no, and is the expected answer to any significant new proposal. Name when you will reply so it does not read as a brush-off.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这个我需要跟团队商量一下，周五之前给您答复。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhège wǒ xūyào gēn tuánduì shāngliang yíxià, zhōuwǔ zhīqián gěi nín dáfù.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">I need to talk this over with my team; I'll give you an answer before Friday.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">接受不了</span>
          <span class="card-py">jiēshòu bù liǎo</span>
          <span class="card-en">cannot accept</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">A clear, polite refusal. Stronger than 有点难, softer than 不行. Pair it with the reason and what you could accept.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我理解你们的成本压力，不过这个涨幅我们接受不了。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ lǐjiě nǐmen de chéngběn yālì, búguò zhège zhǎngfú wǒmen jiēshòu bù liǎo.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">I understand your cost pressure, but we can't accept an increase of this size.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">如果交期能提前两周，价格我们可以再考虑。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Rúguǒ jiāoqī néng tíqián liǎng zhōu, jiàgé wǒmen kěyǐ zài kǎolǜ.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">If delivery could come two weeks earlier, we could reconsider the price.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">误会</span>
          <span class="card-py">wùhuì</span>
          <span class="card-en">misunderstanding</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">Frames a disagreement as a communication gap rather than bad faith, which lets the other side correct course without losing face. Use it even when you suspect it was no accident.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这里可能有点误会，我们当时说的是三一六不锈钢。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhèlǐ kěnéng yǒudiǎn wùhuì, wǒmen dāngshí shuō de shì sān yī liù bùxiùgāng.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">There may be a small misunderstanding here; what we agreed at the time was 316 stainless steel.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">长期合作</span>
          <span class="card-py">chángqī hézuò</span>
          <span class="card-en">long-term cooperation</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">The phrase that justifies concessions on both sides: the factory gives on price for the sake of future orders, you give on terms for the sake of a stable partner. Invoke it when asking for something; hear it as a request when the other side uses it.</div>

          <div class="example">

            <div class="ex-py">Wǒmen kànzhòng de shì chángqī hézuò, bú shì yì dān shēngyì.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">What we value is a long-term partnership, not a single order.</div>
          </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="closing"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">收尾</span>
      <span class="sh-py">shōuwěi</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Closing the Meeting</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">散会前 sànhuì qián · Before Everyone Stands Up</span>
      <strong>总结 zǒngjié</strong> → 我简单总结一下今天谈的。Let me briefly sum up today.<br>
      <strong>待办 dàibàn</strong> → 我们这边要做的是……，你们那边是……。On our side we'll ..., on yours ....<br>
      <strong>时间 shíjiān</strong> → 我们下周二电话跟进。We'll follow up by phone next Tuesday.<br>
      <strong>感谢 gǎnxiè</strong> → 感谢各位今天抽时间。Thank you all for your time today.<br>
      <strong>祝愿 zhùyuàn</strong> → 希望我们合作愉快！Here's to a good partnership!
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="尾">
      <div class="scholar-label">收个好尾 shōu ge hǎo wěi · End on the List</div>
      <p>收尾 (shōuwěi) is "gather the tail," to wrap up. A factory meeting often dissolves into tea, a tour, or lunch without anyone summarising, and an unsummarised meeting produces three different memories of what was agreed. Stand up last: say 我简单总结一下, read the decisions back with owner and date, ask 有没有遗漏的？(yǒu méiyǒu yílòu de, "anything missed?"), and promise the minutes. Then the courtesies: 今天谈得很愉快 (today's talk went very well), 辛苦各位了, and the handshake line 合作愉快.</p>
    </div>
