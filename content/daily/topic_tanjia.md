      <h1 class="topic-hero-title">谈价</h1>
      <span class="topic-hero-title-py">tánjià</span>
      <p class="topic-hero-desc">The money half of a factory negotiation: reading a quotation, tax and invoices, trade terms, deposits and balances, haggling lines, and the numbers that trip learners up.</p>
    </header>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="quote"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">报价</span>
      <span class="sh-py">bàojià</span>
      <span class="sh-en">The Quotation</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="价">
      <div class="scholar-label">一个价格，五个问题 yí ge jiàgé, wǔ ge wèntí · One Price, Five Questions</div>
      <p>谈价 (tánjià) is "talk price"; 讲价 (jiǎngjià) and 砍价 (kǎnjià, "chop the price") are the market-stall versions. A unit price on its own tells you little. Before comparing two factories, establish five things about each number: the currency, whether tax is included, the trade term (where the goods are handed over), the quantity it assumes, and how long it is valid. Two quotations of "4.20" can differ by fifteen percent once those are fixed. The phrases in this section are the questions that pin them down, and the sections below explain the answers you will hear.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">报价单</span>
          <span class="card-py">bàojiàdān</span>
          <span class="card-en">quotation sheet</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">报价 (bàojià) is both the verb "to quote" and the noun "quote." Ask for it in writing, line by line: 分项报价 (fēnxiàng bàojià) is an itemised quote that breaks out the product, packaging, tooling, and printing.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">麻烦您做一份分项报价，包装和印刷单独列出来。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Máfan nín zuò yí fèn fēnxiàng bàojià, bāozhuāng hé yìnshuā dāndú liè chūlái.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Could you prepare an itemised quotation, with packaging and printing listed separately?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">单价</span>
          <span class="card-py">dānjià</span>
          <span class="card-en">unit price</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">The price per piece; 总价 (zǒngjià) is the total, 金额 (jīn'é) the amount on a line. The usual follow-up is what the unit price includes: 包括 (bāokuò) and 不包括 are the words to listen for.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这个单价包括彩盒和外箱吗？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhège dānjià bāokuò cǎihé hé wàixiāng ma?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Does this unit price include the colour box and the master carton?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">有效期</span>
          <span class="card-py">yǒuxiàoqī</span>
          <span class="card-en">validity period</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">How long a quote holds. Factories tie it to material prices, especially steel, and to the exchange rate (汇率 huìlǜ). A quote with no date can be reopened at any time.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这个报价有效期多久？如果钢材涨价，你们会调价吗？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhège bàojià yǒuxiàoqī duō jiǔ? Rúguǒ gāngcái zhǎngjià, nǐmen huì tiáojià ma?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">How long is this quote valid? If steel prices rise, will you adjust the price?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">阶梯价</span>
          <span class="card-py">jiētījià</span>
          <span class="card-en">tiered pricing</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">"Staircase price": the unit price falls as quantity rises. Asking for a tier table is the cleanest way to learn the factory's real cost curve, and it moves the conversation from "lower the price" to "at what quantity."</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">能给一个阶梯价吗？一千、三千、五千、一万分别是多少？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Néng gěi yí ge jiētījià ma? Yìqiān, sānqiān, wǔqiān, yíwàn fēnbié shì duōshao?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Could you give tiered pricing? What is the price at 1,000, 3,000, 5,000, and 10,000 respectively?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">成本</span>
          <span class="card-py">chéngběn</span>
          <span class="card-en">cost</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">What the factory pays, as opposed to 价格 (jiàgé), what you pay. 原材料 (yuáncáiliào, raw materials) and 人工 (réngōng, labour) are the two cost lines you will hear cited when a price goes up. 利润 (lìrùn) is profit.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">您说原材料涨了，大概占成本的多少？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nín shuō yuáncáiliào zhǎng le, dàgài zhàn chéngběn de duōshao?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">You say raw materials went up. Roughly what share of the cost are they?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="tax"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">发票</span>
      <span class="sh-py">fāpiào</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Tax and Invoices</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="税">
      <div class="scholar-label">含税还是不含税 hánshuì háishi bù hánshuì · Tax In or Tax Out</div>
      <p>China's value-added tax, 增值税 (zēngzhíshuì), is 13% on most manufactured goods sold by a general taxpayer. A price can be quoted 含税 (hánshuì, tax included) or 不含税 (bù hánshuì, tax excluded); you will also hear 裸价 (luǒjià, "naked price"), which some suppliers use for the tax-free figure and others for the bare product without packaging, so ask which. The gap is the full 13%, so ask every time. As a general pattern, a price quoted in US dollars for export is usually tax-exclusive, because exported goods are zero-rated and the exporter of record can reclaim input VAT as 出口退税 (chūkǒu tuìshuì, export tax rebate). A price quoted in RMB often assumes a domestic buyer who wants a VAT invoice, and includes the tax. If you are paying in dollars and the price is 含税, ask what you are paying the tax for.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">含税</span>
          <span class="card-py">hánshuì</span>
          <span class="card-en">tax included</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">含 is "to contain." The pair 含税 / 不含税 appears on almost every RMB quotation; 含运费 (hán yùnfèi) is freight included, 含包装 packaging included. The same 含 works for anything the price might or might not cover.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这是含税价还是不含税价？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè shì hánshuì jià háishi bù hánshuì jià?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Is this price with tax or without?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">发票</span>
          <span class="card-py">fāpiào</span>
          <span class="card-en">invoice; official tax receipt</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">In China 发票 means an official, tax-registered invoice, not a bill a company can print for itself. 专票 (zhuānpiào) is the special VAT invoice that lets a Chinese business buyer deduct input tax; 普票 (pǔpiào) is the ordinary one. For export trade the documents are different: 形式发票 (xíngshì fāpiào) is the proforma invoice (PI) you pay against, and 商业发票 (shāngyè fāpiào) the commercial invoice that travels with the goods.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">请先发形式发票，我们确认以后安排定金。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Qǐng xiān fā xíngshì fāpiào, wǒmen quèrèn yǐhòu ānpái dìngjīn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Please send the proforma invoice first; once we've checked it we'll arrange the deposit.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">出口退税</span>
          <span class="card-py">chūkǒu tuìshuì</span>
          <span class="card-en">export VAT rebate</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">The refund of input VAT on exported goods, claimed by whoever exports them. A factory with its own export licence (自营出口 zìyíng chūkǒu) claims it directly; one without exports through a trading company (外贸公司 wàimào gōngsī), which adds a layer of margin. Asking who exports tells you who is in the chain.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">你们有自己的出口权吗？还是通过外贸公司出口？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nǐmen yǒu zìjǐ de chūkǒuquán ma? Háishi tōngguò wàimào gōngsī chūkǒu?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Do you have your own export rights, or do you export through a trading company?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>

      <span class="sh-cn">贸易条款</span>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">谁付到哪儿 shéi fù dào nǎr · Who Pays How Far</span>
      <strong>EXW 出厂价 chūchǎngjià</strong> → ex works: you collect at the factory gate and pay everything after<br>
      <strong>FOB 离岸价 lí'ànjià</strong> → free on board: the factory pays until the goods are loaded on the ship at the named port<br>
      <strong>CIF 到岸价 dào'ànjià</strong> → cost, insurance, freight: the factory pays sea freight and insurance to your port<br>
      <strong>DDP 完税后交货 wánshuì hòu jiāohuò</strong> → delivered duty paid: the seller covers freight, import duty, and delivery to your door

      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">离岸价</span>
          <span class="card-py">lí'ànjià</span>
          <span class="card-en">FOB price</span>
        </div>

          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这个价格是FOB哪个港口？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhège jiàgé shì FOB nǎge gǎngkǒu?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This price is FOB which port?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">运费</span>
          <span class="card-py">yùnfèi</span>
          <span class="card-en">freight charge</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">海运 (hǎiyùn) is sea freight, 空运 (kōngyùn) air freight, 快递 (kuàidì) express courier for samples. 货代 (huòdài) is the freight forwarder. 整柜 (zhěngguì) is a full container load, 拼柜 (pīnguì) a shared one.</div>

            <div class="ex-en">We have our own forwarder, so you only need to quote FOB.</div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="payment"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">付款</span>
      <span class="sh-py">fùkuǎn</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Payment</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="定">
      <div class="scholar-label">定金不是订金 dìngjīn bú shì dìngjīn · Two Deposits That Sound the Same</div>
      <p>The most common terms are 30% deposit and 70% balance, written 百分之三十定金，百分之七十尾款 or simply "30% T/T". Before you write the word "deposit" in Chinese, know that two characters are pronounced dìngjīn and mean different things in law. 定金 (with 定, "to fix") is a legal earnest under Articles 586 and 587 of China's Civil Code: if the buyer walks away it is forfeited, and if the seller fails to perform it must pay back double. It is capped at 20% of the contract value; any amount above that does not count as 定金. 订金 (with 订, "to book") has no such rule and is treated as an advance payment, refundable against the price. 预付款 (yùfùkuǎn, prepayment) is the neutral term. Which word appears in your contract decides what happens to the money if the deal fails, so choose it deliberately and have a lawyer check it.</p>
    </div>

      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">

          <span class="card-py">wěikuǎn</span>

            <div class="ex-py">Wěikuǎn wǒmen jiàn tídān fùběn fù, zhè shì wǒmen de biāozhǔn tiáokuǎn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">We pay the balance against a copy of the bill of lading; that's our standard term.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">验货通过以后再付尾款。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Yànhuò tōngguò yǐhòu zài fù wěikuǎn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The balance is paid after inspection passes.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">电汇</span>
          <span class="card-py">diànhuì</span>
          <span class="card-en">wire transfer (T/T)</span>
        </div>

        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">付款方式是电汇，银行手续费各付各的。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Fùkuǎn fāngshì shì diànhuì, yínháng shǒuxùfèi gè fù gè de.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Payment is by T/T, and each side pays its own bank charges.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">水单</span>
          <span class="card-py">shuǐdān</span>
          <span class="card-en">bank remittance slip</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">The bank's proof that you sent the money, also 汇款水单. The factory will ask for it the moment you say you have paid, and production often waits for it. Send it promptly; it is the fastest way to start the lead-time clock.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">定金今天已经汇出，水单发您微信了，请查收。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Dìngjīn jīntiān yǐjīng huìchū, shuǐdān fā nín Wēixìn le, qǐng cháshōu.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The deposit went out today; I've sent you the remittance slip on WeChat, please check.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">账户</span>
          <span class="card-py">zhànghù</span>
          <span class="card-en">bank account</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">收款账户 (shōukuǎn zhànghù) is the receiving account. Fraudsters impersonate suppliers by email and announce new bank details. Treat any 换账户 (huàn zhànghù, change of account) message as suspect until confirmed by phone or video with a person you know, and check that the account name matches the company on the contract.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">账户变更我们必须电话确认，收款人要和合同上的公司名称一致。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhànghù biàngēng wǒmen bìxū diànhuà quèrèn, shōukuǎnrén yào hé hétong shang de gōngsī míngchēng yízhì.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">We must confirm any change of account by phone, and the payee must match the company name on the contract.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="haggle"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">讲价</span>
      <span class="sh-py">jiǎngjià</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Haggling Lines</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="让">
      <div class="scholar-label">有来有往 yǒu lái yǒu wǎng · Give and Take</div>
      <p>A factory expects to be asked for a better price, and its first quote usually leaves room for the request. Asking flatly and repeatedly for a lower number tends to produce a cheaper material rather than a smaller margin. The more productive move is the trade: offer something the factory values, such as a larger quantity, a faster deposit, a longer forecast, or fewer colours, and ask for a price in return. The softeners that keep this courteous, 能不能 (néng bù néng), 麻烦 (máfan), and 我们也理解 (wǒmen yě lǐjiě, "we understand too"), are covered on the meeting-room page; the lines below are the price-specific ones.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">您会听到 nín huì tīngdào · What You Will Hear Back</span>
      <strong>这已经是最低价了 zhè yǐjīng shì zuì dī jià le</strong> → "this is already the lowest price": sometimes true, often the opening line of the next round<br>
      <strong>我们几乎没有利润 wǒmen jīhū méiyǒu lìrùn</strong> → "we make almost no profit": a standard line, not a statement of accounts<br>
      <strong>原材料涨了 yuáncáiliào zhǎng le</strong> → "materials went up": ask by how much and which material<br>
      <strong>量大可以谈 liàng dà kěyǐ tán</strong> → "with volume we can talk": name a volume and get a number<br>
      <strong>我去申请一下 wǒ qù shēnqǐng yíxià</strong> → "let me apply for it": the salesperson needs the boss's sign-off, and is likely on your side
    </div>

        </div>
        <div class="card-def">The basic request, best softened. 价格有点高 (the price is a bit high) states the problem without accusing; 还有空间吗 (is there still room?) invites a counteroffer without naming one.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">

        </div>
        <div class="card-def">Naming your target moves the discussion from "lower" to "can you reach this." Give it with a reason, such as a retail price point or a competing quote (另一家的报价 lìng yì jiā de bàojià), without naming the other factory.</div>

          </div>

        </div>
        <div class="card-def">让 is "to yield." 让利 (rànglì) is giving up margin; 降价 (jiàngjià) is simply lowering the price, 涨价 (zhǎngjià) raising it, 还价 (huánjià) making a counteroffer. 底价 (dǐjià) is the floor price a side will not go below.</div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="numbers"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">数字</span>
      <span class="sh-py">shùzì</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Numbers That Trip You Up</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="万">
      <div class="scholar-label">按万数 àn wàn shǔ · Counting in Ten-Thousands</div>
      <p>English groups digits in thousands; Chinese groups them in ten-thousands. 万 (wàn) is 10,000 and 亿 (yì) is 100,000,000, so 100,000 is 十万 (shí wàn, "ten ten-thousands") and a million is 一百万. Quantities and totals in a factory meeting are spoken this way, and a slip of one place is a tenfold error. The safe habit is to write every number down and ask the other side to read it back. Percentages and discounts carry their own trap, below: 打八折 does not mean 80% off.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">大数 dà shù · Large Numbers</span>
      <strong>3,000</strong> → 三千 sānqiān<br>
      <strong>12,000</strong> → 一万二 yíwàn èr, or 一点二万 yī diǎn èr wàn<br>
      <strong>50,000</strong> → 五万 wǔwàn<br>
      <strong>100,000</strong> → 十万 shíwàn<br>
      <strong>1,000,000</strong> → 一百万 yìbǎi wàn<br>
      <strong>10,000,000</strong> → 一千万 yìqiān wàn<br>
      <strong>100,000,000</strong> → 一亿 yí yì
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">折和点 zhé hé diǎn · Discounts and Points</span>
      <strong>打九折 dǎ jiǔ zhé</strong> → pay 90% of the price (10% off)<br>
      <strong>打八五折 dǎ bā wǔ zhé</strong> → pay 85% (15% off)<br>
      <strong>九五折 jiǔ wǔ zhé</strong> → pay 95% (5% off)<br>
      <strong>降三个点 jiàng sān ge diǎn</strong> → lower by three percent<br>
      <strong>百分之三 bǎi fēn zhī sān</strong> → 3%, the formal way to say it<br>
      <strong>翻一倍 fān yí bèi</strong> → to double
    </div>

      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">块 · 毛 · 分</span>
          <span class="card-py">kuài · máo · fēn</span>
          <span class="card-en">units of currency, spoken</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">Spoken prices use 块 for the main unit, 毛 for tenths, 分 for hundredths; written prices use 元, 角, 分. The trailing unit is often dropped: 四块一 is 4.10, 三块八五 is 3.85. Factories apply the same words to dollars, so add 美金 (měijīn) or 人民币 (rénmínbì) when it could be either.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">您刚才说的是三块八五人民币，还是三块八五美金？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nín gāngcái shuō de shì sān kuài bā wǔ rénmínbì, háishi sān kuài bā wǔ měijīn?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Did you just say 3.85 in RMB, or 3.85 in US dollars?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">打折</span>
          <span class="card-py">dǎzhé</span>
          <span class="card-en">to give a discount</span>
        </div>
        <div class="card-def">折 counts tenths of the price you pay, not the amount taken off. 八折 means you pay eight tenths, a 20% discount. Learners hear "eight" and think "80% off." In a negotiation 点 (diǎn, a percentage point) is more common than 折 for small moves.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">第二个订单能打九五折吗？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Dì-èr ge dìngdān néng dǎ jiǔ wǔ zhé ma?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Could the second order get 5% off?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">汇率</span>
          <span class="card-py">huìlǜ</span>
          <span class="card-en">exchange rate</span>
